# C909 — four-slot Plücker-descent defect: priority and exact local quotient

Date: 2026-08-11  
Status: bounded literature/concept audit; no manuscript, PDF, mirror, Lean,
or commit change

## Immediate verdict

The quotient

\[
   (\mathbf Z/p^a)^{\binom g4}
\tag{A}
\]

is **not** a theorem supplied verbatim by integral `SL_2` invariant theory or
standard monomial theory.  What is classical is the unweighted Plücker
module: over `R = Z` the degree-two, four-distinct-slot piece of the
`SL_2` minor algebra is free of rank two, with two standard matching
monomials and one coefficient-`±1` Plücker relation.  The exponent `p^a` in
(A) is a new (and elementary) Smith-factor calculation for the graph-
isogeny lattice.  It is a lattice-under-isogeny statement, not a rational FFT
statement.

More precisely, in the equal-depth split chart over an unramified DVR `O`
with `v(p)=1`, the four-slot graded summand for each
`S={i,j,k,l}` is

\[
 \frac{(W_S\cap\bigwedge^4 L_O)}
 {\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{Sym}^2NS(A)\otimes O)\cap W_S}
 \simeq O/p^aO,
\tag{B}
\]

and the direct sum over the `\binom g4` slot sets is
`(O/p^aO)^{\binom g4}`.  Faithfully flat descent gives the corresponding
`(Z_p/p^a)^{\binom g4}` four-slot summand when the graph lattice and both
submodules descend.  This is a statement about the four-distinct-slot
piece; a claim about the whole codimension-two defect additionally requires
the already separate checks for repeated-slot pieces.

## Exact Smith calculation

For four split slots write `δ_rs=t_s-t_r`, with every `δ_rs` a unit.  Put
`A=Ω_12Ω_34` and `B=Ω_13Ω_24`, where

\[
 Ω_{rs}=p^{-a}B_{rs}+p^{-2a}δ_{rs}X_rX_s,
 \qquad B_{rs}=X_rY_s+X_sY_r.
\]

The two ordinary divisor-product generators are
`C_1=p^{4a}A` and `C_2=p^{4a}B`; the third matching is an integral
`±1`-linear combination by the Plücker relation.  In the exterior basis,
the coefficient row of `X_1X_2X_3X_4` is

\[
   (δ_{12}δ_{34},\,-δ_{13}δ_{24}),
\]

while the row of `X_1Y_2X_3X_4` is

\[
   p^a(δ_{34},\,δ_{13}).
\]

Every row of `(C_1,C_2)` containing one `Y` is divisible by `p^a`, and
every row containing two `Y`'s by `p^{2a}`; modulo `p^a` the two columns
therefore have rank at most one.  The displayed two-by-two minor is

\[
 p^aδ_{13}δ_{34}(δ_{12}+δ_{24})
 =p^aδ_{13}δ_{34}δ_{14},
\]

a unit times `p^a`.  Thus the row lattice has Smith invariants `1,p^a`.
Its coefficient-dual (the integral Hodge lattice inside the rational
two-dimensional matching space) contains the product lattice with quotient
`O/p^aO`.  This proves (B), not merely the existence of one non-product
class.

The explicit primitive class can be taken as

\[
 Q=δ_{13}δ_{24}Ω_{12}Ω_{34}+δ_{12}δ_{34}Ω_{13}Ω_{24},
 \qquad H=p^{3a}Q.
\]

The leading `p^{-4a}X_1X_2X_3X_4` terms cancel.  A correction to the earlier
fixed-dimension audit is important: the second matching **does** contribute
to `X_1Y_2X_3X_4`, through `X_1X_3` in `Ω_13` and `X_4Y_2` in `Ω_24`.
The total coefficient in `H` is
`δ_13δ_34(δ_24+δ_12)=δ_13δ_34δ_14`, a unit.  The prior sentence saying
that this monomial came only from the first matching was inaccurate; the
conclusion remains correct and the corrected statement is recorded in
`notes/2026-08-11-c909-fixed-dimension-hodge-lattice-audit.md`.

## What is classical, and what is not

For the standard lattice `V_Z\otimes Z^g`, the diagonal `SL_2` invariant
contractions are the `2\times2` minors.  The relevant polynomial invariant
algebra is the Plücker algebra of `Gr(2,g)`.  De Concini--Eisenbud--Procesi,
*Hodge algebras* (Astérisque 91, 1982), §11, explicitly allows `R=Z` and
states Theorem 11.1: the subring generated by the maximal minors of a
generic matrix is an ordinal Hodge algebra.  Their Hodge-1 axiom gives a
free `R`-basis of standard monomials and Hodge-2 gives integral straightening
relations.  In the four-slot degree-two component this is exactly rank two
plus the usual coefficient-`±1` matching relation.

That theorem concerns the coordinate/minor algebra for a fixed free module.
The graph lattice

\[
 L_O=\bigoplus_r O\langle X_r,Y_r\rangle,
 \quad X_r=p^ax_r,\quad Y_r=y_r-t_rx_r,
\]

is generally not an `SL_2(Z_p)`-stable copy of the standard module.  The
integral Hodge module is an intersection after this rational shear and
rescaling, while the divisor products are a weighted image.  Standard
monomial freeness does not preserve this intersection or its Smith factors.
Consequently it explains the rank/torsion-free Plücker skeleton and the
coefficient-`±1` relation, but it does not imply (A).

The rational FFT likewise says only

\[
 I_A^2\otimes Q=P_A^2\otimes Q,
\]

where `I_A^2` is the rational `SL_2`-invariant Hodge space and `P_A^2` is
the divisor-product image.  It is silent on the finite quotient of integral
lattices.  The latter is exactly what the graph calculation detects.

## Priority wording

Safe wording is:

> The Plücker generators, matching relation, and unweighted standard-
> monomial basis are classical over `Z`.  The `p^a` four-slot Smith factor,
> its direct-sum multiplicity `\binom g4`, and its interpretation as the
> defect between the graph-isogeny integral Hodge lattice and divisor
> products are a new application/lattice calculation here.  We found no
> source stating this weighted finite-etale graph quotient.

Do **not** say that integral invariant theory proves (A), or that the exact
quotient is known from the rational FFT.  Do say that the mechanism is a
weighted Plücker descent defect built on classical integral straightening.

## Bottom line

The exact four-slot quotient is proved by the local weighted coefficient
matrix and its Smith invariants.  DCEP's integral Hodge-algebra theorem is
the correct classical predecessor for the unweighted Plücker/standard-
monomial part, but no located source subsumes the graph-lattice rescaling,
the cancellation, or the descended quotient (A).
