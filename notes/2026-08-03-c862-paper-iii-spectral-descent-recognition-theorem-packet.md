## Purpose and recommendation

The spectral-algebra vision is landable without completing the integral model or
solving the remaining marking problem.  It should not be forced into one oversized
theorem.  The clean publication unit is a theorem--proposition--converse packet:

1. **golden spectral descent:** the marked incidence residue algebra is the
   coefficient algebra generated by the conference operator;
2. **norm factorization:** the commutator determinant is the quadratic norm of the
   determinant of the diagonal-linearity defect;
3. **shadow recognition:** on the scalar sign locus, nonzero coincidence of the
   triangle and Pfaffian shadows recovers the unique order-six conference class and
   its outer orientation.

The first two statements synthesize mathematics already present in Paper III.  The
third is the theorem proved in C809.  What is new is the exact composition: the
quadratic fibre does not merely label the two golden eigenspaces; it acts on the
six-axis carrier, and the cubic is the oriented square root of the norm of the failure
of the diagonal algebra to respect that action.

## Conventions and hypotheses

Let

\[
 E=\mathbf Q[t]/(t^2-t-1),\qquad \sigma(t)=1-t,
\]

and let \(W=\mathbf Q^6\) with its standard symmetric pairing.  Let \(C\in
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbf Q}(W)\) be the symmetric, zero-diagonal, marked
order-six conference operator used in Paper III, so

\[
 C^2=5I.
\]

For \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_6)\), write \(D_x=\operatorname{diag}(x_1,\ldots,x_6)\).
Over \(E=\mathbf Q(\sqrt5)\), with \(\sqrt5=2t-1\), put

\[
 P_\pm=\frac12\left(I\pm\frac C{\sqrt5}\right),\qquad
 V_\pm=P_\pm(W\otimes_{\mathbf Q}E),\qquad
 B_x=P_-D_xP_+\colon V_+\longrightarrow V_-.
\]

The phrase **marked incidence residue algebra** means the quadratic algebra already
computed by Paper III at the marked fibre \([xyz]\), together with the paper's chosen
bridge from that fibre to \(C\).  Nothing below removes the need for that bridge.

## Theorem I — golden spectral descent

**Theorem.**  There is an isomorphism of quadratic \(\mathbf Q\)-algebras

\[
 \Phi:E\stackrel{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf Q[C],
 \qquad
 \Phi(t)=\frac{I+C}{2}.
\]

It has the following properties.

1. The nontrivial automorphism is carried to the restriction of
   \(C\mapsto-C\):
   \[
   \Phi(\sigma(t))=I-\Phi(t)=\frac{I-C}{2}.
   \]
2. The rational carrier \(W\) is a vector space of rank three over
   \(\mathbf Q[C]\).
3. Replacing \(C\) by \(-C\) does not change the embedded subalgebra, while a
   switching or relabelling \(C'=SCS^{-1}\) conjugates it:
   \[
   \mathbf Q[-C]=\mathbf Q[C],\qquad
   \mathbf Q[C']=S\mathbf Q[C]S^{-1}.
   \]
   Hence the unoriented quadratic algebra is intrinsic up to conjugacy, but its
   oriented generator is relative to the marking.
4. After base change from \(\mathbf Q\) to \(E\), the two embeddings of the
   quadratic algebra split \(W\otimes E\) as \(V_+\oplus V_-\), and
   \(\sigma(V_+)=V_-\).

### Proof

The matrix \(C\) is not scalar and satisfies \(C^2=5I\).  Since \(X^2-5\) is
irreducible over \(\mathbf Q\), it is the minimal polynomial of \(C\), so

\[
 \mathbf Q[C]\simeq\mathbf Q[s]/(s^2-5).
\]

Set \(\theta=(I+C)/2\).  Directly,

\[
 \theta^2=\frac{I+2C+C^2}{4}
 =\frac{3I+C}{2}=\theta+I.
\]

Thus \(t\mapsto\theta\) defines a homomorphism \(E\to\mathbf Q[C]\).  It is
surjective because \(C=2\theta-I\), and both sides have dimension two over
\(\mathbf Q\), hence it is an isomorphism.  The involution formula follows from
\(\sigma(t)=1-t\).  Because \(E\) acts faithfully on the six-dimensional rational
space \(W\), dimension division gives \(\dim_EW=6/2=3\).  The switching and
relabelling assertions are immediate from conjugation.  Finally,

\[
 P_++P_-=I,\quad P_+P_-=0,\quad C P_\pm=\pm\sqrt5P_\pm,
\]

and \(\sigma(\sqrt5)=-\sqrt5\), so \(\sigma(P_+)=P_-\).

## Proposition II — the commutator determinant is a quadratic norm

**Proposition.**  With the conventions above, \(B_x=P_-D_xP_+\) is the cross
component of the failure of the rational diagonal operator \(D_x\) to be linear over
the golden coefficient algebra.  More precisely,

\[
 [D_x,C]
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 0&-2\sqrt5\,B_x^{\mathsf T}\\
 2\sqrt5\,B_x&0
 \end{pmatrix}
 \quad\text{on }V_+\oplus V_-.
\]

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{\det[D_x,C]=8000\,\mathcal N(\det B_x),}
\]

where \(\mathcal N(\det B_x)\in\mathbf Q\) is the determinant-line norm defined
below.  In compatible conjugate oriented bases it is written

\[
 \mathcal N(\det B_x)=\det(B_x)^2.
\]

For the Hodge/Pfaffian orientation used in Paper III,

\[
 \operatorname{Pf}[D_x,C]=4Z_C(x),\qquad
 \det[D_x,C]=16Z_C(x)^2,
\]

and therefore, after orienting the determinant lines,

\[
 \boxed{Z_C(x)=\pm10\sqrt5\det(B_x).}
\]

The sign in the last display is orientation data; the norm and squared identities do
not depend on it.

### Determinant-line formulation

There is no canonical scalar \(\det(B_x)\) before bases are chosen.  Its invariant
home is

\[
 \delta_x:=\det(B_x)\in
 \mathcal L:=\det_E(V_-)\otimes_E\det_E(V_+)^{-1}.
\]

Galois conjugation exchanges \(V_+\) and \(V_-\), so
\(\sigma\mathcal L\simeq\mathcal L^{-1}\).  The standard symmetric pairing and
the symmetry of \(D_x\) identify \(\sigma(B_x)\) with \(B_x^{\mathsf T}\).  Hence

\[
 \mathcal N(\delta_x):=
 \delta_x\otimes\sigma(\delta_x)
 \in\mathcal L\otimes_E\mathcal L^{-1}\simeq E
\]

is fixed by \(\sigma\) and therefore lies in \(\mathbf Q\).  This is the invariant
meaning of the square in the block determinant.

### Proof of the normalization

The projector relations give

\[
 P_-[D_x,C]P_+=2\sqrt5P_-D_xP_+=2\sqrt5B_x,
\]

and symmetry gives the opposite block.  For two three-dimensional summands,

\[
 \det
 \begin{pmatrix}0&-aB_x^{\mathsf T}\\aB_x&0\end{pmatrix}
 =a^6\det(B_x)^2.
\]

Taking \(a=2\sqrt5\) gives

\[
 (2\sqrt5)^6=64\cdot125=8000.
\]

Paper III's existing Pfaffian/triangle normalization then gives
\(16Z_C^2=8000\det(B_x)^2\), or
\(Z_C^2=500\det(B_x)^2\), which is the asserted oriented formula.

## Theorem III — the shadow recognizes the operator

For a symmetric zero-diagonal \(6\times6\) matrix \(A=(a_{ij})\), define

\[
 T_A(x)=\sum_{|S|=3}\tau_Sx_S,
 \qquad
 \tau_{\{i,j,k\}}=a_{ij}a_{jk}a_{ki},
\]

and, with the paper's Hodge orientation,

\[
 H_A(x)=\operatorname{Pf}[D_x,A].
\]

Equivalently, the coefficient of \(x_S\) is

\[
 h_S=-\operatorname{sgn}(S^c,S)\det A[S^c,S].
\]

**Theorem.**  Let the ground field have characteristic different from two, suppose
all off-diagonal entries of \(A\) are nonzero, and suppose

\[
 H_A=\mu T_A,\qquad \mu\ne0.
\]

Then \(A^2=\lambda I\) for some scalar \(\lambda\).

If, moreover, \(A=sB\) over a characteristic-zero field, with \(s\ne0\) and all
off-diagonal entries of \(B\) in \(\{\pm1\}\), then

\[
 H_A\text{ is a nonzero scalar multiple of }T_A
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B^2=5I.
\]

Up to switching and relabelling, \(B\) is the unique symmetric conference matrix of
order six.  In the gauge \(b_{0i}=1\), its twelve labelled pentagons split into six
solutions of \(H_B=4T_B\) and six solutions of \(H_B=-4T_B\).  The sign records the
two outer orientations.

### Proof

Translation by the all-ones vector does not change the commutator:

\[
 [D_{x+u\mathbf1},A]=[D_x,A].
\]

Thus \(H_A\), and by nonzero proportionality \(T_A\), is translation invariant.  The
coefficient of \(u x_ix_j\) in
\(T_A(x+u\mathbf1)-T_A(x)\) is

\[
 \sum_{k\ne i,j}\tau_{ijk}
 =a_{ij}\sum_k a_{ik}a_{kj}
 =a_{ij}(A^2)_{ij}.
\]

Every \(a_{ij}\) is nonzero, so all off-diagonal entries of \(A^2\) vanish.  Write
\(A^2=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_6)\).  Since \(A\) commutes with \(A^2\),

\[
 a_{ij}(d_j-d_i)=0,
\]

and connectivity of the nonzero support gives \(d_1=\cdots=d_6=\lambda\).

On the sign locus, the diagonal entries of \(B^2\) are all five, so \(B^2=5I\).
After switching to \(b_{0i}=1\), the equations \((B^2)_{0i}=0\) say that every
vertex in the positive-edge graph on \(\{1,\ldots,5\}\) has degree two.  That graph
is a pentagon.  All labelled pentagons form one \(S_5\)-orbit, hence one switching
and relabelling class.  Its dihedral stabilizer lies in \(A_5\), so the orbit splits
into two six-element orientation classes under even relabelling.  The Hodge compound
changes sign under odd relabelling while the triangle table does not, giving the two
ratios \(\pm4\).  Conversely, complementary-minor expansion on the standard
pentagon representative gives \(H_B=4T_B\), and odd relabelling gives the other
orientation.

### Why the recognition occurs only in order six

For an even \(n\times n\) operator, \(\operatorname{Pf}[D_x,A]\) has degree
\(n/2\), whereas the triangle shadow has degree three.  Unhomogenized
proportionality therefore forces

\[
 n/2=3,\qquad n=6.
\]

Order six is the unique degree at which this triangle/Pfaffian recognition can occur
without auxiliary homogenizing data.

## Corollary — the closed source-to-shadow loop

Relative to the marked Paper III bridge, the three results compose as

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{gathered}
 \text{incidence residue algebra }E
 \simeq\mathbf Q[C]\\
 \Downarrow\\[-2mm]
 W\text{ is a rank-three }E\text{-module}\\
 \Downarrow\\[-2mm]
 \det[D_x,C]=8000\,\mathcal N(\det B_x),\quad
 \operatorname{Pf}[D_x,C]=4Z_C\\
 \Downarrow\\[-2mm]
 \text{nonzero triangle/Pfaffian coincidence recovers the conference class.}
 \end{gathered}}
\]

This is the strongest quotable Paper III upgrade currently available.  It makes a
classical conference normal form the conclusion of the paper's shadow criterion,
while retaining Hitchin's fibre geometry and the HMSV relations as credited inputs.

## Integral corollary and its hard boundary

On the raw coordinate lattice \(\mathbf Z^6\), the order generated by \(C\) is

\[
 \mathbf Z[C]\simeq\mathbf Z[\sqrt5]
 \subsetneq
 \mathcal O_E=\mathbf Z\!\left[\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\right]
\]

with index two.  The maximal-order generator \((I+C)/2\) is not integral in the raw
coordinate basis.  This gives a precise conductor-two seam and a legitimate bridge
to Paper I.

It does **not** yet prove that Paper III's geometric integral lattice is the raw
coordinate lattice, nor that the full incidence Stein algebra identifies with the
maximal order over every base.  Those stronger statements require the explicit
integral model comparison, base-change control, and a paper-specific lattice map.

## Claims the packet deliberately does not make

- It does not canonically identify four oriented sign torsors.  The chart lift and
  cross-identification of the five-labelled systems remain marked inputs.
- It does not define a global action of the incidence algebra on a family over the
  whole base; the theorem is at the distinguished rational fibre.
- It does not recover the HMSV Segre--Igusa relations from the conference shadow.
- It does not classify all weighted real or complex solutions of \(H_A=\mu T_A\).
  It proves the quadratic relation globally on the nonzero-edge torus, the golden
  classification on the sign locus, and local rigidity at the two golden points.
- It does not make the Pfaffian and compound shadows independent conditions; their
  equality is universal.
- It does not make the cross-golden determinant an additional recognition condition;
  defining its block already presupposes the quadratic splitting.
- It does not identify Paper I, II, and III's cubics.  That is Rosetta work for the
  eventual Paper V, not a condition on Paper III.

## EJ + Tao stress pass

The cheap strengthening is the determinant-line formulation.  It prevents the central
norm claim from depending on an invisible choice of eigenbases and explains exactly
where the outer-orientation sign enters.  The second strengthening is logical order:
state recognition after the norm factorization, but prove its causal core by
translation invariance.  This avoids presenting the finite sign census as the reason
the theorem is true.

The main attempted overcompression fails cleanly.  The same involution that makes
\(\mathbf Q[C]=\mathbf Q[-C]\) intrinsic erases the oriented generator.  Therefore
the algebra bridge descends farther than the sign bridge, and the paper should treat
that asymmetry as structure rather than as a remaining cosmetic defect.
