# C923 — the pencil's Eckardt locus, proved from reflection groups

**Lane:** `cubic-threefolds` · **Date:** 2026-08-19 · **Task:** C923

## Result

The Gröbner elimination behind `prop:A5-not-coprime` is gone from the proof.
The Eckardt locus of the nonstandard `A_5`-cubic pencil is now determined by a
classification a referee can check by hand, and the proposition proves more
than it did: exactly two members, interchanged by the normalizer of `A_5`, each
carrying exactly thirty Eckardt points, both projectively equivalent to the
Fermat cubic threefold.

The mechanism in one sentence: an Eckardt point is the centre of a reflection
fixing the defining form, so a cubic threefold whose automorphism group acts
irreducibly has its form invariant under an irreducible complex reflection
group of rank five, and three is an invariant degree of only two such groups.

## The proof

Three steps, none of them computational.

**1. Eckardt points are reflection centres** (`lem:eckardt-involution`). Call
`r` in `GL_5(C)` a reflection if it has order two and fixes a hyperplane
pointwise, and its centre the line on which it acts by `-1`. For a smooth
cubic threefold `X = {F = 0}`, sending `r` with `F ∘ r = F` to its centre is a
bijection onto the Eckardt points of `X`.

Forward: invariance leaves only even powers of `x_0`, so `F = x_0^2 L + C` with
`L`, `C` in the other four variables, the centre lies on `X`, the Hessian there
has the coefficients of `L` in its first row and column and nothing else, so
rank at most two, and `L ≠ 0` by smoothness. Backward: at an Eckardt point,
`F = x_0^2 L + x_0 L R + C`, and the substitution `x_0 → x_0 − R/2` removes the
odd part, after which `x_0 → −x_0` is the reflection. Injectivity: a second
reflection with the same centre has mirror `{x_0 = ℓ}`, and invariance in the
coordinate `u = x_0 − ℓ` kills the term `2 u ℓ L`, forcing `ℓ = 0`.

The correspondence is with the *linear* reflection, not with its class in
`PGL_5(C)`. Of the two lifts of an Eckardt involution exactly one fixes `F`,
and it is the one with a single eigenvalue `-1`; the other multiplies `F` by
`-1`, which for a smooth `X` cannot happen to a reflection.

**2. Irreducible symmetry forces Fermat** (`prop:eckardt-reflection-group`).
Let `X` be a smooth cubic threefold whose automorphism group acts irreducibly
on `C^5`, with an Eckardt point. Let `R` be generated by all reflections
fixing `F`. Then `F` is an `R`-invariant of degree three, and every reflection
of `R` fixes `F`, so it is itself one of the generators — the Eckardt points
are in bijection with the reflections of `R`, not merely with a generating set
of them.

`R` is finite: its image in `PGL_5(C)` sits in the finite `Aut(X)`, and every
generator has determinant `-1`, so a scalar in `R` is a tenth root of unity.

`R` is irreducible. The group of matrices carrying `F` to a multiple of itself
normalizes `R`, hence permutes the isotypic components of `C^5`; the sum over
an orbit is invariant, so the permutation is transitive and the number of
components divides five. Five components would make `R` diagonal, its
reflections the single-coordinate sign changes, and transitivity would put all
five in `R` — but a cubic form invariant under all five is even in every
variable, hence zero. One component whose constituent is a line would make `R`
scalar, with no reflection. So `C^5` is irreducible under `R`, which is
therefore an irreducible complex reflection group of rank five.

Its invariant ring is polynomial on generators of degree at least two, so a
degree-three invariant is a scalar multiple of a generator of degree three.
The irreducible complex reflection groups of rank five are `G(1,1,6)`, the
`G(m,p,5)` with `m ≥ 2` and `p | m`, and `G_33`, with degrees `2,3,4,5,6`, then
`m,2m,3m,4m,5m/p`, and `4,6,10,12,18`. Three occurs only for `G(1,1,6)` and
for `m = 3`; the last degree `5m/p` cannot be three, since that needs `p > m`.
`G(1,1,6)` is the symmetric group on six letters on the sum-zero subspace of
`C^6`, whose degree-three invariant is `e_3` and whose zero locus is the Segre
cubic threefold, with ten nodes — excluded by smoothness. `G(3,p,5)` has
degree-three invariant `y_1^3 + ... + y_5^3`. So `X` is the Fermat cubic
threefold.

The count follows from the same data. `R` is generated by order-two
reflections, and those of `G(3,1,5)` generate `G(3,3,5)`, whose degrees are
`3,6,9,12,5`; an irreducible reflection group has `Σ(d_i − 1)` reflections,
here `2+5+8+11+4 = 30`.

**3. Exactly two members** (`prop:A5-not-coprime`). Every member of the pencil
is `A_5`-invariant with `A_5` irreducible on `W_5`, so step 2 applies to each
smooth member; the pencil contains the Fermat cubic threefold, which is
separated and so has an Eckardt point. Hence the members with an Eckardt point
are exactly those projectively equivalent to the Fermat cubic threefold, and
they are one moduli point.

To count them, write `A` for the image of `A_5` in `PGL(W_5)` and `N` for its
normalizer. Schur's lemma makes the centralizer scalar, so `N` embeds in
`Aut(A_5) ≅ S_5` with `A` as the inner automorphisms, and the six-point model
realizes the outer one through `PGL(2,F_5)`; thus `[N : A] = 2`. Any two
irreducibly acting copies of `A_5` in `PGL_5(C)` are conjugate, since a
five-dimensional projective representation of `A_5` is linear — the faithful
representations of `2.A_5` have dimensions two, four and six — the degree-five
character is unique, and it is fixed by `Aut(A_5)`.

Sending a parameter to the `Aut(X_F)`-class of the transported `A` is then a
surjection onto the classes of irreducibly acting copies of `A_5` in
`Aut(X_F)`, with the `N`-orbits as fibres. With `Aut(X_F) = C_3^4 ⋊ S_5`
(Wei–Yu, Example 3.1), a copy of `A_5` meets `C_3^4` trivially, so it is a
complement, and complements are classified by

    H^1(A_5, C_3^4) = H^1(A_5, F_3^5) = H^1(A_4, F_3) = Hom(A_4, F_3) ≅ F_3,

using that `F_3^5` splits off its diagonal line because five is invertible mod
three, that `A_5` has no map to `F_3`, and Shapiro's lemma. The trivial class
is the reducible permutation copy; an element over a transposition inverts
`H^1`, because conjugation by a transposition inverts the cyclic quotient of
`A_4`, so the two nontrivial classes fuse. One class survives, so the Fermat
parameters form one `N`-orbit. It has two elements: an `N`-fixed member would
put an irreducibly acting `S_5` inside `Aut(X_F)`, and
`H^1(S_5, C_3^4) = H^1(S_4, F_3) = 0` leaves only the reducible permutation
copy.

## The other-dimension check

The argument uses the dimension only at the end. For a smooth cubic
hypersurface in `P^N` with `N ≥ 3` and irreducibly acting automorphism group,
an Eckardt point forces the Fermat cubic or the diagonal cubic
`{e_1 = e_3 = 0}`, the degree-three invariant of the symmetric group on `N+2`
letters on the sum-zero subspace of `C^{N+2}`.

The diagonal cubic is singular exactly when `N` is even. A singular point needs
`∇e_3` proportional to `∇e_1`; with `e_1 = 0` the `i`-th entry of `∇e_3` is
`e_2 + x_i^2`, so all `x_i^2` agree, the coordinates are `±t` in equal numbers,
and that needs `N+2` even.

- `N = 3`: both survive, as the Fermat and Clebsch cubic surfaces. Their
  reflection counts `Σ(d_i − 1)` are eighteen for `G(3,3,4)` with degrees
  `3,6,9,4` and ten for `S_5` with degrees `2,3,4,5` — the classical Eckardt
  counts of those two surfaces.
- `N = 4`: the diagonal cubic is the Segre cubic threefold, singular, and only
  Fermat survives, with `3·4·5/2 = 30`.
- `N = 2` is genuinely different and the restriction is needed: `G(3,3,3)` has
  degree three twice, its degree-three invariants are the Hesse pencil, and
  every smooth plane cubic has inflection points accordingly.

Reproducing 10, 18 and 30 from the degrees of three different reflection groups
is the strongest available check that the mechanism is the right one.

## Mystery ledger

- **Why the Eckardt condition cuts out exactly the extra-symmetry locus.**
  Settled, and this was the open question C921 left. The Eckardt condition is
  not a hundred equations that happen to have a small solution set; it is the
  existence of a reflection in the automorphism group, and reflections are
  classified. The extra symmetry is not a by-product of the Eckardt points —
  it is the same object.
- **The factor `b` in the elimination.** Settled. `b(b^2+3b+9)` is not one
  answer with a spurious root: the two factors are the two branches of the same
  classification, `G(1,1,6)` giving the Segre cubic at `b = 0` and `G(3,3,5)`
  giving the Fermat pair. The manuscript now says so.
- **Thirty Eckardt points.** Settled. Thirty is `Σ(d_i − 1)` for the degrees
  `3,6,9,12,5` of `G(3,3,5)`, so the count is read off the same table that
  identifies the member. C921 had it only as a detector output matching a
  Fermat control.
- **Why the Klein cubic has none.** Settled in passing. `PSL(2,11)` acting on
  `W_5` has character value one on its involutions, so they have eigenvalue
  multiplicities three and two and none of them is a reflection. The same
  computation on `A_5` itself gives value one, and on the odd elements of
  `PGL(2,F_5)` value minus one, so no member's Eckardt point can come from the
  pencil's own symmetry.
- **`7b^2+3b+9` beside `b^2+3b+9`.** Still open, and untouched here. Both
  discriminants are `-3` times a square, which is what makes the singular-member
  count and the Eckardt-member rationality switch together at `q mod 3`; whether
  the near-agreement of coefficients means anything is unexplained. Nothing
  depends on it. Owner: none allocated.
- **The possible Eckardt counts of an arbitrary smooth cubic threefold.** Open,
  and newly reachable. Without the irreducibility hypothesis the group generated
  by the Eckardt reflections is still a reflection group with a degree-three
  invariant, but a reducible one, so it is a product of irreducible factors on
  orthogonal summands plus a fixed space, and the Eckardt count is the sum of
  the factors' reflection counts. Enumerating the products whose invariant ring
  carries a degree-three element in five variables would give the full list of
  possible Eckardt counts, and in particular reprove the bound of thirty with no
  hypothesis. Logged to the discovery track; not allocated.
- **Whether `thm:separation-family` can now name the Fermat point.**
  Mathematically yes, and no computation stands in the way any longer. The
  remaining gate is the Lean side: sharpening the theorem's statement without
  sharpening `Applications.SeparationFamilyConclusion` would drop its claim-map
  row from a conditional deduction to a fragment. That is C910 work.
