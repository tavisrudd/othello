# C1109 — certified reconstruction and exact frame boundary

**Lane:** `continuation`. **Status:** complete.

## Results

The written recognition algorithm recovers tangent traces from four-clique seeds,
recovers the four pencils, completes a cyclic division table using missing rows
and columns, and tests generator images in a supplied finite field. Its coarse
bound is O(n^6) adjacency/field operations; checking a coordinate transport is
O(n²). Completeness is for true frame inputs; soundness on arbitrary inputs comes
from a final exhaustive adjacency/bijection check.

Exact finite classification (unordered four-pencil resolutions):

| q | vertices | full automorphisms | ambient | block cliques | parallel classes | resolutions |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 6 | 48 | 24 | 12 | 8 | 2 |
| 7 | 20 | 24 | 24 | 180 | 1352 | 1 |
| 8 | 30 | 144 | 72 | 48 | 16 | 2 |
| 9 | 42 | 48 | 48 | 28 | 4 | 1 |
| 11 | 72 | 24 | 24 | 36 | 4 | 1 |
| 13 | 110 | 24 | 24 | 44 | 4 | 1 |

At q=5 and q=8 all resolutions form a two-element orbit and the ambient group is
its sheet stabilizer. The certificate records generators and a representative
for the nonambient coset, which describes every nonambient automorphism. At q=5
the resolutions share all 12 blocks; at q=8 they share none. These are four-point
frames, not the six-point Clebsch seed at q=11.

## Evidence and replay

All files live in `papers/continuation-graph-rigidity/verification/` and are
exportable without private dependencies. `boundary.json`, `SHA256SUMS` and
`file-sizes.json` bind the certificate, exact generators, field models and code.
The source/checker manifest records SHA-256 hashes and byte counts, rather than
copying potentially stale hashes into this report.

From the paper root:

- `nix develop --command make check`
- `make boundary` (read-only Sage regeneration requiring certificate byte equality)
- `make benchmark` (fresh timings, not deterministic-byte evidence)

Generation used SageMath 10.7 and was byte-reproduced under the pinned SageMath 10.9
environment, using determinant incidence and the Sage automorphism
algorithm. Independent replay uses standard-library polynomial field arithmetic,
four-word adjacency, direct fixed-size clique search and nauty/dreadnaut group
orders. The exact-cover recursion is shared, openly stated in the public README.
It exhausts all vertex covers and edge covers with no budget cutoff. Every group
element generated by the recorded permutations is checked on the edge set;
nauty supplies an independent full-order upper bound. This is not Lean validation.

The pinned Nix environment, formulas for extension-field encodings, fixed domain,
search termination, seeds and trust boundary are in `verification/README.md`.
No extrapolation of finite checks supplies the q>=13 theorem.

## Algorithm validation and performance

The reference model implements prime fields and explicit orders 8 and 9; stable
recognition tests use q=13,17,19 with deterministic relabelling seeds 20260907+q.
It rejects duplicate coordinates, a cloned vertex, edge deletion, and a
specified degree-preserving switch. Arbitrary prime-power support is a property
of the supplied-field algorithm, not of this small reference field library.

Single-run samples in `benchmark.json` compare reconstruction with Sage isomorphism
at q=13,17,19, excluding graph construction. The prototype is slower on all three
controls. No speedup claim is made. The recorded sample was rerun after the final
certificate cardinality check and cloned-vertex rejection test were added. Timing
values are observational; exact mathematical outputs are separately replayed.

## ej/tt closeout and mystery ledger

- Settled: coordinate recovery need not enumerate arbitrary alphabet permutations;
  missing entries complete a cyclic division table, reducing candidates to generator images.
- Settled: q=7's 1352 local partitions do not imply global ambiguity; only one
  four-class exact cover exists.
- Partly settled: q=5 loses pencil grouping while retaining the blocks; q=8 loses
  the block system itself. The two exceptional index-two actions are fully checked.
- Open: a computation-free explanation of the q=8 exceptional structure and q=7
  uniqueness. This remains a manuscript open problem, not a release-proof gap.
- Open: a practically competitive recognizer. Polynomial time and representation
  savings alone do not establish an end-to-end performance benefit.

The initial Sage/Bliss cross-check was unavailable in this Nix build; nauty was
used instead. Exploratory scripts were replaced by the committed generator and
independent replay. No Lean command or build was run.
