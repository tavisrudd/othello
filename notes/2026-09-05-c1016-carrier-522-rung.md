# C1016 — scoring the carrier `Z/522` rung above an exact `q174` hit

**Lane**: `complete-ports` · **Task**: C1016 · 2026-09-05.

The predecessor (`2026-09-05-c1016-paired-transfer-and-the-two-opt-census.md`)
closed the `q174` neighbourhood in both directions — single transfers were
already exact and full, paired transfers are now exact, full and empty — and
named the one rung nothing had ever scored: the carrier `Z/522` itself, which
sits above an exact `q174` hit with correlation classes of its own. This is
that rung, built and measured.

Three results.

**The rung is 99.5% of the problem at the point the campaign has reached.**
Lift any of the twelve banked `q174` plateau states uniformly into the carrier
fibre above it and its carrier correlation error is about 365,000, of which
between 99.46% and 99.55% lives in the part no level of the ladder below the
carrier can see. That is the same reading the `q174` pass gave one level down,
at the next level up and with a different quotient.

**A `q174` hit is liftable in expectation, and the exact free rank is 173, not
174.** The carrier energy is identically on target and pins the class 174
through the `q174` energy, so an exact `q174` hit leaves 173 canonical classes
free rather than the 174 the class count suggests. Each costs about 4.55 bits
on the lattice the deviation actually lives on, against an orbit fibre of about
829 bits, leaving roughly `2^43` exact carrier solutions above one `q174` hit.
The rung is not a wall — but it is a second descent of about 786 bits, which is
larger than the whole `q174` descent below it.

**The rung searches, and it plateaus, and the wider move set loses again.** A
full-neighbourhood tabu step on the orbit arrangements drives the carrier error
from 365,000 to about 14,400 and stops there, insensitive to a hundredfold
per-lift budget range and to a run two and a half times longer. Widening the
move set from the orbit to the whole column — which is free to spend the `q174`
rung as well — is 2.0% *worse* at matched wall clock and loses four rounds of
five. That is the third time in this campaign that a strictly wider exact
neighbourhood has lost to the narrower one.

## The carrier, and what a `q174` hit leaves free

The whole ladder is one law: four blocks of length 522 whose combined periodic
autocorrelation is `2088` at the zero shift and `-4` at every other one, and
every registered level is that law aggregated over a subgroup of shifts. The
`q174` view is the last quotient before the carrier, with `Z/522 -> Z/174`
reduction and kernel `{0, 174, 348}`, so

```text
A174(g) = A522(g) + A522(g + 174) + A522(g + 348).
```

The carrier correlation is even, so it has 262 canonical shift classes against
the `q174` view's 88, and those 88 aggregation blocks partition the 262 classes
exactly: `{0, 174}`, `{87, 261}`, and one triple `{g, 174-g, g+174}` for each
`g` in `1..=86`. Each block is one linear condition on the deviation
`E = A522 - target`.

The naive free count is `262 - 88 = 174`. It is one too many. The carrier
energy `A522(0) = 2088` holds identically — it is the number of entries, not a
condition — so the block `{0, 174}` reads `2 E(174) = D(0)`, and the class 174
is pinned by the `q174` energy alone. The free rank above an exact `q174` hit
is therefore **173**, and the measurement sees exactly that: over 256 uniform
lifts of each of the twelve banked states, the classes that never move are
`{0, 174}` and no others, on every state.

In the character basis the split is the part of `E` that factors through
`Z/174` — whose value at `h` is `D(h mod 174) / 3` — against the part supported
on the characters whose order is divisible by nine. Writing
`S3(h) = 3 E(h) - D(h mod 174)`, orthogonality gives the exact integer identity

```text
sum_h S3(h)^2 = 9 sum_h E(h)^2 - 3 sum_g D(g)^2,
```

which is what the tools report as `above_q174` and what a gate checks on every
state any tool in this report prints.

Two lattice facts bound what the rung can do. Every carrier deviation is a
multiple of four: a block's correlation is `522 - 2d` with `d` the number of
disagreeing pairs, `d` is even because the product of all the terms is a
square, so each block is `2 (mod 4)` and the four together are `0 (mod 4)`. And
the self-inverse class 261 is on a finer lattice still: its toggle effect is
`2 y_{x+261}`, so a transfer moves it by a multiple of eight. The measured
per-class steps agree exactly — median four, and exactly one class of the 260
moving ones on the eight lattice.

Finally, a `q174` residual forces a floor on the carrier objective. Each
aggregation block must carry its own deviation, and the least squared norm a
block can carry is `D(g)^2` divided by the squared length of its aggregation
vector: `D(g)^2 / 3` for a triple, `D(87)^2 / 5` for `{87, 261}` — whose vector
is `(2, 1)` because the class 87 is hit twice — and `D(0)^2 / 4`. On the twelve
banked states that floor runs 1,213 to 1,344, and only an exact `q174` hit puts
it at zero.

## What the rung costs above a `q174` hit

Over 256 uniform lifts of each banked state (`evidence/carrier-spectrum.json`):

| quantity                                       | across the twelve states |
|------------------------------------------------|--------------------------|
| carrier error of a uniform lift (median)        | 359,504 to 370,496       |
| best of 256 lifts                               | 233,664 to 279,952       |
| rung's share of that error                      | 99.46% to 99.55%         |
| classes the fibre cannot move                   | `{0, 174}`, on every state |
| per-class spread `sigma` (geometric mean)       | 37.01 to 37.56           |
| orbit fibre                                     | `2^827.4` to `2^830.5`   |
| cost of one free class, mod-four lattice        | 4.531 to 4.553 bits      |
| **expected exact carrier solutions per `q174` hit** | `2^40.7` to `2^44.7`, median `2^42.8` |

The fibre size is exact — each orbit of count one or two admits three
arrangements — and the per-class spreads are measured. Only the last row is a
model: a Gaussian envelope per free class on its own lattice, the same shape of
estimate the predecessors used.

That model is where the lattice matters, and it is worth being explicit,
because the two conventions straddle one. Counting a class as landing on target
with probability `1 / (sigma sqrt(2 pi))` — unit spacing — gives `2^-304`
solutions per `q174` hit, and the answer would be that a hit essentially never
lifts. Counting it on the mod-four lattice the deviation provably lives on
gives `2^42.8`, and the answer is that a hit lifts about `2^43` ways. The
mod-four reading is the correct one and it is now gated.

**This corrects the predecessor's own density figures in the same direction.**
The `q174` congruence pass priced its 73 free equations at unit spacing —
`2^399.5` to `2^401.1` for the set, one solution in about `2^400` states. Its
own deviations are equally on the mod-four lattice, so each equation is two
bits cheaper than charged: the same measured spreads give about `2^255` to
`2^267`, and the expected `q174` solution count per shell rises from `2^1322`
to roughly `2^1460`. The conclusion it drew is unchanged and strengthened — the
object is solution-rich and the search is the bottleneck — but the "one in
`2^400`" figure should not be quoted again.

## What the sector costs without solving `q174` first

The same tool priced from the `q29` shell instead of the banked `q174` state
(`evidence/carrier-spectrum-shell.json`), which is what a search that goes
straight at the carrier faces. The carrier fibre above a shell is `2^1718.8` to
`2^1726.6` states — reproducing exactly the range the congruence pass computed
independently — with 246 free classes rather than 173, and a per-class spread
of 44.5 rather than 37.4.

Read flatly, at 4.80 bits per class, that gives about `2^545` exact carrier
solutions per shell. Read with the coordinate structure — 73 of those free
directions are `q174` aggregates whose measured spread is 17.9, so they cost
3.49 bits each, and the remaining 173 are rung directions at 4.55 — it gives
about `2^680`. The two-stage accounting agrees with the second: about `2^632`
distinct `q174` count-hits per shell, each carrying about `2^43` carrier lifts.

The 135-bit gap between the flat and structured readings of the same
measurement is the model's own error bar, and it is larger than any of the
quantities being compared at the level of individual states. What survives it
is the sign: the sector is solution-rich at the carrier level, by a wide margin
and under every convention tried.

## The search

Both scopes minimize the same objective, the whole carrier correlation over the
262 canonical classes, and differ only in what they may move. The `fibre` scope
rearranges one orbit of three carrier positions, which holds every orbit count
and therefore the whole `q174` correlation exactly fixed; it is the rung above
a `q174` hit, and nothing else. The `column` scope moves between any two
positions of one column of `Z/29`, preserving only the `q29` shell, and so
searches both rungs at once. The column neighbourhood strictly contains the
fibre one.

Twelve workers over the banked corpus, best of the run:

| arm                          | wall clock | carrier error | its floor | `q174` part |
|------------------------------|-----------:|--------------:|----------:|------------:|
| fibre, banked `q174` states  |       120s |        14,432 |     1,223 |       3,712 |
| fibre, same                  |       300s |        14,256 |     1,320 |       3,952 |
| column, fresh shell lifts    |       120s |        15,152 |     4,018 |      12,080 |
| column, banked lifts         |       120s |        14,656 |     4,002 |      12,432 |

The plateau does not move with the per-lift budget: 500, 5,000 and 50,000 steps
per lift give 14,704, 14,400 and 14,688 at sixty seconds each. Two and a half
times the wall clock buys 1%.

Five interleaved thirty-second rounds, twelve workers, the same seeds and
corpus, alternating which arm ran first (`evidence/carrier-scope-ab.tsv`):

| round | `fibre` | `column` | ratio |
|------:|--------:|---------:|------:|
| 1     |  15,040 |   15,200 | 1.011 |
| 2     |  14,896 |   15,200 | 1.020 |
| 3     |  14,896 |   15,424 | 1.035 |
| 4     |  14,672 |   15,200 | 1.036 |
| 5     |  14,624 |   14,624 | 1.000 |

The column arm is **1.020 times worse** in geometric mean with a paired
log-ratio `t = 2.94`; it loses four rounds and ties one. It takes 217,000 steps
in the window against the fibre arm's 1,686,000 — 7.8 times fewer for a
neighbourhood about nine times wider — and it spends the `q174` rung to buy
nothing: its `q174` residual runs 12,704 to 15,856 against the banked 3,712 to
4,048 the fibre arm holds exactly. **On this objective, solving `q174` first
and then repairing the fibre beats searching both rungs together.** That is a
routing conclusion for the campaign, not just a benchmark.

## What the plateau looks like

The independent oracle rebuilds each retained state's deviation class by class.
Across the 24 banked fibre-arm states, of the 262 canonical classes an average
of 58 are exactly on target, 95 are one lattice step off, 62 are two steps off
and 47 are three or more.

Put in units of the lattice step, the two plateaus of this campaign are the
same object at two levels. A uniform carrier lift sits at 9.3 steps per class.
The carrier plateau at 14,432 is 1.86 steps per class; the registered `q174`
plateau at 3,344 is 1.54 steps per class over its own 88. Both searches
compress every class to within about one and a half to two steps of its target
and then stop, on different groups, different neighbourhoods and different
objectives.

The fibre step costs the same as the `q174` step the campaign is already

because the carrier's toggle effects are `+-2` or zero and the whole table is
one byte per entry. The column step is 7.8 times the fibre step, which is the

The `q174` row is also the regression control: the same run on the retained
executable `hadamard-a5c345b-q174-final` measures 2,684,661 instructions per
step against 2,684,663 here, with the same best score, so adding the carrier
view costs the registered path nothing. Peak resident memory over twelve
workers is 12.5 MB for the fibre scope and 12.8 MB for the column scope,
against 12.5 MB for the `q174` arm.

## Provenance

The aggregation law, the block partition, the free rank of 173, the orthogonal
identity, the delta algebra, both lattice facts and the floor are exact and
gated. The searches are heuristic: only a direct replay of the defining
equations certifies a positive, and every miss here is a heuristic miss with no
negative authority over the sector. The expected-solution counts are a Gaussian
model on a measured lattice, not a bound, and the 135-bit gap between their
flat and structured readings is stated above rather than resolved.

## Mystery ledger

- **The rung is scored, and it is the larger half.** The predecessor's open
  item is answered: the carrier rung above an exact `q174` hit has 173 free
  classes costing about 786 bits, against the `q174` descent's own 255 bits
  below it. Calling it "the last factor of three" understated it by a factor of
  three in bits. Settled by this pass.
- **A `q174` hit lifts about `2^43` ways, if the lattice reading is right.**
  The estimate is `2^42.8` on the mod-four lattice the deviation provably lives
  on and `2^-304` on unit spacing, so the whole verdict rests on the lattice,
  which is why it is now proved and gated rather than assumed. What remains
  unmodelled is the correlation between classes: the flat and structured
  readings of the shell-level count differ by 135 bits, and nothing here
  measures the joint distribution.
- **The carrier plateau at 14,400 is unexplained, and it is the same shape as
  the one below it.** Budget insensitivity across a hundredfold range, two and
  a half times the wall clock, and a nine-times-wider exact neighbourhood all
  leave it where it is. In lattice steps per class the two plateaus are 1.86
  and 1.54 — the searches stop at the same place on two different groups. That
  coincidence is the strongest hint yet that what stalls the descent belongs to
  the move set or the objective's landscape rather than to any one rung, and
  nothing tests it.
- **The wider neighbourhood has now lost three times.** Paired transfers lost
  to single transfers at `q174`; the column scope loses to the fibre scope
  here; and the earlier group-scoped search lost its premise to the two-opt
  census. Every widening has cost more per step than it bought in reach.
- **The whole condition has a one-line spectral form nobody is searching in.**
  Summing the carrier law against a character says exactly that
  `sum_b |z_b(w)|^2 = 2092` for every nontrivial 522nd root of unity `w`, with
  `2092 = 4 * 523` — a flat spectrum off the trivial character, the same form
  the plain-523 route already speaks. Every engine in this campaign searches in
  the position domain against a squared-error objective. Whether the character
  domain admits a move set or a construction with better structure is
  untested.
- **Twelve shells is still the whole corpus.** Every state measured here
  descends from the same twelve banked `q29` shells, as in the two passes
  before it. The margin step still produces new shells in seconds.

## Next

The carrier is the top of the ladder: there is no rung above it, and a zero of
this objective is a Goethals--Seidel solution of the sector outright. So the
open question is no longer which level to score but why every level stalls at
one and a half to two lattice steps per class.

Two moves follow from this pass, and they are not the same kind of move. The
cheap one is corpus width: every number in the last three passes rotates the
same twelve shells, the margin step makes new ones in seconds, and the carrier
objective is the first scale that orders states by distance from an actual
solution. The structural one is the spectral form — the condition is a flat
character spectrum at `4 * 523`, every engine here works in the position
domain, and the plain-523 route already speaks that language. Pricing a
character-domain move set against the position-domain plateau is the first
thing that would test whether the plateau belongs to the problem or to the
search.
