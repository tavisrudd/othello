## Proposed title and abstract

**Mathieu hexads from concurrent chords on a finite conic**

> A six-subset `H` of the twelve rational points of a nonsingular conic in `PG(2,11)` is a block of
> one of the two `S(5,6,12)` Steiner systems on the projective line if and only if its fifteen chords
> have no accidental triple concurrence. We prove this by identifying accidental concurrent
> matchings with fixed-point-free involutions stabilizing `H` and combining that bridge with the four
> classical `PGL₂(11)` orbits on six-subsets. An elementary chord--extension identity, valid for
> every six-arc in every finite projective plane, shows equivalently that the Mathieu hexads are
> exactly the on-conic six-arcs with maximal extension locus. Exhaustive tracked computations verify
> both Steiner systems, every orbit row, and the q=11 spectrum. The analogous octad construction on
> `PG(1,23)` fails: no eight-subset attains the no-accidental-concurrence bound.

The abstract deliberately does not say “first”, does not call the orbit table new, and does not
claim a Mathieu tower.

## Main theorem package

Let `C` be a nonsingular conic in `PG(2,11)` and `H⊂C(F₁₁)` have six points. Let `t(H)` count
unordered concurrent triples among the fifteen chords, including the forced
`6 binom(5,3)=60` triples at the points of `H`; let `U(H)` be the points on no chord.

The note should state the following equivalence as its single spine:

1. `H` is a block of one of the two `S(5,6,12)` systems on `PG(1,11)`;
2. no three chords are concurrent away from `H`;
3. `t(H)=60`;
4. `|U(H)|=22`, the maximum among conic six-subsets;
5. `Stab_{PGL₂(11)}(H)` has odd order (equivalently, it is the `C₅` orbit).

The proof has four short inputs.

### 1. Chord concurrency is an involution count

Three chords concurrent away from the conic use disjoint endpoint pairs and therefore form a
perfect matching of `H`. The pencil through their concurrence point induces an involution of the
conic whose three transpositions are that matching. Consequently

\[
t(H)=60+\#\{\tau\in PGL_2(11):\tau(H)=H,
              \ \tau\text{ has no fixed point in }H\}.
\]

This is the new bridge. C157 must replace the inferred textbook citation for the pencil--involution
correspondence with a verified source or a self-contained two-line coordinate proof.

### 2. The classical four-orbit table

Cameron--Omidi--Tayfeh-Rezaie classify the four `PGL₂(11)` orbits on six-subsets. The concurrency
bridge assigns the geometric columns:

| orbit size | stabilizer | fpf involutions on `H` | `t(H)` | `|U(H)|` |
|---:|:---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 264 | `C₅` | 0 | 60 | 22 |
| 330 | `V₄` | 2 | 62 | 20 |
| 220 | `S₃` | 3 | 63 | 19 |
| 110 | `D₁₂` | 4 | 64 | 18 |

The orbit sizes and stabilizers are not new. The note's contribution is the concurrency and
extension interpretation of the rows.

### 3. The chord--extension identity

C174 proves in the stronger setting of every six-arc in every finite projective plane that

\[
t(H)+|U(H)|=q^2-14q+115.
\]

At `q=11` the constant is 82. Therefore `t=60` is equivalent to the maximal extension count
`|U|=22`. Present this as a direct corollary of classical secant-index equations and as the `w=6`
refinement adjacent to BDMP's printed `w=5` chord/coverage count, not as an independent new theory.

### 4. Identifying the 264-orbit

The tracked computation constructs two disjoint `PSL₂(11)`-invariant Steiner systems of 132 blocks,
verifies the Steiner property for each, and verifies that their union is exactly the `t=60` orbit.
The elementary orbit description is `{one fixed point of an order-five element} ∪ {one five-orbit}`.
C156 must supply a citable source for the classical two-system split or the note must state that
this realization is being established computationally here.

## Negative control at q=23

The same null for an eight-subset is `8 binom(7,3)=280`. Exhaustion of all `binom(24,8)=735471`
subsets gives minimum `t=295`, so no subset avoids accidental concurrence. The Mathieu octads lie at
`t=304`, but that constancy is forced by their `PSL₂(23)` orbit and does not characterize them.
This belongs as one compact final proposition/remark: the q=11 mechanism needs `6=2·3`, so a
concurrent chord triple is a perfect matching; at eight points it covers only six endpoints and no
longer determines the whole subset.

## Positioning and claim boundary

- Cite Halbeisen--Hungerbühler for the characteristic-zero contrast: no accidental concurrence is
  generic there, so the finite q=11 reversal is the phenomenon.
- Cite Havlicek/Coxeter/Pellegrino as the nearest rival geometric characterization of hexads, in a
  different ambient space.
- Cite Cameron--Omidi--Tayfeh-Rezaie for the orbit/stabilizer table.
- Cite BDMP prominently for the existing normal-rational-curve coverage machinery and `w=5` count.
- Do not claim the stabilizer-parity table as new; it repackages the classical orbits.
- Do not claim the identity itself as a conic-specific novelty; C174 shows it is a six-arc corollary
  of classical index equations.
- The plausible new result is the bridge
  `concurrent matching ↔ fixed-point-free stabilizing involution`, and hence the equivalence between
  the Mathieu design, concurrence minimum, and extension maximum.
- The extension-count priority statement remains conditional until C169 reads the inaccessible
  sources. The two-system literature statement remains conditional until C156.
