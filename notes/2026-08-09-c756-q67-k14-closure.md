## Exact domain and normalization

The search selects thirteen distinct line directions, requires every pairwise
intersection to be an internal node, and forbids triple concurrency.  In the
mixed model there are 33 directions and 2,211 line vertices.  The all-passant
model uses the anisotropic norm-torus directions.  In both models central
inversion sends seed offset \(s\) to \(-s\), so offsets 0 through 33 are a
complete representative set.

For q=67 and thirteen selected lines,

\[
 \delta=\binom{13}{2}-67=11,
 \qquad h_{\rm int}=\frac{67+1}{2}-13=21.
\]

Hence the all-passant simultaneous-projection window begins at
\(E_{\delta+1}=E_{12}\) and ends at \(E_{h_{\rm int}-1}=E_{20}\).
The search evaluates the centered homogeneous divided elementary forms over
\(\mathbf F_{67}\); ordinary integer division is never used.

## EJ + TT closeout

**EJ.**  The all-passant residual layer has a sharper exact signature than
mere failure at \(E_{12}\).  Of its 92 stars, 66 have no zero of \(E_{12}\)
on any anisotropic model direction.  The remaining 26 have exactly two such
zeros, exactly one of them selected.  Thus no leaf comes close to the 21
unused-direction zeros needed to force the covering identity.

**TT.**  The promising structural replacement for the all-passant census is
therefore not universal pointwise nonvanishing.  It is a root-count theorem
for the restriction of \(E_{12}\) to the anisotropic direction torus: prove
that a geometric thirteen-line star has zero profile either \((0,0)\) or
\((2,1)\), where the entries count all model-direction and selected-direction
zeros.  This is a short Laurent/Fourier polynomial on the norm torus and may
extend to the q=71 and q=73 first coefficients \(E_8\) and \(E_6\).

The mixed result points to a different structural mechanism.  Pairwise
internal-node compatibility alone readily concentrates lines into pencils;
the exact obstruction is the interaction with the no-three-concurrent
hypergraph.  A human replacement should therefore seek a stability theorem
that forces a large compatible set toward a pencil, which an arc cannot
admit, rather than a bare Paley-clique bound.
