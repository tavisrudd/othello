For a row `pi=(x,y,z)`, use symmetric-square coordinates


global information-basis change and nonzero coordinate scalings.

## Strong-multiplicativity proof

Fix dealer `d` and write `P` for the seven participants. For two shared information vectors `w,w'`,
participant `i` locally computes `pi_i(w) pi_i(w')`. As in C228, product reconstruction from a
survivor set `S` is equivalent to

```text
nu_2(pi_d) in span {nu_2(pi_i) : i in S}.                (2)
```

Strong multiplicativity asks for (2) after deleting every unqualified adversary `A`, here every
`A subset P` with `|A| <= 2`.

For the GRS representation, deleting at most two participants leaves at least five evaluations of
a degree-at-most-four product polynomial. Any five distinct evaluation points interpolate that
polynomial, including its value at the distinct dealer parameter. Equivalently, every five rows
of the square representation form a basis of its rank-five span. Thus (2) holds for every dealer
and every unqualified deletion.

For the generic representation, no deletion leaves seven square rows and a singleton deletion
leaves six; because the square matroid is `U(6,8)`, those survivors span the full six-dimensional
square space, so ordinary multiplicativity and every singleton-deletion test pass. After deleting
two participants, exactly five squares survive. The dealer square together with those five is a
six-subset of `U(6,8)` and is independent, so the dealer square is outside their span. Therefore
every two-participant deletion fails.

The exhaustive counts per representation are:

```text

unqualified adversaries per dealer:    1 + 7 + C(7,2) = 29
GRS passes by adversary size:           8, 56, 168
generic passes by adversary size:       8, 56, 0
generic failures at size two:           168
```

The first recorded generic witness chooses row `0` as dealer and deletes participants `1,2`:
the five survivor squares have rank five, while adjoining the dealer square raises the rank to six.

## Prior-art boundary

- Cramer, Daza, Gracia, Jimenez Urroz, Leander, Marti-Farre, and Padro give the ideal tensor-square
  criterion, define strong multiplicativity by survivor reconstruction against every adversary,
  and record that Shamir `(k,n)` is strongly multiplicative exactly when `n >= 3k-2` in
  [*On Codes, Matroids, and Secure Multiparty Computation From Linear Secret-Sharing Schemes*](https://eprint.iacr.org/2004/245.pdf).
  The GRS half here is the boundary case `k=3,n=7`.
- Gauthier, Otmani, and Tillich state that the square of a dimension-`k` GRS code is a GRS code of
  dimension `2k-1`, and use this low square dimension as a code distinguisher in
  [*A Distinguisher-Based Attack on a Variant of McEliece's Cryptosystem Based on Reed-Solomon Codes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.6459).
- Cascudo, Cramer, Mirandola, and Zemor study the contrasting generic behavior in
  [*Squares of Random Linear Codes*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.0848): squares typically fill the
  available ambient space when the length is at most about `k(k+1)/2`. C237 does not use their
  probability theorem; its generic matrix and every required minor are checked explicitly.

These sources contain the two standard ingredients separately: strong multiplication for Shamir
sharing and Schur-square dimension as a GRS-versus-generic fingerprint. A focused title/keyword
search found no source presenting this exact support-identical `U(3,8)` pair as a separation of
ideal-LSSS strong multiplicativity. No general priority claim is made. The contribution recorded
here is the explicit fixed-field pair, its all-dealer/all-adversary certificate, and its role as the
positive boundary immediately above C228's `U(2,4)` negative.
