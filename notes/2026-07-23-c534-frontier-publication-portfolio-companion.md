### 2. Flatland overlap assessment

The user asked whether Flatland overlapped any other pending papers.  The response separated direct
mathematical overlap from broad thematic proximity:

- **C481--C485:** direct overlap, but not then an allocated paper.  Both constructions use labelled
  six-point quotients and line-projection compatibility.  C481 identifies the atlas with labelled
  `M_0,6`; C482--C484 give the residual dimensions, four-view Gale pair, conic branch, and
  Kummer/Artin--Schreier descent.
- **`continuation-graph-rigidity`:** the strongest pending-paper conceptual overlap.  It reconstructs
  a finite projective configuration from derived compatibility/incidence data.
- **`arcs_complete_outside_conic`:** moderate overlap in the broad reconstruction theme, but a
  different functor.  Its input is the literal complete extension/uncovered locus, from which it
  recovers an unlabelled parent arc.
- **`clebsch-hexagon-code`:** weak, indirect overlap through recovery of a special six-arc and its
  symmetry from a deep-hole/conic locus; no line-image or camera input.
- **Baer, repair ports, Nofil, and dihedral:** no material Flatland overlap.

The controlling distinction was that Flatland reconstructs labelled planar configurations from
common line images and camera data in the generic real/complex setting, while the internal papers
use finite-field extension loci, continuation complexes, or deep-hole loci.  C537 was assigned the
exact functor comparison so that a shared `P1` quotient would not be mistaken for equality of
inverse problems.

### 3. Authorization to redesign the paper portfolio

The user authorized proposals for mergers and alternative papers, with Clebsch excluded from
restructuring.  The response treated all other publication boundaries as mutable and proposed the
following pre-pivot portfolio.

#### A. Conditional continuation--Gale merger

The highest-upside merger was:

> *Reconstruction functors for finite projective configurations: full faithfulness, Gale
> ambiguity, and arithmetic descent.*

The proposed spine contrasted two exact fibre behaviours:

- C295's stable-range full faithfulness for continuation graphs of projective frames;
- C481--C485's degree-two Gale ambiguity, conic branch locus, and finite-field descent;
- C490's finite exceptional fibres and complete-child recovery; and
- C537's comparison with Flatland's explicit multi-view joint-consistency gap.

This would replace a continuation paper limited to N1 if, and only if, C537 supplied a clean
functor-level bridge.  `arcs` was deliberately excluded from the merger because its defect identity,
relative-conic parameter, and exact q=16 result already form a coherent finished paper.

#### B. Redundancy-five PRS as an immediate standalone admission

Before the later PRS pivot, the recommendation was to admit C491 as a standalone paper ahead of
weaker conditional candidates.  Its case was:

- unconditional covering radius for every prime power `q>=7`;
- complete redundancy-five deep-hole classification;
- new tame osculating, characteristic-three nucleus, and wild Artin--Schreier families;
- exact sporadic fields and orbit tables; and
- a sharp `q>=23` exceptional-cover completeness mechanism.

This recommendation was based on the clean match to the published frontier, which classified PRS
deep holes through redundancy four.  It was subsequently reconsidered only after the cutoff of
this companion.

#### C. One modular-polar paper, with a conditional Hessian split

The proposed default was a single paper combining C512, C525, C529/C530, and the fixed-level
applications under a title such as:

> *Coherent polar flags and divided-Hessian geometry in characteristic two.*

The split rule was exact:

- if C535 proved a base-scheme-natural or genuinely higher-degree modular Hessian theorem, that
  result could support a short standalone invariant-theory paper;
- if the result remained cubic-local, it should be a theorem block inside the coherent-polar
  paper;
- C536's classical-versus-Lucas Fano boundary remained a prerequisite for either manuscript claim.

#### D. At most one Crowns paper from the two proposed packages

The “one torsor, one swap” and Modular Gateway packages were judged too specialized to support two
papers without a new audit.  A possible single narrative was:

```text
orientation torsor
  -> pointed modular carrier
  -> information lost after projectivization.
```

The q=7/q=11 realizations, q=23 boundary, modular contraction, and exact non-identification theorem
could belong to one paper if a claim-specific novelty audit cleared the combined spine.  Otherwise
the results should remain supporting material rather than be forced into publications.

#### E. Refocus Complete Repair Ports

The proposed stronger paper kept:

- the exact complete-repair-hypergraph transfer theorem;
- prescribed realization;
- the two explicit infinite code families; and
- the twisted-cubic completed seed as the flagship example.

Reliability/EXIT, pointed-Tutte structure, and broad cubic-versus-quartic comparisons were to move
to appendices or later work unless they materially strengthened the main transfer theorem.

#### F. Boundaries retained

The response recommended:

- keep `arcs_complete_outside_conic` standalone;
- keep the focused Baer/Q25 paper standalone;
- keep Nofil and Dihedral separate because their mechanisms and audiences differ;
- leave Clebsch unchanged by explicit user instruction;
- allocate no standalone paper for extension complexes, fixed-size equivalence algorithms,
  isolated Lucas counts, or the twisted-cubic weight distribution alone.
