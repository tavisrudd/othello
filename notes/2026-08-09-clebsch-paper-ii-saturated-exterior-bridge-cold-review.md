# Review-sub-agent-only memo: can saturated exterior geometry replace Paper II's modular exclusion?

**Date:** 2026-08-09  
**Status:** adversarial cold assessment, not routing, handoff, manuscript, or Lean material  
**Verdict:** **not presently plausible as a proof simplification**

## Question tested

For a perfect matching (M) of the (q+1) rational points of a nonsingular
conic, regard its ((q+1)/2) secant equations as coefficient points of the
dual discriminant conic. Could Paper II's global hypothesis


\[
 (L^{\circ 2})^\perp
 \text{ is a line generated by a two-valued vector}
\]

force those coefficient points

1. to form an arc, and
2. to have every pairwise join passant?

If it could, the saturated-exterior theorem transferred from C756 to C894
would give (q\in\{3,7,11\}); the two-sheet conclusion would eliminate
(q=3), and the one-orbit clauses at (q=7,11) would identify the two Paper
II survivors without the modular-Hom, outer-parity, and Dickson exclusion.

I tried to derive that bridge rather than presuming it. The attempt fails at
the passage from a global relation on an orbit of **unmarked matching
products** to local character and determinant conditions on **marked factors
inside one matching**.

The C894 result needed here is exactly:

> If (A) consists of ((q+1)/2) exterior points of a nonsingular conic,
> (A) is an arc, and every join of two points of (A) is passant, then
> (q\in\{3,7,11\}), with one conic-stabilizer orbit in each field.

Thus C894 does **not** supply either proposed bridge hypothesis. They are its
inputs.

## Verdict on plausibility

The implication may be true as a corollary of Paper II's completed
classification, but there is currently no credible shorter proof of it from
the trade hypothesis. The apparent simplification moves the entire hard step
into a new lemma:


\[
 \text{two-valued global trade on }G\cdot M
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \begin{cases}
 \det(P_e,P_f,P_h)\ne0&\text{for all distinct }e,f,h\in M,\\
 \chi(\operatorname{Res}(e,f))=-1&\text{for all distinct }e,f\in M.
 \end{cases}
\]

Neither implication is a formal consequence of strength two, the
four-endpoint switch, or the existence of two (G^+)-sheets. Establishing
them would require a new local-to-global theorem at least comparable in
difficulty to the modular exclusion it is meant to replace.

My recommendation is therefore: **do not replace the modular proof by this
route.** Record it, at most, as a conditional alternate endpoint or as a
future research question.

## Exact hypothesis comparison

Let (G=\operatorname{PGL}_2(q)), (G^+=\operatorname{PSL}_2(q)), and
(M) be a matching in the full orbit (Omega=G\cdot M).

### What is automatic

- Every edge (e=\{a,b\}) of (M) gives a split nonsingular binary
  quadratic, hence an exterior coefficient point (P_e) of the dual conic.
- Distinct matching edges have disjoint root pairs, so
  (\operatorname{Res}(e,f)\ne0).
- The trade line is (G)-stable. A two-valued generator has full support,
  and its two level sets are the two (G^+)-orbits; its values are opposite.
  This part of Paper II precedes the modular exclusion.
- The stabilizer (K=\operatorname{Stab}_{G^+}(M)) is a (p')-group, since
  a nontrivial unipotent cannot preserve a perfect matching.

### What C894 additionally assumes

- **Pairwise passant:** for every two edges (e,f\in M),
  
  \[
  \chi(\operatorname{Res}(e,f))=-1.
  \]

  Disjointness gives only nonvanishing; it does not determine the character.
- **Arc:** for every three edges (e,f,h\in M),
  
  \[
  \det(P_e,P_f,P_h)\ne0.
  \]

  Equivalently, no three secants in the primal plane are concurrent.

These are local conditions indexed by the two- and three-subsets of the edge
set of one matching. Paper II's hypothesis is instead a rank-one annihilator
condition on quadratic functions evaluated at the orbit points
(x_N=(P_N-P_{M_0})/Q), (N\in\Omega). The indexing sets and the logical
levels are different.

## Strongest derivation attempted

### 1. Pull the trade back to products

The projective-trade pullback is valid. Equal signed moments of the quotient
points through degree two yield signed tensor identities among the matching
products (P_N). This compares products indexed by whole matchings in
(Omega). It does not mark, order, or separately evaluate their irreducible
secant factors.

Unique factorization recovers the factor set from an individual (P_N), but
factor extraction is not a linear or quadratic polynomial operation in the
quotient coefficients. The resultant character
(\operatorname{Res}(e,f)^{(q-1)/2}) is a high-degree multiplicative
predicate on two recovered factors, while collinearity is a determinant on
three factor coefficient points. Neither appears as an identified element of
the affine evaluation space (L) or its Hadamard square.

### 2. Try the four-endpoint switch

For two edges ({a,b},\{c,d\}\subset M), the local identity


\[
 L_{ab}L_{cd}-L_{ac}L_{bd}=[a,d][b,c]Q
\]

does expose a bracket scalar, and after multiplication by the untouched
factors it gives a quotient direction. This is the closest available route
to the local resultant.

It does not connect to the trade because the switched matching need not lie
in (Omega). Paper II explicitly warns at lines 289--293 that a selected
orbit need not contain an intermediate switch. This is not a technical edge
case. In the (H_3) survivor, a matching has
(2\binom 62=30) distinct one-switch neighbours, whereas its entire full
orbit has only (22) points, including the original matching. At least nine
switch neighbours therefore lie outside the orbit. The trade gives no value
or sheet sign at those points.

Even orbit membership for selected switches would be insufficient without a
new rule identifying the switch's sheet parity with the quadratic character
of the four-endpoint resultant.

### 3. Try to use the square hyperplane as interpolation

The one-dimensional trade annihilator says that (L^{\circ2}) is a
hyperplane in the function space on (Omega). If a marked local predicate
were known to be a degree-two function of (x_N), its signed **average** over
the two sheets would agree. That still would not give pointwise
nonvanishing or a fixed character for every pair or triple of factors.

There are two further losses:

- a matching orbit has no edge marking, so a function referring to “the
  (i)-th and (j)-th factors” is not naturally a function on (Omega);
- the unmarked aggregate counts of bad pairs or concurrent triples are
  projective invariants of the matching and hence constant on the whole
  (G)-orbit. Their signed sheet sums vanish tautologically and reveal
  nothing.

To recover individual local statements one would need an incidence lift to
flags ((N,e,f)) and ((N,e,f,h)), together with enough separation on those
flag orbits. No such lifted trade or evaluation algebra is present.

### 4. Try to import factorization or Paley symmetry

This is circular. Paper II obtains sheet size (q) only from the modular
nonprincipal-sheet exclusion (lines 1273--1280). It proves the surviving
sheets are one-factorizations only after the (q=7,11) classification
(lines 1331--1334). The Paley carrier appears later still, after the
edge-selected alternating-cycle calculation (lines 1407--1414).

Accordingly one may not assume before the modular step that

- a sheet has (q) matchings;
- each endpoint edge occurs exactly once in a sheet;
- a translation subgroup acts regularly on a sheet;
- the cross-sheet incidence matrix is Paley; or
- the matching stabilizer is transitive on pairs or triples of its edges.

Each of these would provide precisely the local uniformity missing from the
moment argument, but each is an output of the existing classification or its
survivor analysis.

### 5. Compare with the C894 proof itself

C894 uses the local hypotheses at its first substantive steps. Pairwise
passancy gives the nonsquare resultant condition and makes the root pairs a
matching. The arc condition makes (s\mapsto s\phi(s)) a bijection. Only
then does Segre's lemma force the local Paley automorphism, which is reduced
by character sums and Hasse to (q=3,7,11).

There is no reversible implication in that proof from a global quadratic
moment identity to the resultant signs or the determinant bijection. Running
C894 backward would require reversing Segre's tangent lemma and several
character-sum inequalities, none of which is valid in that direction.

## Concrete degrees-of-freedom obstructions

1. **Matching is weaker than saturated exterior.** Three disjoint conic
   secants can be concurrent, and a collection of secants through an
   off-conic point pairs their distinct conic intersections. Thus perfect
   matchings with collinear coefficient triples exist; arcness is not
   automatic.
2. **Disjointness is weaker than passancy.** For two disjoint root pairs the
   resultant is merely nonzero. Its square class varies with their cross
   ratio, so both secant and passant joins of coefficient points occur.
3. **Moment equalities permit cancellation.** Arcness is a family of
   nonvanishing assertions and passancy is a family of character assertions.
   Finite-field signed averages have no positivity principle that turns an
   aggregate equality into either pointwise condition.
4. **The known off-carrier fixed line is a warning.** The exact same trade
   persists on (q-2) nonmatching orbits. Although those points have no
   factor sets to test, this proves the trade does not intrinsically retain
   factorization data. Any recovery of local factors must use the matching
   carrier plus a new theorem, not the trade alone.

These observations are not counterexamples to the proposed implication
under all Paper II hypotheses; the completed Paper II theorem rules such
counterexamples out. They show why the implication cannot be obtained from
the currently visible low-degree identities by a formal or local argument.

## Exact missing lemmas

Any replacement proof needs all of the following before C894 can be invoked.

### Lemma A: flag lift or factor extraction

Construct, functorially from the affine orbit configuration and its
strength-two trade, enough functions on the two- and three-edge flag spaces
of a matching to evaluate pairwise resultant characters and triple
determinants. Prove these functions are controlled by (L^{\circ2}) without
assuming a one-factorization or survivor module structure.

### Lemma B: local passancy

For every (M\in\Omega) and distinct (e,f\in M), prove


\[
 \chi(\operatorname{Res}(e,f))=-1.
\]

An averaged character sum is not enough; C894 requires every pair.

### Lemma C: local arcness

For every (M\in\Omega) and distinct (e,f,h\in M), prove


\[
 \det(P_e,P_f,P_h)\ne0.
\]

Again, an average number of concurrent triples is insufficient.

### Lemma D: noncircularity

Prove Lemmas A--C using only the preclassification consequences of the trade:
the two (G^+)-sheets, (K\le G^+), and the projective quotient identities.
The proof may not use sheet size (q), one-factorization, regular
translations, the Paley incidence carrier, `S_4/A_5` stabilizers, or the
already classified fields.

Lemmas B and C are the desired bridge; Lemma A is the missing mechanism, and
Lemma D is the audit condition that prevents a disguised use of the modular
classification.

## Safe partial simplifications

Two limited uses are sound.

1. **Conditional endpoint proposition.** Add, outside the main proof, the
   statement: if a trade-bearing matching also has an exterior coefficient
   set that is an arc with all joins passant, then C894 gives
   (q\in\{3,7,11\}), and the already established two-sheet property removes
   (q=3). This is correct but adds no unconditional classification.
2. **Postclassification geometric identification.** For the displayed
   (B_3/F_7) and (H_3/F_{11}) matchings, verify the determinant and
   resultant conditions directly. C894 then gives an independent geometric
   uniqueness statement for their coefficient-point sets. This may shorten
   or clarify the final identification of the survivor orbits, but it cannot
   replace the proof that no other trade-bearing orbit exists.

A still narrower safe observation is automatic and worth recording if this
dictionary is used: the factors of any matching give ((q+1)/2) exterior
coefficient points with pairwise nonzero resultants. It must not be called an
“exterior set” in C894's technical sense unless pairwise passancy has also
been proved.

## Revised proof-change size

This is not a drop-in shortening.

- **Without new Lemmas A--D:** zero safe reduction of the modular-Hom and
  outer-parity argument. The C894 theorem can only be a conditional remark or
  postclassification cross-check.
- **If Lemmas A--D were proved independently:** the field and orbit endpoint
  could replace Lemma 3.3 and the Dickson/order/block-system endgame, and
  likely most of Lemma 3.2's use in the classification. The manuscript could
  remove a substantial modular section.
- **But proving Lemmas A--D is a major new project.** A self-contained route
  through C894 also imports Segre tangent functions, local-Paley rigidity,
  primitive Jacobi-sum collision control, a Weil bound, and a Hasse bound.
  Since the C894 companion is not yet through its human safeguard gates,
  Paper II could not presently replace its proof by a short stable citation.
  Reproducing the C894 proof would shift rather than reduce complexity.

Estimated change class: **large architectural replacement, not a local
revision**. Until a genuinely short flag-lift lemma is found, the existing
modular spine should remain the primary proof.

## Bottom line

The proposed bridge has excellent explanatory value: it says exactly which
finite geometry the surviving matchings secretly carry. It does not yet have
proof value at the exclusion step. The global trade sees whole matching
products; C894 starts after every local pair and triple of factors has
already passed a character/determinant test. The missing passage is the new
theorem, and every currently tempting way to supply it uses one-factorization
or Paley data obtained only after Paper II's existing classification.
