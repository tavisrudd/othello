# C904 Jordan-scalar minimal-class priority audit

Date: 2026-08-10

Status: bounded primary-source priority audit; no manuscript or Lean changes

## Executive verdict

The proposed theorem has a real unpreempted core, but its consequence must be
positioned carefully.

The standard Weyl ppav construction, the modular curve for type A, and the
unpolarized decomposition into elliptic curves are classical.  Moreover,
Beckmann--de Gaay Fortman already prove the integral Hodge conjecture for
one-cycles for every product of Jacobians, hence for every abelian variety
isomorphic to a product of elliptic curves.  Their equivalence theorem also
implies that the minimal class of any principal polarization on such an
abelian variety is algebraic.  Thus neither

- algebraicity of the minimal class, nor
- the integral Hodge conjecture for the standard Weyl families

is a new crown.

No predecessor was located for the stronger integral saturation assertion:
the primitive minimal class lies in the integral subgroup generated by
ordinary intersection products of divisor classes, uniformly for every
Jordan-scalar principal elliptic-power quotient and, in particular, for every
scalar Weyl-stable root--weight gluing of type A.  The local mixed-adjugate
lemma and the all-prime assembly also were not found in the ppav,
Prym--Tyurin, Hodge-cycle, or polarization-classification sources screened.

This is a bounded audit, not a MathSciNet or exhaustive forward-citation
closure.  The safe external formulation remains "we do not know a previous
integral divisor-product saturation theorem of this form."

Eight individually discussed primary sources were consulted: two at full-text
depth and six at partial depth.  All eight are recorded below with version,
access path, and cache hash.

## Candidate theorem audited

The strongest stable formulation currently visible is:

> Let (A, Theta) be a principally polarized complex abelian variety obtained
> from a polarized power of an elliptic curve by a principal isotropic gluing
> whose p-primary symplectic lattice is Jordan-scalar on every block.  If
> g = dim A, then the minimal class
> gamma_Theta = Theta^(g-1)/(g-1)! is in the image of the integral
> intersection map generated by divisor classes.  Equivalently, there are
> integral divisor classes D_ij and integers n_i such that
> gamma_Theta = sum_i n_i D_i1 ... D_i,g-1 in H^(2g-2)(A,Z).

The type-A corollary uses the root--weight form

G_N = N I_(N-1) - J_(N-1),  N >= 3,

and quantifies over every scalar Weyl-stable principal gluing, not just one
displayed cyclic quotient.

Because products of divisor classes are algebraic cycles, Beckmann--de Gaay
Fortman's Theorem 1.1 then gives the integral Hodge conjecture for one-cycles.
That implication is correct.  For members already known to be isomorphic to
products of elliptic curves, however, the IHC conclusion is prior art; the new
content is the much more rigid location of gamma_Theta inside the integral
divisor-product lattice.

## Exact antecedents

### 1. Weyl ppavs and the type-A modular curve

Carocca--Gonzalez-Aguilera--Rodriguez construct

X(B,tau) = V/(L + tau L*)

from an invariant integral form B.  Proposition 2.4 says that this ppav is
rho-decomposable and isomorphic as a complex torus to a product of elliptic
curves.  Theorem 2.5 gives the converse for absolutely irreducible rho, and
Corollary 2.8 repeats the product decomposition for every such ppav.

For root systems, Proposition 3.1 gives the one-dimensional Weyl families.
Proposition 4.1 makes the product decomposition explicit.  In type A_n it is

E_tau^(n-1) x E_(tau/(n+1)).

Theorem 5.4 identifies the type-A_n family with H/Gamma_0(n+1).  In current
notation n=N-1, so the standard family is X_0(N).  The proof constructs the
principal quotient from the root-system abelian variety by an appropriate
cyclic isotropic subgroup.

This source therefore owns the standard root/weight ppav, its modular curve,
its cyclic quotient description, and its unpolarized elliptic-product
decomposition.  It does not discuss minimal classes, integral Hodge classes,
Chow groups, divisor-product lattices, mixed adjugates, or the index of the
integral Lefschetz subring.

The exact relation between the current collection of all scalar local gluing
types and Carocca's single rho-decomposable family still needs a formal
comparison.  Different gluing presentations could parametrize the same ppav
or different cusps/level structures.  Novelty should be attached to the
uniform integral saturation theorem, not to an unverified claim that every
local gluing produces a new Weyl family.

### 2. What Beckmann--de Gaay Fortman already gives

Their Theorem 1.1 proves, for a principally polarized complex abelian variety,
the equivalence between algebraicity of the minimal class and their integral
Fourier-transform/IHC conditions.  Their Theorem 1.2 proves IHC for
one-cycles on every product of Jacobians.  Since an elliptic curve is its own
Jacobian, this includes every product of elliptic curves.

Consequently, if a Jordan-scalar quotient is proved to be isomorphic, rather
than merely isogenous, to a product of elliptic curves, both IHC and
algebraicity of its minimal class are already known.  Proposition 4.6 shows
why a bare isogeny is not enough for an automatic integral conclusion: their
transport arguments require prime-to-p control for each relevant prime.

Nothing in Theorem 1.1 says that the primitive minimal class is an integral
combination of ordinary divisor intersections.  Condition 7 says that divided
powers of algebraic cohomology classes are algebraic.  It does not identify
them with the Z-submodule generated by non-divided products of divisor
classes.  This is the precise gap filled by the candidate adjugate theorem.

### 3. Rational divisor generation is weaker

Abdulali's Theorem 6.1 proves the general Hodge conjecture for products of
elliptic curves.  In its proof he states the classical fact that their Hodge
ring is generated by divisors, with ancestry to Tate (unpublished) and
detailed proofs in Ichikawa and Murty.

The paper works with rational Hodge structures and the rational Hodge ring.
It therefore proves generation after tensoring with Q, not saturation of the
integral lattice.  A finite index between

image(Sym^r NS(A)) and Hdg^(2r)(A,Z)

is invisible to this theorem.  The proposed result is exactly about killing
one such possible index for the primitive minimal class.

### 4. Polarization classification is adjacent, not preemptive

Lange identifies principal polarizations on products of pairwise isogenous
non-CM elliptic curves with equivalence classes of positive definite
unimodular Hermitian matrices; for a self-product these become positive
definite unimodular integral quadratic forms.  This is the right classical
language for the candidate theorem, but the source counts/classifies
polarizations and does not study the integral image of higher divisor
intersection products.

Auffarth characterizes elliptic curves and abelian divisors in a ppav through
intersection identities in NS(A).  Theorem 1.1 detects abelian divisors by a
primitive square-zero NS class, and later results characterize split elliptic
factors.  Again, the paper does not compute the codimension-(g-1)
divisor-product lattice or a mixed-adjugate saturation index.

### 5. Prym--Tyurin multiples do not give the primitive result

Izadi--Lange--Strehl state the classical boundary cleanly: a Prym--Tyurin
variety of index m contains a curve representing m times the minimal class,
obtained from the Abel curve by D-id.  Their generalized split-polynomial
correspondences similarly produce computable multiples after isogeny.

Carocca--Lange--Rodriguez--Rojas construct Prym--Tyurin varieties of arbitrary
exponent through Hecke correspondences and recall Kanev's Weyl-group
constructions.  These results concern an effective curve representing a
controlled multiple of the minimal class.  They do not divide that multiple
integrally, and they do not provide a signed divisor-product expression for
the primitive class.

This contrast is useful framing: the classical correspondence technology
naturally gives m gamma_Theta, whereas the candidate local adjugate theorem
removes the multiplier inside the integral Lefschetz subring.

### 6. The 2026 matroid obstruction sharpens the significance

Engel--de Gaay Fortman--Schreieder prove that, in a matroidal family attached
to a non-cographic regular matroid, every curve representing a multiple m of
the minimal class on a very general fiber has m even.  Their main examples
include the graphic matroid of K_5.  This does not preempt a positive theorem
on a special one-dimensional Weyl/elliptic-power locus.

There is a potentially strong contrast: G_N=N I-J is the reduced Laplacian
form of the complete graph K_N, while complete-graph matroidal families become
non-cographic from N=5 onward.  One must verify that the degeneration/matroid
identification is literally the same before writing this as a theorem.  As
framing, however, it suggests that the scalar Weyl locus is an integral
saturation locus at the opposite extreme from the very-general parity
obstruction.

## The actual novelty boundary

The candidate should be sold as integral Lefschetz saturation, not as an IHC
example.

A safe theorem-level punchline is:

> Every Jordan-scalar principal elliptic-power quotient has primitive
> minimal class in its integral divisor-product lattice.

The type-A corollary can then say:

> Every scalar A_(N-1) root--weight principal quotient lies on the primitive
> Lefschetz locus, for every N>=3.

Here "primitive Lefschetz locus" should be introduced as new terminology only
if it is used systematically; it is not a standard term located in this audit.
Define it explicitly as the locus where gamma_Theta belongs to the integral
subring generated by NS.

Do not say merely "the minimal class is integral"; it is cohomologically
integral for every principal polarization.  Do not say "represented by a
minimal curve"; the constructed object is a signed algebraic cycle assembled
from divisor intersections.

## Proof and normalization gates

The literature audit cannot validate the local mixed-adjugate proof.  Before
the theorem can carry a paper, the following points need theorem-grade closure.

1. **Exact domain.** Define Jordan-scalar gluing intrinsically and prove that
   it is invariant under allowed changes of symplectic/Jordan basis.

2. **Product versus isogeny.** State exactly which quotients are isomorphic to
   products of elliptic curves.  Use Beckmann--de Gaay Fortman directly only
   in the isomorphic case; do not silently transport IHC through an arbitrary
   isogeny.

3. **Intersection normalization.** Check that the polarized determinant sends
   an ordered product of g-1 divisor classes to the intended mixed adjugate
   with no missing factor of (g-1)!.

4. **Off-diagonal normalization.** In symmetric-matrix coordinates, specify
   whether an off-diagonal NS generator is E_ij+E_ji or half that class.  This
   is the most likely hidden factor-of-two failure.

5. **Principal class.** Verify in every local block that the cofactor target is
   exactly Theta^(g-1)/(g-1)!, not the pullback of this class multiplied by the
   degree of the quotient.

6. **Local-to-global saturation.** Prove equality of the integral lattice by
   p-primary localization and then reassemble over Z; a Q-span argument is
   insufficient.

7. **Composite scalar types.** Treat every permitted k in a p^a Jordan block,
   not merely the cyclic endpoint types.  This is where the theorem exceeds
   the N=3,...,7 census.

8. **CM fibers.** Extra CM divisors can enlarge NS.  The displayed witness
   should be built from the generic/non-CM divisor sublattice so that
   specialization to CM does not change the argument or its normalization.

9. **Type-A comparison.** Prove how the local scalar gluing types map to the
   standard X_0(N) Weyl family and its level structures.  Do not claim new ppav
   families unless this comparison establishes it.

10. **Exceptional boundary.** The exotic F4 cubic case is outside the scalar
    theorem.  Preserve it as a clearly stated boundary or counterexample, not
    as an unexplained omission.

## Venue ceiling

### As a bare IHC/minimal-algebraicity result

Preempted for the standard Weyl/elliptic-product family.  No crown.

### As the proved all-N type-A divisor-saturation theorem

Strong specialized theorem, plausibly Compositio or JEMS level.  If the local
lemma is unusually clean and the complete scalar-gluing classification is
included, Inventiones is plausible.  It is not by itself an Annals theorem:
the underlying ppavs and IHC consequence are known, and the advance is an
integral refinement on a special modular locus.

### As the general Jordan-scalar theorem

The broader theorem materially raises the ceiling.  A conceptual
local-to-global criterion covering arbitrary elliptic-power principal
quotients, with the Jordan-scalar theorem as the positive half and an explicit
obstruction group/counterexample theory as the negative half, could be
Inventiones/JAMS and Annals-adjacent.

The clean Annals-scale version would be a classification theorem:

> Determine exactly, prime by prime, which principal gluings of polarized
> elliptic powers have primitive minimal class in the integral Lefschetz
> subring, and identify the obstruction for every remaining gluing.

An ADE/Weyl classification with the type-A infinite theorem, the exceptional
F4 behavior, and a matroidal parity interpretation would also be much stronger
than the type-A corollary alone.

paper coupling the Carocca Weyl family to primitive integral divisor-product
saturation.  Exact "mixed adjugate" searches found determinant/apolarity and
matrix-analysis papers, not the lattice-saturation theorem.

## EJ + TT closeout and mystery ledger

### Cheap upgrades exposed

1. State the theorem as an integral Lefschetz-saturation result and demote IHC
   to a corollary/consistency check.
2. Put the rational-versus-integral distinction in the first paragraph of any
   future literature discussion; it is the main priority boundary.
3. Use Prym--Tyurin multiples as contrast: classical geometry supplies
   m gamma, while the adjugate argument removes m in the divisor subring.
4. Compare the complete-graph Laplacian with the 2026 matroid parity
   obstruction, but only after checking that the degeneration realizes the
   same matroidal data.

### Mysteries

- **All scalar gluings versus the standard X_0(N) family:** unresolved.  The
  evidence gap is an explicit equivalence/classification map between local
  scalar gluing types and Carocca's rho-decomposable ppavs.
- **Hidden classical lattice lemma:** unresolved.  No exact predecessor was
  found, but monographs and MathSciNet remain uncovered.  A later
  forward-citation/monograph audit owns this gap before a public priority
  sentence.
- **Complete-graph parity reversal:** unresolved as a theorem.  The shared
  Laplacian combinatorics is exact, but the same-matroid degeneration claim
  has not been checked.
- **F4 exception:** mathematically unexplained by this audit.  It may indicate
  the obstruction half of an arbitrary-gluing classification and is the
  highest-value route to a larger theorem.

## Bottom line

GO on developing the all-prime mixed-adjugate theorem.  MAJOR correction to
any framing that sells IHC or minimal-class algebraicity as new for Weyl
elliptic products.  The strongest currently defensible crown is the primitive
integral divisor-product saturation theorem; an exact arbitrary-gluing
classification is the route from a strong specialized result toward an
Annals-scale statement.
