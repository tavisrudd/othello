# C756 covering as a dual star-blocking problem

## Verdict

The saturated-internal all-\(k\) gate can be weakened substantially.  It is
not necessary to prove that coherent equality supports do not exist.  It is
enough to prove that their chords fail to cover one off-conic point.

Under conic polarity, a saturated-internal arc \(Y\) of size
\((q+3)/2=m+1\) becomes an arrangement of \(m+1\) passant lines.  The poles
of the chords of \(Y\) are exactly the pairwise intersection points of this
arrangement.  Conic-filling is equivalent to this star-configuration vertex
set meeting every non-tangent line.  Thus the saturated gate is the following
strictly weaker finite-geometric statement:

> For \(q>5\), the pairwise intersections of \(m+1=(q+3)/2\) passant lines
> in dual-arc position cannot block every non-tangent line.

This is the first live saturated formulation that uses the covering
hypothesis rather than trying to classify all equality supports.
The Clebsch hexagon at \(q=11\) belongs to the already closed
saturated-external branch and therefore is not an exception to this
internal/passant-star statement.

## 1. Dual star configuration

Let \(C\) be a nonsingular conic in \(\mathrm{PG}(2,q)\), \(q\) odd, and
let \(Y\) be an internal conic-external arc of size
\[
 n=m+1=\frac{q+3}{2}.
\]
For \(P\in Y\), write \(L_P=P^\perp\).  Because \(P\) is internal, \(L_P\)
is a passant line.  For distinct \(P,Q\in Y\), write
\[
 B_{PQ}=(PQ)^\perp=L_P\cap L_Q,
\qquad
 \mathcal B(Y)=\{B_{PQ}:P,Q\in Y,\ P\ne Q\}. \tag{1}
\]

> **Proposition 25 (dual star-blocking equivalence).**
>
> 1. Every \(B_{PQ}\) is internal, and the points in (1) are distinct.
>    Hence \(|\mathcal B(Y)|=\binom n2=m(m+1)/2\).
> 2. For every \(P\in Y\), the \(m\) points
>    \(B_{PQ}\), \(Q\in Y\setminus\{P\}\), are exactly all internal points
>    of the passant line \(L_P\).
> 3. For every off-conic point \(X\),
>    \[
>    X\text{ lies on a chord of }Y
>    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
>    X^\perp\cap\mathcal B(Y)\ne\varnothing. \tag{2}
>    \]
> 4. Consequently the uncovered locus of \(Y\) is exactly \(C\) if and
>    only if \(\mathcal B(Y)\) meets every non-tangent line.  The \(q+1\)
>    tangent lines automatically avoid \(\mathcal B(Y)\).

**Proof.**  Every chord \(PQ\) is passant by conic externality, so its pole
\(B_{PQ}\) is internal.  Distinct pairs give distinct chord lines because
\(Y\) is an arc; polarity preserves distinctness.  This proves the first
claim.

The point \(B_{PQ}\) lies on \(P^\perp=L_P\).  The \(m=n-1\) such points are
distinct, and a passant line has exactly \(m\) internal points, proving the
second claim.

Polarity reverses incidence:
\[
 X\in PQ
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 (PQ)^\perp\in X^\perp.
\]
Taking the union over all chords proves (2).  The polar of an off-conic
point is a non-tangent line, and every non-tangent line is the polar of an
off-conic point.  Finally, conic tangents contain no internal points, so they
avoid \(\mathcal B(Y)\). \(\square\)

The lines \(\{L_P:P\in Y\}\) are in dual-arc position: no three are
concurrent, because concurrency would dualize to three collinear points of
\(Y\).  Thus \(\mathcal B(Y)\) is the ordinary star configuration of this
line arrangement, with the additional facts that all arrangement lines are
passant and all vertices are internal.

## 2. Equivalent domination statement

For an off-conic \(X\notin Y\), put
\[
 t_X=\#\{P\in Y:XP\text{ is passant}\},
\qquad
 d_X=\#\{\text{chords of }Y\text{ through }X\}.
\]

> **Corollary 25.1.**  One has \(t_X=2d_X\).  In particular \(X\) is
> covered if and only if \(t_X>0\).

**Proof.**  The \(m\) chords through any \(P\in Y\) exhaust all \(m\)
passant lines through the internal point \(P\).  Hence every passant \(XP\)
is a chord line and contains exactly one further point of \(Y\).  Conversely
each chord through \(X\) contributes its two endpoints to \(t_X\). \(\square\)

For internal \(X\), \(t_X\) is exactly the number of neighbors of \(X\) in
the external-join support graph lying in \(Y\).  Therefore it suffices to
show that every coherent equality support has one internal common
nonneighbor.  One need not classify the support.

## 3. Algebraic carrier

Let
\[
 F_Y=\prod_{P\in Y}L_P
\]
be the degree-\(n\) equation of the dual line arrangement.  Its rational
double-point locus is \(\mathcal B(Y)\), and its star-configuration ideal is
generated by the \(n\) degree-\((n-1)\) products
\[
 F_Y/L_P,\qquad P\in Y. \tag{3}
\]
The new gate asks whether the rational singular locus of this very special
passant-line arrangement can meet every non-tangent line.

This carrier has two advantages over equality classification:

1. it uses only the property all-\(k\) actually needs--covering; and
2. it exposes standard blocking-set, line-arrangement, and Rédei-polynomial
   interfaces without discarding the no-three-concurrent condition.

It also has an exact stop rule.  A raw cardinality bound on
\(|\mathcal B(Y)|\) is too weak: the set has quadratic size.  A useful proof
must exploit the star ideal (3), the internal type of every vertex, or the
fact that each arrangement line contains all of its internal points in
\(\mathcal B(Y)\).

## EJ + TT closeout

**EJ.**  The chord-pole set is not an arbitrary blocking candidate.  On
every arrangement line \(L_P\), it contains the complete internal half of
that passant line.  This converts saturation into a union of \(m+1\)
maximally filled internal line sections, with every vertex counted twice.
That structure is invisible in the signed eigenvector alone.

**TT.**  The hostile question is whether this merely dualizes the original
problem.  It does not preserve its difficulty verbatim: the target has
weakened from nonexistence of an arc to nonblocking of its star vertices,
and the covering condition is now explicit.  But incidence counting alone
will still be loose because \(|\mathcal B(Y)|\asymp q^2/8\).  The next proof
must use the line-arrangement ideal or a restricted blocking theorem, not
another first/second moment.
