# Clebsch magic gateway: chained-opportunity brainstorm

**Lane:** `crowns` with read-only inputs from `clebsch` and other source lanes

**Date:** 2026-07-20

**Status:** working synthesis; candidate chains are recorded, while literature depth, effort/risk,
scores, and final rankings are still being completed

## Question

What becomes possible only after the Clebsch results are composed with one another and with
classical or recent work?  The unit of analysis here is not an isolated analogy or edge.  It is an
ordered chain whose later moves use structure recovered by the earlier ones.

The working standard is:

1. start from a proved local interface;
2. preserve markings until the next theorem has consumed them;
3. place a cheap falsifier before a costly generalization;
4. ask whether two routes commute or invert one another;
5. continue through the second and third useful moves; and
6. score the best credible chain, not the fame of its endpoint.

This report does not change the Clebsch manuscript plan and does not allocate work.  Existing
successors C413--C436 retain their owners.  Items below that overlap them are extensions or
reframings, not duplicate tasks.

## Landed gateway primitives

The following interfaces, rather than the individual famous objects, are the reusable assets.

- **Arithmetic phase and reconstruction:** C368/C399 give one integral `H3/A5` object whose
  reductions are GRS at q=5, empty at q=9, a non-GRS-parent-to-GRS-deep-child transform at q=11,
  and intrinsically parent-recovering for the stable fields.
- **Fourier and coherent refinement:** C372 gives a primitive rank-eight Fourier-self-dual
  translation scheme; C378 places the two golden fissions in a rank-16 `A4` common refinement
  with a four-dimensional outer-odd Fourier block.
- **Unmarked reconstruction:** C373 recovers affine addition, six directions, `A5`, and the
  unordered `10+10` chirality torsor from one uncoloured Cayley graph.  C295 supplies a second
  graph-to-geometry full-faithfulness theorem and an intrinsic q=11 two-sheet recovery.
- **Surface compatibility:** C376 proves that Clebsch-cubic double-six/blowdown exchange and code
  chirality are the same `S5/A5` character.  C377 supplies the integral golden involution while
  also fixing the classical descent boundary.
- **Decorated inversion:** C379 shows that the bare deep-hole transform loses the parent, while a
  perfect-matching decoration restores it.  The 22 parents form two one-factorizations and a
  biplane.  C405 supplies a codimension-four analogue in which a failed twisted-cubic target
  produces a Hermitian Cayley octad, a self-dual non-GRS MDS child, and eight parent decorations.
- **Forgetting and first memory:** C403 identifies the exact conic-ideal quotient generated by
  four-endpoint switches; C406 recovers the two balanced sheets and proves that their first signed
  tensor memory is cubic; C409 identifies the shared exact-strength-two trade filtration.
- **Compression with a modular explanation:** C411 realizes the six `1,4,6 / 1,4,6` profiles as
  mixed `A4\PGL_2(11)/A5` matrix coefficients and preserves cubic-first survival through a
  rank-two map.  C412 explains the target rank drop by `P(1)^A4/soc(P(1))`, constructs a distinct
  canonical relative-cubic Tate plane, and proves why the natural planes do not coincide.
- **Quantum endpoints:** C374 gives an explicit `A5`-symmetric `AME(6,11)` class LU-inequivalent
  to every six-point GRS class; C375 gives a three-gate preparation and an exact obstruction to a
  strictly `A5`-fixed Bell-triangle layout.

## Intermediate unranked candidate chains

### G1. Finite-field orbit recovery: cubic memory -> Hecke tomography -> intrinsic reconstruction

**Link.**  C406 is an exact finite-field orbit-recovery example: two hidden sheets have identical
moments through degree two, the third moment carries the orientation, and its stabilizer is exactly
`PSL_2(q)`.  C411 is then a nontrivial tomographic compression: a rank-two incidence projection
still separates all six double cosets and preserves the first informative moment degree.  C412
adds the modular obstruction theory for why some natural compressions lose one more dimension.

**Best move order.**

1. Define the moment-identifiability degree of a signed pair of finite-group orbits in a module.
2. Prove the B3/H3 cases conceptually, consuming C430/C414 rather than duplicating them.
3. Characterize which mixed-Hecke incidence maps preserve that degree and which modular socles kill
   it; C411/C412 are the worked example.
4. Compose with C373/C413 so the recovered orientation selects the intrinsic geometric parent.
5. Only then lift to noisy real or complex observations and compare with bispectrum/orbit-recovery
   algorithms.

**Unlock.**  A finite-field, modular analogue of low-order invariant-moment orbit recovery, with a
proved sufficient statistic and a worked failure of semisimple intuition.  The chain reaches signal
processing and cryo-EM-style tomography without pretending that the Clebsch data are physical
images.

**Cheap gate.**  On the B3 sheet pair, check whether every natural double-coset incidence map that
separates labels preserves the cubic threshold.  One counterexample will show which extra
representation-theoretic hypothesis the general theorem needs.

**Boundary.**  The ordinary real/compact-group third-moment and projected-moment theories are
recent active prior art.  The plausible new surface is finite characteristic, signed orbit halves,
and nonsemisimple rank-drop control.

### G2. Arithmetic monodromy local system: code chirality -> double-six orientation -> `W(E6)`

**Link.**  C376 identifies the code and double-six exchange characters at the Clebsch point.
Basak's 2026 monodromy calculation labels the 27 lines by the 15 edges and 12 pentagons of the
Petersen graph and gives ten explicit meridians generating `W(E6)`.  This supplies an explicit move
set that the original gateway note did not have.

**Best move order.**

1. Pull Basak's ten line permutations into C376's `E_i,L_ij,Q_i` labelling.
2. Compute their action on the distinguished double-six, ten Eckardt matching triples, and C373
   `10+10` supports; stop if the code torsor is not functorial under the relevant stabilizer.
3. If positive, construct the resulting two-sheeted local system on the marked cubic-surface
   locus, with the Clebsch fibre equal to code chirality.
4. Compose with C429's split/inert/ramified outer-symmetry theorem so arithmetic Frobenius and
   topological monodromy act on the same orientation cover.
5. Compare the induced character with Frobenius on the Picard/27-line representation; only after
   equality is exact should Hilbert modular or automorphic consequences be considered.

**Unlock.**  A genuine path-independence theorem between a code-reconstructed torsor, cubic-surface
monodromy, and arithmetic reduction.  Later, zeta/Picard data could predict code/scheme phases
without recomputing syndromes.

**Cheap gate.**  Exact permutation transport for one Basak meridian, using `S5` symmetry to reduce
the other nine.

**Boundary.**  Monodromy of the 27 lines is classical and Basak owns the Petersen generator model.
The candidate theorem is the marked code local system and its arithmetic compatibility.

### G3. Decorated deep-hole transforms as a birational/discriminant calculus

**Link.**  C379 and C405 exhibit the same unexpected pattern in consecutive codimensions:

```text
non-GRS parent -> highly structured deepest locus -> bare transform terminates
               -> a canonical incidence decoration recovers a finite parent fibre.
```

For q=11 the carrier is a conic with a perfect matching and weak-degree-two `A1` roots.  For q=9
it is a Hermitian Cayley octad whose quadric-net discriminant is a 28-point quartic and whose eight
decorations recover parents.

**Best move order.**

1. State a common decorated transform category: objects are MDS projective systems, arrows are
   deepest loci plus the minimal incidence decoration needed for inversion.
2. In the two proved examples, identify the decoration intrinsically as the singular locus of the
   next blow-up/linear-system problem: roots and matchings for C379, singular quadrics/bitangents
   for C405.
3. Prove a two-example structure theorem before searching new fields: decoration is the branch or
   discriminant data of the linear system cutting out the child.
4. Use C398/C401's carrier bounds to seek the next finite list of conic/cubic cases, not a raw arc
   census.
5. Ask whether composition of decorated arrows is associative or whether an obstruction class
   measures termination.

**Unlock.**  A new derived operation on MDS codes that interfaces covering radius, moduli of point
configurations, weak del Pezzo/root systems, nets of quadrics, and discriminant curves.  It could
turn exceptional finite computations into a small birational dynamical theory.

**Cheap gate.**  On C405's fixed octad, test whether the 28 singular quadrics/bitangents recover
the eight parent decorations equivariantly.  This is already C435-adjacent and should be consumed,
not duplicated.

**Boundary.**  Deep-hole/MDS-extension equivalence, Cayley octads, and bitangent dictionaries are
classical.  The possible new object is the decorated transform and its inversion/termination law.

### G4. Extension complexes -> higher-order MDS -> exact list-decoding obstructions

**Link.**  C379 describes all one-column MDS extensions; C295 computes a simultaneous-extension
complex with faces `1,12,36,20`; C405 gives a complete child with no further extension.  Recent
higher-order MDS theory turns minimal intersections of several column spans into the structural
engine behind optimal list decoding.  The local extension complexes may therefore be small exact
shadows of higher-order MDS failure or success.

**Best move order.**

1. Compute the precise `MDS(l)` profile, for the smallest meaningful `l`, of the Clebsch parent,
   its GRS child, and the Hermitian-octad child.
2. Determine whether minimal nonfaces of the simultaneous-extension complex correspond exactly to
   higher-order intersection witnesses.
3. If so, express those witnesses using C379 matchings or C405 bitangents and derive exact small-list
   decoding radii/list sizes.
4. Generalize only through C399's `A3/B3/H3` family or C395's arithmetic pencil, where an actual
   parameter varies.
5. Compare the resulting structured non-GRS families with the generic polynomial-code and random
   Reed--Solomon higher-order-MDS theorems.

**Unlock.**  The first conceptual bridge from the Clebsch deep-hole geometry to the current
higher-order-MDS/list-decoding programme, potentially giving explicit highly symmetric non-GRS
test families with geometric obstruction certificates.

**Cheap gate.**  A small exact linear-algebra script on the already frozen q=11 and q=9 matrices.

**Boundary.**  A one-column extension theorem alone says nothing new about list decoding.  Promotion
requires an exact equality between extension-complex and higher-order-span data plus a nontrivial
decoding consequence.

### G5. Equivariant information lattices as Blackwell/Hecke sufficiency

**Link.**  The sequence `22 -> 6 -> 2 -> 1` is more than four orbit counts.  Each arrow is a
controlled loss of recoverable structure: parent+matching, double-coset profile, orientation sheet,
bare conic.  C411 supplies mixed-Hecke coordinates; C412 identifies the modular socle responsible
for one rank drop; C379 supplies inversion after restoring the missing statistic.

**Best move order.**

1. Formalize each arrow as a `G`-equivariant observation channel and order the channels by
   deterministic/Blackwell sufficiency.
2. Prove that the H3 lattice is intrinsic and minimal, consuming C413 and C434.
3. Characterize when `K\G/H` profiles are sufficient as sets but not linearly faithful, as in C411.
4. Add the moment filtration: which observation levels preserve sheet recovery at degrees 1, 2,
   and 3?
5. Export the theorem to another group pair, preferably B3, before invoking general biset or Hecke
   categorical language.

**Unlock.**  A portable theorem connecting subgroup/double-coset geometry, modular permutation
modules, sufficient statistics, and reversible data loss.  It would give a principled language for
why decorations are necessary in coding transforms and why some symmetry quotients are fatal.

**Cheap gate.**  Enumerate all `G`-equivariant quotient partitions of the 22-point H3 set and verify
that the claimed four levels are the only ones compatible with the recovery predicates.

**Boundary.**  Abstract subgroup and Hecke categories are classical.  The value must come from a
classification of recoverable-information levels or a theorem transporting moment identifiability.

### G6. Pluecker/cluster/skein realization of conic factorization memory

**Link.**  C403's local generator

```text
L_ab L_cd - L_ac L_bd = [a,d][b,c](XZ-Y^2)
```

is simultaneously a four-endpoint matching switch, a Pluecker-type exchange, and the exact reason
factorizations agree on the conic but differ after division by its equation.  This turns the older
`Gr(3,6)`/cluster suggestion into a concrete exchange relation rather than a numerical analogy.

**Best move order.**

1. Identify the exact invariant/coordinate ring in which the normalized secant products and
   four-endpoint switches live; decide between binary-form invariant theory, `Gr(2,n)`, and a
   matchgate/Pfaffian algebra.
2. Test whether one Coxeter one-factorization is a compatible seed/web family or merely a set of
   crossing monomials.
3. If compatible, compute the action of golden `J` on exchange coordinates and ask whether the two
   sheets are opposite chambers or cluster charts.
4. Express C406's cubic orientation as a cluster/canonical-basis coefficient.
5. Only then ask whether the deep-hole transform is mutation or tropical wall crossing.

**Unlock.**  A canonical algebraic explanation of factorization memory, possible positivity or
canonical-basis control of the two sheets, and a route from coding transforms to cluster/tropical
geometry.

**Cheap gate.**  For q=7 and q=11, check cluster compatibility of the edges in each matching sheet
and whether four-endpoint switches stay inside a finite exchange class.

**Boundary.**  Pluecker identities and matching skein relations are established infrastructure.
If the sheets are not selected by cluster compatibility or a canonical basis, stop.

### G7. Oriented finite-field designs and symbolic balanced-half rigidity

**Link.**  Bamberg--Klawuhn place one-factorizations inside the perfect-matching association
scheme; Chien--Kang characterize spherical two-design group orbits by second moments.  C406 adds a
new orientation phenomenon: exactly two complementary halves balance through degree two and their
signed third moment is nonzero.  C409 supplies the general trade language.

**Best move order.**

1. Prove C430's balanced-half uniqueness symbolically from matching-scheme eigenspaces.
2. Formulate an oriented two-design/trade whose orientation class is the first nonzero odd moment.
3. Prove the B3/H3 uniform theorem and explain A3 nonsplitting.
4. Search the classified highly symmetric one-factorizations for further cubic-oriented examples.
5. Derive either a new existence/nonexistence theorem or a reconstruction theorem; do not stop at
   terminology.

**Unlock.**  A design-theoretic family in which low moments certify the un-oriented object and the
next odd moment certifies orientation.  This could feed cubature, invariant theory, orbit recovery,
and construction/classification of symmetric factorizations.

**Cheap gate.**  Decompose the signed half indicator into the known matching-scheme eigenspaces and
test whether the radical argument already forces uniqueness.

**Boundary.**  Raw design status and the one-factorizations are pre-empted.  Only symbolic rigidity,
the oriented invariant, or a portable classification has value.

### G8. Exact invariant hierarchy for code equivalence and geometric learning

**Link.**  C403/C408 show that characteristic, Tutte, enumerator, low moments, and some scalar-tower
data can forget pointed geometry.  C295/C373 show that small uncoloured graphs recover full
projective geometry, while the q=11 pilot has exact WL dimension two.  Together they supply both
positive and negative levels of an invariant hierarchy.

**Best move order.**

1. Select a bounded pair of inequivalent arrangements or six-arcs already separated by the local
   certificates.
2. Compute the exact ladder `matroid/Tutte -> weighted adjoint -> spectrum -> coherent closure ->
   k-WL -> isomorphism -> recovered marking`.
3. Prove the first separating level rather than reporting software output.
4. Turn the proof into a canonical-form or equivalence algorithm for the stable frame/Clebsch
   class.
5. Use the examples as rigorous lower/upper bounds for symmetry-aware graph learning only after the
   combinatorial theorem is complete.

**Unlock.**  Explicit realization-sensitive benchmark families for graph isomorphism, WL, code
equivalence, and invariant/equivariant learning, plus a practical canonicalization algorithm.

**Cheap gate.**  Run 2-WL/coherent closure on one C408 collision pair and one inequivalent q=11
six-arc pair.

**Boundary.**  A benchmark dataset is not a crown.  The deliverable needs an exact hierarchy or a
uniform reconstruction/canonicalization theorem.

### G9. Eight-parameter quantum tomography and a chirality-sensitive noise observable

**Link.**  C372 reduces every `A5`-invariant syndrome distribution or Pauli channel to eight
orbital parameters; C378 isolates a four-dimensional odd sector; C406/C411 supply a cubic
orientation statistic landing there; C374/C375 give an explicit AME state and circuit.

**Best move order.**

1. Define the physically valid `A5`-covariant Pauli-channel cone in the rank-eight algebra.
2. Derive the exact convolution/Fourier update and logical fidelity/recovery law for the Clebsch
   AME encoder.
3. Find the minimum set of symmetry-adapted measurements estimating all eight parameters.
4. Test whether an odd-sector third-order statistic distinguishes the two chiral preparations or a
   symmetry-breaking coherent error invisible to ordinary weight enumerators.
5. Compare the protocol with recent symmetry-adapted classical shadows only after a measurable
   advantage or exact lower bound is proved.

**Unlock.**  An operational Fourier--MacWilliams theorem, compressed process tomography for a
non-GRS perfect tensor, and possibly a chirality-sensitive calibration observable.

**Cheap gate.**  Symbolically propagate the eight primitive idempotent coefficients through one
round of the six-party stabilizer recovery map and check whether the odd sector survives any
physically admissible channel family.

**Boundary.**  Association-scheme diagonalization alone is not a quantum result.  Promotion
requires fewer measurements, an exact recovery/fidelity law, or a detectable error class.

**Unlock.**  A bridge from finite-geometry moduli to LU classification and circuit complexity of

separation.

**Cheap gate.**  Compute the candidate invariant on the existing exact q=11 fixtures before any
continuous optimization or all-field search.

**Boundary.**  Infinite LU families and the new invariants are prior art.  Value requires an exact
formula or separation tied to the Clebsch geometric/arithmetic structure.

### G11. Exceptional obstruction calculus across `D5-E6-E7-E8` and Cayley octads

**Link.**  C376 turns the six-arc into a marked `E6` surface; C379 translates every seventh point

**Best move order.**

1. Package the exact dictionary: code extension obstruction <-> effective root <-> singularity
   type of the weak del Pezzo model.
2. Prove functorial deletion/puncturing squares from `E6` to `D5`, consuming existing line data.
3. Compare the singular C379 route and smooth C405 octad route at the level of bitangent/root
   incidence, not abstract `E8` lattices.
4. Define an obstruction polynomial or root-subsystem invariant that predicts simultaneous MDS
   extension size.
5. Test portability on one non-Clebsch seven/eight-arc family.

**Unlock.**  A usable exceptional-geometry calculus for extension problems rather than a famous
object dictionary.  It could give geometric proofs of code-extension bounds and classify which
singularity transitions are allowed.

**Cheap gate.**  Verify whether the C379 simultaneous-extension complex is determined solely by
the marked effective-root intersection matrix.

**Boundary.**  C382 already closes the icosian comparison.  No route may be reopened from a shared
`E8` name or root count.

### G12. Arithmetic dynamics of code transforms and exact-degree layers

**Best move order.**

1. Define deepest-locus and arrangement-complement data on exact-degree Frobenius orbits.
2. Prove that the C368/H3 layers decompose into regular Cayley pieces away from the finite

**Unlock.**  An arithmetic-dynamical package for whole towers of codes and decorations, potentially
turning sporadic extension computations into rational generating functions and finite phase laws.

**Cheap gate.**  Compute exact-degree matching/deep-locus counts for the q=11 conic child through
degree three and test the predicted regular-layer decomposition.

**Boundary.**  Closed-point orbit formulas are classical.  The code-transform/decorated zeta law
must add a new invariant or phase theorem.

### G13. Matching switches as operational repair moves

**Link.**  C403's four-endpoint switch generates the conic factorization kernel.  In the repair
and service results, conic projection involutions and matching superpositions generate feasible
recovery schedules, while C408 proves global enumerators forget pointed service geometry.  The
same local switch may therefore be the algebraic move underlying both codeword equality and repair
schedule reconfiguration.

**Best move order.**

1. Identify one fixed recovery hypergraph where a four-endpoint switch preserves the served demand
but changes the pointed schedule.
2. Compute the switch graph on integral schedules and its connected components.
3. Determine whether component invariants are exactly the C406 sheet/cubic orientation or the
   C408 pointed defect.
4. Derive a mixing, reconfiguration, or robustness theorem.
5. Only then reconnect to the paused game-value programme; C294 remains paused.

**Unlock.**  A common local-move calculus for factorization memory, integral scheduling, and
possibly game interfaces.  The strongest outcome would be a value-preserving quotient or an exact
connectivity obstruction.

**Cheap gate.**  Enumerate switch connectivity for the q=7/q=11 recovery fixtures already used by
C353--C360.

**Boundary.**  This does not authorize C294 work.  A mere visual similarity between matching moves
is insufficient; the same switch must preserve a stated operational quantity.

### G14. Famous-object satellites with strict one-test gates

These are not independent programmes.  They are cheap tests that may attach to stronger chains.

- **Mathieu/Witt:** C436 should test whether the generated action or induced six-subsets actually
  leave `PGL_2(11)` and satisfy the Steiner property.  If not, close permanently.
- **Bring curve:** C387 should test whether theta/tritangent data detects C381's seven-decomposition
  packet; shared `S5` is not enough.
- **Hermitian tower:** C435 should test `7:3 < PSL_2(7) < PGU(3,3)` on the 36 determinantal classes
  and the 28 pair/bitangent incidence; this is the first move of G3/G11.
- **Genus-two/Segre--Igusa:** construct a canonical curve or moduli point from the Clebsch marking
  before identifying `10+10` with theta orientations.  Complementary triples alone are classical.
- **Cluster/tropical:** use the exact C403 switch relation in G6; do not begin from object names.
- **Hilbert modular/automorphic:** require the G2 arithmetic monodromy character first.
- **Jordan/octonionic/icosian:** no action.  C382's category/character obstruction is decisive.

## Cross-chain compositions worth protecting

Several candidates become more valuable only in sequence.

```text
G7 balanced-half theorem
  -> G1 finite-field moment identifiability
  -> G5 sufficient-information lattice
  -> G9 chirality-sensitive tomography.

G3 decorated transform
  -> G4 higher-order MDS obstruction complex
  -> G11 exceptional-root obstruction calculus.

C429 arithmetic outer phase
  -> G2 monodromy local system
  -> G12 exact-degree/zeta dynamics.

G6 exchange-coordinate model
  -> G7 symbolic sheet rigidity
  -> G13 operational switch connectivity.
```

The move order is load-bearing.  In particular, the modular and representation-theoretic gates
should precede geometric generalization; exact operational survival should precede quantum or
repair rhetoric; and canonical incidence should precede every famous-object corridor.
