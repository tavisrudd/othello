# C1062 plan review: adversarial read of the SCM probe plan

**Lane**: `complete-ports`
**Reviewed**: `2026-09-04-c1062-exploration-log.md` (the plan) against
`2026-09-04-c1062-ergodis-causal-brief.md` (the brief), with
`2026-09-03-c1061-exploration-log.md` as the pace and verdict-style calibration.
**Sources consulted**: the module doc headers of `observational.rs` (plus its `FinitePresentation`,
`GeneratorSpec`, `FiniteContextLanguage` definitions), `continuation.rs`, `contextual.rs`,
`query_design.rs`, `residual_hitting.rs`, `predicate_cover.rs`, `sat.rs`, `feature_dag.rs`,
`composition.rs` in `~/src/ergodis`, and `planted_gap_corpus.rs`, `feature_synthesis.rs`,
`repr_grammar.rs` in `~/src/ergodis-private`; the C1039 report's admission description.
**Status**: review only; nothing implemented.

The one-paragraph verdict. The brief's lowering is not well-typed as written: a generator must map
states to states, and `do(V := a)` applied to an exogenous assignment `u` does not yield an
exogenous assignment. The repair is forced — the state is the pair (exogenous assignment, current
pin set with values) — and it moves the `3^20` the brief claims to avoid from the generator count
into the state count, where the observational compiler materializes it. On that carrier the
intervention monoid is idempotent (every word collapses to its final partial assignment), so the
word-closure the compiler exists to compute adds nothing over a one-pass signature partition, and
probe 1 as written cannot fail. What the compiler does add — the congruence structure on intervened
states — is what probe 3 actually needs for amortization, and the plan has it gated behind probe 6.
The plan also drops the only optimization proposal in a spike for an optimization compiler, and
its probe 3 rests on an unverified "no engine exists" claim that I believe is false (Ibrahim and
Pretschner's SAT/ILP formulation and HP2SAT). Details and repairs follow.

## A. Faithfulness to the brief

Proposal by proposal, what the plan keeps, weakens, or drops.

- **Proposal 1 (causal quotient compiler).** Kept as probe 1, but the object computed is not the
  one the brief names. The plan quotients exogenous contexts; Beckers–Halpern abstraction merges
  endogenous *settings* across variables so that "27 internal states are one causal variable".
  These live on different carriers (section B). The plan should say which one it computes.
  Silent weakening, high value, because proposals 1 and 8 and the whole "abstraction" framing
  depend on the variable-merging reading.
- **Proposal 2 (actual causality as optimization).** Kept as probe 3. The native problem is stated
  for a fixed singleton cause with the search over `W` only; under the modified definition the
  plan says to implement first, a variable can be *part of* a conjunctive cause `X'` (the
  disjunctive forest fire, which is in the plan's own fixture list, is exactly this case: neither
  lightning nor match is a cause alone, the conjunction is, each has responsibility `1/2`). The
  search must range over `(X', W, x')` with `X ⊆ X'`, and the responsibility denominator counts
  `|X'|` as well as `|W|` under the modified definition. The brief's `1/(k+1)` is the original
  Chockler–Halpern formula. This is a correctness gap in the plan's problem statement, not a
  scoping choice; probe 0 should pin the exact formula from Halpern's *Actual Causality*, chapter 6.
- **Proposal 3 (meet-semilattice).** Kept as probe 4, but reduced to property tests of set
  intersection being associative, commutative, and idempotent. Those tests cannot fail and prove
  nothing about causal reasoning. Cut them; the semilattice is a triviality, not a result.
- **Proposal 4 (active discovery by decision regret).** Kept in probe 4. The brief's "do not identify
  structure the decision does not need" is the standard value-of-information argument; the plan's
  measured gap is the right deliverable, but see section E for the negative control it needs.
- **Proposal 5 (EDA and formal debug, the brainstorm's 5/5 target).** Effectively dropped. RTL is
  sequential; the plan's scope condition "acyclic finite deterministic, no dynamic models" excludes
  it, and the only remnants are "a small combinational circuit" in probe 2 and a probe 8 candidate.
  The brainstorm's other 5/5 targets (incident root cause, breach accountability, fault injection)
  are also time-indexed. The obvious bridge — bounded unrolling into an acyclic window, exactly the
  C1061 window pattern — is one sentence and would keep the highest-ranked targets in scope.
  Also dropped: "disable-block" and "force-signal" are mechanism replacements, not value
  assignments; the generator set must admit declared mechanism replacements (still total, still
  finitely many) or those interventions are inexpressible.
- **Proposal 6 (optimization over interventions, robust and minimax-regret policies).** Dropped
  entirely. No probe computes `best_intervention`, yet probe 4 silently needs it (its
  decision-equivalence key is "the induced optimal action") and probe 8 needs it ("minimal
  corrective intervention"). This is the largest faithfulness gap: Ergodis is an exact optimization
  compiler and the plan contains no optimization probe. It is also where the compiled quotient
  plausibly *pays*: "find the cheapest intervention word reaching `O = o`" is a search over the
  monoid action on `Q`, which is what compilation makes cheap, whereas the plan's warm-query
  comparison (one re-solve versus one class lookup) is a race a twenty-variable re-solve wins in
  nanoseconds.
- **Proposal 7 (blame).** Kept as gated probe 7. Correctly sized; it is a formula once 3 and 4 exist.
- **Proposal 8 (mechanistic interpretability).** Out of scope, stated. The stated reason (no
  finite substrate) is the weaker one; the stronger reason is the carrier problem in section B —
  the plan's quotient cannot express "these states are one variable" at all.
- **Proposal 9 (decision-relevant loss).** Dropped, justified: training is not Ergodis. Its exact
  half (the decision-relevant quotient as the target) survives inside probe 4.
- **Proposal 10 (the general causal object and query API).** `cause`, `responsibility`,
  `best_experiment` have probes. `counterfactual` — Pearl level 3 with abduction — has none, even
  though brief section 5 asserts "the twin network becomes a path" as a selling point of the state
  choice. That assertion is never tested. `best_intervention` has none (above).
- **Probabilistic layer.** Brief section 5 lists it as the first genuinely hard thing and demands a
  *measured* negative control against a junction-tree-class baseline. The plan's probe 2 refuses
  that comparison and drops class weights and `P(y | do(i))` entirely. Scoping to the deterministic
  layer is defensible, but it is a silent reversal of the brief's own requirement and should be
  written as a decision with a reason. The right native negative control is not a package; a
  variable-elimination enumerator over the same finite model is about a hundred lines of Rust.
- **Evolve half.** Separator-as-counterexample (probe 5), vocabulary quotient (probe 6), naming the
  classes (probe 5) are all kept. What fell through is the *control*: the brief's claim is that
  separating interventions are *unusually good* counterexamples. C1039's admission is an exhaustive
  replay against the direct model that returns a counterexample on rejection; any counterexample
  works for soundness. The claim being tested is about which counterexample gets fed back, so
  probe 5 needs an A/B against a random or first-row counterexample, or its success cannot be
  attributed to the separator.

## B. The load-bearing technical claim

Brief section 5 says: state = exogenous assignment, generators = atomic `do(V := a)`, contexts =
words, and forty generators replace `3^20` interventions. Five attacks.

**1. The lowering is ill-typed as stated, and the repair puts the exponential back.** A
`GeneratorSpec` in `observational.rs` is a total map from source-sort states to target-sort states,
given as an explicit transition table. If the state is `u`, then `do(V := a)(u)` is a solution of a
*different model* and is not an element of `U`; the generator has nowhere to land. The only faithful
closed carrier is `(u, I)` with `I` the current partial assignment of pinned variables (later
overrides earlier). Every `I` is reachable from every `u`, so the materialized state count is
`|U| · ∏_V (|D_V| + 1)` — for twenty binary variables, `2^20 · 3^20`, and the compiler's cost is
linear in that times the generator count. The brief's forty-generator observation is true of the
*vocabulary* and false of the *compile*; the plan's probe 2 must report the materialized state count
as "raw states", not `|U|`. Two exceptions keep `state = u` valid: interventions only on root
variables (pinning a root is re-assigning its exogenous value, so `U` is closed), and models with no
non-root interventions at all. The plan's own fixtures (chain, fork, collider, diamond) intervene on
non-roots and need the pair carrier.

Repairs, in order of usefulness: bound intervention arity `k` and let the sort be the pinned set
(size at most `k`), so states number `|U| · Σ_{j ≤ k} C(n, j) d^j`; or express the bound as a
`FiniteContextLanguage` over generator words; and treat arity as a dial reported in the results,
with `continuation.rs` giving the tower `~_{≤1} ⊇ ~_{≤2} ⊇ …` for free. The tower does not
stabilize in general (a threshold-`t` outcome keeps refining until arity near `t`), so
stabilization is a reporting object, not a stopping rule.

**2. On that carrier the word closure is vacuous.** Because override composition is idempotent and
commutative on distinct variables, every word reduces to its final partial assignment, and the
reachable set from `u` is exactly `{(u, I)}`. So `u ~ u'` iff `O(M_I(u)) = O(M_I(u'))` for every
`I` in the vocabulary — a one-pass signature partition. Partition refinement recomputes it at the
same asymptotic cost. The compiler's contribution is therefore not the quotient on `U` but (a) the
uniform separator certificate and (b) the congruence on the intervened states `(u, I)`, i.e. the
monoid action on `Q`. The plan should say this plainly; probe 1's "no semantic change needed"
kill criterion is then true and uninformative. The word closure becomes non-vacuous only when the
context alphabet has genuine sequential structure: time-indexed models (unrolled windows),
observe-then-act sequences in the epistemic layer, or non-idempotent operations such as soft or
shift interventions. That is where the "SCM is just another context language" thesis has content,
and none of those are in scope.

**3. Which equivalence is induced.** With `O` = declared outcome, the compiled relation is: `u ~ u'`
iff no admissible intervention distinguishes them at the outcome. That is a coarsening of the
*exogenous* space with the endogenous variables and intervention vocabulary unchanged; in
Beckers–Halpern terms `τ` is the identity on endogenous settings and the commuting square is
trivial. It is closest to a targeted reduction (Kekić–Schölkopf–Besserve's "targeted reduction of
causal models", which probe 0 must check) and, with `O` = all endogenous variables on the canonical
exogenous space, it is exactly Balke and Pearl's 1994 response-function partition: `do(pa(V) := p)`
for every `p` exposes `f_V(p, u_V)` directly, so `u ~ u'` iff every variable's response function
agrees. That is a known object and a mandatory fixture: the compiled full-observation quotient must
equal the product of per-variable response-function partitions, or the lowering is wrong. It is
also a novelty risk for probe 0. Variable-merging abstraction (the B–H object) needs states that
are endogenous settings, and there the plan's carrier is *finer* than B–H's — `(u, I)` remembers
whether `V = 1` is natural or pinned, which `τ : Val(V_L) → Val(V_H)` cannot — and the full atomic
vocabulary is exactly what makes nontrivial merges impossible: `do(V_3 := 1)` on one of 27
micro-variables has no macro image, which is why B–H restrict the allowed intervention set. The
linear-vocabulary claim and the interesting-abstraction claim pull in opposite directions; the plan
hides this behind "declared intervention vocabulary" without saying the interesting case is the
restricted one with block interventions, whose count is not linear.

**4. Totality holds; the observation choice is wrong for two of the three consumers.** Acyclic
deterministic re-solve is a function, so every generator is total and typed on the pair carrier;
the compiler also observes every intermediate `(u, I)`, so prefix outcomes are automatically in the
certificate. But `O` = outcome only is too coarse for the plan's own downstream uses:

- *Level 3.* Abduction needs the evidence set `{u : E(u) = e}` to be a union of classes, which
  holds iff `O ⊇ E`. With `O ⊇ E ∪ {Y}` the twin network is indeed two words from one state and
  `P(Y_{x'} = y' | e)` is exact on the weighted quotient. With `O` = all endogenous variables on a
  root-exogenous model the quotient is the identity (the roots are in `v`). So `O` is a dial from
  coarse to identity, and the plan must declare the query-relevant variable set as part of the
  presentation and report `|Q|` as a function of `|O|`. Brief section 5's twin-network sentence is
  right only under that declaration.
- *Actual causality.* The verdict "X = x caused φ in u" needs the actual values of `X` and every
  candidate `W`, none of which are in `O` = φ. Two contexts merged by `~` can carry different
  actual values and different verdicts, so the plan's sentence "the compiled quotient from probe 1
  is the substrate, so repeated queries share one compile" is unsupported as written. What *is*
  true and useful: for a fixed actual context `u*`, the contingency states `(u*, W := w*)` are
  states of the compiled system, and two contingency sets in the same class have identical futures
  under every `do(X := x')`. So the quotient prunes the contingency search by class, and the
  exhaustion half of the certificate can be "these `m` classes cover every smaller `W`, and each
  fails" — compact exactly when `m` is far below `Σ_{j < k} C(n, j)`. That is the real amortization
  mechanism, it is the probe 6 structure, and it is the measurable form of probe 3's kill
  criterion. The plan gates probe 6 behind 1–2 and never connects it to 3.

**5. Which interventions are generated.** Later-overrides-earlier words generate every hard
partial assignment and nothing else: no soft or mechanism-replacement interventions (proposal 5's
"disable block"), no policy interventions `do(V := g(pa(V)))` (needed for proposal 6's "compiled
policy"), no release-to-mechanism. All of these are total typed generators from a declared finite
set, so the fix is to widen "generator" to "declared atomic mechanism edit"; the linear-vocabulary
statement then reads "linear in the declared edit set", which is what it always meant.

## C. Probe ordering and kill criteria

- **Probe 1 is not the cheapest fatal test; it is a test that cannot fail.** Its three kill
  conditions are (a) a semantic change to the compiler — none is needed once the carrier is fixed;
  (b) separators not recoverable in intervention terms — generator IDs *are* interventions, so
  trivially recoverable; (c) identity quotient on every natural fixture — the fixtures are
  hand-picked to compress. The cheapest fatal test is the pencil analysis in section B (carrier,
  state count, signature collapse, arity bound): under an hour, no code, and it decides whether
  probe 1 is an adapter worth writing or an infrastructure step whose value lies entirely
  downstream. Run it as probe 1a before any lowering code.
- **Probe 0's second question has no consequence.** The audit asks whether an actual-causality
  engine exists but says nothing about what changes if one does. I believe one does: Ibrahim and
  Pretschner's "From checking to inference: actual causality computations as optimization
  problems" (ATVA 2020, MaxSAT and ILP formulations, responsibility included) and HP2SAT (2019),
  both to be verified in the audit. If confirmed, probe 3's framing changes from "no engine exists"
  to "a compiled, certificate-carrying engine versus a per-query SAT/ILP encoding", which is a
  different and narrower claim; the plan should state that branch now. The audit must also check
  bisimulation minimization: the quotient over words of atomic interventions with an outcome
  observation *is* bisimulation of the interventional labelled transition system, and partition
  refinement for it is Kanellakis–Smolka / Paige–Tarjan. Beckers–Halpern-adjacent literature to
  check: Rubenstein et al. 2017, Beckers–Halpern 2019, Beckers–Eberhardt–Halpern 2019,
  Rischel–Weichwald 2021, Geiger et al. 2021/2023, Zennaro et al. 2023, Massidda et al. 2023,
  Kekić et al. targeted reduction, Balke–Pearl 1994.
- **Probe 3's kill criterion is an `and` that should be an `or`.** "No compact negative *and* no
  amortization" lets a probe with no compact certificate pass on amortization alone. Either
  failure means re-implementation rather than compilation.
- **Probe 3's internal A/B is against a solver that does not exist.** `sat.rs` is a structured-CNF
  recognizer for colouring instances (clique/pigeonhole certificates, no CDCL). There is no in-tree
  general SAT solver. Either write a small DPLL for these tiny models — feasible at this pace and
  useful elsewhere — or drop the A/B and say so.
- **Probe 4's adapter hypothesis is likely false on the module's own terms.** `query_design.rs`
  handles *binary* queries as hypothesis bitmasks with greedy constructors and exact verification;
  an intervention on a finite SCM has a `d`-ary outcome partition, and binarizing it overcounts
  experiments. Expected decision regret needs a prior and a utility that the module does not have.
  Probe 4 is a new module that reuses the verification pattern, and its kill criterion ("needs a
  rewrite rather than an adapter") fires on day one unless rephrased.
- **`residual_hitting.rs` does not obviously fit probe 3.** Feasibility of a contingency set is not
  monotone under the modified definition (pinning a mediator at its actual value can *un-flip* the
  outcome), so minimal-`W` is not a hitting-set problem in general. The hitting-set flavour appears
  only under the original definition's overdetermination cases. The plan's "reuse where the
  structure is a hitting problem" should say when it is, or drop the reference.
- **Parallelism.** Probes 3 and 4 are independent of each other and of 2; both can start once the
  lowering exists. Probe 5 has a hidden dependency on probe 2's failure-domain fixture. Probe 8 has
  a hidden dependency on the unplanned best-intervention probe. Probe 3 has a hidden dependency on
  the class-pruning half of probe 6. The gating table should be redrawn: 0 ∥ 1a → 1 → {2, 3, 4, 5}
  with 6's pruning half folded into 3, and 8 after both 3 and the optimization probe.
- **Sizes.** Against C1061's calibration (thirty-one probes in a day, each carrying a number), the
  S/M/L sizes are fine; what is missing is a predeclared *number* per probe. C1061's rows read
  "break-even 1.13 updates", "11.4x", "15 of 18 cells". The C1062 rows should carry thresholds:
  quotient-to-orbit ratio, certificate verifier work as a fraction of search, experiment-count gap.

## D. Missing probes

1. **Probe 1a, the pencil probe.** Closed carrier, materialized state count as a function of
   `(|U|, n, d, k)`, the signature-collapse theorem for hard interventions, and the arity-bounded
   sort structure. Written up in one page; decides what probe 1 is for.
2. **The Balke–Pearl fixture.** Compile a canonical-exogenous model with `O` = all endogenous
   variables and check class-for-class agreement with the product of per-variable response-function
   partitions. External truth, not a self-written oracle.
3. **The optimization probe.** `best_intervention`: minimum-cost word (or minimum-arity
   intervention) reaching a declared outcome, first by direct enumeration, then over the monoid
   action on `Q`, with the class-level search as the compiled form. This is the query the quotient
   was compiled for, and it also gives probe 4 its decision key and probe 8 its "minimal repair".
   Add minimax-regret over a small model set as its second half.
4. **A level-3 probe.** Fix a model, a prior over `U`, an evidence set `E`, a counterfactual
   query; compute it on the weighted quotient with `O ⊇ E ∪ {Y}` and against direct enumeration;
   then repeat with `O` = outcome only and show the quotient gives the *wrong* answer. Turns brief
   section 5's twin-network sentence into a measured statement with its precondition.
5. **The observation tower.** For one model, `|Q|` as `O` grows from outcome to every variable, and
   the arity tower `~_{≤k}`. Cheap, and it is the only measurement that tells a user which
   declaration to make.
6. **The compositional lowering.** Probe 6's "a hundred thousand variables become a fifty-state
   machine" is unreachable by materializing `(u, I)`. The only route is compositional: quotient each
   mechanism locally (its response classes) and compose along the DAG with `composition.rs` and the
   C1061 retained-tree machinery, checking the composed quotient against the flat one on small
   models. This is the Rischel–Weichwald direction and the only scalable one; it should replace
   probe 6's grand claim.
7. **The unrolled-window fixture.** One sequential toy (a two-state machine over a bounded window)
   lowered by unrolling, to show whether the 5/5 targets are one sentence away or genuinely out.
8. **A shared-misreading control for the oracle.** The Python oracle and the Rust lowering are
   written by the same author from the same understanding. The oracle must enumerate partial
   assignments directly (not "words up to the diameter", which imports the compiler's view), and
   the textbook HP cases must be checked against the *published* verdicts and responsibility
   values, not against the oracle.

## E. Risk of self-deception

- **Trivial compression counted as a win (probe 2).** Two things compress `U` for free: pruning
  non-ancestors of the outcome (relevance), and the symmetry of interchangeable components (in the
  reliability family the classes are just the failed-count orbits). Guard: report classes against
  three baselines — `|U|`, `|U|` after ancestor pruning, and orbits of the declared automorphism
  group — and count only the residue as intervention-driven compression. Also report the
  materialized `(u, I)` count, not `|U|`, as the compile cost.
- **Probe 1 passing by construction.** Guard: the report must state that the compiled quotient
  equals a one-pass signature partition of the same cost, and identify what the compiler added
  (certificate, congruence on intervened states). A probe 1 report that says only "partitions
  agree" has measured nothing.
- **A strawman warm-query baseline (probe 2).** One re-solve of a twenty-variable model is
  nanoseconds and a class lookup cannot beat it meaningfully. Guard: the query family must be one
  that costs enumeration without the quotient — best-intervention, or "does any intervention of
  arity ≤ k reach `o`" — and the baseline must be a memoized re-solve, not a naive one.
- **An exhaustion certificate that is the search (probe 3).** A list of every smaller `W` with its
  outcome is a re-run. Guard: measure the verifier's work (solves performed, bytes read) and require
  it to be a small fraction of the search; require the certificate to name classes or dominance
  arguments rather than candidates. Predeclare the ratio threshold.
- **Wrong variant, right-looking numbers (probe 3).** Singleton-cause search under the modified
  definition returns "not a cause, responsibility 0" for each disjunctive-forest-fire cause, which
  looks like a clean verdict and is wrong. Guard: the published verdicts and responsibility values
  for each textbook fixture, entered before the run.
- **Decision-sufficient identification "much cheaper" by construction (probe 4).** If the objective
  ignores most of the model the gap is guaranteed. Guard: include a predeclared family where the
  decision needs full identification and expect a gap near zero there; report both.
- **Corpus-perfect terms and false recoveries (probe 5).** Admission replays exhaustively, so an
  admitted term is exact; the risk is elsewhere. First, `feature_synthesis.rs` fits integer
  targets, and class labels are arbitrary integers, so the fitness must be kernel equality (a
  partition distance), not label regression, or Evolve chases a permutation. Second, "recovered the
  planted coordinates" must be judged up to reparameterization: a term whose kernel is the quotient
  but whose coordinates are a bijective re-encoding is a full success. Third, the informativeness
  claim needs the random-counterexample A/B named in section A, with generations-to-seal as the
  measure; without it, a success is attributable to admission, not to separators.
- **Semilattice property tests (probe 4).** Cannot fail. Delete.
- **"Natural fixtures" in three kill criteria.** Hand-picked fixtures make "never merges on natural
  families" unfalsifiable. Guard: predeclare each family's verdict under the shape classifier before
  building it, including at least one predicted loss per probe, and count a predicted-win-that-loses
  as the only failure mode — which the plan already does for probe 2 and should do everywhere.

## F. EV ranking

Given the pace (a probe an hour is normal here) and the native-implementation preference, ranked
by information per hour:

1. **Probe 0 plus probe 1a, in parallel, first.** Under two hours combined. Probe 0 can reframe
   probe 3 (HP2SAT and the MaxSAT/ILP formulation), reframe probe 1 (bisimulation minimization,
   Balke–Pearl, targeted reduction), and settle the responsibility formula. Probe 1a decides the
   carrier and the cost model before any code. Nothing else should start before both are on disk.
2. **Probe 1, restated.** Still worth building: the typed `CausalPresentation`, the pair-carrier
   lowering with arity sorts, the direct-enumeration oracle, the Balke–Pearl fixture, and the
   observation tower (missing probe 5). Its deliverable is infrastructure and a stated cost model,
   not a verdict.
3. **The optimization probe (missing probe 3), merged with probe 2.** This is the first probe that
   can show the compile paying for itself on a query that is expensive without it, and it unblocks
   4 and 8. Probe 2's economics table should be built around this query with the three baselines.
4. **Probe 3, restated.** Modified definition with conjunctive causes, class-level pruning from the
   intervened-state congruence, verifier-work measurement, textbook verdicts pre-entered, and either
   a small in-tree DPLL or no A/B. Sized L correctly; its risk is running before probe 0 answers
   the engine question.
5. **Probe 5 with the random-counterexample control and kernel fitness.** The Evolve half's
   load-bearing claim, cheap once 1 and 2 exist.
6. **The level-3 probe (missing probe 4).** One to two hours; converts a brief assertion into a
   measured precondition.
7. **Probe 4, reframed as a new k-ary design module.** Lowest EV of the spike proper: the
   decision-sufficient thesis is standard value-of-information, and the module work dominates the
   insight. Keep, but after the optimization probe it depends on, and without the property tests.
8. **Probe 6, replaced by the compositional lowering (missing probe 6).** Its pruning half moves
   into probe 3; the vocabulary quotient `J` acting on `Q` stays as a one-line measurement.
9. **Probe 7** is a deliverable of probe 4 once 3 exists, not a probe. **Probe 8** is the demo; keep
   it but never count it as evidence, and gate it on the optimization probe as well as on 3.

Cuts: probe 7 as a standalone row; probe 4's semilattice tests; probe 3's `sat.rs` A/B as written;
probe 1's three kill criteria as written. I would run probe 0 first, because it is the only probe
whose answer can delete a session-sized probe before it starts.
