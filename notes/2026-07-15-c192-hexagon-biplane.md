## The K₁₂ reframe — the part Edge does not state

Flagged by the [Fable advisory](2026-07-15-gems-theory-gaps-method-fable-advisory.md) as "what you're
not seeing", and confirmed by computation (same script, both conics):

Under the polarity, **an external point's polar is a chord meeting the conic twice** — Edge's own §5,
in his vocabulary (`c-line`, external ⟺ polar is a chord). So the 66 external points are exactly the
66 chords, i.e. the 66 **edges of K₁₂** on the conic's 12 points. Two external points whose join is an
`s-line` (skew) have polar chords meeting off the conic, hence sharing no conic point — so a Clebsch
hexagon's 6 vertices give **6 disjoint chords: a perfect matching of the twelve**. A system of 11
hexagons partitions the 66 external points, hence the 66 edges: **each system is a 1-factorization of
K₁₂**. Verified: 22/22 hexagons are perfect matchings; both systems are 1-factorizations.

In plain terms: the conic's 12 points are 12 teams, each hexagon is one round of 6 games, and each
system of 11 is a full round-robin season.

**It is not perfect**: 0 of 55 factor-pairs in either system union to a Hamiltonian 12-cycle. So this
is **not** GK₁₂, the textbook perfect 1-factorization built from PGL(2,11) on P¹(F₁₁) — which was the
first guess on seeing this data, and it was wrong. Recorded as an observation, not a theory: 0/55 is
not generic imperfection, since a random 1-factorization would show some perfect pairs, and unanimous
failure is itself structure. Nobody has explained it and nobody should pretend to.

**Edge does not state any of this.** His synthemes are of each hexagon's own 6 vertices (K₆), never of
the conic's 12 points; the paper contains no *twelve*, no *matching*, no *factorization* in this sense.
He has every ingredient in §5 and never assembles them.

**Absent from Edge is not novel.** 1-factorizations of K₁₂ carrying PSL(2,11) are classical ground, and
Edge's Klein 1879 pointer says where this configuration already lives. If anything here is pursued, it
is the 0/55 anomaly — and only after a search of the answer, not the question.
