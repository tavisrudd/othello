## Opening summary

**Read-depth accounting, as required.** This report names 25 sources. **One was read at full text**:
Van den Nest, Dehaene and De Moor, arXiv:quant-ph/0411115v2, the paper the audited note claims to
subsume. Seven were read at `partial` depth with the sections recorded in each entry (Rains,
Hostens–Dehaene–De Moor, Bahramgiri–Beigi, Gross–Van den Nest, Englbrecht–Kraus, arXiv:2507.09416,
arXiv:2607.26214). The remaining seventeen are `abstract/metadata only` and are named in order to be
dismissed. No source here rests on a review service or on a secondary characterisation.

**Verdicts, in one line each.** No prior work was located that states Theorem P at intermediate index
size, Corollary G in its generation form, Lemma M above local dimension two, or Corollary C for CSS
coset states — but Lemma R is substantially anticipated at local dimension two by the established
semi-Clifford dichotomy, and the audit forces one correction on the note: its "cheapest falsifier"
prediction about LU-but-not-LC counterexamples is the contrapositive of a published theorem, not a
prediction of the new framework. Nobody has generalized Van den Nest, Dehaene and De Moor's Theorem 1
beyond qubits; the arbitrary-local-dimension claim is the strongest and most unoccupied of the seven,
and a `D = 9` three-party example published in 2025 shows local unitary and local Clifford
equivalence genuinely differ for qudit stabilizer states, so a sufficient criterion there is not
vacuous. Two further obligations fall out: Theorem P's `r ≥ 3` boundary must be credited to
tensor-decomposition uniqueness (whose literature this half did not search), and the note's
finiteness-of-local-symmetries corollary must be scoped above local dimension two, because Englbrecht
and Kraus already determine every local symmetry of every qubit stabilizer state.

## Claims under audit

1. Theorem P — partial-Weyl marginal criterion, index set of size `N` with `2 < N < d²`, `r ≥ 3`.
2. Lemma R — the recognition group is a subgroup; induced label map injective additive; Cliffordness ⇔ full.
3. Corollary G — generation criterion from diagonal marginals.
4. Lemma M — minimal-support label group injects at every party, arbitrary local dimension.
5. Subsumption of Van den Nest–Dehaene–De Moor Theorem 1.
6. Arbitrary (non-prime-power) local dimension.
7. Corollary C — CSS coset states over a prime field.

## Sources

### S1. Van den Nest, Dehaene, De Moor, "Local unitary versus local Clifford equivalence of stabilizer states"

- Identifiers: arXiv:quant-ph/0411115v2 (23 Nov 2005); DOI 10.1103/PhysRevA.71.062323;
  OpenAlex W2018296419; Semantic Scholar bab3782203d53ae2bbd92b471667dcc4a92dba6e.
- Read depth: **full text**. Access: shared literature cache, key `arXiv:quant-ph/0411115`,
  sha256 `c0f8e192552369d5af9304ebf08995f59b6917e243a570f37ff1b29f3b4cb735`, poppler `pdftotext`
  extraction (8 pages). Version read: arXiv v2, which the arXiv comment states is the published
  version. Sections relied on: II (stabilizer formalism, binary and GF(4) representations), III
  (minimal supports; Lemma 1, Lemma 2, Theorem 1, Corollary 1, Lemma 3), IV (GF(4)-linear
  characterisation, Theorem 2).
- What it actually contains, at its strongest accurate level:
  - Lemma 1: for a minimal support `ω` of a qubit stabilizer state, `A_ω(ψ) ∈ {1,3}`, and `A_ω = 3`
    forces `|ω|` even. Proof is the observation that two distinct elements with the same minimal
    support must differ at every coordinate of `ω`, so `{M_i, M'_i, (MM')_i} = {σx,σy,σz}`.
  - Lemma 2 (attributed by them to Rains): a local unitary carrying one LC-copy of
    `ρ[2m,2m−2,2]` to another, `m ≥ 2`, is local Clifford.
  - Theorem 1: if `σx, σy, σz` all occur at every qubit inside `M(ψ)` (the subgroup generated by
    minimal elements), then `LU(ψ) = LC(ψ)`.
  - Corollary 1: four sufficient conditions (i) `S(ψ) = M(ψ)`; (ii) GF(4)-linearity; (iii) every
    non-minimal element's support strictly contains a minimal support with `A_{ω'} = 3`;
    (iv) a cover by minimal supports each with `A_ω = 3`.
- Directly load-bearing for the audit, quoted structure of their Theorem 1 proof: in the
  `A_ω(ψ) = 1` branch they derive `U_1 M_1 U_1† = ±M'_1` and `U_1 N_1 U_1† = ±N'_1` with
  `M_1 = σx`, `N_1 = σz`, and conclude "The identities (16) show that `U_1` is a Clifford
  operation." *Auditor's inference (mine, not theirs):* this final step is the `q = 2`,
  two-generator instance of Corollary G — they use, without isolating or naming it, that a
  one-qubit unitary recognising `σx` and `σz` as Paulis is Clifford. They do not state it as a
  lemma, do not identify the recognised set as a group, do not consider generation from more than
  two labels, and never leave local dimension two.
- Everything in the paper is at local dimension two. The words qudit, prime power, and `Z_d` do not
  occur; the GF(4) discussion is about the `F_4` structure on qubit labels, not about qudits.

### S5. Gross, Van den Nest, "The LU-LC conjecture, diagonal local operations and quadratic forms over GF(2)"

- Identifiers: arXiv:0707.4000v2 (31 Jul 2007); DOI 10.26421/qic8.3-4-3; Semantic Scholar
  bc0377572a0ef9b6362cd1d84dfa78c3352de663; DBLP journals/qic/GrossN08.
- Read depth: **partial**. Access: shared literature cache, key `arXiv:0707.4000`, sha256
  `fe898b71325ddd465218629b97e48f82a48ad058ea7c0471f80db5cdf3c74188`, poppler `pdftotext`, 24 pages.
  Version read: arXiv v2. Sections relied on: the abstract; § 3.1 (Proposition 1, Lemmas 1–3);
  § 3.2 (Theorem 4, Corollary 1); § 3.3 (Corollaries 2 and 3). Sections 4 and 5 (the reduction to
  quadratic forms over GF(2), and the code-to-state reduction) were not read beyond their statements
  in the introduction.
- What it contains that bears directly on the recognition group:
  - They define `S⟨i⟩ := {g ∈ S : g_i = I}` and prove (Lemma 1) `[S : S⟨i⟩] ∈ {1,2,4}`, and (Lemma 2)
    that `|S|`, `|S⟨i⟩|` and the index are local-unitary invariants.
  - Lemma 3: if `[S : S⟨i⟩] = 2` then `U_i` is semi-Clifford, where semi-Clifford means (their
    definition) that `U σ U†` is in the Pauli group for at least one `σ ∈ {X,Y,Z}`.
  - Theorem 4 and Corollary 1: every local unitary relating two fully entangled qubit stabilizer
    states on `n ≥ 3` qubits is a *local semi-Clifford* operator.
  - Corollary 2: up to local Cliffords all factors of such a unitary are diagonal.
- *Auditor's inference, marked as mine:* in the C804 note's vocabulary, "semi-Clifford at party `i`"
  is exactly "`Λ_i ≠ {0}`" and "Clifford" is "`Λ_i = F_2²`". Gross and Van den Nest therefore prove
  that the recognition group is nontrivial at every party of a fully entangled qubit stabilizer
  state, and their Corollary 2 is the statement that the residual freedom lives in the intermediate
  case. They never call this a group, never index it, and never leave local dimension two — but the
  *set* `Λ_i` is the object their semi-Clifford dichotomy is about. This is the strongest partial
  anticipation of Lemma R located in this audit.
- Note on terminology collision: "semi-Clifford" also denotes an unrelated notion in the Clifford
  hierarchy literature (arXiv:0712.2084, arXiv:0810.5108, both **abstract/metadata only** from the
  Semantic Scholar citing list of this work). Any manuscript adopting Lemma R should not reuse that
  word.

### S6. Englbrecht, Kraus, "Symmetries and entanglement of stabilizer states"

- Identifier: arXiv:2001.07106v1 (20 Jan 2020).
- Read depth: **partial**. Access: shared literature cache, key `arXiv:2001.07106`, sha256
  `fecef9717dbf5807c3d6d7890c16c7bc2c89ac60c85003c14355929b1ffc1cac`, poppler `pdftotext`, 19 pages.
  Version read: arXiv v1. Sections relied on: abstract and introduction; § II definitions
  (semi-Clifford, Theorem 1 quoting Gross–Van den Nest, Observations 1–2); § III Theorems 2 and 3,
  Corollaries 3.1–3.3.
- Abstract claim: "We characterize all local symmetries of arbitrary stabilizer states and provide
  an algorithm which determines them."
- Load-bearing content: their Theorem 3 states that if a graph state has a local symmetry with a
  factor `∝ σ₁ exp(iα σ₂)` where `α` is not a dyadic-rational multiple of `π`, then that vertex is a
  leaf up to local complementation; Corollary 3.3 then extracts a local Clifford symmetry of order 4
  in the remaining cases. All of this is at local dimension two (`|ψ⟩ ∈ (C²)^{⊗n}`).
- *Auditor's inference:* the C804 note's "free" corollary — finiteness of the local symmetry group
  for every stabilizer state whose recognition groups are full — is, at local dimension two, weaker
  than what Englbrecht and Kraus already prove, since they determine the entire local symmetry group
  of every qubit stabilizer state and identify exactly when continuous symmetries occur (leaves up
  to local complementation). The C804 corollary retains value only above local dimension two.

### S7. Wong and Jiang, "Local unitary decomposition of tripartite arbitrary leveled qudit stabilizer states into p-level-qudit EPR and GHZ state"

- Identifier: arXiv:2507.09416v2; authors Yat Wong and Liang Jiang (author list retrieved from the
  arXiv API, `id_list=2507.09416`).
- Read depth: **partial**. Access: fetched `https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.09416` on 2026-08-02, added
  to the shared cache, key `arXiv:2507.09416`, sha256
  `eb3c7445b6f124aba353f2ed2461eb40909115687db864feb09c65c13656e80a`, poppler `pdftotext`, 8 pages.
  Sections relied on: abstract; § III "Special properties of prime power dimension stabilizer
  states".
- Load-bearing content, verbatim from § III: the `D = 9`, `N = 3` state stabilized by
  `S = ⟨X₁X₂X₃, X₁³X₂⁶, Z₁Z₂Z₃, Z₁³Z₂⁶⟩` "is not connected to the GHZ state, stabilized by

  qudit stabilizer states at `D = 9`, at only three parties. This is not a criterion and not a
  predecessor, but it is the strongest located evidence that a general-`d` sufficient criterion is a
  non-vacuous thing to want.

### S9. Further works inspected and dismissed

All named here carry read depth **abstract/metadata only**, retrieved from the arXiv API
(`https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?id_list=…`) unless stated otherwise.

- arXiv:2603.25219, "The 27-qubit Counterexample to the LU-LC Conjecture is Minimal" (2026). Proves
  that for graph states on up to 26 qubits, LU and LC equivalence coincide, via 2-local
  complementation, triorthogonal codes and Reed–Muller codes. Qubits only; no general criterion.
- arXiv:2409.20183, "Local equivalence of stabilizer states: a graphical characterisation". A
  generalisation of local complementation that graphically characterises LU-equivalence of qubit
  graph states, built on minimal local sets. Qubits only.
- arXiv:2502.06566, "Deciding Local Unitary Equivalence of Graph States in Quasi-Polynomial Time".
  Algorithmic; also improves the bound to: LU and LC coincide for graph states on up to 19 qubits.
  Qubits only.
- arXiv:2402.10678, "Covering a Graph with Minimal Local Sets". Shows every graph is covered by
  minimal local sets, in polynomial time, and extends the definitions and the covering result to
  `q`-multigraphs, the graphical counterpart of qudit graph states. This is the graph-theoretic
  analogue of the "coverage is free" remark in the C804 note, and its qudit extension is the closest
  located general-dimension statement in the minimal-support direction — but it is a graph covering
  theorem, not an LU-to-LC criterion.
- arXiv:1611.06938, "Graph states and local unitary transformations beyond local Clifford
  operations". Systematic construction of LU-but-not-LC graph state pairs. Qubits only.
- arXiv:2410.03961, "Algorithm to Verify Local Equivalence of Stabilizer States". Qubit graph and
  stabilizer states; reduces LU equivalence to modular linear equations.
- arXiv:quant-ph/0611214, Zeng, Chung, Cross, Chuang, "Local unitary versus local Clifford
  equivalence of stabilizer and graph states". Read depth **abstract/metadata only** beyond the
  title page, from the cached extraction, key `arXiv:quant-ph/0611214`, sha256
  `ee703ee6f3552af34a9497b6dfc57b977c99431c1963cc9b26ec334ffbb062fd`. Broadens the LU⇔LC class to
  graph states with no cycles of length 3 or 4, and to a class of distance-2 stabilizer states, and
  shows distance-2 states lie beyond Van den Nest, Dehaene and De Moor's criterion. Qubits only.
  *This is the one located work that extends the LU⇔LC class in a direction their criterion does not
  cover*, and any adoption should position against it: it is not subsumed by Corollary G, because
  the states it covers are exactly ones where the minimal-support mechanism does not reach.
- arXiv:0709.1266, Ji, Chen, Wei, Ying, "The LU-LC conjecture is false". Read depth
  **abstract/metadata only** — title page and abstract from the cached extraction, key
  `arXiv:0709.1266`, sha256
  `0aa5dd849cff1bf4f8386744f442a865510c105d155927980ba17acfb873acb9`. Qubits only.
- arXiv:2601.19677, Tan, "Transversal gates of the ((3,3,2)) qutrit code and local symmetries of the

## Verdicts

Per claim. "No predecessor located" is a searched-and-found-nothing negative over the coverage
recorded above; it is not a claim that none exists.

### Claim 1 — Theorem P, partial-Weyl marginal criterion with `2 < N < d²`

**No predecessor located, with a mandatory citation obligation.** The two extremes are published:
`N = 2` is the second branch of Van den Nest–Dehaene–De Moor's Theorem 1 (S1) and, in a different
guise at the level of whole stabilizer codes rather than minimal-support marginals, Gross–Van den
Nest's Lemma 3 (S5); `N = q² = 4` at `q = 2` is Rains's Theorem 13 (S2), imported as their Lemma 2
and as Gross–Van den Nest's Proposition 1. Nothing located interpolates. However the C804 note's own
`tt` pass is right that Theorem P is an instance of Kruskal-type tensor decomposition uniqueness;
that identification is a genuine mathematical predecessor for the *mechanism* and any manuscript
adoption must cite the tensor-uniqueness literature rather than present the `r ≥ 3` floor as new.
This audit did not attempt to close the tensor-decomposition literature — see the coverage
statement; that is a gap owned by the other half of C807 or by a successor task.

### Claim 2 — Lemma R, the recognition group

**Substantially anticipated at local dimension two; not located as stated at general `d`.** The set
`Λ_i` is the object behind the established semi-Clifford/Clifford dichotomy: Gross–Van den Nest (S5)
prove `Λ_i ≠ {0}` at every party for fully entangled qubit stabilizer states, and "Clifford"
has always meant `Λ_i` everything. At `q = 2` the group property has no content, since every subset
of `F_2²` containing `0` and one other element is a subgroup and size 3 is impossible. So Lemma R
should be presented as a definition plus an elementary observation that becomes non-vacuous only
above local dimension two, not as a new theorem. Two naming cautions: do not call it semi-Clifford
(collides with the Clifford-hierarchy usage), and credit Gross–Van den Nest for nontriviality of
`Λ_i` in the qubit case.

### Claim 3 — Corollary G, generation criterion

**No predecessor located for the general statement; the two-generator case at `q = 2` is used,
unnamed, inside a published proof.** Van den Nest, Dehaene and De Moor's Theorem 1, second branch,
derives `U₁ σx U₁† = ±M'₁` and `U₁ σz U₁† = ±N'₁` and concludes `U₁` is Clifford (their equation 16
and the sentence following it). That is Corollary G with two recovered labels at `q = 2`. The
generalisation — an arbitrary family of marginals, arbitrary numbers of recovered labels, arbitrary
`d`, with the conclusion driven by *generation* of the label group — is not located.

### Claim 4 — Lemma M at arbitrary local dimension

**No predecessor located in the quantum literature; the classical shadow is standard.** Van den
Nest–Dehaene–De Moor's Lemma 1 (S1) is the `q = 2` case, stated as `A_ω ∈ {1,3}` plus a parity
remark. For classical linear codes the corresponding facts (minimal codewords, and that a linear
code is spanned by them over any finite field) are standard and are cited by them as their reference
[16]; the C804 note already treats that as free. The additive/`F_p`-subspace form at arbitrary `d`
was not located. It is a routine argument, and the audit's recommendation is that it be stated as
the general-dimension form of their Lemma 1 with explicit attribution, not as an independent
contribution.

### Claim 5 — subsumption of their Theorem 1, and whether anyone else generalized it

**Two parts.** (a) The subsumption is sound but is a unification of their own two arguments plus
Rains, not a strengthening at `q = 2`: their `A_ω = 3` branch is Rains's Theorem 13, their `A_ω = 1`
branch is the `N = 2` case, and their concluding step is Corollary G with two generators. Wording
should say "unifies", not "generalizes", when the local dimension is two. (b) **No one located has
generalized their Theorem 1 beyond qubits.** Basis: the full 68-record Semantic Scholar citing set
of the seed, screened; the 35-record Semantic Scholar citing set of Gross–Van den Nest, screened;
three zbMATH searches; nine arXiv abstract/title searches; two web searches. Every located extension
of the LU⇔LC class — Zeng–Chung–Cross–Chuang, the graphical-characterisation line
(arXiv:2409.20183, arXiv:2502.06566), the minimality result arXiv:2603.25219 — is at local dimension
two.

**Correction the audit forces on the C804 note.** Its "cheapest falsifier available" section presents
as a new falsifiable prediction that every LU-but-not-LC counterexample state must have a qubit at
which the minimal-element label projection is proper. That is the contrapositive of Van den Nest,
Dehaene and De Moor's Theorem 1 as published, and it is not a prediction of the new framework at
all. The prediction is already known to hold, unconditionally, at local dimension two. That
paragraph must be rewritten before any adoption.

### Claim 6 — arbitrary, including non-prime-power, local dimension

**No predecessor located.** The setting exists and is standard: Hostens, Dehaene and De Moor (S3)
give the Pauli group, Clifford group and stabilizer states over `Z_d` for arbitrary `d`. Qudit local
*Clifford* equivalence has been studied (Bahramgiri and Beigi, S4, with an efficient decision
algorithm in the non-binary case). Qudit graph-state minimal local sets have been studied
(arXiv:2402.10678, extended to `q`-multigraphs). And local unitary equivalence is now known to be
strictly weaker than local Clifford equivalence for qudit stabilizer states, with an explicit
`D = 9`, three-party example (S7). What is not located anywhere is a sufficient condition under
which a local unitary between qudit states is forced to be Clifford. This is the strongest of the
seven claims: it is unoccupied territory, and the `D = 9` example makes it clear that the territory
is not vacuous.

### Claim 7 — Corollary C, CSS coset states over a prime field

**No predecessor located.** Van den Nest, Dehaene and De Moor's Corollary 1 has no CSS case, and its
closest entry, GF(4)-linearity, is a condition CSS stabilizers characteristically fail. No located
work states a rigidity criterion for CSS states in terms of `X`-type and `Z`-type minimal supports
at every coordinate. The caveat that belongs with this verdict is not a priority caveat but the
operational one their own paper anticipates: they explicitly call their cases (iii) and (iv) "much
more operational than theorem 1", and exhibiting the required minimal supports is the same kind of
computational burden.

### Cross-cutting finding on the "free" finiteness corollary

The C804 note's finiteness-of-the-local-symmetry-group corollary is, at local dimension two, weaker
than Englbrecht and Kraus (S6), who characterise all local symmetries of every qubit stabilizer
state and pin the occurrence of continuous symmetries to leaves up to local complementation. The
corollary should be claimed only above local dimension two, and Englbrecht and Kraus cited.
