## 1. What is inserted

A matched sharpness pair for the dimension-five ambient integral Hodge quotient. Both
halves concern the prescribed graph Neron--Severi lattice of a marked polarized
elliptic-power presentation `E^5 --> A` with finite-etale slope algebra; `P^k` denotes the
degree-`k` ordinary divisor-product image, `Hdg^{2k}` the integral Hodge classes.

Print three environments.

**Lemma (Integral multidegrees on an elliptic power).** Decompose the integral Hodge
lattice of `E^5` by multidegree. In dimension five the admissible types are
`(2^{k-l}, 1^{2l})` with `l <= 2`, so at most four slots contribute in any degree.

**Theorem (Integral Hodge saturation of the six-axis packet).** For a non-CM member of
the six-axis family, `Hdg^{2k} = P^k` in every degree `k`. Equivalently the full integral
Hodge lattice of the descended packet is generated by ordinary products of divisors, with
no factorial denominators and no residual quotient in any degree.

**Proposition (A sharp neighboring defect).** For the one-depth packet with five distinct
roots and finite unramified splitting, `Hdg^4 / P^2 ≅ Hdg^6 / P^3 ≅ (Z/p^a)^5`, the
quotient vanishes in every other degree, and multiplication by theta is the canonical
complement isomorphism between the two nonzero quotients.

Why: the paper currently proves that the six-axis packet is saturated but never shows the
saturation is *special*. The distinct-root packet is the nearest neighbor and it fails, by
an exactly computed amount. The pair converts an existence result into a sharpness
statement and gives the repeated-root hypothesis visible work to do.
