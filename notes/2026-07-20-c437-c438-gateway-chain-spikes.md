## C437 — signed-half residual-list orbit-recovery spike

**Dependency:** C430.  C414 is a compatible B3 control but not a blocking dependency.

**Question.**  Does the symbolic balanced-half theorem admit an intrinsic orbit-recovery
formulation in which the two sheets are exactly the residual list through degree two, the cubic
tensor reduces that list from two to one, and C411's mixed Hecke map preserves this first
informative degree?

### Bounded moves

1. In the smallest group module generated by the H3 signed-half vector, define the residual-list
   profile `L_d(x)` from invariant tensors through degree `d` without naming either sheet.
2. Consume C430's symbolic uniqueness theorem to prove or refute `L_2=2` and `L_3=1`; use B3 as a
   control, not as a second census.
3. Identify the orbit-span dependence and the relevant radical/socle layers in characteristic 11,
   stating exactly which infinite-field or linearly-independent-orbit hypothesis of generic cubic
   recovery fails.
4. Express C411's rank-two mixed `A4\PGL_2(11)/A5` incidence map as a projected moment map and test
   whether it preserves the `2 -> 1` list drop.

### Pass, stop, and output

- **Pass:** a presentation-independent proposition giving `L_2=2`, `L_3=1`, plus one exact
  preservation or obstruction theorem for the C411 projection.
- **Stop:** the list depends on a supplied sheet label; the cubic statistic is only a coordinate
  restatement of C406; or the C411 projection does not admit a canonical moment-map formulation.
- **No promotion yet:** do not formulate an all-finite-group or noisy-statistical theorem.  A
  general G1 programme is earned only by the exact finite-field seam.
- **Evidence:** if exact computation is load-bearing, commit a report/script/certificate/checksum
  bundle under the C437 stem; otherwise give a conceptual proof and a small independent character
  or tensor check.

## C438 — q=9/q=11 decorated-transform seam spike

**Dependency:** C435.  The frozen C379 and C405 bundles are read-only mathematical inputs.

**Question.**  Are the q=11 conic matching-decorated deep-hole transform and the q=9 Hermitian
Cayley-octad construction two instances of one category-correct decorated incidence/discriminant
transform, rather than two exceptional count coincidences?

### Bounded moves

1. Consume C435's canonical q=9 group/transitivity dictionary and package the eight parent
   decorations and 28 singular quadrics/bitangents as one marked incidence object.
2. Package C379's 22 matching-decorated parents, 66 child secants, obstruction pairs, and root
   intersections in the same source/child/decoration/obstruction schema.
3. Test only three shared invariants: stabilizer-equivariant recovery of a parent from its
   decoration, the square/involutivity defect of the transform, and the discriminant or root type
   recording failure of the next extension.
4. Seek one natural commuting square between the two schemas.  Counts, subgroup inclusions, and
   separate incidence tables do not constitute a pass.

### Pass, stop, and output

- **Pass:** one functorial transform statement with the q=9 and q=11 objects as honest
  specializations and with a shared obstruction invariant consumed by the proof.
- **Stop:** the two cases require incompatible morphisms or markings; only numerical analogies
  survive; or no invariant remains after forgetting presentation-dependent labels.
- **No promotion yet:** do not allocate G4 higher-order-MDS or G11 exceptional-tower work until
  this seam passes.
- **Evidence:** any finite identification must use a deterministic report/script/JSON/checksum
  bundle under the C438 stem with an independent incidence replay.

## Shallow literature check (2026-07-20)

**Depth and verdict.**  This was a bounded discovery check, not a novelty audit: **0 of 6 newly
consulted sources were read at full text**.  It found no direct statement of either spike, but that
is not an absence-of-prior-work conclusion.  It did find close work that materially narrows C437
and supplies a better prior-art substrate for C438.  The two spikes remain sensible, with the
following boundaries.

- **C437 / G7 -> G1: proceed only with the narrowed claim above.**  Kemper--Lopatin--Reimers
  already study all-orbit separating invariants over finite fields, including permutation-group
  examples.  Reimers--Sezer already obtain mostly degree-at-most-three generic separating
  invariants for modular cyclic modules, together with one degree-`p` invariant.  Two 2026
  projected-MRA papers also make third-moment recovery after an orientation-losing projection
  direct prior art.  None of these sources, at the depths read, gives the H3 signed-half residual
  list, its `2 -> 1` drop, or preservation by a mixed finite-field Hecke incidence map.  Thus
  “finite characteristic” and “projection preserves cubic recovery” are pre-empted umbrellas; the
  exact all-orbit list profile plus modular-module and Hecke mechanism is the remaining seam.
- **G7 terminology: use “first nonzero odd invariant” carefully.**  Misawa--Munemasa--Sawa study
  spherical designs with prescribed odd harmonic indices and antipodality.  Their abstract does
  not address signed halves in the perfect-matching scheme or cubic orientation recovery, but it
  makes raw odd-moment/antipodality language prior art rather than a contribution.
- **C438 / G3: proceed, but consume octad pictures rather than rebuilding them.**  Van Bommel et
  al. construct coordinate-invariant Cayley-octad pictures from degeneration data, an explicit map
  from those decorated pictures to stable-reduction types, and a compatible `Sp(6,2)`/Cremona
  action proved for a single building block and conjectured generally.  This is close structural
  prior art for a marked obstruction transform on the q=9 side.  It does not connect Cayley octads
  to q=11 conic matching-decorated deep holes or provide the cross-field commuting square.  C438
  should therefore import the octad-picture invariant as the q=9 obstruction object and reserve
  its claim for the category-correct q=9/q=11 bridge and shared involutivity/discriminant law.
- **Ranking effect:** no reranking is justified by this shallow pass.  C438 gains external
  structural support but loses any claim to inventing the q=9 decorated obstruction map; C437
  remains sharper and riskier because its defensible contribution is now representation-specific.
