the chordal/conference distinction and substitutes two nonstandard phrases for
the objects. Concept (d) is elegant but opaque outside the paper and makes the
late Frobenius feature appear to be the whole theorem.

## Recommendation

**Submission title:** *Chordal and Conference Cubics: Reconstruction and a
Residual \(C_2\)-Torsor*

**Short running/slug form:** *Chordal--Conference Reconstruction*
(`chordal-conference-reconstruction`).

This title leads with the two recognizable geometric objects and advertises the
precise obstruction that prevents the unmarked construction from being an
equivalence. It also leaves room for the two-graph and lattice results without
pretending that either is the sole subject.

## Terminology flags

- **shadows:** Mildly misleading in a standalone title. It is internal category
  language for the chordal side, but is not a standard external keyword and can
  suggest a projection or degeneration. It is especially doubtful when applied
  symmetrically to the conference cubic.
- **reconstructing:** Sound if the title names the recovered carrier or
  conference data, or if the subtitle signals the residual ambiguity. By itself,
  “reconstructing the cubics” would misdescribe what is reconstructed.
- **exact:** Misleading unless immediately qualified by the selected chordal
  line, or by “up to a residual \(C_2\)-torsor.” The unselected theorem is
  explicitly not an equivalence.
- **icosahedral:** Potentially misleading. The statements use the
  five-dimensional \(A_5\)-representation; “icosahedral five-space” is not a
  standard object and may evoke the familiar three-dimensional action instead.
  “\(A_5\)-invariant” is safer and more discoverable.
- **correspondence:** Acceptable only with “marked,” “selected-line,” or an
  explicit residual-quotient qualification. Unqualified, it conceals the
  distinction between the equivalence of enriched groupoids and the unselected
  \(C_2\)-quotient.
