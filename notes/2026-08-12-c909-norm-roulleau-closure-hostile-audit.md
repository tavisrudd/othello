## Verdict

**MINOR with one exact equivariance repair; otherwise the Albanese/connected-
kernel closure is sound.** The connected-fibre argument really does remove
the hidden target isogeny. The character calculation correctly forces the
\(D_5\)-action on the elliptic target to be trivial, provided equivariance of
the Roulleau quotient has first been established.

The earliest defect is the sentence “\(F_H\) is \(H\)-stable, so \(H\) acts
on the fibration.” Stability of one fibre divisor alone does not, in general,
force an automorphism to preserve a chosen fibration over an elliptic curve.
This should be replaced by the \(H\)-stable tangent-subspace/Albanese
construction used in Roulleau's proof (or by a printed uniqueness lemma for

repair, the rest of the comparison closes the primitive scalar gate.

## 1. Albanese universal-property lemma

Let \(a:S\to J=\operatorname{Alb}(S)\) be the Albanese map, with image
generating \(J\), and let \(\gamma:S\to C\) be a surjective morphism to an
elliptic curve with connected fibres. After translating \(C\), Albanese
universality gives
\[
 \gamma=q\circ a,\qquad q:J\to C
\]
with \(q\) a surjective homomorphism.

Factor \(q\) canonically as
\[
 J\xrightarrow{q_0} C_0\xrightarrow{u} C,
\]
where \(q_0\) has connected kernel and \(u\) is an isogeny. Since \(a(S)\)
generates \(J\), \(q_0a\) is surjective. If \(\deg u>1\), then for a general
\(c\in C\),
\[
 \gamma^{-1}(c)
 =\coprod_{c_0\in u^{-1}(c)}(q_0a)^{-1}(c_0),
\]
a disjoint union of at least two nonempty closed pieces. This contradicts
connectedness of the fibres of \(\gamma\). Hence \(\deg u=1\), so \(q\) has
connected kernel.

Therefore the Rosati dual \(q^\dagger:C\hookrightarrow J\) is a primitive
closed immersion. This is the precise reason that a nontrivial elliptic
isogeny cannot remain hidden in the Roulleau target.

Roulleau's Theorem 11(D) supplies the \(D_5\) fibration with fibre
\(F_H=\sum_{g\in H,\;g^2=1}D_g\). The word “fibration” must be used with the
connected-fibre convention, or its Stein-factor statement must be printed.

## 2. Equivariance and the \(D_5\) character check

The closure note's character arithmetic is correct:
\[
 \dim W_5^{D_5}
 =\frac{5+5\cdot1+4\cdot0}{10}=1,\qquad
 \dim\operatorname{Hom}_{D_5}(\mathrm{sgn},W_5)
 =\frac{5-5\cdot1+4\cdot0}{10}=0.
\]
The only nontrivial one-dimensional rational character of \(D_5\) is the
reflection sign. Any action of \(D_5\) on an elliptic curve fixing the origin
factors through its abelianization \(C_2\), so on \(H^1(C,\mathbf Q)\) the
only alternatives are the trivial plane or two copies of the sign. Since
the sign does not occur in \(W_5\), the latter is impossible inside
\[
 H^1(J,\mathbf Q)\simeq W_5\otimes M_\mathbf Q.
\]
Thus an equivariant Roulleau quotient has trivial \(D_5\)-action and its
homomorphism \(q:J\to C\) is \(H\)-invariant.

What is not automatic is the first word “equivariant.” The fact that the
single divisor \(F_H\) is \(H\)-stable does not alone prove that every
\(h\in H\) preserves the map \(\gamma_H\). The repair is short but must be
explicit:

* in Roulleau's Albanese proof, form the subspace \(W_H\subset H^0(\Omega_S)^*\)
  generated by the tangent spaces of the five \(D_g\);
* \(W_H\) is \(H\)-stable because the five curves are permuted by \(H\);
* the quotient \(J\to C_H\) is the one-dimensional quotient determined by
  \(W_H^\perp\), hence is \(H\)-equivariant up to the induced origin-fixing
  automorphism of \(C_H\).

Alternatively, prove that the relevant Albanese quotient is uniquely
determined by the \(D_5\)-stable fibre configuration. Without one of these
arguments, the character calculation is conditional rather than a proof.

## 3. Equality with the norm image

Once \(q\) is \(H\)-invariant, its Rosati dual image lies in the pointwise
\(H\)-fixed abelian subvariety. On rational homology this is
\[
 W_5^H\otimes M_\mathbf Q,
\]
the same one-dimensional rational subspace as \(\operatorname{im}(n_H)\).
Connected elliptic subgroups of an abelian variety with the same Lie algebra
are equal. Hence
\[
 \operatorname{im}(q^\dagger)=E_H=(\operatorname{im}n_H)^0.
\]

The norm-image inclusion is primitive because \(J/E_H\) is an abelian
variety. The dual inclusion \(q^\dagger\) is primitive because \(q\) has
connected kernel. Since both maps are embeddings onto the same elliptic
subvariety, their source identifications differ only by an elliptic
automorphism, not a nontrivial isogeny. Consequently the pulled-back origin
divisor agrees:
\[
 q^*[0]\big|_S=F_H
\]
after choosing the fibre's point as origin. This closes precisely the scalar
risk \(D(6I-J)D\) identified in the earlier hostile audit.

The norm itself remains
\[
 n_H=10P_H=2\,i_Hq_H,
\]
not \(i_Hq_H\). Roulleau's fibre intersections then give \(q_Hi_H=[5]\)
and \(q_Hi_{H'}=[-1]\), hence \(6I_5-J_5\).

## 4. Relative globalization

The relative norm images \(E_H\) globalize from the integral \(A_5\)-action
and constant rank. Conjugation gives the six transports. To extend the
primitive Gram identities, it is enough to establish them on the generic
non-CM fibre: the relative Hom scheme of elliptic abelian schemes is
unramified/discrete, so an equality of relative homomorphisms on the generic
fibre extends over the connected smooth base. CM fibres can acquire extra
*geometric* endomorphisms, but they do not create new global sections of the
relative Hom scheme.

This proves the relative integral isogeny
\[
 f:E^5\to J,\qquad f^*\Theta=6I_5-J_5,
\]
with \(|\ker f|=6^4\) and \(\ker f\subset E^5[6]\), once the equivariant
Albanese comparison above is printed. The relative family of graph kernels
is then an actual finite-etale kernel, not only a rational VHS packet.

## 5. Separate audit: \(K[2]\) versus the explicit Tate/Prym system

The closure is correct to leave this as a separate gate.

From \(f^*\Theta=6I_5-J_5\), the actual kernel
\[
 K=\ker f\subset E^5[6]
\]
has a \(2\)-primary part \(K_2\subset E[2]^5\) determined by the actual norm
elliptic scheme \(E\) and the integral five-axis map. The abstract
\(A_5\)-classification says that the \(A_5\)-stable principal \(2\)-kernels
form
\[
 \mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_4)
 =\mathbf P^1(\mathbf F_2)\sqcup\{\omega,\omega^2\},
\]
and generic Torelli selects the exotic pair. This proves only that \(K_2\)
lies in the exotic two-element packet.

It does **not** identify the packet's monodromy cover with the discriminant
cover \(r^2=T\) of the displayed Tate/Prym elliptic model. Equality of the
\(j\)-map, Picard–Fuchs equation, or rational rank-two local system is
insufficient for this integral \(2\)-torsion claim. One needs either:

1. a family isomorphism between the norm elliptic scheme and the explicit
   Tate/Prym factor; or
2. an explicit isogeny of odd degree (or another comparison whose restriction
   on \(2\)-torsion is an isomorphism) together with the induced identification
   of graph kernels.

A quadratic twist of a common Weierstrass model is harmless for its displayed
\(2\)-torsion points, but that harmlessness applies only after the actual
model comparison is written. An arbitrary isogeny can change \(2\)-torsion,
so it cannot be silently substituted.

Thus \(r^2=T\) is presently a correct resolvent for the explicit elliptic
multiplicity system and the signed parameter relation \(T=81t^2\), but its
transfer to the actual geometric \(K_2\) requires the Tate/Prym comparison.
This gate is irrelevant to the fibrewise saturation theorem, which needs
only the actual finite-etale graph kernel and its abstract exotic
classification.

## Bottom line

**GO after one minor equivariance repair.** The connected-Stein/Albanese
argument genuinely proves degree-one target identification; the
\(D_5\)-character computation then forces trivial base action and identifies
the dual quotient image with the norm axis. Print the \(H\)-stable
Albanese-subspace construction (or a uniqueness lemma) before claiming that
\(F_H\)-stability alone preserves the fibration. Keep the explicit
\(r^2=T\) Tate/Prym marking as a separate, narrower \(K[2]\) comparison gate.
