## Fermi predictions, written before any code

These are written from milestone (a)'s measured lowering stage (81.56 instructions per source byte
on the `datalog` cohort, 255 per node, of which the build traversal and the passes are about 42 per
cent, dictionary interning 27 and the canonical fingerprint 10) and from the core's own bounds
(`MAX_ARITY` 4, `MAX_DOMAIN` 65,536, `MAX_UNIVERSE` 2^30, `MAX_RELATIONS` 64).

1. **Complement size.** A negated relation `R` of arity `n` over a value dictionary of `d` entries
   contributes `d^n − |closure(R)|` complement facts. The closure grows with what the source
   derives; the complement grows as `d^n`, so the complement dominates as soon as `d` is more than
   a handful. For the hand-written fixtures `d` is between 2 and 8 and `n` is 1 or 2, so a
   complement is 2 to 64 tuples and invisible. On a synthetic series that doubles the domain at
   arity 2 I predict the complement fact count to quadruple per doubling and the
   complement-to-closure ratio to double, so at `d = 64` the complement is about 4,000 facts and at
   `d = 512` about 262,000. At arity 3 the same `d = 512` is 134 million, which is past
   `MAX_UNIVERSE` and must be a declared refusal rather than an attempt. **The prediction that
   matters for ADR 0004**: per-stratum complement facts are affordable for arity 1 and 2 with a
   domain in the low hundreds, and the `Negative`-atom-kind alternative becomes the right route at
   arity 3 or at a domain in the thousands. I expect a declared bound to be necessary, not
   optional.
2. **Per-stratum overhead.** Each layer is one `rule_contract::Program`, and `datalog::admit`
   computes the source identity by serializing the whole program to JSON and hashing it with
   SHA-256. So the backend boundary cost of a negated program is dominated by the complement facts
   twice over: once to materialize them as `Fact` records with an owned relation `String`, and once
   to serialize and hash them. I predict 150 to 400 instructions per complement fact for the two
   together, so 262,000 complement facts cost 40 to 100 million instructions — two orders of
   magnitude above the whole lowering stage on a comparable source. That is the number the ADR
   question needs, and I predict it says the complement route is a correctness-first
   implementation rather than the fast one.
3. **Instruction cost of the lowering stage itself.** Every change inside the measured stage is a
   branch that a program without `not` or `forall` never takes: binarization stops skipping a body
   that carries a negative literal, the closing pass gains a complement-universe check that runs
   only for a relation some literal negates, and the build pass gains a `forall` arm in `enter` and
   a witness-expansion arm in `distribute`. None of the five frozen cohorts writes a negation the
   lowering reaches (`ascii` and `unicode` reject at their second definition, `comment-string` at
   the relation budget, the two `malformed` cohorts at parse), and the `datalog` cohort is purely
   positive. I predict `scan`, `parse` and `admit` at 1.000000 within the protocol's few parts per
   million on every cohort and both variants, and the `lower` stage within 1.000000 ± 0.003 — with
   the caveat the playbook records three times in this lane: the workspace builds with thin LTO and
   one codegen unit, so a swing of a few tenths of a per cent on `comment-string` or `datalog` is
   code layout and not this change. I will not read such a swing as a win or a loss.
4. **Expected differential disagreements.** C1189 found three, all in the rejection surface or in
   name resolution, none in the closure of a program both sides accepted. This milestone changes
   that distribution, because for the first time the two sides compute the fixed point by
   *different routes* rather than by one route with different indexes: the lowering materializes a
   finite complement relation and joins it, the reference evaluator tests absence from a completed
   stratum. I predict 1 to 3 genuine disagreements and, unlike C1189, that **at least one is in a
   closure**. The three shapes I expect, in order of likelihood: (a) the domain each complement
   column ranges over — the lowering's value dictionary against the evaluator's active domain,
   which differ if any value is interned and never appears in a fact or a rule constant; (b) the
   free-variable set of a `forall` witness relation, where the two sides could disagree about which
   variables the witness carries; (c) the layer a relation lands in when it is both read positively
   and negated by rules of one stratum.
5. **Where the risk is, and it is not the complement.** The complement construction is arithmetic
   over a finite universe and either agrees or does not. `forall` is the part I expect to cost the
   most and to be wrong first, because the only route that keeps it inside the fragment introduces
   a witness relation whose body must copy the enclosing disjunct's positive literals to
   range-restrict the witness's own columns. That copying interacts with the variables budget
   (eight per rule), with binarization, and with stratification all at once. I predict at least one
   instructive negative there.

**How they came out.** Prediction 1 was right in shape and in magnitude: the complement fact count
quadruples per doubling of the domain at arity two, to four significant figures, and the
complement-to-closure ratio doubles. Prediction 2 was right that the backend boundary is dominated
by the complement and wrong about which part of it dominates — the serialization is not a second
cost beside materialization, it *is* the cost, and it crosses the core contract's own
`MAX_BYTES` before the fact count comes anywhere near the declared complement bound. Prediction 3
was right. Prediction 4 was **wrong**: the corpora found no disagreement at all, and because
"no disagreement" is the weakest possible result to report on its own, this task added two
deliberate mutations to show that the corpora would have caught the two defects the prediction
named. Prediction 5 was right — `forall` cost the most and is where every instructive negative sits.

## What was built

### The lowering

Three changes inside the measured stage, in `src/rel_frontend/lower/`.

**Binarization chains a negative literal like a positive one.** Milestone (a) left a body alone
when it carried anything but positive literals, because backend v1 refused it anyway. A body is now
chained when every literal names a relation and is positive or negative; a comparison or aggregate
literal names none, so a body carrying one is still left alone and still refused. The join-order
function is renamed from `order_positives` to `order_body`, which is what it now orders. Placing a
negative literal anywhere in the chain is exact because the backend materializes its complement:
the literal becomes a positive atom over a finite relation of the same arity over the same terms,
and range restriction has already put every one of its variables into a positive literal of the
unbinarized rule.

**`forall` desugars to a negated existential through a witness relation.** `forall(x in D: F)`, with
`F` an application, is `not exists(x: D(x) and not F)`. The build pass stages the guards `D(x)` and
the negated quantified formula `not F`, marks the latter, and `distribute` expands one witness
relation per disjunct: its columns are the free variables of `F` — every variable the guards do not
bind, in ascending order — and its one rule derives it from the disjunct's own positive literals
together with the guards and `not F`. The enclosing rule then reads the witness negatively over
exactly those columns. The disjunct's positive literals are there to range-restrict the witness's
columns, and that is what makes the desugaring exact: the enclosing rule asks about the witness only
at values those same literals produce, and at such a value the witness holds exactly when some
quantified value falsifies `F`. The witness is `REL_AUXILIARY` with a `RELATION_WITNESS` flag, named
`fa_<id>` by the closing pass, filtered from readout and kept in the certificate.

**The closing pass marks and prices every negated relation.** A relation some rule negates gains
`RELATION_NEGATED`, and the pass checks `values^arity` against a declared bound before anything is
built, so a complement that would not fit is a `REL0503` with a span and its numbers rather than an
exhausted materialization. `Lowering` gains `negated`, `complements` and `witnesses`.

### The stratified backend

`src/rel_stratified.rs`, new. It projects the normalized relational IR layer by layer, and it is
where `Demand` is driven, so `ergodis-rules` moves from a dev-dependency of the private crate to a
real one; one implementation of the loop serves the operator tool and the differential harness alike
rather than being written twice.

**Layers, not strata, are the programs.** The stratifier's strata are a topological order of the
dependency graph's strongly connected components, and a purely positive program has as many of them
as it has components — the transitive-closure fixture has two. Splitting there would change the
contract program of every positive source milestone (a) already lowers, for no gain, because the
demand-driven evaluator computes the joint least fixed point of any positive program in one pass. So
the split happens only where negation forces it: `layer(s)` is the maximum over the rules of stratum
`s` of `layer(s') + 1` for a negated body relation in stratum `s'` and `layer(s')` for a positive
one. A program with no negation has exactly one layer, and one layer is one program.

**Each layer's program** declares the relations it derives, the relations it reads from an earlier
layer as inputs holding their certified closures, and one complement per negated relation. It is
evaluated, certified, replayed by the derivation checker, cross-checked against the ranked checker,
and — new here — its checker replay is compared tuple-wise against the evaluator's own rows before
either is believed. The closure the checkers establish is what the next layer reads, so a complement
is taken against a closure an independent checker established and not against one the evaluator
merely reported.

**Every complement is recorded and re-checkable.** `ComplementRecord` carries the negated relation's
id and mangled name, the complement relation's name in the layer program that reads it, the arity,
the domain each column ranges over, the product of those domains, the certified closure's tuple
count, the complement's own fact count, the layer, and a SHA-256 over the complement's canonical
tuple bytes. `verify_records` rebuilds every complement from the closure it names and compares the
digest, and asserts that the counts sum to the universe; the operator tool and every corpus program
of the differential run it.

### The operator tool

`rel-lower` now runs the stratified backend, prints the layer reports (relations, inputs, rules,
facts, the canonical program bytes, rounds, derived tuples, probes, candidates and derivations) and
the complement records, and reports `complement_records_verified`.

### The oracle

`tests/rel_reference/mod.rs` computes the stratified least fixed point. Reachability is a boolean
matrix closed by relaxation; a level is one more than the level of anything a rule of its own
component negates, and every member of one component shares a level; the levels are evaluated in
order, each to its own least fixed point by full naive iteration. A negated atom is a filter, so a
body is matched positives-first — which is forced by the filter reading and is not the lowering's
join order, since the lowering orders every literal by variable sharing and may place a negative
literal anywhere. `forall` gets the same witness desugaring, written independently from the node
pool. The witness is filtered from readout on both sides, so the two readouts stay comparable.

The enumeration path — every assignment of a rule's variables tried and every body atom tested,
which is the most literal reading of Figures 3 and 4 over a finite domain — is stratified too and
tests a negative atom for non-membership.

### The generators

`negation(seed)` emits programs over twelve parameterized templates: difference over an input
relation, difference over a derived one, non-reachability over a transitive closure, a two-level
negation chain, a negated literal with a constant argument, negation of a module member with its
leading column supplied, negation in one branch of a disjunction, negation of a free external name,
a restricted universal, a universal over a derived relation, a binary relation with both columns
negated, and a three-atom body with a negative literal that binarizes. Templates rather than a
random walk over bodies, because a negated literal has two properties a random walk breaks at once:
every variable of it must be bound by a positive literal of the same rule, and the relation it names
must not lie in the negating rule's own strongly connected component. Each template randomizes the
domain, the fact sets, the projections and the constant arguments, so a wrong reading of which
tuples a complement holds changes the closure.

`near_miss` gains seven negation shapes: a negated unbound variable, a negative self-loop, mutual
recursion through negation, a negated disjunction, nested negation, an unrestricted universal, and a
closed universal.

## Semantics adopted

Written down as this stage's own contract, for the reason milestone (a) gave: no executable Rel
reference semantics is available to this lane, and the paper defines the denotation of an expression
and of a formula without defining the semantics of a program.

**Which programs are admitted, beyond milestone (a)'s fragment.** A body literal may be `not F`
where `F` is an application, and a body conjunct may be `forall(Bindings: F)` where `F` is an
application and each binding is `x` or `x in D`. Everything else is as milestone (a) left it.

**Which fixed point is computed.** The stratified least fixed point. A negated relation lies in a
strictly earlier stratum than the rule that negates it, because the stratifier refuses a
non-monotone edge inside one strongly connected component with `REL0502`; so its closure is complete
when the negation is evaluated, and the fixed point is the standard one of stratified Datalog. A
non-stratifiable program is rejected, never approximated.

**Which domain a complement column ranges over: the whole value dictionary, for every column.** The
relational IR carries a column *type* per column but no per-column domain, so backend v1 collapses
every column into one global domain and this projection inherits that. The reading is exact: range
restriction puts every variable of a negative literal into a positive body literal of the same rule,
so its value is a dictionary id, and a constant in a negative literal is a dictionary id by
construction, so no tuple outside `dictionary^arity` is ever asked about. It is also the cost — a
complement is `domain^arity` facts however narrow its columns really are — and the
per-column-domains successor ADR 0004 names is what removes it. A program with no interned value has
no reachable tuple at all, so its complements are empty rather than the one tuple the contract's
`domain` floor of one would otherwise suggest.

**The complement reading and the absence reading are the same reading.** The lowering materializes
`R̄` and joins it; the reference evaluator tests absence from a completed relation. These agree
exactly, for the range-restriction reason above, and that is a statement about the fragment rather
than about either strategy. It is why the oracle knows nothing about complements.

**Deliberate readings, each of which could have gone the other way.**

- **The witness of a `forall` carries the free variables of the quantified formula, and its rule
  copies the disjunct's positive literals.** The alternative is a witness over a narrower column set
  with a guard supplied some other way, which would need a notion of which positive literal binds
  which column that the fragment does not have. The copy is what makes range restriction decide the
  question instead.
- **One witness per disjunct, not one per `forall`.** A `forall` under a disjunction appears in
  several disjuncts, and each disjunct's positive literals are different, so each needs its own
  witness. Sharing one would make the witness depend on literals its consumer does not carry.
- **The witness's columns are in ascending definition-local variable order.** So the witness is a
  function of the rule rather than of the order the quantified formula happened to write its
  arguments.
- **An unrestricted `forall` binder is admitted and then fails range restriction**, rather than
  being refused at the construct. `forall(y: F)` is a universal over the infinite `Values` of
  Figure 3; the diagnostic names the binder, which is where the unboundedness is. This moves the
  `[[forall((x) | F)]]` row of the equation table from `REL0504` to `REL0501`.
- **An anonymous argument under a universal is refused.** `forall(x in D: r(x, _))` is "for every `x`
  there is some `y`", an existential inside a universal, and the fragment's single negation has no
  representation for the inner quantifier. It is `REL0504` at the `_`.
- **A `forall` with no free variable is refused.** Its witness would be a nullary relation, which the
  contract cannot carry. `REL0504` with a note naming the shape, beside the ground-body note
  milestone (a) added.
- **A negated literal is chained by binarization exactly like a positive one**, because the
  complement makes it a join over a finite relation. The alternative — pinning a negative literal to
  the end of the chain — would be the filter reading's constraint imposed on a route that does not
  need it.
- **`{}` under negation is unchanged.** `not {}` is negation of a non-application and so `REL0504`
  at the inner formula, as is `not (F or G)`, `not exists(x: F)` and `not not F`. The false formula
  is still `REL0504` as a conjunct and the true formula is still absorbed.
- **The complement bound is this backend route's own, not the core's.** The core admits an input
  relation of any fact count inside its tuple universe. `MAX_COMPLEMENT` exists because this route
  pays `domain^arity` facts per negated relation whatever its closure, and raising it is a decision
  about how much the route may cost.

## Deviations from the architecture note

1. **The split is by layer, not by stratum.** The note says "each stratum is one contract
   `Program`". Strata are the stratifier's strongly connected components in topological order, of
   which a purely positive program has many, so splitting at every stratum would rebuild the
   contract program of every source milestone (a) lowers and gain nothing. The programs are the
   coarsest grouping that keeps every negated relation complete before it is read, which is what the
   note's route actually requires. A program with no negation is one program, unchanged.
2. **The stratified driver lives in the private library and pulls in `ergodis-rules`.** The note
   leaves the backend as a projection; the complement of a negated relation is only known once the
   stratum deriving it has closed, so the projection has to drive evaluation. `ergodis-rules` is a
   core crate and the dependency arrow still points inward. The alternative, a trait the operator
   tool and the test harness each implement, would have meant two implementations of the one loop
   whose correctness this milestone is about.
3. **`forall` needs a witness relation the note does not mention.** The note's milestone (b) bullet
   covers `not F` over a relation. A universal is a *negated existential*, and the existential's
   witness is a relation the source did not name, so the fragment gains a second kind of
   pass-introduced relation beside binarization's auxiliaries.
4. **The complement is not certified by the core, and could not be.** The note says the chain of
   certificates plus the complement construction is the evidence, which is exactly right, and this
   milestone makes the second half checkable rather than merely stated: every complement carries the
   numbers and a digest that rebuild it from the preceding layer's certified closure.
5. **Per-column domains are still absent**, so a complement is taken over the global dictionary.
   This is milestone (a)'s deviation 6 and gap 8 showing up as a cost rather than as a latent one,
   and the measurement below is what prices removing it.

### The two mutations that show the corpora discriminate

Each mutation is a one-line change to the stratified backend, run with every gate, and then
reverted. Neither is committed; what is recorded is what each broke.

1. **Negation evaluated in its own stratum rather than a later layer.** `layer(s)` stops adding one
   for a negated dependency, so a negated relation lands in the same contract program as the rule
   that negates it and the complement is taken against a closure that is not yet complete. Six of
   the lowering fixtures fail — set difference, non-reachability, the negation chain, the module
   member, the restricted universal, and the agreement with the Python oracle — and on the
   differential side `the_negation_corpus_agrees` and
   `the_committed_fixtures_agree_with_the_reference_evaluator` fail.
2. **The complement taken over the negated relation's own values rather than over the value
   dictionary.** This is the exact confusion Fermi prediction 4 named as the most likely
   disagreement, written in deliberately: the column domain becomes one past the largest value the
   closure holds. Five lowering fixtures fail, and on the differential side four tests fail,
   including `the_negation_corpus_agrees`, the Figure 3/4 table and the surface-construct table.

So the corpora do decide both questions the agreement is about. What the agreement then says is
that the complement construction and the absence filter compute the same fixed point on 2,320
generated programs and every committed fixture, by two routes that share the scanner, the parser
and semantic admission and nothing else.

### What the agreement still does not certify

The residue C1189 recorded is unchanged and one item is added. The two sides share the scanner, the
parser and semantic admission, so a defect in any of those three is invisible to this differential
by construction; those stages have the native/WASM parity replay and the 2026-09-15 admission-chain
audit, and this task adds nothing to either. New: both sides implement the *same* `forall`
desugaring, independently written but to one design. A differential cannot tell whether the negated
existential is the right reading of a universal — only that two implementations of that reading
agree. What supports the reading is the argument in "Semantics adopted" and the fact that
`evaluate_by_enumeration`, which tries every assignment over the active domain and tests membership,
agrees with the backtracking evaluator on every fixture small enough to enumerate.

## Instructive negatives

1. **The witness's columns first carried the wrong span, and the diagnostic for the one `forall`
   shape a reader is most likely to write was therefore useless.** `push_variable_literal` gave
   every synthetic term the negated formula's own span, so an unrestricted `forall(y: e(x, y))`
   reported its `REL0501` at `e(x, y)` rather than at `y`. The fix carries each free variable's
   first-occurrence span from the staged terms into the witness head and keeps the two arrays in
   step through the insertion sort. The lesson generalizes past this case: a pass-introduced literal
   needs its terms' spans threaded from the source terms it was derived from, or every diagnostic
   that reaches it names the construct instead of the mistake.
2. **The complement-bound fixture's first construction was refused by the wrong stage, and it dumped
   23 KB of generated source into the test log.** Reaching 4,194,305 complement facts at arity two
   needs 2,049 dictionary values, and 2,049 tuples in one `;`-chain is one left-nested node chain
   that admission's depth capacity refuses at 255. The fixture is now 46 values and an arity-four
   negated relation, `46^4 = 4,477,456` against the bound, which also demonstrates that every other
   bound of the close is met at the same numbers — the tuple universe is the same 4.5 million
   against `2^30` and the join index key space the same figure against `2^24`. The log dump was a
   second, separate mistake: the `lower` helper panics with `{source:?}`, which is right for a
   hand-written fixture and wrong for a generated one.
3. **The stratified cohort hit the same depth capacity, for the same reason, at a domain of 512.**
   Several `def` clauses of one relation are what Rel offers instead of one long `;`-chain, and the
   relation is the same either way, so the cohort writes its fact sets in chunks of 32 tuples. This
   is the second time in this task that a scaled fact set met a nesting bound rather than a
   capacity bound.
4. **The stratified cohort's first shape measured a universal that never fired.** Its `e` was a bare
   path through the domain, so no value was related to every value, `total` was empty and the
   witness's own complement was zero facts — a measurement of a code path that never produces a
   tuple. Adding one value related to every other makes `total` exactly one tuple and the witness's
   complement exactly one fact, which is what a cohort for this route should exercise.
5. **`rel_frontend.rs`'s cohort determinism test had a cohort shape baked into it.** It asserted
   that every cohort writes at least 512 definitions and more references than definitions. The
   stratified cohort's count parameter is its value dictionary's size, not its item count, because
   what has to scale for this measurement is the domain a complement is taken over. Both assertions
   needed a branch, and the branch states plainly what the parameter means per cohort.
6. **Fermi prediction 4 was wrong, and the useful response was not to hunt harder for a
   disagreement.** I predicted one to three, at least one in a closure, on the reasoning that the
   two sides now compute the fixed point by different routes. There are none. Two deliberate
   mutations were the way to find out whether that is a strong result or an empty one; they say it
   is strong. The prediction's reasoning was sound and its conclusion was wrong for a reason worth
   keeping: the two routes differ in *strategy* but agree by a one-line argument — range restriction
   makes every asked-about tuple a dictionary tuple — and a difference that a short proof closes is
   not a difference that generates defects.
7. **The complement-fact bound is not the bound that binds, and finding out cost a measurement
   rather than a Fermi.** `MAX_COMPLEMENT` was set at `2^22` facts from the Fermi's reasoning about
   materialization cost. The series then showed the layer program's canonical serialization crossing
   the core contract's own `MAX_BYTES` of one mebibyte between a domain of 128 and one of 160, which
   is a quarter of the way to the fact bound. The complement facts are affordable; the *program that
   carries them* is not, because each fact serializes to about forty-five bytes of JSON with its
   relation name repeated. Both bounds are now checked, and the useful one is the one the Fermi did
   not think to look for.

domain. For arity two it is affordable to a domain in the low hundreds, which covers every fixture,
every generated program and every realistic small program, and stops well short of a dictionary the

their closure's values in the binding column. That is exact by the same range-restriction argument
that makes the dictionary version exact, and on this cohort it would cut `e`'s complement from
`domain^2` to `domain × domain` restricted to the columns `e` actually occupies — no saving here,
because `e` occupies the whole domain in both columns, which is exactly why the successor needs a
cohort whose columns are disjoint value sets rather than this one.

## Remaining gaps

1. **The complement route is bounded at a domain of about 150 for one negated binary relation.**
   That is the measurement above and it is the gap that matters. The next step is per-column domains
   with the exact construction stated in the measurement section, and after that ADR 0004's
   `Negative` atom kind in the core `Rule`. Milestone (a)'s gaps 1, 2 and 8 are unchanged and gap 8
   is now priced.
2. **The layer program is serialized and hashed once per layer, and that is the backend's cost.**
   Each layer calls `encode_source` for the byte check and `datalog::admit` serializes and hashes
   again for the source identity, so a program's canonical form is built twice. Removing the double
   work needs either a core entry point that returns the identity from an encoding the caller
   supplies, or the direct constructor into the demand evaluator's prepared form that ADR 0004
   already names as the removal of the boundary allocation. Unmeasured separately from the
   complement, because at these sizes the complement dominates both.
3. **`forall`'s witness copies the whole disjunct's positive literals, and the variables budget is
   what stops it.** A universal inside a rule whose body already has several literals can exceed
   eight variables in the witness rule and be refused as a backend divergence. No generated program
   reaches it, because the negation generator's templates are small; a cohort that did would price
   the narrower construction, which is to copy only a minimal subset of positive literals that binds
   the witness's columns.
4. **Aggregation and arithmetic are milestone (c) and are unchanged.** A comparison or aggregate
   literal is represented in the relational IR, range-restricted, stratified, and refused by the
   backend; it is now the only sign the backend refuses.
5. **`exists(x in D: F)` is still `REL0504`**, and `forall(x in D: F)` now is not. The asymmetry is
   real and is the obvious next coverage row: the machinery a restricted universal needed — a guard
   literal conjoined with a quantified formula's body — is exactly what a restricted existential
   needs, and it is now written. Cheap, and deliberately not smuggled into this milestone.
6. **`not F` takes an application and nothing else.** A negated conjunction, disjunction,
   existential or negation is `REL0504`. Admitting `not (F and G)` needs a witness relation per
   negated subformula, which is the same machinery `forall` now has, so this is a second cheap
   coverage row rather than a new mechanism.
7. **The `&` and `?` argument sigils are still not parsed**, which is milestone (a)'s gap 6 and a
   parser gap.
8. **The relational IR still reserves no symmetry table**, which is milestone (a)'s gap 9.

## Mystery ledger

1. **Settled: whether the complement route is affordable, which is the question ADR 0004 left
   open.** For arity one it is free. For arity two it affords a value dictionary of about 150
   entries, and the bound that stops it is not the complement fact count but the layer program's
   canonical serialization against the core contract's own one-mebibyte budget — a quarter of the
   way to the fact bound the Fermi set. For arity three the same budget is a domain of about 28.
   The ADR's `Negative`-atom alternative is the right route at arity three, or at arity two over a
   domain in the hundreds; per-column domains are the intermediate step and the exact construction
   is written down in the measurement section. *Nothing about this item is open.*
2. **Settled: the complement reading and the absence reading are the same reading.** Range
   restriction puts every variable of a negative literal into a positive body literal, so every
   tuple the negation is asked about is a dictionary tuple, so materializing the complement over the
   dictionary and testing absence from a completed relation agree. The 600-program negation corpus
   is the evidence and the two mutations are the evidence that the corpus could have said otherwise.
3. **Settled, and it reverses a prediction: two routes to one fixed point produced no
   disagreement.** Fermi prediction 4 said one to three, at least one in a closure. There are none.
   The reason is item 2: the routes differ in strategy and agree by a one-line argument, and a
   difference a short proof closes does not generate defects. What makes the null result reportable
   is the two mutations, which show that the corpora decide exactly the two questions the prediction
   named.
4. **Open: why `datalog` gained 4,194 instructions' worth of speed on a bit-identical program.**
   0.32 per cent on the lowering stage, with an interval of a few parts per million, on a cohort
   whose lowered program is byte-identical across arms and whose eight binarized rules are the only
   rules that reach the one predicate that changed shape. Eight rules cannot be 4,194 instructions.
   This is the fourth thin-LTO layout swing this lane has recorded and it is the reverse of the loss
   C1189 measured on the same cohort. *Evidence gap*: a disassembly of `lower::run` and
   `passes::binarize` in both binaries, bucketed by address range, which is the method that has
   settled this twice before. Not attempted here because the swing is in the direction that costs
   nothing and because nothing in this milestone's acceptance depends on it.
5. **Open: whether the witness's copy of the disjunct's positive literals can be narrowed, and what
   it would buy.** The copy is what range-restricts the witness's columns, and copying all positives
   is the conservative choice; a minimal binding subset would be narrower and would push the
   variables budget further away. *Evidence gap*: no generated program reaches the budget, because
   the negation templates are small, so the narrowing has nothing to be measured against. A cohort
   with a universal inside a four-literal body would price it. Owner: whoever adds `exists(x in D:
   F)`, which needs the same guard machinery.
6. **Open: the layer program's canonical form is built twice per layer.** Once for the byte check
   and once inside `datalog::admit` for the source identity. At the sizes this route affords the
   complement dominates both, so the double work is invisible; it stops being invisible exactly when
   per-column domains make the complement small. *Evidence gap*: a measurement at a domain where the
   complement is narrow, which needs per-column domains first. This is the same boundary ADR 0004
   names as the planned removal of the backend's allocation.
7. **Open, inherited and unchanged: milestone (a)'s interning cost, the relation-resolution scan and
   the admission repeated-spelling quadratic.** Milestone (a)'s mystery ledger items 3, 4 and 5.
   Nothing in this task moved any of them, and the `stratified` cohort's 145 instructions per source
   byte against `datalog`'s 82 is item 3 showing up again on a fact-heavy source rather than a new
   finding.
8. **No genuine mystery remains about the semantics.** Every construct the stratified fragment does
   not cover is in the Figure 3/4 table or the surface-construct table with its exact outcome, every
   new rejection is in the table above with its code and its spans, and both scanner variants decide
   every row identically.

## ej/tt closeout

Run after the acceptance gate passed, as the lane requires.

**Cheap upgrades taken.** The layer program's canonical serialization is now checked against the
core contract's own `MAX_BYTES` and refused with the numbers, which turned out to be the bound that
actually decides whether this route is affordable — that check *is* the measurement the ADR question
needed, and it cost six lines. `verify_records` gained a test that tampers with the digest, with
each count and with the column domain in turn, so the one step of the chain the core cannot see is
no longer only ever exercised on records that describe their own complement. The enumeration
evaluator is compared against the
backtracking one over the negation corpus as well as the in-fragment one, so the most literal
reading of Figures 3 and 4 is checked under negation. The allocation gate gained the `stratified`
cohort, a nested-disjunction source and a cycle through negation, which closes both exits milestone
(a) recorded as uncovered. Each layer's checker replay is compared tuple-wise against the
evaluator's own rows rather than by count. The witness relation is named `fa_<id>` rather than
sharing binarization's `aux_<id>`, so a certificate's relation names say which pass built them.

**Doors this opens.**

1. **`exists(x in D: F)` is now cheap.** The reason milestone (a) refused it was that a restriction
   inside a quantified formula needs its literal conjoined with that formula's body, which the build
   pass did only for a definition parameter. `forall`'s guard machinery does exactly that. The
   asymmetry — a restricted universal is admitted and a restricted existential is not — is the
   obvious next coverage row and needs no new mechanism.
2. **`not (F and G)` and the other negated non-applications are the same.** Each needs a witness
   relation per negated subformula, which is what `forall` now builds. That would take the fragment
   from "negation of an atom" to "negation of a conjunctive query", which is most of what a reader
   would write.
3. **Per-column domains now have a priced target and an exact construction.** The complement need
   only be built over the product of the value sets each argument position can take, and those sets
   are unions of binding columns of the positive literals' closures. The measurement says what it
   saves and the cohort says what it needs: a cohort whose columns are disjoint value sets, which
   this one deliberately is not.
4. **Milestone (c) inherits a driver that already materializes a closure between layers.**
   Aggregation at a stratum boundary is the same shape as a complement at a stratum boundary: read
   the certified closure of an earlier layer, compute something from it, and feed it to the next
   layer as an input relation with the result interned into the dictionary. `rel_stratified` is
   where that goes, and the `ComplementRecord` pattern — record the inputs, the counts and a digest
   so a checker can rebuild it — is the pattern an aggregate needs too.
