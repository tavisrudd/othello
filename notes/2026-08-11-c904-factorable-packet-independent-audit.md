# C904: independent audit of the factorable-quadric packet

Date: 2026-08-11
Status: theorem-grade exact audit; novelty/source polish still required
Scope: no manuscript or Lean edits; no commit

## Verdict

The exact packet calculation is sound, and the value `15` is not a special
random-example accident.

For a general globally generated charge-three bundle `E` on a smooth cubic
threefold, with `h^0(E)=4` and `det(E)=O_X(2)`, the uniquely lifted wedge
quadrics have a canonical null-correlation hyperplane normal form.  In that
normal form the factorable packet has a compact intersection-theoretic count

\[
                         15.
\]

The rational exact example realizes the generic count: its packet is reduced
and prime of degree `15` over `Q`.  An independent characteristic-five audit
finds a simple split packet point whose section pencil contains two prime
twisted cubics meeting in two reduced points.  Hensel lifting and Galois
transitivity then show that all fifteen characteristic-zero geometric packet
points in the exact example are of type `(3,3)`.

The resulting generic `D_{3,3}` fibre over `M_9` is birational to the union of
fifteen projective section pencils.  Equivalently, its normalization is
birational to the tautological `P^1`-bundle over the degree-fifteen packet.

## 1. What the primary scripts prove

The Sage construction verifies exactly that:

1. the six restricted quadrics are the wedges of four globally generating
   sections of `N(1)` on the distinguished `P^3`;
2. their lifted Pfaffian is `zF`, with `F` a smooth cubic;
3. the six lifted quadrics have no common point on `X=V(F)`;
4. one section has a smooth prime residual curve with Hilbert polynomial
   `6t` and Hilbert values `H(1)=5`, `H(2)=12`;
5. the rank-at-most-two packet on `Gr(2,4)` is reduced of projective length
   `15`, and it contains no rank-one quadric.

The independent Macaulay2 replay additionally proves that the homogeneous
packet ideal is prime over `Q`.  Thus it is a single closed point of residue
degree `15`; after extension to `Qbar` it consists of fifteen reduced points
on which the absolute Galois group acts transitively.

One comment in the primary Sage source needs correction: the degree-two
hyperplane residual of a regular section of `N(1)` is a pair of skew lines,
not a conic.  Indeed

\[
 c_2(N(1))=2H^2,
 \qquad
 \omega_Z=(\omega_{P^3}\otimes\det N(1))|_Z=O_Z(-2),
\]

so `p_a(Z)=-1`.  This correction makes the liaison genus calculation agree
with the smooth elliptic residual and does not alter any computation.

## 2. The constructed bundle really is a general charge-three bundle

Let `V` be the chosen four-dimensional section space.  On `X`, the six
quadrics satisfy the Pluecker relation and have no common zero, hence define
a morphism

\[
                         f:X\longrightarrow Gr(2,V).
\]

Pulling back the universal quotient gives a rank-two bundle

\[
                         E=f^*Q.
\]

It is globally generated by `V` and has determinant `O_X(2)`.  On the
hyperplane cubic surface `S=X\cap\{z=0\}`, the Pluecker morphism is the one
defined by the four chosen sections of `N(1)`, so

\[
                         E|_S\simeq N(1)|_S.
\]

It follows that `deg c_2(E)=6`; equivalently `E(-1)` has `c_1=0` and charge
three.  The exact section curve has three independent containing quadrics,
because `H_C(2)=12` inside the fifteen-dimensional space of quadrics.
The Serre sequence therefore gives

\[
                         h^0(X,E)=1+3=4.
\]

Put `F=E(-1)`.  Stability reduces to `H^0(X,F)=0`.  If a section existed,
restrict it successively to `S`.  Since `F|_S=N|_S`, the null-correlation
monad and Bott vanishing give

\[
 H^0(S,N(-m)|_S)=0\qquad(m\ge0).
\]

The section would therefore be divisible by every power of the hyperplane
equation, an impossibility.  Hence `F`, and thus the corresponding point of
`M_9`, is stable.  The computed prime smooth curve of Hilbert polynomial
`6t` is a connected elliptic sextic.  Smooth connected elliptic sextics are
then the zero curves of general sections by openness.

## 3. Null-correlation hyperplane normal form and dominance

The construction is not confined to a special locus.  Start from a general
globally generated bundle `E` in the main component of `M_9`, choose a basis
of `V=H^0(X,E)`, and let `q_ij` be its six wedge quadrics.  Since `X` has no
quadratic equation, every `q_ij` has a unique lift to `P^4`.  Their Pluecker
quartic vanishes on `X`, so uniquely

\[
 q_{01}q_{23}-q_{02}q_{13}+q_{03}q_{12}=F\ell
\]

for a linear form `ell`.  On the nonempty open where `ell` is nonzero, put
`H=V(ell)`.  On `H` the quadrics satisfy the Pluecker relation identically.
On the further open where they have no common zero, they define a generated
rank-two bundle `B` on `H=P^3` with

\[
                         \det B=O_H(2).
\]

Its restriction to the cubic surface `X\cap H` equals `E|_{X\cap H}`.
Writing `c_2(B)=bH^2`, restriction gives `3b=deg c_2(E)=6`, hence `b=2`.
Thus

\[
                 c_1(B(-1))=0,
                 \qquad c_2(B(-1))=1.
\]

Stability of `B(-1)` is open and holds in the exact example.  The classical
classification of stable rank-two bundles on `P^3` with `(c_1,c_2)=(0,1)`
then identifies `B(-1)` as a null-correlation bundle.  A standard exact
source is Okonek--Schneider--Spindler, *Vector Bundles on Complex Projective
Spaces*, Example 4.3.1, pp. 187--193; it constructs `M_{P^3}(0,1)` and proves
that these bundles are precisely the null-correlation bundles.  Conversely, four
generating sections of `N(1)` and arbitrary linear lifts of their six wedge
quadrics recover `F` by dividing the Pfaffian by `ell`; smoothness and
base-point freeness are open and occur in the exact example.

This gives mutually inverse normal forms on dense opens.  It also resolves
the apparent dimension issue: the hyperplane `H` is not an extra imposed
condition; it is canonically recovered from the linear factor `ell` of the
Pfaffian.

The moduli input used here is exactly Voisin's Section 2: the degree-six
elliptic-curve space is irreducible of dimension `12`; the associated stable
bundles form a nine-dimensional `M_9`; `h^0(E)=4`; and the curve-to-bundle
map is dominating with general fibre `P^3`.  Source: arXiv:1005.5621,
cached PDF SHA-256
`ca7103f6529128a24425dbfc1c87589402b17b12719329239fccdb590f74b547`,
printed pages 9--11.

## 4. Conceptual degree-fifteen calculation

For the chosen general four-section normal form, the restricted universal
quadric is the orthogonal sum of two binary quadrics.  On `Gr(2,4)` their two
binary determinants agree by the Pluecker relation.  The rank-at-most-two
discriminant is therefore the join of two Veronese conics.  Its normalization
is

\[
 Y=P_{P^1\times P^1}
   \bigl(O(-2,0)\oplus O(0,-2)\bigr).
\]

Write `h_1,h_2` for the two base hyperplane classes and
`xi=c_1(O_Y(1))`.  A point of `Y` represents

\[
                         q_H=aL^2+bM^2.
\]

The off-hyperplane part of the lifted quadric is

\[
                         z(ell_A+ell_B)+cz^2.
\]

For the full quadric to have rank at most two, first

\[
                         ell_A\in\langle L\rangle,
                         \qquad ell_B\in\langle M\rangle.
\]

These are divisors of classes `xi+h_1` and `xi+h_2`.  On their common zero
locus write `ell_A=alpha L`, `ell_B=beta M`.  The coefficient classes are

\[
\begin{array}{c|ccccc}
       &a&b&\alpha&\beta&c\\ \hline
 c_1   &\xi-2h_1&\xi-2h_2&\xi-h_1&\xi-h_2&\xi.
\end{array}
\]

The remaining rank condition is the determinant of the quadratic form in
`L,M,z`:

\[
                         4abc-a\beta^2-b\alpha^2=0.
\]

It has class `3xi-2h_1-2h_2`.  For
`O(-2,0)\oplus O(0,-2)` one has

\[
 \pi_*\xi=1,
 \qquad
 \pi_*\xi^2=2(h_1+h_2),
 \qquad
 \pi_*\xi^3=4h_1h_2.
\]

Therefore

\[
\begin{aligned}
 \#R_{3,3}(E)
 &=\int_Y(\xi+h_1)(\xi+h_2)
              (3\xi-2h_1-2h_2)\\
 &=\int_Y\bigl(3\xi^3+\xi^2(h_1+h_2)-\xi h_1h_2\bigr)\\
 &=12+4-1=15.
\end{aligned}
\]

The two boundary sections of `Y` contract to the rank-one conics of the
join.  The exact packet has empty intersection with that rank-one locus;
this is open.  Hence the displayed intersection has no boundary correction
for a general lift.  This proves deformation-invariant generic length `15`
without extrapolating from one random fibre.

## 5. Characteristic-five type certificate

The independent finite-field audit reduces the exact integral matrix modulo
five.  It proves:

- the full special packet still has Hilbert polynomial `15`;
- it has exactly one `F_5`-rational projective point,
  `(1,4,3,2,0,4)`;
- the corresponding quadric has rank exactly two and splits into two
  distinct `F_5` hyperplanes;
- the packet Jacobian has rank five, so its projective tangent space is zero;
- for pencil parameter `lambda=3`, the sextic has Hilbert polynomial `6t`;
- its two saturated components are prime with Hilbert polynomial `3t+1`,
  hence are twisted cubics;
- their intersection is reduced of length two.

Let `P_Q` be the prime degree-fifteen packet and take its schematic closure
over `Z_(5)`.  The closure is finite flat of rank `15`.  The raw special
packet also has length `15`, so the special fibre of the closure is the raw
packet: there is no vertical or saturation discrepancy.  The simple rational
point therefore lifts uniquely over `Z_5`.  Its two distinct linear factors
and the open type-`(3,3)` configuration lift as well.

Because `P_Q` is prime, its fifteen geometric points form one Galois orbit.
The property that a generic section pencil consists of two twisted cubics
meeting in two points is invariant under Galois.  One lifted point has this
property, so every characteristic-zero geometric packet point has it.

## 6. Comparison with Voisin's `D_{3,3}` fibre

For a regular section `s` with zero curve `C_s`, the Serre sequence and
`h^0(E)=4` give

\[
 V/\langle s\rangle\xrightarrow{\sim}H^0(I_{C_s}(2)),
 \qquad \bar t\longmapsto s\wedge t.
\]

If `C_s=A\cup B` is of type `(3,3)`, its components span unique hyperplanes
with equations `l_A,l_B`.  Their product determines a unique `t` modulo
`s`, hence a unique two-plane `U=<s,t>`.  Every point of `P(U)` has the same
wedge quadric `l_A l_B`; on a dense open of that pencil its zero curve again
has the two degree-three components.  Conversely, a factorable packet point
and a regular section in its tautological two-plane recover such a curve.

Thus, on the generic open used by Voisin,

\[
 \bigl(D_{3,3,E}^{\mathrm{norm}}\bigr)_{\mathrm{generic}}
 \dashrightarrow
 P\bigl(U|_{R_{3,3}(E)}\bigr)
\]

is birational.  The conceptual count and type audit show that over an
algebraic closure this is a union of fifteen projective lines.  This agrees
with Voisin's dimension-one generic fibre and makes the zero-cycle index of
the normalized fibre equal to the index of the degree-fifteen packet.

## 7. Remaining boundaries

1. The theorem is mathematically closed, but novelty has not been audited.
2. The result determines geometric packet length and the exact rational
   example's degree-fifteen residue field.  It does not by itself compute the
   deck action of the packet over the exotic marked base.
3. The normalization/birational comparison is on the regular type-`(3,3)`
   open; special nonregular sections and other splitting strata remain
   excluded, exactly as in Voisin's generic-fibre argument.
