## Adversarial proof-and-scope pass

The recognition theorem does not use minimum-codeword generation.  Its proof
uses only the partial-Weyl marginal criterion, additive closure of the
recognition group, and the explicit generation hypothesis.  The stabilizer-AME
headline theorem instead uses the independent support-cardinality squeeze and
bijective coordinate projections.  Deleting the blanket sentence therefore
removes no premise or proof step.

The stability claims now keep their quantifiers in the correct direction.  A
bound on traceless generators implies a defect estimate on the displayed
coordinate region; it does not imply that every unitary in a defect ball admits
such generators.  Conversely, the explicit decomposition theorem begins with a
small-defect hypothesis and rounds to a Clifford, paying the stated exponential
factor.  The abstract, introduction, order-three discussion, post-threshold
discussion, theorem hierarchy, and excluded-boundary row all make that
distinction.

The section split changes presentation only.  The theorem statements,
hypotheses, proof order, labels, and release trust classes are unchanged.
Rendered numbering follows the intended blocks: equations (3.1)--(3.3),
(4.1)--(4.4), (5.1)--(5.5), (6.1)--(6.4), (7.1)--(7.8), and (8.1).

## Extra-juice and Tao closeout

The cheap structural gain was to make the section split enforce the conceptual
distinction the prose needed: local frame recognition, atlas classification,
local stability, and exact logical images no longer share one section number.
Auditing every manual equation tag exposed the same distinction in the trust
maps, so those surfaces were corrected rather than leaving the PDF alone.

The strongest referee question is the direction of the stability implication:
does the growing coordinate radius contain a comparably large defect ball?  The
answer is not proved and is no longer suggested.  The manuscript states the
available inverse theorem separately and identifies its exponentially small
threshold.
