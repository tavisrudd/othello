# C305: the generic `q=512` sweep is not a feasible finite closure

**Lane**: `relconic`

Date: 2026-07-18.

## Result

The weighted `p=1` chart for the C210 collision cover is lossless on the target
generic scope `a*b*delta*p!=0`: it preserves rational collision points, the
committed `H,J` reconstruction, and genuineness.  The proposed `512^4` sweep,
however, is not a lossless census of coefficient specializations.  After
setting `p=1`, the cover still has the seven free coefficient parameters

    (e,delta,a,b,w,h0,h1),             delta*a*b != 0.

The omitted `e,h0,h1` occur nontrivially in the resultant, with respective
degrees `4,2,2`.  The exact normalized scope therefore contains

    (q-1)^3*q^4 = 9,169,434,325,723,119,616

specializations at `q=512`, not `q^4=68,719,476,736`.  Removing the three
already-closed factorization branches leaves

    9,169,364,232,123,189,248

generic off-divisor specializations.  Even after every symmetry and Frobenius
quotient certified below, there are at least

    994,939,695,325,867

representatives.  A full exact sweep is therefore rejected by the C305
resource gate.  The generic off-divisor `q=512` gap remains open; this report
does not promote the successful benchmark shard into a finite-field theorem.

## Lossless scaling chart

Give `u,t,e,delta,b,p,r` weight one, `h0,h1` weight two, and `a,w` weight zero.
Exact sparse arithmetic in the committed trace-one pullback gives

| polynomial | weighted degree |
|---|---:|
| `A,D` | 1 |
| `B,E` | 2 |
| `C,F` | 3 |
| `H=D*B+A*E` | 3 |
| `J=D*C+A*F` | 4 |
| resultant `R` | 8 |

Thus, for `lambda!=0`,

    (u,t,e,delta,b,p,h0,h1)
      -> (lambda*u,lambda*t,lambda*e,lambda*delta,lambda*b,lambda*p,
          lambda^2*h0,lambda^2*h1)

maps `R` to `lambda^8*R`, `H` to `lambda^3*H`, and `J` to
`lambda^4*J`.  Hence the reconstructed root `r=J/H` maps to `lambda*r`.
Taking `lambda=p^-1` gives a bijection with `p=1` on the intended `p!=0`
scope.  In the seed-height-zero coordinates used by the collision equations,
the three affine points map by `(x,h)->(lambda*x,lambda^2*h)`; equality and
collinearity are preserved.  The chart is therefore lossless for the exact
one-seed collision and genuineness question needed here.  This does not claim
that the same scaling fixes the entire two-seed plane configuration, the
distinction already recorded on the C210 constant-height boundary.

The chart removes only `p`.  Forgetting `e,h0,h1` is not another chart or
quotient: those parameters remain in `R`, and before elimination the two
collision equations depend on both heights through the nonzero matrix in the
algebraic-reduction section below.

## Exact quotients

The following actions preserve the normalized collision problem.

1. `w -> w+1` fixes `theta=w^2+w+1`, hence fixes `R,H,J`.  It is a free
   involution.
2. Reversing the repair layers sends

       (e,h0,h1,r) -> (e+delta,h0+k0,h1+k1,r+u).

   Exact substitution fixes `R,H` and sends `J` to `J+u*H`.  It is free because
   `delta!=0`.
3. A subfield translation `x->x+d`, followed by repair reparametrization, sends

       h1 -> h1+a*d^2+b*d.

   For `a*b!=0`, the additive map has kernel `{0,b/a}`, image size `q/2`, and
   exactly two orbits.  They are distinguished by
   `Tr(a*h1/b^2)` and represented by `h1=0` and `h1=b^2/a` because
   `Tr_GF(512)/GF(2)(1)=1`.
4. Simultaneous Frobenius `x->x^(2^j)`, `0<=j<9`, preserves all equations and
   has orbit size at most nine.

The product of the maximum orbit sizes is only

    (q/2)*2*2*9 = 9,216.

The three factorization branches have exact normalized union size

    2*(q-1)^3*q^2 + 2*(q-1)^2*q^2 - 4*(q-1)^2*q
      = 70,093,599,930,368.

Dividing the off-divisor count by `9,216` and rounding up gives the displayed
`994,939,695,325,867` representative lower bound.  This is deliberately
optimistic for enumeration: shorter Frobenius orbits can only increase the
number of representatives.  No unproved projective-equivalence quotient is
used; that broader normal-form question belongs to C297.

## Deterministic shard benchmark

The checker uses `GF(512)=GF(2)[x]/(x^9+x^4+1)`, with integer `2` as a
primitive element.  A fixed counter permutation selects 512 normalized
off-divisor configurations after the exact `h1` translation quotient.  It has
no random state or seed.  For each configuration, the checker scans `(u,t)` in
lexicographic order, tests the committed resultant identities, reconstructs

    r = J/H,

checks both original quadratic equations, and independently evaluates the
three-point height-interpolation incidence equation in the quadratic
extension.  It then requires the left repair, right repair, and seed points to
be pairwise distinct.

Exact shard result:

- 512 configurations and 512 genuine collision witnesses;
- 509,519 candidate `(u,t)` pairs examined;
- mean `995.154296875` and maximum `9,301` candidates per configuration;
- witness digest
  `12aa65e3b4cd24099262b8bb1d3d60749cf3083669f8919d99ce30f1687229df`.

On Python `3.13.12` on an AMD Ryzen AI 9 HX 370, three runs of

```bash
python3 analyze_c305_q512_generic_closure.py --configurations 512 --benchmark
```

took `1.73223`, `1.64013`, and `1.75766` seconds.  The median run processed
about `295.6` configurations or `294,141` candidate pairs per second.  A
straight extrapolation of that measured kernel to the certified orbit lower
bound is about `106,666` single-core years.  Even the incorrect bare `q^4`
scope would take about `7.37` years in this implementation.  These timing
extrapolations are feasibility evidence, not complexity lower bounds; a native
kernel could be much faster.  The approximately fifteen orders of magnitude
remaining after the valid quotients make a full run unjustified without a new
algebraic theorem.

## Algebraic reduction retained from the failed sweep

Before taking the resultant, the two collision equations are affine-linear in
the two height parameters.  Their exact coefficient matrix is

    [ u      delta   ]
    [ delta  delta+u ],

with determinant

    Q(u)=u^2+u*delta+delta^2.

On the odd tower, `Q` has no rational zero: after division by `delta^2` it is
`z^2+z+1`, whose roots require `GF(4)`.  Therefore every fixed tuple
`(e,delta,a,b,w,u,t,r)` determines a unique height pair `(h0,h1)`.

This replaces the infeasible outer-height census by a precise image problem:
for each fixed skeleton `(e,delta,a,b,w)`, determine whether the genuine
triple-to-height map covers every height pair, modulo the exact translation
twist.  A proof of surjectivity, or an exact classification of its missing
values, could close the bounded field.  Another coefficient sweep merely
repackages the same enormous domain and is not justified.
