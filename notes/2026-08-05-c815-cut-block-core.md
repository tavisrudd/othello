## What was proved

New module `lean/RelativeConicArcs/ConferenceCutBlocks.lean`, all statements
over an arbitrary commutative ring and, where the size is not forced,
an arbitrary finite index type:

- `mul_transpose_eq_of_sq_smul` — order the coordinates by a subset and its
  complement, so the matrix is `fromBlocks A B Bᵀ E`. If it squares to `q • 1`,
  then `B * Bᵀ = q • 1 - A * A`. This is the principal block of `C² = qI` on the
  chosen subset and is the whole algebraic content of the manuscript's
  cross-block spectrum formula. Symmetry of the full matrix is never used; only
  the shape of the lower-left block matters.
- `trace_mul_self` — for a zero-diagonal matrix whose off-diagonal entries
  multiply pairwise to one, the trace of the square is `d(d-1)` with `d` the
  cardinality of the index type. This is the only input to the manuscript's
  first exchange moment.
- `fourSetWeight`, `fourSetWeight_eq_three_or_neg_one` — the three signed
  Hamilton-cycle products of a zero-diagonal symmetric sign matrix on four
  labels satisfy `(w - 3)(w + 1) = 0`. The proof is the manuscript's own reason
  rather than a case table: every edge occurs twice in the product of the three
  cycles, so that product is one, and three ring elements squaring to one with
  product one have sum `3` or `-1`. A four-set is aligned exactly when the
  weight is `3`.

Two pieces of this row turned out to be already proved and only needed to be
recognized as such. The order-three principal-block identity `A² = 2I + τA`,
which is what makes the order-six exchange spectrum sign-independent, is
`ConferenceCutSpectrum.signedTriangle_sq`. The Ramsey input `R(3,3) = 6` is
`AlignedTwoGraph.exists_monochromatic_triple` together with
`no_monochromatic_triple_five`; the gap inventory had proposed proving it, and
the aligned-design work had already done so.

## What OPER-3 still needs, stated precisely

- The support-sorted organization of the fourth trace,
  `tr(A⁴) = d(d-1) + 12·C(d,3) + 8·Σ_K w(K)`. This is the one genuinely
  combinatorial piece: it needs the quadruples of indices classified by the
  cardinality of their support and each class counted. Nothing about it is
  hard, but it is a real Finset proof rather than a polynomial identity, and it
  is what converts the four-set weights above into the second exchange moment.
- The inclusion-rank descent. The manuscript cites Jolliffe for full column
  rank of the four-set-to-`d`-set inclusion matrix in characteristic zero; that
  is a substantial classical theorem and is not a candidate for cheap
  formalization.
- Every eigenvalue and singular-value statement. Nothing in this module leaves
  polynomial algebra: the identity `B * Bᵀ = q • 1 - A * A` is what makes the
  spectrum of the exchange operator `{1 - α²/q}` over the reals, but that step
  is not formalized and the module says so.

Because the inclusion-rank input is not realistically formalizable here, this
row will stay at partial coverage even after the fourth-trace count lands. That
is a boundary to state in the manuscript's verification section rather than a
gap to close.

**Superseded 2026-08-06.** The author instruction is that Paper III's theorems
carry no cited-theorem boundary, exactly as the Dye inputs were retired from
Paper I: OPER-3 must reach full coverage and the paragraph above must not be
copied into the manuscript's verification section. The judgement it rests on is
also too pessimistic. The manuscript does not need the general inclusion-matrix
rank theorem; it needs the single consequence that equal sums of the
aligned-four-set indicator over all balanced halves force that indicator to be
constant on four-sets. That follows by one-element swap descent: two
`d`-sets differing in one element give
`Σ_{S ⊆ K ∩ K', |S| = 3} (f (S ∪ {a}) - f (S ∪ {b})) = 0`, and the same
argument applied to the difference function descends the subset size until the
difference vanishes pointwise, whence connectivity of the swap graph makes `f`
constant. That is an induction over `Finset` cardinality, not a rank theorem,
and the Jolliffe citation stays as attribution rather than as a dependency.

## Cheap next win this exposed

The four-set weight is defined here from the operator side, as Hamilton-cycle
products of a sign matrix, while `AlignedTwoGraph` defines aligned four-sets
from the two-graph side, as Boolean triangle data. A bridge theorem — the
weight is `3` exactly when the corresponding four-set is aligned in the
two-graph sense — would connect the two halves of the Paper III formal surface
that currently only meet in prose, and it is a finite symbolic statement rather
than new mathematics. It is not started.
