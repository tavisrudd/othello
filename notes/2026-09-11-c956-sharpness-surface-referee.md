## Correctness assessment

### 1. Quotient criterion and descent

Packet §§11–14 correctly replace a possible generic-injectivity shortcut with an actual isomorphism open. The equation

\[
\chi^{w_j-w_0}(t)=\kappa_j/\kappa_0
\]

has a unique solution because the differences form an integral basis. All projective weight translations cancel. Reordering weights changes the formula presentation but not the orbit intersection. Consequently uniqueness descends the corrected point, and no individual split coordinate needs to descend.

The witness in the slice ensures that the retraction image meets the tangent-projection isomorphism open. Since the slice contains the projection centre, its intersection with that open is the inverse image of an open in a linear projective space of dimension `n-r`. This proves rationality of the correct component and establishes the two rational inverse maps. The inverse-graph component and nonvanishing restrictions in §13 are essential; the packet retains them.

There is no root extraction and no hidden finite cover in this index-one argument. Conversely, the existence of these rational maps does not itself provide expanded ground-field formulas. The packet accurately identifies the additional data needed for an executable parametrization.

### 2. Saturation, opens, and the universal torsor

The sublattice generated by the last three standard cocharacters is visibly saturated. The determinant-two sign basis would instead parametrize its torus through a degree-two isogeny, so counting its kernel as a quotient degree would be erroneous. The packet explicitly avoids that mistake.

The openness argument has the right order. A witness for every smooth geometric Cox parameter establishes a nonempty evaluation open on each model. Intersecting that open with the relative tangent-projection isomorphism open is legitimate because the product is geometrically integral. Density is then used twice to obtain a ground-field pair. It does not assert that a printed split witness descends or lies on the final isomorphism open.

The decisive finite gate is still the **evaluation-and-smoothness cover for the actual twenty-quadric Cox model**. An eight-row determinant check alone does not close it. I have reviewed the claimed use of that gate, not rerun or independently certified the four witnesses. The parent audit owns this check.

The generic torsor splitting is valid: a stable-permutation identity gives `T × Q ≅ Q'` with both auxiliary tori quasi-trivial, hence `H¹(F,T)=0` for every extension. A generic section supplies a T-equivariant product, so quotienting by the scalar torus and the selected rank-three subtorus really gives `S × (T₀/T₃)`. An unequivariant stable-birational assertion would not suffice, but is not what is used. TZ v2 Remark 2.2 states precisely the needed vanishing and generic splitting; §3 supplies the linear descended Cox model.

The nonminimal reduction is also appropriate: a ground-field contraction of disjoint exceptional curves raises degree from four to at least five, carries the rational point along, and invokes rationality of the higher-degree del Pezzo surface. No lower bound is claimed for these already rational surfaces.

### 3. Three-parameter family and Picard action

The two quadrics in (F3) reconstruct the cubic-surface fibre by `wQ₁+zQ₂`, and `Q₂` determines `h` on `w≠0`. The displayed rational point is smooth. Distinctness of the pencil roots follows from the odd valuations of `(a²+3)³` at its simple zeros; this argument remains valid for every constant choice of `λ, μ, ν` in characteristic zero. It does not assume irreducibility of the cubic factor.

The identities for the components are consistent. At a cubic root, multiplication by `ρᵢ-a` factors the binary quadratic using `dᵢ²=3(A-ρᵢ²)`. The factor equations are nonzero because `c(a)` has the nonzero linear coefficient `-9/4`. At the other roots,

\[
(s-a)(-s-a)=-3,\qquad
c(s)c(-s)=\beta^2-A^3/16,
\]

so (F8) follows with the stated factor `1/9`. Odd permutations negate the cyclic root differences and force the odd exchange parity among the last two component pairs. Even permutations force even parity. This gives containment in (F9) whether or not the cubic or the quadratic splitting fields have the generic degrees.

The cited representation identification is genuine: TZ v2, end of Proposition 5.3, displays exactly the four generators in (F9) and identifies that subgroup with type I₃. Their §4 explicitly explains why components of the conic bundle on the blown-up surface encode the ten rulings and therefore the Picard action. Thus the group-order check is not being asked to prove the representation identification.

One elementary bridge should be added for this new family. Let `P=[0:0:0:0:1]` on (F3). A line on S through P has direction in `w=z=0`, and the remaining equations would be

\[
u^2+3v^2=0,\qquad -au^2+6uv+3av^2=0.
\]

For a nonzero direction they force `u=-av` and `a²+3=0`, impossible in `k(a)`. Thus P lies on no exceptional line. Its blowup is the smooth cubic surface with distinguished line used for the residual conic bundle, so the §4 Picard interpretation applies directly. This is a short repair of an underexplained interface, not evidence that the family theorem is false.

### 4. Exact levels, relatives, and other extensions

Finitely generated descent of an alleged rationalization, its inverse, and their identities correctly transfers the complex lower bound to an arbitrary characteristic-zero field. ### 5. Finite index and zero-cycles

In characteristic zero the orbit equations are transverse at the witness: after removing the common character, the derivative is the product of an invertible maximal-minor matrix and the differential of an étale isogeny. Hence the rational component selected by the tangent open dominates the quotient.

Over algebraically closed constants, the generic orbit is a split-torus torsor and has a rational point. Its intersection with the slice is then a torsor under a constant group H of order d. The absolute Galois action is by translations through a subgroup of H, so all field factors have degrees dividing d. Geometric integrality of C ensures its degree remains e after extending constants. This justifies `e | d`; neither `e=d` nor arbitrary nonsplit factor-degree divisibility is justified. The baseline proof spells out this argument more clearly than the packet's reference to generic incidence components and should be retained.

The subsequent universal degree-zero Chow annihilation follows from moving cycles to a finite-flat locus and push-pull from a smooth rational resolution. The rational source gives a ground-field point. Evaluation at the generic point and localization give the stated decomposition of the diagonal, with support on the first factor. The packet correctly avoids deriving rationality from this conclusion.

The ground-field object is the kernel of evaluation at x; only its split description is the coordinate-sum hyperplane. The baseline avoids this slip.

4. Retain the caveat about a fourth `xy²` coefficient. If `c(a)=0`, the printed factor chart can degenerate; a new chart is required before asserting the four-coefficient theorem.
5. Retain the baseline's detailed constant-group argument in the finite-index proof. It is short and resolves the most plausible false inference in that extension.
