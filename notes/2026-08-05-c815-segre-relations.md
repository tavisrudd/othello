## What was proved

New module `lean/RelativeConicArcs/ClebschOuterSegreRelations.lean`:

- `outerReindex`, `outerSign`, `outerCubic` — the six signed translates of the
  oriented triangle cubic of the fixed order-six integral conference matrix,
  taken over the six reorderings that fix the labels `0`, `1`, `2` and permute
  `3`, `4`, `5`, each weighted by the sign of the reordering. This transversal
  is what the relabelling rule `g · Z_T = sgn(g) · Z_{gT}` turns into the
  manuscript's signed coloured-triangle frame.
- `sum_outerCubic` — the six translates sum to zero.
- `sum_outerCubic_cube` — their cubes sum to zero.

Both are polynomial identities in the six coordinates over an arbitrary
commutative ring, proved by expanding the twenty triangle monomials of each
translate. The cube identity is a degree-nine identity in six variables and is
the expensive one; the module still elaborates in about fifty seconds at
default heartbeat and recursion limits, and the gate build peaks at 3.5 GB.

This is stronger than the manuscript, which does not prove the Segre relations
at all: section 05 cites them as classical from Hunt--Mueller--Schwarz--Vakil
and derives the diagonal Clebsch section from them. The Lean proof is direct
and takes no literature input.

## Incidental finding

Every one of the fifteen pairwise sums of the outer cubics is exactly twice a
perfect-matching product, and the fifteen matchings so obtained are distinct,
so the fifteen pairs are in bijection with the fifteen perfect matchings of six
labels. This was not sought and is almost certainly classical Segre-cubic
combinatorics. It is logged with its exact replay bundle in
`notes/2026-07-14-clebsch-discovery-track.md` as an open, unpromoted lead
toward the outer matching-frame identification.
