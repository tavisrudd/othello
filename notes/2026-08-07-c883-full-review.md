## Verdict

The manuscript is not ready to freeze, but I found no second mathematical
contradiction comparable to the terminal residual-coordinate error.  The
headline R5–R10 statements are consistently qualified by field range, radius
gate, and package hypotheses; the certificate boundary is unusually explicit;
and the full deterministic replay completed without a discrepancy.

Three repairs should precede another submission candidate:

1. Narrow the prior-work sentence saying that none of the twisted-cubic papers
   treats projective Reed–Solomon syndromes, covering radius, or deep holes.
   Blokhuis–Pellikaan–Szőnyi explicitly define syndromes for the generalized
   Reed–Solomon `[q+1,q-3,5]` code and compute its extended coset-leader weight
   enumerator.  The defensible distinction is that the cited papers do not give
   this manuscript's redundancy-five **syndrome-as-pencil** classification and
   all-field radius/deep-hole promotion.
2. Synchronize the formal and citation documentation: correct two wrong source
   pinpoints in the Lean R5 header, reconcile the `71` versus `75` theorem-label
   count, and either cite or remove the independent `PG(4,8)` largest-arc
   sentence.
3. Close or explicitly waive the PRS-relevant trust-registry warnings before
   advertising a referee-ready formal release.  The named gates compile and the
   aggregate fact has no project axioms or opaque constants, but the area-wide
   trust audit is not currently green.

The C883 coordinate repair itself landed during this review as commit
`932225c4`; it is mathematically green and is no longer an open source repair.

A post-reread delta review also found no paper regression in the two preceding

## Findings and required manuscript corrections

### 1. Terminal residual prime: correction verified and landed

The independent C883 note remains the controlling analysis.  In bottom Hankel
coordinates the residual prime is parametrized by

\[
 [u:v:w]\longmapsto
 [6u^2:3uv:v^2+2uw:3vw:6w^2],
\]

not by the plain-coefficient square map.  The corrected map annihilates the
seven printed generators, gives

\[
 f_c=6(uX^2-vXY+wY^2)^2,
\]

and has `D=(4uw-v^2)^3`.  The stable-component elimination makes the generators
authoritative.  The replacement witness `[3:0:1:0:3]` lies on the residual
prime and has `D=8`.  The original reported residuals are exact, with the
qualification that all acquire a factor `5`, so the two surfaces coincide
accidentally in characteristic five.

Commit `932225c4` lands all three repairs in
`sections/07-recursive-carriers.tex`, and the resulting source is internally
consistent.  The next release candidate must be built from that commit or a
descendant and receive a new PDF hash; the old immutable candidate cannot be
silently reused.

The subsequent supporting-note commit `490ded4b` correctly concludes that the
Kaipa--Pradhan elliptic curve is the same fibre-square cover, but its new claim
that their quartic is exactly `3/2` times the manuscript's residual
discriminant is not correct after the line coordinates and projective scale are
matched.  On the affine patch
\(\Delta=a_0a_2-a_1^2\ne0\), direct substitution of a basis of the Hankel
kernel into Kaipa--Pradhan's equations (8) and (20) gives

\[
 D_L=\frac{\operatorname{disc}(h_x)}{36\Delta^2}
     =\frac{D_f}{9\Delta^2},
 \qquad D_f=(3\Delta)^2D_L.
\]

Thus the manuscript's function-field square-class statement is right and no
quadratic twist occurs.  The `3/2` in the supporting note comes from comparing
quartic invariant normalizations without first matching the projective scale
of the Pl\"ucker representative.  Correct that note and its symbolic flag; no
paper theorem or count needs to change.

### 2. Prior-work framing overstates the separation from BPS

The introduction says, after citing the twisted-cubic line and incidence
literature, that none of it treats projective Reed–Solomon syndromes, covering
radius, or deep holes.  This is false as written for `BPS2022`:

- its abstract says it computes the extended coset-leader weight enumerator of
  the generalized Reed–Solomon `[q+1,q-3,5]_q` code;
- Section 2 defines the syndrome `s=Hr^T`, identifies cosets with syndromes,
  and interprets coset weight by minimal column spans; and
- the enumerator determines the distribution of coset-leader weights and in
  particular exposes the covering-radius layer of that lower-redundancy code.

This does not erase the manuscript's contribution.  BPS studies the
codimension-four twisted-cubic code and uses line classes in its enumerator;
it does not classify the present codimension-five syndrome directions, where a
single syndrome produces a pencil of cubics, nor promote that classification
to the all-field R5 deep-hole theorem.  State that narrower distinction in both
the introduction and the R5 literature remark.

### 3. The uncited `PG(4,8)` maximum-arc sentence is stronger than needed

The q=8 remark already proves nonextendability through Seroussi–Roth and Dür.
Its final independent sentence additionally asserts that the largest arc in
`PG(4,8)` has nine points.  That is a stronger global maximum-arc statement
than the nonextendability of this normal rational curve, and no source is
attached.  Either cite a primary finite-geometry/MDS theorem for this exact
case or delete the sentence; it contributes nothing to the proof.

### 4. Abstract/title framing is acceptable but could be made harder to misread

“Exact classifications through redundancy ten” is not literally an all-field
claim: R8–R10 are stated only for `q>=43,53,59`.  The abstract supplies those
ranges later and the main theorem states them clearly, so I do not regard this
as false advertising.  Adding “in the stated field ranges” to the first
sentence would make the intended reading immediate.
