## Paper-by-paper disposition

### 1. Nofil finite-geometry outcomes

**Cheap/current-paper answers.** Make the mirror theorem's reusable game-semantic interface
explicit; extract the actual reply strategy wherever a P-result is presently certified; use the
q=5 octahedral and bounded q=7 exterior scouts only if they yield clean game statements (C189,
C198).

**Best genuine leveler.** Solve one non-mirror projective family by an adaptive strategy. The q=9
intrusion/C13 route and the fixed-locus/Witt C120 route already own this question. A new task would
only duplicate them.

**Follow-on, not current scope.** Classify the cyclic mod-6 law over all finite abelian groups;
characterize when P in a transitive triple system comes from a fixed-point-free involution; compute
nimbers rather than only P/N outcomes.

### 2. Dihedral Schreier Node Kayles

**Cheap/current-paper answer (C199).** For every published zero-nimber template, give a direct
pairing/symmetry strategy or state explicitly that the proof is Grundy decomposition; for every
nonzero template, give a canonical winning move rule. A table entry without extractable play is not
enough.

**Moderate conceptual answer (C200).** Compute automorphism groups, spectra, and orbital/coherent-
configuration data for the bounded D/S4/A5 template catalogue; compare against known Möbius
ladders, dihedral Cayley graphs, and cross-ratio/Schreier families. Promote only a structural
identification or a genuinely new family, not a list of invariants.

**Follow-on.** Polynomial-in-log(q) value evaluation for generic PSL2/PGL2 residuals and a
Burnside-ring factorization of the template value map are high-upside but not cheap.

### 3. Arcs complete outside a conic

**Cheap/current-paper answers already queued.** State the exact implication diagram separating
BSW complete exteriority from relative completeness (C195); use the q=7 BSW four-arc as a strict-
containment foil (C196); add rho_C(5)=4 from the q=5 sibling (C188). These directly answer the most
predictable terminology and significance objections.

**Best next theorem (C201).** Start from the q=16 quadratic evaluation-rank obstruction and ask for
an infinite even-field statement. In the same computation, classify the equality and first-excess
orbits rather than reporting only the optimum. The bounded first gate is symbolic reduction plus a
q=64 test; a negative result must identify the obstruction that fails.

**Follow-on.** A polarity dual in blocking/tangent language and a structural stability theorem for
near-extremizers would be more exciting than another isolated value of rho_C(q), but should follow
the C201 evidence.

### 4. Clebsch hexagon code

The expert questions are unusually well covered by the live queue: low-degree locus hierarchy
(C184), decoder and ambiguity geometry (C185), conceptual A5 orbit proof (C186), k<=7
classification (C187), Dye's all-field uncovered formula (C194), and the BSW Pasch foil (C197).
The correct order by value is C194, C185, C186, C187, C184, C197; C168/C182 remain the release
gates. Do not create another Clebsch task until these are disposed.

The strongest follow-ons are the q=31 BSW Petersen configuration, Dye's five-valent graph of
Clebsch hexagons, k>=8 conic filling, and higher-dimensional/higher-genus deep-hole varieties.
They are questions to pose, not partial sections to add now.

### 5. Complete repair hypergraphs

**Bounded structural upgrade (C202).** For the q=9 completed cubic-axis seed, classify minimum
blockers and maximum pairwise-disjoint repair families up to the monomial automorphism group, not
only their cardinalities tau and nu. Emit an independently replayable ILP/orbit certificate and
test whether the optimizer types persist symbolically for q=3^h.

**Operational scout (C203).** Retain coefficient-labelled repair equations and determine what is
and is not implied about helper access, bandwidth, and availability. The deliverable is either one
new exact operational guarantee or a precise negative boundary explaining why support completeness
does not control those metrics. This should precede any comparison plot against LRC bounds.

**Follow-on.** A MacWilliams/refined-enumerator transform and optimization of the outer AG family
could broaden the audience, but neither should be advertised before the bounded coefficient scout.

### 6. Equivariant robust completion

No new task is allocated here because the owning Baer/alternate-repair lanes already cover the
expert questions: equality and phase-diagram classification (C148–C151), conceptual removal of the
PG(2,25) certificate (C152), and higher-parameter repair bounds. The most valuable later question
is whether quadratic conjugate pairs generalize to higher Galois orbits without internal
dependence. Routing remains with that lane's owner.

### 7. Continuation-graph rigidity

**First recognition gate (C204).** For the N1 graph, compute exact automorphism groups, spectra,
orbitals, and coherent-configuration intersection data over a bounded small-q range; compare with
known cross-ratio and finite-geometry graph families. Use the data to formulate and prove a general
automorphism theorem or report the exact exceptional cells. This is a prerequisite to claiming a
new graph family.

**Follow-on.** General M_0,n reducts, minimum relational data for reconstruction, and robustness to
corrupted continuation data are potentially headline results, but they depend on N1 becoming a
manuscript and the N2 prior-art gate being closed.

## Submission-versus-program rule

An upgrade enters a current manuscript only if it does one of three things: replaces computation
by explanation, gives the paper's invariant an operational consequence, or closes a likely
priority/definition objection. Broader classifications, stability programmes, and higher-
dimensional analogues stay as explicitly posed follow-ons until they produce a theorem.
