## The theorem I understand

The intended headline theorem is a recognition theorem inside a sharply
specified carrier. For a full `PGL_2(q)`-orbit of perfect matchings of the
`q+1` rational points of a conic, form the affine configuration obtained by
dividing differences of normalized secant products by the conic equation.
If the annihilator of the square of its affine evaluation space is a line
generated by a two-valued vector, then the orbit is exactly the balanced
`B_3/F_7` or `H_3/F_11` orbit. In either survivor the line recovers the two
`PSL_2(q)` one-factorization sheets up to interchange, the first nonzero
signed moment is an outer-anti-invariant cubic, and the homogenized point set
is self-associated and arithmetically Gorenstein with that cubic as the
Artinian inverse system. The matching carrier is sharp: the relevant
stabilizer fixes an affine line with one Chow point but `q-2` nonmatching
orbits having the same trade.

The strongest result I can independently regard as proved from the human
text is the **positive and boundary half** for the two displayed orbits:
conditional only on the explicitly identified classical subgroup/module
facts and finite input rows, the quotient construction, exact trade,
intrinsic sheet recovery, cubic-first orientation, direct Gorenstein
duality, and fixed-line/unique-Chow-point boundary form a coherent proof. I
cannot certify the all-odd-`q` uniqueness assertion from the text because
its decisive modular Hom and outer-parity lemma is not proved at a
referee-checkable level. If the missing arguments in Findings 1–3 are
supplied, the causal chain does support the full stated theorem.

## Reconstructed proof spine

1. Proposition 2.1 pulls every normalized matching product back to the same
   binary form on the conic. Since the Veronese kernel is `(Q)`, differences
   divide by `Q`; the four-endpoint Plücker switch supplies the local form of
   the same fact. Changing the reference translates the quotient.
2. A hypothetical two-valued one-dimensional quadratic trade has full
   support. Transitivity makes its two level sets the two `PSL_2(q)` sheets,
   with opposite values. A matching stabilizer `K` has no `p`-element, hence
   is a `p'`-group; its sheet permutation module is projective and has size
   `q lambda`.
3. Lemma 3.1 pulls each relevant simple sheet-socle constituent and both of
   its outer extensions into the quadratic moment module. Either its linear
   moment vanishes, contradicting the claimed absent outer parity in
   `Sym^2 F`, or a pulled-back affine extension would split.
4. Lemma 3.2 is meant to calculate the actual finite-group Hom basis in
   `F = Sym^d L(2)`, determine the two outer parities in `Sym^2 F`, and, for
   prime fields, contract the pullback back to a nonzero multiple of the
   point-vector cocycle. Steinberg, `L(q-7)`, and small prime-field Fischer
   summands then exclude every sheet with `lambda > 1`.
5. Dickson–Giudici subgroup reduction and order comparison force
   `|K|=(q^2-1)/2`, hence `q=5,7,9,11` and then `K=A_4,S_4,A_5` as applicable.
   The `q=5` matching has an outer stabilizer, `q=9` has no subgroup of order
   40, and the unique block systems at `q=7,11` yield the two survivors.
6. Equivariant Fischer decompositions and one edge-selected alternating
   cycle give quotient ranks `3,6,10`. The modular permutation module on a
   `q`-point sheet proves the square is precisely the equal-sheet-sum
   hyperplane, so its annihilator recovers the sheet sign.
7. A full-support quadratic hyperplane relation forces the cube of the
   evaluation space to be all functions. Hence the signed cubic is nonzero.
   Signed Gale duality gives self-association; maximal isotropy for the signed
   coordinate form gives a perfect degree-1/degree-2 pairing and a
   one-dimensional degree-3 socle, proving Gorensteinness directly.
8. Finally, stabilizer invariants give a fixed affine line. Unique
   factorization and the unique invariant block system select its single
   Chow point, whereas radial translation preserves the exact trade away
   from one coalescence parameter.

## Ranked findings

### 1. Major proof gap: the asserted finite-group Hom basis is not derived

**Location:** Lemma 3.2(i), especially lines 584–657 and equations
(3.2c1)–(3.2c3).

The manuscript correctly distinguishes finite-root equations from an
algebraic highest-weight test and correctly notes the degree-`q-1` aliasing
hazard. But the decisive conclusion is compressed into the assertion that
“Lucas's theorem factors its coefficient matrix into the one-digit
recurrences,” after which every finite-group map is declared to be one of
the displayed digitwise maps. The coefficient matrix is not displayed, its
indices and carry/borrow conventions are not given, and the passage from
the actual target `W tensor W` to the tensor products of the one-digit
socle lines is not proved. In particular, the text does not let a reader
exclude additional simple submodules of `W tensor W` that do not lie in
`soc(W) tensor soc(W)`, nor independently verify that the two aliased torus
weights are coupled with no extra kernel. McDowell–Wildon does not supply
this conclusion.

This is the earliest point I cannot independently justify, and it is
load-bearing for the all-field exclusion. I do not assert that the formula
is false; I find that the human proof presently stops at its essential
calculation.

### 2. Major proof gap: the first-wall/outer-parity vanishing is incomplete

**Location:** Lemma 3.2(ii), lines 666–855, especially the omitted-divided-
power step at lines 776–797 and the Steinberg case at lines 812–816.

The construction from an opposite-parity finite-group map to an algebraic
map out of `S tensor L(1) tensor L(1)^(e)` is inventive and plausible, but
the claim that the Chevalley factorization supplies every omitted divided-
power relation is stated rather than coefficient-checked. More concretely,
the subsequent vanishing for `S=St` is inferred only from the source having
simple head `L(2q-2)` above the top weight of the target. A high simple head
alone does not rule out a homomorphism whose image comes from a lower
subquotient; one must prove the source is generated in the required sense
and that any map is detected on that head/highest generator, or calculate
the relevant Hom space. The later `L(q-7)` argument does perform generator
checks, which highlights what is absent here.

Until this is repaired, the opposite-parity obstruction used in every
extension-field branch is not established by the human paper.

### 3. Major-to-minor proof/citation gap: arbitrary `p'` subgroup exhaustion

**Location:** Lemma 3.3, lines 978–986; compare the later maximal-overgroup
argument in Theorem 3.4.

Giudici's cited theorem classifies maximal subgroups of `PSL_2(q)`. The
paper needs a classification of an arbitrary `p'` matching stabilizer and
says that induction through a proper subfield subgroup simply repeats. In
the square-subfield branch the maximal subgroup is of `PGL_2(q_0)` type,
and an arbitrary `p'` subgroup need not begin inside `PSL_2(q_0)`. The text
does not formulate the induction parameter, treat the index-two part, or
show that no additional extension of an exceptional subgroup occurs. This
may well be a standard corollary of Dickson's full subgroup theorem, but it
is stronger than the cited maximal-subgroup statement as presented.

A precise corollary with proof or an exact citation would likely make this
a minor repair. As written it is part of the claimed exhaustive case split
and therefore affects the headline theorem.

### 4. Minor omission: projective intrinsicity under changing endpoint lifts

**Location:** Section 2, lines 217–229 and Proposition 2.1, especially
lines 247–252.

Reference-matching independence is proved, and `GL_2` transport is tracked.
The manuscript instead declares fixed vector representatives to be part of
the marked data and never explicitly proves independence under rescaling
those representatives. The repair is short: replacing each endpoint lift
`a` by `lambda_a a` multiplies every perfect-matching product, and hence
every quotient difference, by the same scalar `product_a lambda_a`.
Consequently the affine configuration changes by a common homothety and all
rank, trade, sheet, and cubic-line assertions are unchanged (the cubic
itself scales cubically). This should be stated because the abstract and
conclusion call the recognition condition intrinsic and projective.

### 5. Minor citation/exposition issue: the modular bottleneck is too dense

**Location:** the strategy paragraph preceding Lemma 3.2 and the proof of
Lemma 3.2, lines 552–900.

This single lemma simultaneously handles lower/upper symmetric-power
conventions, finite-field torus aliasing, Frobenius digitization, outer
extensions, distribution-algebra relations, tilting socles, and affine
cohomology. Only Hermite reciprocity and Steinberg are cited at the start;
the tilting-socle assertions and distribution-algebra identities used later
have no exact sources. Even after Findings 1–2 are filled, this material
needs to be split into independently checkable lemmas, with precise
citations for standard inputs and a small worked two-digit example. This is
exposition friction and citation debt, not a separate claim of falsity.

## Hypothesis and exceptional-case audit

- **Fields:** odd characteristic is used correctly for the quadratic
  polarization and sheet induction; characteristics 7 and 11 make `6`
  invertible for the cubic. Scalar extension to the algebraic closure is
  logically separated from the final `F_q` statements. The prime-field and
  extension-field branches are visibly separated, and `q=9` is explicitly
  excluded by `p'` and order considerations.
- **Groups and orbits:** the derivation from a two-valued trade to two
  `PSL_2(q)` sheets is sound for `q>3`; `q=3` is separately disposed of.
  The no-unipotent-fixed-matching argument correctly makes `K` a `p'`
  subgroup. The `q=5` outer-normalizer fusion, the `q=7` `S_4` block, and
  the `q=11` `A_5` block are coherently handled. The unresolved issue is the
  general subgroup descent in Finding 3, not the final small-case arithmetic.
- **Equivalence and choices:** changing the reference matching is a
  translation and is handled throughout, including for the cubic. Sheet
  exchange changes only the sign/orientation. Arbitrary rescaling of point
  representatives is harmless but unstated (Finding 4).
- **Projective trade pullback:** Proposition 2.1 is correct: all matching
  products have the same conic restriction, the Veronese kernel is `(Q)`,
  and the four-endpoint switch has the right role. Lemma 3.1's pullback
  dichotomy and cocycle formula are also internally justified.
- **Direct Gorenstein argument:** this portion passes. The signed coordinate
  form makes `L` maximal isotropic; the quotients
  `L/<1>`, `L^2/L`, and `V/L^2` are paired perfectly; `L^3=V` gives the
  one-dimensional top degree. Nonvanishing of the homogenizing coordinate
  gives regularity, and degree-three polarization is legitimate in
  characteristics 7 and 11. The PRS criterion is correctly presented as
  context rather than as the proof.

I found no false numerical statement or mishandled `q=5,7,9,11` endpoint in
the portions I could check. Findings 1–3 are proof gaps or citation gaps;
Finding 4 is an intrinsicity omission; Finding 5 is exposition/citation
friction.

## Novelty and priority

Relative to the supplied literature extracts, the genuinely new statement
is: **inside the full projective perfect-matching carrier, an exact
two-valued quadratic trade recognizes precisely the two classical
`B_3/F_7` and `H_3/F_11` one-factorization sheets, while the fixed-line/Chow
theorem identifies the exact boundary of that recognition and the first
signed cubic orients the recovered pair.**

The exceptional one-factorizations, their principal automorphism groups,
and the general self-dual-code/Schur-square/Gorenstein mechanism are not new,
and the manuscript now says so clearly. The conic-ideal quotient,
all-field modular exclusion, off-carrier fixed-line ambiguity with unique
Chow selection, and configuration-specific orienting cubic are the apparent
contributions. This is a substantial JCTA-level package if the modular
exclusion is made human-verifiable. I did not perform a fresh literature
search, so this is a priority assessment relative to extracts G2, K1, W1,
W2, S1, and S2, not an independent absence-of-prior-art certification.
