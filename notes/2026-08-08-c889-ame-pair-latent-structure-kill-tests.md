## Phase 1 proofs

### Inter-code LU equivalence

Let $C,D\leq\mathbb F_q^{2m}$ be linear `[2m,m,m+1]` MDS codes and
write a product-Clifford transporter as

\[
 F_i=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_i&\beta_i\\\gamma_i&\delta_i\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Mapping $L_C=C_X\oplus C_Z^\perp$ to
$L_D=D_X\oplus D_Z^\perp$ gives the four exact lines

\[
 D_\alpha C\subseteq D,\quad D_\gamma C\subseteq D^\perp,
 \quad D_\beta C^\perp\subseteq D,\quad
 D_\delta C^\perp\subseteq D^\perp.                 \tag{C889.1}
\]

C631 proves that every nonzero member of any one of these multiplier
spaces has full support, gives equality, and spans the whole space.  If
$D_\alpha\ne0$, then $C$ and $D$ are diagonally equivalent.  If
$D_\alpha=0$, the coordinatewise determinant equations
$\alpha_i\delta_i-\beta_i\gamma_i=1$ force both
$D_\beta,D_\gamma\ne0$, hence $C$ is diagonally equivalent to
$D^\perp$.  This proves necessity; LU-to-LC rigidity supplies (C889.1)
from the original LU transporter.

Conversely, if $D=AC$ for a nonsingular diagonal $A$, the blocks
$\operatorname{diag}(a_i,a_i^{-1})$ map $L_C$ to $L_D$.  If
$D=AC^\perp$, the blocks

\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0&a_i\\-a_i^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}
\]

do so.  Product-Pauli character correction promotes label preservation to
state-ray equivalence.  This proves the converse.

There is no hidden mixed-orientation exception.  A direct line automatically
supplies its dual direct line, and an off-diagonal line supplies its paired
off-diagonal line.  Both orientations coexist exactly on the diagonally
isodual stratum; after diagonal frame changes, the four scalar coefficients
satisfy one ordinary $2\times2$ determinant-one equation.  Thus the mixed
case enlarges the transporter family but does not create a third orbit
relation.

With party motion, apply the fixed-party statement to $C$ and the permuted
target code.  Hence

\[
 \Pi_C=\{\pi:\pi C\text{ is diagonally equivalent to }C
                  \text{ or }C^\perp\}.              \tag{C889.2}
\]

The exact cross-check enumerates all 720 permutations in each C624 case and
computes both multiplier nullities.  It matches the stored party image in all
twelve cases.  The seven non-GRS/H3 rows split evenly within each row into direct
and dual orientations; every GRS permutation admits both orientations.  This
determines only $\Pi_C$.  C624's factor sets and complements remain separate
evidence for splitting.

### Logical rounding

At C838's radius, write the recovered exact symmetry as
$W_vK$, with $K=\bigotimes_iK_i$ and
$d_2(U_i,K_i)\leq8\varepsilon$.  Choose party $i$ as the input of the
punctured encoder.  The projection of a minimum-support stabilizer subgroup
onto the $i$-th Weyl plane is surjective, so choose a stabilizer label
$\ell$ with $\ell_i=-v_i$.  Multiplying by its stabilizer lift changes the
input factor of $W_vK$ projectively to $K_i$ while preserving an exact
product-Clifford state symmetry.  The other factors therefore implement the
exact logical Clifford

\[
 L_i'=(K_i^{\mathsf T})^{-1}.
\]

The Choi identity gives the intended logical action as the same
transpose--inverse transform of $U_i$.  Transpose and inverse preserve the
phase-optimized normalized Hilbert--Schmidt metric, proving
$d_2(L_i,L_i')\leq8\varepsilon$.  This does not bound the physical Pauli
correction away from the input and therefore does not contradict C838's
global obstruction.

### High-distance multiplier lemma

Let $s\ne0$ satisfy $\operatorname{diag}(s)E\subseteq F$, and let $S$
be its coordinate support.  A nonzero image word lies in $F$ and is
supported on $S$, so $|S|\geq d(F)$.  The kernel on $E$ is supported on
$S^c$, whose size satisfies

\[
 |S^c|\leq n-d(F)<d(E).
\]

Thus the restricted diagonal map is injective.  Equal dimensions make it
surjective.  Since $F$ has full coordinate support, surjectivity forbids a
zero coordinate of $s$.  Two independent multipliers would have a nonzero
linear combination vanishing at a chosen coordinate, a contradiction.  Hence
the multiplier space has dimension at most one.

The strict inequality is sharp.  At equality take

\[
 E=F=\{(a,a,b,b):a,b\in\mathbb F_q\}\leq\mathbb F_q^4.
\]

Both distances are two, both codes have full coordinate support, and the
multiplier space consists of vectors $(r,r,s,s)$, of dimension two.

This multiplier space is the standard Schur-product adjoint, often called a
code conductor.  Couvreur--M\'arquez-Corbella--Pellikaan, arXiv:1401.6025,
Lemma 4, identify it with $(E*F^\perp)^\perp$.  **Read depth:** partial,
Section 2 through Lemma 4 and its proof, preprint version; cache key
`arXiv:1401.6025`, SHA-256
`88f7907907d16c94219dcee46a7d1557b30b1e6935e7b36d51e7ba5ce8674db6`.

## Phase 2: prime-field holonomy classification

### Atlas and logical image

C649's transition multigraph is already the proposed Weyl-frame groupoid.
Fixing a base party turns a compatible gauge family into one base block; path
independence is exactly commutation with every loop transport.  Conversely, a
base block centralizing the holonomy propagates consistently, preserves every
minimum-support subgroup, and therefore preserves the full stabilizer label
group by minimum-support generation.  Product-Pauli correction removes the
remaining character.  Thus gauge equivalence of atlas representations is
exactly the existing atlas equivalence; no ad hoc full-stabilizer relation is
needed.

For prime $p$, evaluation at the chosen input identifies the linear logical
image with $C_{\mathrm{SL}_2(p)}(H_\psi)$.  Surjectivity of the state
stabilizer onto the input Weyl plane supplies every logical Pauli translation,
and character correction supplies every compatible linear block.  Therefore

\[
 T_\psi=\mathbb F_p^2\rtimes
 C_{\mathrm{SL}_2(p)}(H_\psi)                       \tag{C889.3}
\]

as a projective logical group.  The semidirect product is projective; it does
not assert a unitary section of the Heisenberg scalar extension.

### Five centralizer types

Let $H\subseteq\mathrm{SL}_2(p)$.  If every element is central, its
centralizer is the full group.  Otherwise choose noncentral $h\in H$.
Its characteristic polynomial has one of three types.

- Two distinct eigenvalues in $\mathbb F_p$: $C(h)$ is a split torus.
- Irreducible characteristic polynomial: $C(h)$ is a nonsplit torus.
- Repeated eigenvalue $\pm1$: $C(h)$ is the signed unipotent group
  $\{\pm\begin{psmallmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{psmallmatrix}\}$, after conjugacy.

Each of these three centralizers is abelian.  If every element of $H$
commutes with $h$, the common centralizer remains $C(h)$; otherwise its
intersection with the centralizer of a noncommuting element is the center.
Hence the only types are

\[
 \mathrm{SL}_2(p),\quad T_{\rm split},\quad T_{\rm nonsplit},\quad
 U_{\pm},\quad Z.
\]

For $p=2$, the center and split torus collapse to the identity, leaving
$S_3,C_3,C_2,1$.  For $p=3$, the split torus equals the center and cannot
occur as a distinct noncentral type.  All five types occur for $p\geq5$.

The exact falsifier enumerates every ordered pair in
$\mathrm{SL}_2(p)^2$ for $p=3,5,7$: 576, 14,400, and 112,896 pairs.
Direct brute-force centralizers agree in every case with the independent
discriminant/commutation classifier.  One noncentral element and, if needed,
one noncommuting second element determine every common centralizer, so pairs
also cover arbitrary tuples.

In the MDS--CSS family the common fixed-party torus is always present.  For
For $p>3$, any holonomy centralizer containing that split torus is therefore
either the split torus or all of $\mathrm{SL}_2(p)$; C622 identifies the
second case exactly with diagonal isoduality.  At $p=3$, the nonisodual
split-torus branch is the center.  This explains why the MDS--CSS theorem sees
only its two existing phases.
