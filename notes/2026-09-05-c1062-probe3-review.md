### 2.3 The prediction that the certificate compacts with model size is refuted, and there is a structural reason it cannot — defect

The report's defence of the missed threshold: "A search space of 55 is far too small for a
class-based negative to compress: the class count is bounded by the quotient size, which is a fixed
property of the model, while the candidate count grows combinatorially in the pool size and the size
bound. **The prediction that follows is that the fraction improves sharply with model size, and it
is currently untested.**"

The premise conflates two axes. Growing the *size bound* on a fixed model does grow the candidate
count against a fixed quotient. Growing the *model* grows the quotient too, so nothing is gained.
The report predicts improvement on the axis where the argument does not apply.

Measured, in the independent Python, on queries with a genuine exhaustion obligation — a voter on
the losing side of a bare-majority vote, who is not a cause, so the search must exhaust — with the
class oracle enabled and the arity set to the size bound:

| query | pool | size bound | candidates | pruned | records | distinct classes | verifier fraction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| losing voter, 5 voters | 5 | 2 | 15 | 2 | 12 | 8 | 70.59% |
| losing voter, 7 voters | 7 | 2 | 21 | 4 | 17 | 11 | 68.00% |
| losing voter, 9 voters | 9 | 2 | 27 | 6 | 22 | 14 | 66.67% |
| losing voter, 11 voters | 11 | 2 | 33 | 8 | 27 | 17 | 65.85% |
| losing voter, 5 voters | 5 | 3 | 101 | 0 | 74 | 37 | 73.27% |
| losing voter, 7 voters | 7 | 3 | 225 | 10 | 169 | 82 | 71.91% |
| losing voter, 9 voters | 9 | 3 | 395 | 30 | 300 | 143 | 70.59% |
| losing voter, 5 voters | 5 | 4 | 285 | 0 | 193 | 71 | 67.72% |
| losing voter, 7 voters | 7 | 4 | 1,155 | 0 | 823 | 280 | 71.26% |
| rock-throwing billy | 4 | 3 | 40 | 15 | 32 | 16 | 58.18% |
| rock-throwing billy | 4 | 4 | 48 | 53 | 36 | 17 | 35.64% |

The fraction is flat at roughly seventy per cent across a search space growing from 15 candidates to
1,155, on both axes, and pruning falls to **zero** in three of the larger rows. The one row that
improves is `rock-throwing-billy` at a larger size bound, `58.18%` to `35.64%`, still three and a
half times the predeclared ten per cent.

The mechanism is structural and it settles the question rather than deferring it.
`CompiledObservation` carries one `SortRange` per sort in `class_ranges`, so a class never spans
sorts. A candidate's pinned support is the cause variables plus the witness variables, and that
support *is* the sort. Two candidates with different witness sets therefore live in different sorts
and can never be in the same class, so `seen_classes` — which is memoised per cause — can only ever
collide across different *settings* of one cause with one witness set. The number of settings of a
cause of size `c` over domains of size `d` is `d^c − 1`, which bounds the compaction factor. For the
dominant layer, singleton causes over binary variables, that factor is `1`: no compaction is
possible at all. Meanwhile the candidate count is dominated by the witness-set choice,
`C(|pool| − c, |W|)`, which is exactly the axis pruning cannot touch. That is why the fraction is
invariant to the number of voters.

What would settle it, stated precisely: the certificate can only reach ten per cent when candidates
whose cause is large or whose domains are wide dominate the count *and* their settings collide in
class — concretely, `d^c − 1 ≥ 10` with the collisions actually realized, so binary variables need
causes of size four or more to carry the count, and a domain of size twenty makes a singleton cause
enough. Probe 7's thresholded sources over domain twenty are the natural test bed, and they are
inside the flat envelope: my probe 1 review found the real per-state cost is `27` bytes rather than
probe 1a's `164`, so the carrier ceiling is about `7e7` states rather than `1.2e7`. The measurement
probe 3 wants is a wide-domain fixture, not a many-variable one, and it does not need probe 7 to
exist.

### 2.4 Four of the eight rows cite a source that publishes the shape and not the number — defect

The report's table is headed `Published | Computed | Shape found | Source`, and every row carries a
source. Checking against the sources themselves:

**Rows 1–4, the forest fire, cited to "Halpern IJCAI 2015 Ex. 3.1".** I read Example 3.1 verbatim
from the cached paper. It publishes the *verdicts*: in the conjunctive scenario "each of `L = 1` and
`MD = 1` is a but-for cause of `FF = 1`", and in the disjunctive scenario "According to the modified
definition `L = 1 ∧ MD = 1` is a cause of `FF = 1` … they are both part of a cause, but not
causes." It publishes no degree of responsibility. Searching the whole paper for
`degree of responsibility|responsibility of` returns exactly one hit, a citation of Chockler–Halpern
2004 in an unrelated section. So `dr = 1` and `dr = 1/2` are consequences of applying the
responsibility formula to the published shapes, not published values.

Probe 0 knew this and said so. Its § Q3.3 second table annotates both forest-fire rows "**my
computation from Definition 2.5, not a published number**", while an earlier table in the same
section attributes the same values to "Halpern 2015 § 5". Probe 3 inherited the weaker of probe 0's
two attributions and wrote "Published" over it.

**Rows 5–6, rock throwing, cited to "Ibrahim diss. § 2.4".** The dissertation is not in the cache and
I did not read it. Probe 0 quotes it verbatim — "the responsibility of `ST = 1` is `1/2`, because we
had `W = {BH}`, and the responsibility of `BT = 1` is 0" — and Halpern IJCAI 2015 Example 3.2, which
I did read, independently publishes the shape the value rests on: "to show that `ST = 1` is cause,
we take `W = {BH}` and `w = 0`; that is, we hold `BH` at its actual value of 0", and `BT = 1` is not
a cause. The fixture matches both. One caveat probe 0 recorded and probe 3 dropped: probe 0's first
table notes Halpern makes Suzy's degree depend on which settings the extended model allows, `1` or
`1/2`. The fixture is the `1/2` model, which is the right one, but the report presents `1/2` as
unconditional.

**Rows 7–8, the voting cases.** I read Chockler–Halpern § 1 verbatim: "in the case of the 11–0 vote,
the degree of responsibility of any voter for the victory is 1/6, since 5 changes have to be made
before a vote is critical … if the vote is 5–4, then the degree of responsibility of each voter for
Mr. B for Mr. B's victory is 1; each voter is critical." Both values are correct, and both come from
the same paragraph of the same paper — while the report cites Ibrahim's dissertation for the first
and Chockler–Halpern for the second.

**The deeper point, which applies to the whole table.** Chockler–Halpern's `1/6` for the 11–0 vote
is `1/(k+1)` with `k = 5`, the five other voters *set away from their actual values*; the shape is
`X = {one voter}`, `|W| = 5`. The engine's `1/6` is `1/(|X| + |W|)` with `|X| = 6`, `|W| = 0` — six
voters flipped together under the modified definition. Same number, different object, and the
report's own "Shape found" column records the difference without remarking on it. The same
coincidence holds for the disjunctive forest fire (`1/(1+1)` under the original definition with
`MD` set to 0, against `1/(2+0)` under the modified one) and for rock throwing (`W = {BT}` set to 0,
against `W = {BH}` held at actual). **Every value in the table is one that the original and the
modified definitions both produce.** What discriminates the modified definition is the *shape* — the
disjunctive cause being `|X| = 2` and the rock-throwing witness being `{BH}` at its actual value —
and the report does call those two out as the ones a weaker implementation gets wrong, which is the
right instinct. But the table sells eight published-value reproductions, and what it delivers is two
shape checks plus six definition-agnostic numbers.

### 2.5 The `classes` column counts records, not classes — defect, and it makes the number worse than it needs to be

`certificate.classes` is a `Vec<ExhaustionClass>` and `verifier_fraction` divides its `len()` by the
candidate total. `seen_classes` is created fresh inside the per-cause closure, so the same class
identifier is pushed again under every cause that reaches it. On `rock-throwing-billy` the 32
entries cover **16 distinct classes**, which I measured independently. The report's sentence
"recorded 32 classes" and the column header "classes" both read as a count of classes.

The distinction is not cosmetic. If the certificate is a list of obligations a verifier replays, 32
is the verifier's work and the fraction is `58.18%`. If it is a list of classes whose membership
covers every candidate, 16 is the object and the fraction is `29.09%` — still short of ten per cent,
but half the reported figure, and it changes what the certificate *is*. Deduplicating across causes
is not obviously sound, because the coverage argument is per-cause (the `ClassOracle` docstring says
pruning is sound only among witness sets disjoint from the same cause), so this is a design question
the report should have posed rather than a number to swap. Posing it is worth doing: on the larger
rows in § 2.3 the record-to-class ratio runs about two to three, so the answer is worth a factor of
two to three on the probe's headline measure.

### 2.6 The certificate silently omits any candidate the oracle cannot classify — latent defect

In `responsibility`, the class is `oracle.and_then(|oracle| class_after(...))`, and a surviving
candidate is recorded only inside `if let Some(class) = class`. Two ways to get `None`: no oracle was
supplied at all, or `class_of` returned `None` because the combined support is outside the
lowering's arity bound. In both cases `candidates_searched` is incremented, the candidate is
evaluated, the outcome survives — and nothing is written to the certificate.

The consequences are two. An oracle-free run reports `verifier_fraction() = 0.0`, indistinguishable
from the two legitimate `0.00%` rows the report correctly flags as "not passes"; the API cannot tell
"no negative to certify" from "no certificate was built". And a run whose size bound exceeds the
lowering's arity produces a certificate that does not cover every candidate, so a verifier replaying
it would certify less than the search established.

Neither bites in the shipped measurement: every row passes an oracle, and `maximum` equals the
model's arity in all five (`3` for the forest fires and rock throwing, `4` for `voting-5-4` after
`with_arity(4)`). It is a trap set for the first caller who raises `maximum` without raising the
arity, and it fails open rather than closed.

### 2.7 Probe 1's `class_of` context-bound defect is reachable from here — carried-in defect, latent

`class_after` calls `oracle.lowering.class_of(oracle.compiled, context, &pins)` with a
caller-supplied `context`. `class_of` validates the pin support, rejects duplicate pins and
range-checks pinned values, but never checks `context` against the sort's context count; it computes
`sort_start + context * pin_states + pin_index` and bounds only against the global state array, so
for any sort but the last an out-of-range context returns a later sort's class rather than `None`.

Unlike probe 4, which never touches `class_of`, probe 3 is one of the two consumers. The measurement
is safe — `0b11 < 4` for the forest fires and rock throwing, `0b1_1111_0000 = 496 < 512` for
`voting-5-4` — but a wrong class here does not fail loudly: it prunes a candidate that should have
been searched, which can turn a cause into a non-cause and silently move a degree of responsibility
to zero. That makes probe 3 the module where the three-line fix proposed in my probe 1 review
matters most.

### 2.8 The AC3 witness search is truncated by the caller's size bound — latent defect, correctly stated nowhere

`satisfies_minimality` searches strict nonempty subsets of the cause exhaustively — I verified the
subset enumerator visits every subset of each size including the empty witness exactly once, and my
independent implementation with the same bound reproduces every verdict. But its witness loop is
`for witness_size in 0..=maximum_size.saturating_sub(subset_size)`, so AC3's own witness search is
capped by the *caller's* `maximum_size`, not by anything intrinsic.

The direction of the error is the dangerous one. If a strict subset of the cause would satisfy AC2
with a witness larger than the cap, AC3 misses it, the engine declares a non-minimal cause minimal,
and the reported degree of responsibility is **too small** — it reports `1/(|X| + |W|)` for a cause
that should have been rejected in favour of a smaller one, which would have given a larger degree.
Neither the docstring nor the report states that `maximum_size` must be large enough for AC3 to be
sound, and there is no assertion. On the shipped fixtures it is fine: for `voting-11-0` the found
cause is `|X| = 6` with `maximum = 6`, so subsets of size `k` get witnesses up to `6 − k`, and no
subset of five voters flipped with one voter held can drop eleven ones below a majority of six, so
no strict subset satisfies AC2 and the verdict is right.

### 2.9 Both lane defects are fixed, and one of them is a live demonstration of the probe 1 oracle problem — no issue on the fix, and a finding about the oracle

Both fixes are in the tree and correct.

**The transition range check.** `CausalModel::lower` verifies, before building the presentation, that
every generator's transitions land inside `[sort_start, sort_start + sort_len)` of its declared
target sort, returning a typed `LoweringRange` naming the generator, the target and the sort bounds.

**Support sets as ids rather than positions.** At `b49ebb3` the signature was
`enumerate_supports(variables: u32, arity: u32)` generating `start..variables`, and all three call
sites passed `intervenable.len()`. At `256959b` it is
`enumerate_supports(intervenable: &[u32], arity: u32)`, iterating `intervenable` and taking the
resumption point by `position`, with a docstring that names the trap. Correct.

The finding is about what the bug says regarding probe 1's gate 2. The three pre-fix call sites were
`carrier_cost`, `lower` and **`signature_classes`** — the "independent direct-enumeration oracle".
The lowering and its oracle shared the defective routine identically, so for the thirty-seven
minutes between `b49ebb3` and `925f19c`, probe 1's gate ("all seven fixtures agree") passed while
both sides were wrong for any model whose intervenable set is not `0..n`. The report's own remark is
exactly right and deserves to be carried forward as a general conclusion rather than an anecdote:
"Found by reading rather than by a failing test, which is worth noting: the existing fixtures could
not have caught it." Neither could the oracle, and that is the concrete instance of the
shared-code weakness my probe 1 review argued from structure. The current fix does not disturb the
enumeration order, so sort 0 remains the empty support and nothing in probe 1's or probe 5's use of
the routine changes.

### 2.10 The responsibility formula and the trap guard are right — no issue

`ResponsibilityReport::degree` returns `(1, cause.len() + witness.len())`, or `(0, 1)` when the
literal is in no cause. The minimisation is joint and it really is over the right set:
`for_each_subset_containing` forces the queried variable into every cause it enumerates, the cause is
recorded as `(variable, actual[variable])` so the queried *literal* rather than just the variable is
a conjunct, the outer loop runs `size = |X| + |W|` ascending, and the inner loop over `cause_size`
returns on the first success — so the first cause found minimises `|X| + |W|` jointly, not `|X|` then
`|W|`. Ties inside a size are irrelevant because they share a denominator.

The `1/(k+1)` trap is real and I confirmed it numerically. Under the modified definition the witness
is pinned to `actual[variable]` by construction, so the number of witness variables differing from
the actual context is zero on every candidate. Transplanting Chockler–Halpern's `1/(k+1)` therefore
yields `1/1` on both fixtures whose correct answer is `1/2` — rock throwing (`|X| = 1`, `|W| = 1`)
and the disjunctive forest fire (`|X| = 2`, `|W| = 0`). The guard tests do guard: they assert
`report.degree()` equals `(1, 2)`, so a `1/(k+1)` implementation breaks them. It is a regression
guard on a hard-coded formula rather than an independent derivation, which is what a guard is.

### 2.11 The search's layering and AC3 are as described — no issue

Verified by reading and reproduced by an independent implementation: the layering makes the first
success minimal (§ 2.10); `for_each_setting` enumerates every setting of the cause differing from
the actual context in at least one coordinate, which is the standard `x' ≠ x`; a setting that moves
only part of the cause cannot produce a spurious minimal cause, because AC3's subset search draws
witnesses from `pool \ subset`, which includes the untouched cause variables, so the smaller cause
plus those variables as witness is found and the larger cause is rejected; `for_each_subset` visits
the empty set exactly once when `size = 0` and returns without visiting when `size > pool.len()`;
and `satisfies_minimality` short-circuits correctly on a singleton cause, where there is no strict
nonempty subset.

Class pruning is sound as used, for a reason the code does not check. Two candidates in the same
class have the same observation, so `outcome.holds` agrees on them **provided every outcome variable
is observed** — which holds in all three fixtures, where `observed` is exactly the outcome variable.
Nothing validates it. This is the same shape as the `outcome_is_observed` condition probe 4 checks
and its report omits, and here it is not checked at all. The other half of soundness is free:
`satisfies_minimality` reads only `record.cause` and ignores the witness and setting, so two
candidates sharing a cause share an AC3 verdict.

### 2.12 The amortization half is still unmeasured, and it is now cheap — confirmed

The report says the timing arm "lives in the blocked tools binary" and was never reached, and the
exploration log carries "Probe 3's amortization arm was never measured" forward. Still true:
`tasks/tools/src/actual_cause_report.rs` contains no timing, only two `SplitTranscript` compiles and
the same tables. The foreign obstruction is gone — the `ergodis-tools` binary builds and runs at
`256959b`, as my probe 1 and probe 4 replays demonstrate — so the reason the arm was not measured no
longer exists.

Measuring it costs one flag on the existing subcommand: compile once, run the responsibility query
for every literal in the pool, and compare total wall time against the same queries with
`oracle: None`. The models are milliseconds. The one design question is what "amortization" means
here, since the compile is paid once and the query is cheap; the meaningful comparison is compile
plus `n` queries against `n` oracle-free queries, and the break-even `n` is the number to report, in
the C1061 style the process rules ask for.

### 2.13 No evidence retained on disk — defect

`ergodis-private/evidence/` holds no probe 3 artifact. The five-row certificate table and the
five-row policy table exist only as `--nocapture` terminal output. The certificate table reproduces
exactly at `256959b`; the policy table does not reproduce at all (§ 2.1), and a retained evidence

the third probe in a row with no retained evidence — probe 1 and probe 4 have none either — so it is
a lane-level gap rather than a lapse in one report.

Predeclaration, by contrast, is the cleanest of the three probes I have reviewed. Probe 0's audit,

## 4. What was checked and found sound

  disjunctive `1/2` at `|X| = 2, |W| = 0` for both; rock throwing Suzy `1/2` at `|X| = 1` with

- **Two of those verdicts are confirmed against the primary sources I read directly.** Halpern IJCAI
  2015 Example 3.1 publishes the conjunctive but-for causes and the disjunctive

**Verdict.** Probe 3's engine is correct and its correctness case survives independent
re-derivation — every verdict, every shape, and every cell of the certificate table, from an
implementation sharing no code with the Rust, including a class oracle built from the carrier
congruence that agrees with the compiler exactly. The responsibility formula is right, the trap
probe 0 flagged is real and guarded, and the search's layering delivers the joint minimality it
claims. What fails is the surrounding apparatus. The test named as the regression witness for the
core certificate-policy defect was rewritten to compare `SplitTranscript` against itself in the same
commit that recorded the defect, so the published five-row table cannot be reproduced from the
committed code; the diagnosis attached to that table picks the wrong one of two explanations its own
state column distinguishes, and the right one — the core's 4,096-state multiway admission gate — was
later found independently. The excuse offered for the missed compactness threshold is refuted rather
than merely untested: the verifier fraction is flat at about seventy per cent as the model grows,
and it cannot be otherwise, because compiled classes never span sorts and the witness set determines
the sort, so the combinatorial axis is exactly the axis class pruning cannot compress. The one lever
that could still reach ten per cent is domain width rather than model width, and that fixture is
cheap. Finally, four of the eight verdict rows cite a paper that publishes the cause shape and no
degree at all, which probe 0's audit had already flagged and probe 3 overwrote.
