## 1. What the selected simplex says intrinsically

Let \(\Omega=\{w_0,\ldots,w_r\}\) be the selected Galois-stable projective
weight set. Its affine transformation law includes a common translation.
Let (E/k) be the associated degree-((r+1)) étale algebra. Then

\[
A_\Omega=\ker(\mathbf Z[\Omega]\xrightarrow{\sum}\mathbf Z),\qquad
A_\Omega\longrightarrow X^*(T),\quad (n_i)\longmapsto\sum n_iw_i
\]

is Galois equivariant: the common translation is multiplied by \(\sum n_i=0\).
The differences (w_j-w_0) form an integral basis exactly when this map is
an isomorphism. Since (A_\Omega) is the character lattice of
\(\operatorname{Res}_{E/k}\mathbf G_m/\mathbf G_m\), the conclusion is

\[
T\simeq\operatorname{Res}_{E/k}\mathbf G_m/\mathbf G_m.
\]

Its variety is the norm-nonzero open of \(\mathbf P(E)\), hence rational.
This identifies a particular integral lattice type, not every rational torus.
Rationality of this acting torus does not prove rationality of the quotient;
the tangent-section theorem remains essential.

If the difference lattice instead has finite index (d), its inclusion into
(X^*(T)), read contravariantly, gives

\[
T\longrightarrow\operatorname{Res}_{E/k}\mathbf G_m/\mathbf G_m
\]

with finite kernel of order (d). The direction of this isogeny is important.
It does not turn a degree-dividing-(d) parametrization into one of degree
exactly (d); the existing finite-index theorem retains the right bound.

For the four selected weights in the paper,

\[
(011),(101),(110),(111),\qquad
D=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&1\\-1&0&0\\0&-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\det D=1.
\]

The two affine permutations are ((03)(12)) and ((23)), indexing weights
from zero. The checker verifies the augmentation-lattice intertwiner for
**every element** of the full order-24 input group, not only its generators.

## 2. Residual character lattice and the two kinds of norm torus

Use the manuscript's rank-five character basis. The selected cocharacter
lattice is generated by (e_3,e_4,e_5) (indices here start at one).
The residual **character** lattice, its annihilator, has basis (e_1^*,e_2^*).
Column coordinate vectors transform by

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

They permute

\[
v_0=(0,1),\quad v_1=(1,-1),\quad v_2=(-1,0).
\]

These vectors generate the integral lattice, and their only integral
relation is (v_0+v_1+v_2=0). Thus the residual character lattice is
\(\mathbf Z^3/\mathbf Z(1,1,1)\), the character lattice of a cubic norm-one
torus. Writing (E_3) for the corresponding cubic étale algebra gives

\[
T_0/T_3\simeq\operatorname{Res}^{1}_{E_3/k}\mathbf G_m.
\]

Together the identifications give an exact sequence

\[
1\longrightarrow\operatorname{Res}_{E_4/k}\mathbf G_m/\mathbf G_m
\longrightarrow T_0\longrightarrow
\operatorname{Res}^{1}_{E_3/k}\mathbf G_m\longrightarrow1.
\]

This sequence is not asserted to split. The selected torus uses an
augmentation **character** lattice; the residual torus uses a quotient
character lattice. Its **cocharacter** lattice is the augmentation lattice.
Confusing those dual conventions is precisely the invalid naive
projectivization shortcut already identified in C958.

The manuscript generator computes `residual_actions` but omits them from
its returned `type_i3_lattice_data()` dictionary. The feedback is accurate.
A later implementation should retain and print them with the above convention
and update the existing certificate schema through its ordinary gate. This
audit does not mutate the released certificate.

## 3. Actual orbit correction and descent boundary

With the paper's signed cofactors \(\kappa_j=(-1)^j\det A_{\widehat j}\),
the three difference-character equations are

\[
\frac{t_1}{t_2}=\frac{\kappa_1}{\kappa_0},\qquad
\frac{t_1}{t_3}=\frac{\kappa_2}{\kappa_0},\qquad
t_1=\frac{\kappa_3}{\kappa_0}.
\]

Consequently, on the open where every cofactor is nonzero,

\[
(t_1,t_2,t_3)=
\left(\frac{\kappa_3}{\kappa_0},
\frac{\kappa_3}{\kappa_1},
\frac{\kappa_3}{\kappa_2}\right).
\]

There is no root extraction. These are splitting-field coordinates of the
correction; the retraction (q\mapsto t(q)q) descends by uniqueness of the
orbit intersection with the ground-defined slice. This requires the descended
slice and weight-space sums already present in the theorem. It does not
assert that four individually chosen weight spaces descend.

The supplied checker proves the ratio identity symbolically. The independent
checker additionally starts with an integer (3\times4) evaluation matrix,
computes its signed minors, and verifies the corrected weight scalars lie in
its kernel. The sample is a check of conventions, not the proof of the
geometric slice or of a uniformly nonempty tangent open.

## 4. Exact linearization levels

Let (k) have characteristic zero and let (X/k) be one of the cubic
examples with **proved** exact stabilization level two from the two papers.
Put (K=k(X)). A rationalization identifies

\[
L=K(u,v)\simeq k(t_1,\ldots,t_5).
\]

Transport the action of the split torus (T=\mathbf G_m^2) that fixes (K)
and scales (u,v). It is a faithful rational action on \(\mathbf A^5\).
No regular polynomial action on all of affine space is asserted.

After adjoining (m) fixed variables, its invariant field is

\[
L(s_1,\ldots,s_m)^T=K(s_1,\ldots,s_m).
\]

A linear split-torus representation has a rational invariant field: diagonalize
it and choose an integral basis for the kernel of its weight map. Therefore
linearization forces (m\ge2). At (m=2) the invariant field is rational by
the cubic upper bound; its generators together with (u,v) give coordinates
in which the action is diagonal. The least number of trivial variables is
exactly two.

For a primitive rank-one subtorus (t\mapsto(t^a,t^b)), choose integers
(c,d) with (ac+bd=1). The monomial coordinates

\[
w=u^bv^{-a},\qquad h=u^cv^d
\]

form a field basis over (K), since the exponent determinant is one.
The subtorus fixes (w) and has weight one on (h). Its invariant field is
(K(w)), which has exact stabilization level one. Hence **every** such
restriction is nonlinearizable and needs exactly one added trivial variable.
The checker verifies the Bézout basis for the 176 primitive pairs in
([-8,8]^2); the proof applies to all primitive pairs.

Over \(\mathbf C\), for any finite subgroup (F\subset T), the annihilator
of its characters is a finite-index rank-two sublattice of \(\mathbf Z^2\).
Its monomials give

\[
L^F=K(r_1,r_2),
\]

which is rational by the two-variable upper bound. Thus the rational
fivefold has rational finite-subgroup quotients and nonrational quotients
by every positive-dimensional connected subtorus. All quotients here are
understood through their invariant function fields.

### General form and attribution

If a geometrically integral (Y/k) has finite exact stabilization level (s),
let \(\mathbf G_m^s\) scale the added variables on the rational variety
birational to (Y\times\mathbf A^s). Restriction to a split rank-(r)
subtorus has invariant field

\[
k(Y)(z_1,\ldots,z_{s-r}),
\]

whose exact stabilization level is (r). Indeed, (m) further variables
rationalize it exactly when (s-r+m\ge s). The same diagonal-coordinate
argument gives exact trivial-stabilization linearization level (r).

Popov, arXiv:1110.2410v4, Section 1 defines the inclusions of Cremona groups
by adjoining fixed coordinates. Theorem 4 is the invariant-field criterion;
Lemma 5 treats diagonalizable linear actions. Theorem 5 and Corollary 5
already construct nonlinearizable rank-two torus actions on \(\mathbf A^5\).
Theorem 6 bounds the number of trivial variables by the torus rank. The
contribution of the present deduction is the **exact** bound supplied by the
cubic lower theorem, including every rank-one restriction. The source works
over algebraically closed characteristic-zero fields; the split-coordinate
proof above establishes the stated characteristic-zero extension directly.
No exhaustive claim that these quantitative corollaries are new is made.

## 6. Explicit ej + tt closeout and Mystery ledger

After the main acceptance checks, examine the relation between the quartic
and cubic étale algebras, rather than treating them as unrelated names.

- **Settled: their Galois actions share a quadratic quotient.** For full type
  (I_3), the selected four-point image has order 8, the residual three-point
  image has order 6, and the combined image has order 24. The selected action
  preserves the block system \(\{0,1\}\mid\{2,3\}\). Its permutation of
  these two blocks equals the sign of the residual three-point permutation.
  The checker proves this for all 24 elements. Thus the quartic's quadratic
  subalgebra from this block system agrees with the discriminant quadratic
  algebra of (E_3). The full group is the corresponding fiber product
  of its order-8 image with (S_3) over this (C_2) quotient.
- **Settled: (E_3) is not the ordinary cubic resolvent algebra of (E_4)
  in full type (I_3).** The action on the quartic's three pair partitions
  has image of order 2, whereas the residual image has order 6. The shared
  quadratic relation does not justify a resolvent identification.
- **Settled: exact levels require no new linearization machinery.** The
  monomial invariant-field proof yields all ranks once an exact finite
  stabilization level is available. The geometric burden remains in that
  stabilization theorem.
- **Settled: residual parametrization was already owned.** C958 has the
  chart; the new editorial value is to expose the integral lattice and use
  the chart under its existing field/open conditions.
- **Open: final maps and executable descent.** C958 retains the precise
  unresolved gates in its live card. This audit does not remove them.
- **Open: novelty and the family/moduli assertions.** Owner C1128. The
  positive Popov/LPR checks do not constitute a forward-citation audit or
  validate the proposed three-dimensional family.

No unresolved finite lattice or sign discrepancy remains. The supplied JSON
stores SymPy matrix integers as strings; the independent checker explicitly
normalizes these entries before comparison. This is a serialization issue,
not a mathematical discrepancy.
