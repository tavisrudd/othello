## Verdict

**Minor revision as a mathematical manuscript**, with a separate requirement to complete the public artifact package before submission/release. I found no counterexample to a central theorem. ## Prioritized findings and concrete repairs

### 1. Specify the actual shadow cubic representatives and scalar

Location: `prop:shadow`, `sections/04-shadow.tex:22–24,34–38`; evidence `notes/2026-09-07-c1099-cubic-phase-strengthening/paperv/step8_torsors.py:55–58`.

**Missing normalization/justification, not a demonstrated false theorem.** The prose says that the recorded involution exchanges the two chordal polynomials. The actual test compares `pnorm(qc)` with a normalized projective representative. Projectively equivalent polynomials do not in general define identical phase gates. I checked the stored objects directly: the pullback of `bridge.pkl`'s actual restricted cubic by its recorded `q` is **2 times** the stored normalized second chordal representative, over F11. Thus the evidence is not an exact equality with that normalized representative.

Repair: define the second actual polynomial as the pullback of the first under q, or state the scalar explicitly. With the former convention q is an involution exchanging actual polynomials. Alternatively, since cubing is bijective on F11*, a scalar can be absorbed into a linear change of variables, but that rescaled map need not itself be an involution. Distinguish these statements. A short displayed definition of the invariant pencil and its conference representative would also make the two spectrum claims intelligible without opening a separate manuscript.

The Clifford-product distinction between chordal and conference resources is unaffected: scaling a nonzero cubic leaves its Hessian ranks unchanged.

Exact read-only reproduction from any directory (the source-index order is `step4_bridge.BASIS3`, the weakly increasing triples on five variables):

### 2. Correct the reason given for excluding geometric lifts

Location: `sections/04-shadow.tex:43–45`; supporting `step8_torsors.py:100–113`, especially its final printed explanation.

**Definite invalid inference in the evidence explanation; conclusion repairable.** The script computes the geometric subgroup preserving W and reports order 60, then says none induces q because q is not A5-equivariant. A noncentral A5 element generally also fails to commute with A5, so non-equivariance alone does not exclude group membership.

There is a direct replacement already supported by the construction. `step4_bridge.py:244–275` selects q from an odd permutation of the six axes; its recorded permutation is `(0 1)(2 4)(3 5)`. On the five-dimensional augmentation representation its determinant is -1. Every A5 element has determinant +1 there. Thus, once the computed preserving subgroup is identified with A5, q cannot be its restriction. State this determinant argument, or explicitly compare q with all 60 restricted matrices and assert the result. Do not retain the non-equivariance inference as the justification.

The identity-on-complement exclusion uses exact evaluations at sampled points. Finding inconsistent ratios is a valid counterexample to a polynomial proportionality claim; exhaustive sampling is unnecessary for that negative. Recording the actual witness vectors would make this part easier to audit.

### 3. Explain that sheet reversal also preserves the Clifford resource class

Location: abstract `main.tex:37–38`; `prop:shadow`, `sections/04-shadow.tex:22–24,33–38`.

**Clarity upgrade, not a correction to the stated inverse-gate identity.** For every homogeneous cubic here, F(-u)=-F(u). Consequently coordinate negation C sends |F> to |-F> and satisfies C U_F C^{-1}=U_F^{-1}. The two sign choices may have different geometric meanings, but the distinction is not “one changes the Clifford resource, the other only changes its frame.” Both preserve the relevant Clifford equivalence class, although one inverts the phase in fixed coordinates. Add this identity when discussing operational meanings.

The self-contained proof here supplies the necessary weaker criterion.

I did not conduct a new absence-of-prior-work search and give no independent priority certification. The author's counts of screened papers do not themselves establish novelty; the manuscript correctly acknowledges that limit.

### 5. Preserve the factory theorem's precise scope and improve its headline hypothesis

Location: `thm:factory`, `sections/03-factory.tex:10–38,58–63,99–149`; abstract `main.tex:33–37`.

**The theorem is valid for its stated menu.** This is a useful finite comparison, not a proof of an optimal factory or of superiority to unrestricted separate preparation. Its force is that the target is the same actual block infidelity, the lower bound allows nonuniform distillation depths and error cancellations, and feasible competitors exist throughout the interval. Weighted inputs constitute a genuine favorable relaxation for the competitor.

The abstract should name the independent uniform Z-noise/ideal-Clifford assumptions, since the resource numbers otherwise look more general than they are. The body states them correctly. Consider adding one sentence explaining why a final allowed Clifford preserves the orthogonality-based infidelity argument: it maps the ideal output and every errored output together, preserving overlaps.

The lower bounds 54 and 36 are deliberately coarse: they multiply nine necessary forms by a minimum raw-leaf count, rather than optimize a complete distillation tree. The feasible rows already distinguish this from attainability of the bound; keep that distinction. No additional optimization is necessary to establish the reported strict separation.

### Where readers lose the thread

1. **The opening names a mechanism before establishing the problem.** `01-trades:1–5` says cancellation yields a gate, then immediately defines a signed trade and states the dictionary. A quantum-information reader has not yet been told why these particular coupled gates are useful or why compare them with independent cubic states. An algebraist has not been told what preparation costs or Clifford equivalence measure. The abstract lists results but supplies little reason to care about their connection. Add a short opening question and answer: can symmetric point configurations produce coupled cubic resources whose structure can be certified and whose joint preparation can beat a specified independent-primitive route? State what each example answers, carefully distinguishing the p=11 product exclusion from the p=7 factory advantage. Do not imply that the six-qudit Clebsch state has the stronger product exclusion.

2. **The first theorem does not carry the paper's distinct contribution.** `thm:trade-code` is the main first-page mathematical object, but its framework attribution comes only after the proof, and the paper's characteristic spectra and operational advantage appear later. A reader who skims only the first theorem will misidentify the novelty. Retain the useful dictionary, identify it as the established framework before its statement, and precede it with a compact main-results paragraph pointing to the new finite spectra/product result and the bounded factory theorem. The all-primes translation family should be introduced explicitly as a calibration example showing what symmetry is not needed for. Avoid collecting unrelated scales into one sprawling theorem.

3. **The transition from conic matching coordinates to binary invariants asks quantum readers to reconstruct the geometry.** At `01-trades:122–156`, orbit signs, division by Q, coefficient selection, binary quartics/octavics and transvectants arrive in rapid succession. It is unclear on first reading which ingredients the reader must understand to use the resulting code. Add one orientation sentence: the matching products provide a finite list of evaluation points; their signed moments are the code input; invariant coordinates summarize the surviving logical polynomial. Define s as the scalar coordinate, state the dimension and role of the other coordinates, and say that the matrices and normalization details may be deferred without losing the code/resource argument. Explain the name “Clebsch” once or attribute it precisely to the underlying geometry rather than relying on the title and companion provenance.

4. **The Hessian theorem is clear, but its purpose is delayed.** `02-spectrum:2–13` defines a new entropy and immediately computes it. Before the formula, explain that Clifford operations only permute Pauli overlaps, so their modulus distribution gives an invariant that can obstruct conversion to independent resources. After `thm:hessian`, identify the two later uses: the aggregate fourth moment rules out product states, while the rare minimum-rank directions obstruct short sums of cubes. This makes the language changes causal rather than a list of unrelated calculations. One explicit univariate cubic example, with ranks 0 at zero and 1 elsewhere, would orient adjacent readers and can replace the later isolated comparison sentence rather than add length gratuitously.

5. **The factory proof changes from polynomial rank to probability without enough orientation.** `03-factory:65–70` introduces weighted linear-form rank only implicitly in the proof; define it as the least number of nonzero weighted cubes needed to express F before stating the lemma. A short strategy paragraph before `thm:factory` should explain the three steps: native rejection makes block error smaller than raw error; independence prevents an unpurified primitive from meeting that target; the polynomial rank forces at least nine independently purified terms. This is the actual insight. Keep the exact interval algebra in the proof. The existing injection description and menus are strong and should be preserved.

6. **The shadow section is currently a companion-dependent coda presented at theorem density.** `04-shadow:7–38` refers to an invariant pencil, conference cubic and residual involution without defining the actual pencil or either member. The proposition also reuses z0,...,z4 in new shadow coordinates after using z0,...,z8 for octavic coordinates, without an explicit warning. A reader can easily mistake the shadow for an octavic coordinate slice. Introduce new symbols or explicitly say these are new coordinates on the embedded constituent. Explain chordal as the secant cubic of a rational normal quartic, define the compared conference member at least by a precise displayed formula or normalized reference, and give the linear embedding/intertwiner in a compact appendix if the body would become cluttered. Position this as a geometric interpretation of the resource, not another unexplained main objective.

7. **The audit appendix distracts from mathematical positioning.** `05-evidence:107–122` gives screening counts, database availability and an “explicitly approved coverage exception.” These are useful research records, but neither the reader's mathematical question nor the literature comparison is clarified by an internal approval process. Move the process ledger to supplementary documentation and retain a short public-facing statement of read-depth/priority limits. Likewise, unexplained task codes C1090/C1099/C1102 in `05-evidence:87–90` should become artifact titles with immutable locators. The bibliography must do the work of scholarly attribution.

### A proportionate revision structure

Use a short opening that states the operational question, the exact two-prime distinctions, and the route from moments to logical phases to Pauli invariants to the bounded preparation comparison. Follow with the dictionary and translation calibration, then the two finite inputs. Keep the spectrum and factory sections as the main results. Let the chordal section explain the geometric interpretation and lead into a small number of mathematical questions. Place exact finite inputs and reproducibility limits in the appendix; leave project-management and literature-screening mechanics in the artifact.

This does not require a broad new literature survey, new theorems, or explanatory figures for their own sake. It requires about a page of better-motivated connecting prose, paid for by removing repetitive ledger references and administrative detail. The existing proofs should largely survive intact. A reader should be able to skip the binary-invariant calculations and still understand what has been proved about resources, and skip the factory probability algebra while retaining its exact menu and noise assumptions.

## Central mathematical checks

1. **Trade dictionary (`thm:trade-code`, 01-trades:20–60): sound.** Signed moments make L maximal isotropic for D, hence L-perp=DL. The CSS dimension, basis normalization, transversal cubic expansion, and separate X/Z distance arguments agree. Distinct evaluation points are used exactly where the weight-two Z logical error is established. Polarization is valid because p>=5 and the polynomial degrees are below p.

2. **Translation family (`prop:translation`, 01-trades:75–120): sound for every stated prime.** The two hyperplanes are disjoint; within-sheet differences span ker(sigma), and t supplies the remaining affine direction. On K the quadratic radical is precisely the all-ones line. In the annihilator argument, a symmetric form zero on K x K has the stated 1r^T+r1^T representation. Three indices away from 0 already force their r-values to vanish in odd characteristic; p>=5 provides enough indices. The remaining matrix entries really force constant opposite sheet weights. This proves rigidity without relying on prime sampling.

3. **Hessian formula (`thm:hessian`, 02-spectrum:15–43): sound.** The finite difference has quadratic matrix -H(v)/2 and linear term w-grad F(v). Euler's identity puts grad F(v) inside im H(v), so the advertised unshifted support is correct. The radical factor and nondegenerate Gauss sums give modulus p^{-r/2}, with exactly p^r admissible w. The rank distribution is therefore recoverable from the Pauli-modulus histogram, not merely enough to determine one entropy.

4. **Product ceiling and exclusions (02-spectrum:87–125): sound given the censuses.** The purity/Cauchy–Schwarz normalization is correct. The largest bipartite ceiling is at the endpoints. Clifford invariance applies to an arbitrary final Clifford, so the conclusion is stronger than entanglement in the displayed coordinates. The manuscript correctly refrains from claiming this test establishes the six-qudit Clebsch exclusion.

5. **Waring lower bounds and extension (`lem:waring`, 03-factory:65–88): sound given the censuses.** N0=1 forces essential dependence on all variables. Killing k-1 independent forms gives the first rank bound. At equality, any dependent k-subset contradicts the minimum positive Hessian rank; hence the counted (k-1)-subsets span distinct hyperplanes and force too many minimum-rank lines. The histogram recovers N_r, so the same reasoning applies to homogeneous cubic-phase representatives in the Clifford orbit. This does not extend the claim to arbitrary circuits or arbitrary nonhomogeneous states.

6. **Factory interval (03-factory:108–149): sound.** The accepted weight-two probability, pair union bound, acceptance bounds, Bernoulli estimate, and q<delta comparison all hold on the closed upper endpoint delta=0.01. The convolution max-norm argument excludes a raw unpurified nonzero form even if other form errors cancel. Independence is essential and explicitly assumed. The raw-leaf and feasibility arguments give the claimed bounds under both menus.

7. **Chordal restriction (04-shadow:7–45): reasonable and carefully limited, subject to findings 1–2.** The paper does not confuse restriction with a subcode, a finite rational-point census with the singular scheme, or tested geometric lifts with every possible full-gate lift. These are strengths. The exact bridge matrices remain delegated to finite evidence; I did not independently reconstruct the whole A5 representation and intertwiner.
