\begin{abstract}
Proposition~4.7 of the draft transports framed formal monodromy across the
comparison isomorphisms of Iritani and Iritani--Koto, and the transport step
itself is asserted in one sentence. This memo states and proves the missing
lemma over a differential field, with its hypotheses made explicit; shows that
those hypotheses are jointly unsatisfiable over the coefficient receiver the
draft builds, so that the lemma as proved has no verified instance in the
application; corrects the receiver; and reduces the remaining question to one
inequality that depends on a normalization the draft is free to choose. The
inequality fails under the draft's current instruction to take the separating
weight parameter large, and holds at the cubic endpoint under the smallest
admissible choice. the archived iterated-receiver discussion examines an iterated receiver that
would satisfy the inequality trivially, checks it against the sources, and finds
that it needs a semipositivity hypothesis on the ambient variety which weak
factorization does not supply.

The second version of this memo proposed the framing theory of Hinault, Yu,
Zhang and Zhang as the probable repair, on the ground that its decomposition
gauge is regular in the loop coordinate. Three compatibility checks against the
pinned sources have since been run, and the route does not survive them;
Section~\ref{sec:hyzz} now reports what those checks found. Two consequences are
worth more than the route was. First, no receiver is needed for the comparison
isomorphisms at all: Iritani's $\Psi$ and Iritani--Koto's $\Phi$ are polynomial
in the loop coordinate with inverses in the same ring, so the transport theorem
below applies to them with $\Gamma=0$. Second, the diagnosis of the second
version was wrong --- the pro-Laurent bulk gauge is not the draft's own choice
but Iritani--Koto's own fundamental solution, which the sources meet and handle
by Birkhoff factorization. What is left is a single statement about one bulk
displacement, recorded in Section~\ref{sec:hyzz}.
Section~\ref{sec:blockwise} then attacks that statement directly through the
flatness identities. It proves that multiplicity-one blocks carry no framed
monodromy, which is unconditional and leaves only coalesced blocks in play, and
writes down the exact evolution of the block's leading operator. It does
\emph{not} reduce the coalesced case: the third version of this memo claimed
such a reduction and was wrong twice over, once in dropping a term from the
off-block Sylvester equation and once in modelling a nonsemisimple block by its
compressed pair. Both errors, and the cubic $2\times2$ example that is the
regression test for any repair, are recorded there.

Section~\ref{sec:rigid}, new in this version, closes the coalesced case for a
block of the cubic's type, and with it the residual gap for the cubic ambient
summand. The device is to shear the block before deforming it. Three statements
are proved over the formal even bulk germ, from the flatness identities and
nothing else: the double eigenvalue never splits, because $\det N$ satisfies
$\partial_a\det N=2q_a\det N$ and vanishes initially; no irregularity appears,
because the commutant of a regular block forbids a $z^{-2}$ bulk term and the
$z^{-1}$ flatness equation then forces the surviving pole to vanish; and the
residue of the sheared block moves by conjugation alone, so its characteristic
polynomial is constant. For the cubic that polynomial is $\rho^2+\rho+5/36$
with roots $-1/6,-5/6$, so the primitive-sixth multiplicity is $2$ at every bulk
parameter, including a specialized one. No receiver, no bulk gauge, and no
convergence question enters, because specialization is substitution into
constants.

Section~\ref{sec:endpoint} assembles the one-stabilization statement --- that
$X\times\PP^1$ is irrational for every smooth cubic threefold --- in the atom
formulation, where the intermediate varieties of a weak factorization cancel in
the ledger instead of being visited, so that no framed operator is transported
across a comparison isomorphism and Hypothesis 4.7H does not arise. Every step
is proved there except one, and that one is stated as the gap it is: the
primitive-sixth count must be invariant under change of base point within a
connected component of the spectral cover, which is part of the definition of an
atom. Cai's gauge argument gives that invariance along a formal germ, and it
does not globalize, because the gauge is the pro-Laurent object whose sum over
bulk degrees loses its lower bound in the loop coordinate outside topologically
nilpotent parameters. So the receiver difficulty of the earlier sections
reappears at the globalization step rather than being escaped. Two consequences
are recorded: the low-dimensional side needs no such invariance, because the
sources' nef-canonical claim is pointwise structural; and the sources' own
architecture avoids the gap by decorating with a Serre automorphism, whose
well-definedness on atoms follows from rigidity of representations of a
proreductive group rather than from any constancy of monodromy.
Section~\ref{sec:unconditional} carries the endpoint most of the way. An
earlier version of it claimed the theorem outright by inverting the comparison's
change of variables; that was refuted, since the sources' invertibility is in
displaced coordinates treated as formal variables and the substitution the
argument needed is ill-defined. What survives the refutation is stronger than
what motivated it. For the trivial rank-two bundle the displacement is
$\varsigma_j^\circ=r\lambda_j=\pm2q^{1/2}$ \emph{exactly}, a pure string-direction
shift with no tail --- proved twice, once from the sources' own formula and once
by a spectral argument on the product that needs no transform at all. An $H^0$
shift changes no exponent, so $\nu_6(X\times\PP^1,0)=4$ is a computed fact rather
than a hypothesis, and the standing hypothesis is discharged at the endpoint
rather than relocated. What is
left is a single statement about a single variety: that $X\times\PP^1$ keeps at
least one primitive-sixth eigenvalue along the accumulated displacement --- but
only once the factorization steps are traversed without inverting a change of
variables, which the roof of Section~\ref{sec:endpoint} was meant to supply and
does not, strong factorization being unavailable. Its
$\nu_6$-carrying blocks are two copies of the cubic's rank-two Jordan block,
which are exactly the hypotheses of Section~\ref{sec:rigid}, and the pencil along
which they must be followed is polynomial per slot, so the engine to run there is
Section~\ref{sec:rigid} rather than a bulk gauge.
Section~\ref{sec:corrections} lists what the earlier versions got wrong;
readers of those versions should start there.
\end{abstract}

\section{The assertion at issue}

\sloppypar
References are to Section~4 of the draft, \emph{Framed formal monodromy}. I name
statements by their semantic labels as well as by number, because the edits
proposed in Section~\ref{sec:placement} renumber the section: Definition~4.1
(\texttt{def:framed-sixth-multiplicity}), Definition~4.2
(\texttt{def:pro-laurent-gauge-group}), Lemma~4.3
(\texttt{lem:pv-base-change}), Lemma~4.5 (\texttt{lem:formal-base-shift}),
Proposition~4.7 (\texttt{prop:framed-operations}), Lemma~4.9
(\texttt{lem:divisor-tagging}).

Lemma~4.3 ends with the sentence
\begin{quote}
  \emph{If the gauge uses only integral powers of $z$ at every finite level,
  this conjugacy preserves the original-$z$-disc frame,}
\end{quote}
whose proof is the single sentence ``the deck action fixes every integral power
of $z$ at each finite level, so the compatible gauge is single-valued for the
framed turn.'' Lemma~4.5 then \emph{defines}
\[
  M^{\mathrm{bulk}}=G\,M^{\mathrm{small,shifted}}_{\mathrm f,V}G^{-1}
  \tag{4.1c}
\]
and Proposition~4.7 concludes that ``each center target summand has the framed
monodromy of its specialized small connection.'' The conclusion does not follow
from the display: conjugation preserves a characteristic polynomial for trivial
reasons, but says nothing about whether the conjugated matrix is the intrinsic
turn operator on horizontal solutions of the gauged module. What is missing is a
transport lemma together with an algebra in which the turn acts on solutions.

\section{The lemma over a differential field}
\label{sec:lemma}

Notation for $\operatorname{Exp}_V$, $K=\C\oplus V$ and $\Omega_V$ is the
draft's, from (4.0) and (4.0a). Recall that $\operatorname{Exp}_V$ is defined on
$(K,+)$, not on $\Omega_V$.

\subsection*{The ground field}

Let $\Gamma$ be a totally ordered abelian group and let
\[
  \mathcal H=\Omega_V(\!(\Gamma\oplus\Z e_z)\!)
\]
be the Hahn field of series with well-ordered support and coefficients in
$\Omega_V$, the value group ordered lexicographically with the $e_z$-coordinate
\emph{last}, as in the draft. Write $z=x^{e_z}$ and let $\theta$ multiply the
coefficient at $(\gamma,k)$ by $k$; this is a derivation because $k$ is additive
on the value group, and $\theta z=z$. Its constants are
\[
  \mathcal C:=\mathcal H^{\theta=0}=\Omega_V(\!(\Gamma)\!).
\]
Taking $\Gamma=0$ gives $\mathcal H=\Omega_V(\!(z)\!)$. For $e\ge1$ put
$\mathcal H_e=\Omega_V(\!(\Gamma\oplus\frac1e\Z e_z)\!)$, a degree-$e$ extension
containing $w=x^{e_z/e}$, and $\theta_w=e\theta$, so $\theta_ww=w$. All residues
below are taken with respect to $\theta_w$ on the ramified side; the draft's
$M_{\mathrm{RS},V}$ uses the same convention, and the two differ by the factor
$e$ if one is careless.

\subsection*{The solution algebra and the turn}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:solution-algebra}
Assume the exponential factors under consideration have \emph{constant}
coefficients, that is $\varphi\in w^{-1}\Omega_V[w^{-1}]$. The \emph{universal
formal solution algebra} $\mathcal U_e$ is the $\mathcal H_e$-algebra generated
by symbols
\[
  w^\rho\ (\rho\in K),\qquad \ell,\qquad E(\varphi),
\]
subject to $w^\rho w^{\rho'}=w^{\rho+\rho'}$, $w^n=$ the element $w^n$ of
$\mathcal H_e$ for $n\in\Z$, $E(\varphi)E(\varphi')=E(\varphi+\varphi')$,
$E(0)=1$, with
\[
  \theta_w(w^\rho)=\rho\,w^\rho,\qquad
  \theta_w(\ell)=1,\qquad
  \theta_w\bigl(E(\varphi)\bigr)=\theta_w(\varphi)E(\varphi).
\]
\end{definition}

The two hypotheses buried in that definition --- constant coefficients in
$\varphi$, and exponents $\rho$ in $K$ rather than in $\Omega_V$ --- are not
bookkeeping. The first is false for the modules the draft transports and is
discussed at Remark~\ref{rem:constants-fails}; the second is forced because
$\operatorname{Exp}_V$ is defined only on $K$, so a module whose residues leave
$K$ needs $\operatorname{Exp}$ rebuilt over a larger field.

\begin{lemma}[Structure]
\label{lem:structure}
Fix representatives $\Lambda\subset K$ for $K/\Z$ with $0\in\Lambda$. Then
$\mathcal U_e$ is free as an $\mathcal H_e$-module with basis
$\{w^\rho\ell^mE(\varphi)\}$, $\rho\in\Lambda$, $m\ge0$. In particular
$\mathcal U_e\ne0$ and $\mathcal H\subset\mathcal U_e$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
One cannot argue that $K/\Z$ is torsion-free; it is not, since it contains
$\Q/\Z$, and the rational classes are exactly the ones $\nu_6$ counts. Instead
use base change. The group algebra $\mathcal H_e[K]$ is free over
$\mathcal H_e[\Z]=\mathcal H_e[t,t^{-1}]$ on any set $\Lambda$ of coset
representatives, and $\mathcal U_e$ is obtained from it by the base change
$\mathcal H_e[t,t^{-1}]\to\mathcal H_e$, $t\mapsto w$, a surjection with kernel
$(t-w)$ generated by a nonzerodivisor. Base change of a free module is free on
the image of the same basis. The crossed multiplication $w^\rho
w^{\rho'}=w^nw^{\rho''}$ is well defined, and the derivation is consistent with
the integer identification because $w^n$ sits at $e_z$-exponent $n/e$ and
$\theta_w$ multiplies it by $n$. The factors $E(\varphi)$ form the group algebra
of a torsion-free group and $\ell$ is a polynomial variable.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The turn]
\label{lem:turn}
There is a unique automorphism $\sigma$ of $\mathcal U_e$ with
\[
  \sigma(w)=e^{2\pi i/e}w,\quad
  \sigma(w^\rho)=\operatorname{Exp}_V(\rho/e)\,w^\rho,\quad
  \sigma(\ell)=\ell+\tfrac{2\pi i}{e},\quad
  \sigma\bigl(E(\varphi)\bigr)=E(\sigma\varphi),
\]
acting on $\mathcal H_e$ by multiplying the coefficient at $(\gamma,j/e)$ by
$e^{2\pi ij/e}$. It fixes $\mathcal H$, hence $\mathcal C$, pointwise, and
commutes with $\theta_w$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The action on $\mathcal H_e$ multiplies coefficients by a character of the value
group, hence is a support-preserving ring automorphism, and is the identity on
$\mathcal H$ because there $j\in e\Z$. Multiplicativity in $\rho$ is the
homomorphism property of $\operatorname{Exp}_V$ on $(K,+)$, and the two
prescriptions agree on integral exponents because
$\operatorname{Exp}_V(n/e)=e^{2\pi in/e}$ by (4.0a). Commutation with
$\theta_w$ is not a diagonality argument, since neither
$\sigma(\ell)=\ell+2\pi i/e$ nor $\theta_w(\ell^m)=m\ell^{m-1}$ is diagonal; it
is the direct check
$\sigma\theta_w(\ell^m)=m(\ell+\tfrac{2\pi i}e)^{m-1}=\theta_w\sigma(\ell^m)$,
together with the diagonal cases.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Constants]
\label{lem:constants}
Under the constant-coefficient hypothesis of
Definition~\ref{def:solution-algebra}, $\mathcal U_e^{\theta_w=0}=\mathcal C$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $v$ be the Hahn valuation of $\mathcal H_e$, additive because the
coefficient field is a field; $\theta_w$ multiplies coefficients by integers and
never enlarges a support, so $v(\theta_wc)\ge v(c)$.

Write $x=\sum c_{\rho,m,\varphi}w^\rho\ell^mE(\varphi)$ in the basis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:structure} with $\theta_wx=0$; the equation splits over
$\varphi$. For $\varphi\ne0$, let $M$ be the top $\ell$-degree in that block;
for each $\rho$,
$\theta_w(c_{\rho,M})+\rho c_{\rho,M}+\theta_w(\varphi)c_{\rho,M}=0$. Every
monomial of $\theta_w(\varphi)$ has $\Gamma$-part $0$ and strictly negative
$e_z$-part, so $v(\theta_w(\varphi)c)<v(c)\le v(\theta_wc+\rho c)$ for $c\ne0$
and the lowest term cannot cancel; hence $c_{\rho,M}=0$, and descending in $M$
kills the block.

For $\varphi=0$ the coefficient equations are
$\theta_w(c_{\rho,m})+\rho c_{\rho,m}+(m+1)c_{\rho,m+1}=0$. Descending in $m$:
the top-degree coefficient satisfies the homogeneous equation
$\theta_w(c)+\rho c=0$, and on each monomial this reads $(j+\rho)c=0$ with
$j\in\Z$, so $c=0$ unless $\rho\in\Z$, that is unless $\rho=0$. For $\rho=0$ the
top coefficient is $\theta_w$-constant and the next equation gives
$Mc_{0,M}=-\theta_w(c_{0,M-1})$, whose right side has zero component in
$e_z$-degree $0$; comparing that component gives $c_{0,M}=0$ for $M\ge1$. What
survives is $\rho=0$, $m=0$, $\varphi=0$ with $\theta_w$-constant coefficient,
and the $\theta_w$-constants of $\mathcal H_e$ are those supported in
$e_z$-degree zero, namely $\mathcal C$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The constant-coefficient hypothesis is necessary, and fails in
the application]
\label{rem:constants-fails}
Allow $\varphi$ a Novikov coefficient of positive weight, $\varphi=x^\gamma
w^{-1}$ with $\gamma\in\Gamma$, $\gamma>0$ --- the shape of every exponential
factor the draft actually meets, since the eigenvalues $\lambda$ of $c_1\star$
have positive Novikov weight. Then $v(\theta_w\varphi)>0$ in the
coefficient-dominant order and the domination argument reverses. Worse, the
equation $\theta_w(c)=\theta_w(\varphi)c$ is solved by
\[
  c=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{1}{n!}\,x^{n\gamma}w^{-n}=\exp(x^\gamma w^{-1}),
\]
--- note the sign: $\theta_w\varphi=-\varphi$ here, so matching the coefficient of
$x^{m\gamma}w^{-m}$ gives $-ma_m=-a_{m-1}$, hence $a_m=a_{m-1}/m$ and
$a_m=1/m!$ with no alternation. An earlier version displayed the alternating
series, which solves $\theta_w(c)=+\varphi c$ instead; the support, and with it
the conclusion, is unaffected ---
whose support $\{(n\gamma,-n/e)\}_{n\ge0}$ is increasing, hence well ordered, so
$c\in\mathcal H_e$ and $cE(\varphi)$ is a $\theta_w$-constant outside
$\mathcal C$. Lemma~\ref{lem:constants} is therefore false in that regime, and
with it Proposition~\ref{prop:framed-is-turn}. The structural reason is worth
stating on its own: \emph{in the draft's coefficient-dominant order,
$\exp(-\lambda/z)$ is already a unit of the coefficient field}, so adjoining
$E(\varphi)$ as a free symbol duplicates an existing element and inflates the
constants. In that order the exponential factors are not symbols at all, and the
entire irregularity of the formal solution sits in its divergent unit part,
which is exactly where the rate comparison of Section~\ref{sec:receiver} lives.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Framed operators and transport}

\begin{proposition}[The framed operator is the matrix of the turn]
\label{prop:framed-is-turn}
Let $D$ be a differential module over $\mathcal H$, free of rank $n$, with
connection matrix $\Omega$, and let $Y\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal U_e)$ satisfy
$\theta Y=-\Omega Y$. Then
$\operatorname{Sol}(D)=\{v:\theta v+\Omega v=0\}=Y\mathcal C^{\,n}$, free of
rank $n$ and $\sigma$-stable; the matrix $M$ with $\sigma(Y)=YM$ lies in
$\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal C)$ and is well defined up to
$\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal C)$-conjugacy. For $D$ over $\Omega_V(\!(z)\!)$ in
Levelt--Turrittin form, $M$ is the draft's framed operator: in a solution basis
adapted to one deck orbit of length $d$ the matrix of $\sigma$ is block-cyclic
with the draft's intertwiners $A_i$ as blocks, and the cyclic-block determinant
gives $\chi(X)=\det(X^dI-U)$ with $U=A_{d-1}\cdots A_0$, which is (4.0b).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
From $\theta(Y^{-1})=Y^{-1}\Omega$ one gets $\theta(Y^{-1}v)=Y^{-1}(\theta
v+\Omega v)$, so horizontality of $v$ means $Y^{-1}v$ has $\theta_w$-constant
entries, and Lemma~\ref{lem:constants} identifies those with $\mathcal C$. Since
$\sigma$ commutes with $\theta$ and fixes $\Omega$, it preserves
$\operatorname{Sol}(D)$; so $M=Y^{-1}\sigma(Y)$ has entries in $\mathcal C$ and
is invertible, being a product of invertibles, with inverse
$\sigma(Y^{-1})Y$. Two solution matrices differ on the right by an element of
$\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal C)$, which conjugates $M$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Frame transport]
\label{thm:transport}
Let $D,D'$ be as in Proposition~\ref{prop:framed-is-turn}, with solution
matrices $Y,Y'$, and let $G\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal H)$ satisfy
$\Omega'=G\Omega G^{-1}-\theta(G)G^{-1}$. Then $GY$ is a solution matrix for
$D'$ and $\sigma(GY)=(GY)M$. Consequently
\[
  M_{\mathrm f,V}(D')=C_0^{-1}M_{\mathrm f,V}(D)\,C_0
  \qquad\text{for some }C_0\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal C),
\]
so the two framed operators have the same characteristic polynomial in
$\mathcal C[X]$ and the same multiplicity at every root of unity.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\theta(GY)=\theta(G)Y-G\Omega Y=-\Omega'(GY)$. Since $\sigma$ fixes
$\mathcal H$ pointwise, $\sigma(G)=G$, so $\sigma(GY)=G\sigma(Y)=GYM$. Writing
$Y'=(GY)C_0$ with $C_0\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal C)$ gives $M'=C_0^{-1}MC_0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Which object is $M_{\mathrm f,V}$]
The framed operator is an automorphism of the horizontal module; its matrix
depends on a choice of \emph{solution} frame and is well defined up to
conjugacy over the constants. The conjugate $GM G^{-1}$ appearing in the draft's
(4.1c) is not the matrix of the turn in any solution frame --- $Y'$ differs from
$GY$ by a constant matrix, not by $G$ --- but the matrix of the transported
automorphism read in the \emph{module} frame of $D'$. Both readings are
legitimate, and they give the same characteristic polynomial, but the draft
should say which it means, since conflating them is exactly the step at issue.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The hypothesis on $G$ is the whole content]
Since $\sigma$ fixes $\mathcal H$ pointwise, $\sigma(G)=G$ is automatic once $G$
has entries in $\mathcal H$; what matters is that $G$ lies in $\mathcal H$ and
not in $\mathcal U_e$. No hypothesis-free statement exists: $Y$ itself is a
gauge over $\mathcal U_e$ carrying $D$ to the trivial module, whose framed
operator is the identity. The rank-one case shows what survives: if
$g\in\mathcal H^\times$ and $\theta(g)/g=\delta$, then each monomial gives
$(k-\delta)g_{(\gamma,k)}=0$, so $\delta\in\Z$; a gauge over $\mathcal H$ shifts
residues only by integers, which $\operatorname{Exp}_V$ kills.
\end{remark}

\section{The receiver}
\label{sec:receiver}

The draft's receiver, in the proof of Proposition~4.7, is
\[
  \mathscr R_j=\Omega_{V,j}\otimes_{H_{0,j}}H_{\mathrm{tot},j},
  \qquad
  H_{\mathrm{tot},j}=\C(\!(\Gamma^{\mathrm{coeff}}_j\oplus\Z e_z)\!).
  \tag{4.4c}
\]

\subsection*{The two rates}

Weight a monomial of coefficient weight $d$ and $z$-power $k$ by $a\,d+b\,k$,
$a,b>0$, and let $\varepsilon$ be the minimal weight of a generator of $J_j$.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Pro-Laurent gauges gain one filtration unit per unit of $z$-pole.}
  By the recursion (4.1a) and the draft's own conclusion, $G_\alpha$ has no
  power of $z$ below $-|\alpha|$ and a term of bulk degree $m$ lies in $F^mB$;
  every monomial of $J_j^N$ has weight at least $N$. Their weights grow like
  $m(a-b)$, which together with the draft's finite-below argument gives well
  ordered support when $a>b$.
\item \emph{Splitting gauges lose $w(\Delta\lambda)$ per unit of $z$-power.}
  This is the draft's own construction (4.9d): at order $n$ the block
  off-diagonal coefficient is removed by a Sylvester equation $[K_0,A_n]=B_n$
  with $K_0$ the leading matrix, so one division by an eigenvalue difference per
  order, and $w(A_n)\ge-n\,w(\Delta\lambda)$. The draft's displayed cubic entry
  $-14/(81r^2)$ at $z$-order two exhibits the rate: one factor of $r^{-1}$ per
  unit of $z$-power. Three caveats. After a ramification $z=w^e$ the divisions
  occur once per $w$-order, so the loss is $e\,w(\Delta\lambda)$ per unit of
  $z$-power; at the cubic the draft proves the exponential blocks are
  unramified in $z$, so $e=1$ there. The claim that the regular-singular
  normalization costs nothing needs the homogeneity argument that residue
  eigenvalues are rational, verified in the cubic block where
  $\det L_s=(s+1/6)(s+5/6)$. And $w(\Delta\lambda)$ is not controlled by
  $w(\lambda)$ when the Picard rank exceeds one, since $w$ and the quantum
  grading are different functionals and leading terms can cancel.
\end{itemize}

Both conditions hold together iff
\[
  \boxed{\ e\,w(\Delta\lambda)<\varepsilon\ }
\]

\subsection*{The criterion is not an artifact of choosing an order}

For $C:=e\,w(\Delta\lambda)/\varepsilon\ge1$ the obstruction is not about well
ordering at all. The $z^n$-coefficient of the single product $GP$ of a
pro-Laurent gauge with a splitting gauge is $\sum_mg_mp_{n+m}$, whose terms have
weight at least $m(1-C)-nC$; for $C\ge1$ infinitely many terms have bounded
weight, so that coefficient is an infinite sum in no completion, ordered or not.
Any repair that forms this product must therefore establish $C<1$. A repair that
never forms it --- see route~2 below --- is not bound by this.

\subsection*{The criterion depends on a normalization the draft may choose}

This is the practical point, and it reverses the recommendation of the first
version of this memo. The draft fixes $w(u)=1$ and $w(s_{j,\ell})=1$
independently of $L$, and sets
$w(Q^{i_*d}u^{\rho_C\cdot d})=L(H\cdot i_*d)+\rho_C\cdot d$. Hence
$\varepsilon=1$ always, while the weights of the Novikov generators grow
linearly in $L$. The criterion is therefore
\[
  e\bigl(L(H\cdot i_*d_0)+\rho_C\cdot d_0\bigr)<c_1\cdot d_0,
\]
using that the eigenvalues of $c_1\star$ are homogeneous of quantum degree one
when $Q^d$ has degree $c_1\cdot d$ --- a convention I have checked against the
draft's own low-dimensional vanishing proof, where the $Q^\beta$-component of
$c_1\star$ maps $H^i$ to $H^{i+2-2c_1\cdot\beta}$, and against its explicit
cubic block, where $K_0$ has eigenvalues $\pm6r$, $0$, $0$ with $r=(3q)^{1/2}$
and $c_1\cdot\ell=2$. Consequences:

\begin{itemize}
\item Under the draft's instruction to choose $L$ ``so large that'' separation
  holds, the criterion fails at essentially every comparison with two distinct
  exponential factors --- including the cubic endpoint.
\item Under the smallest admissible $L$ it can hold. For a projective bundle
  with $c_1(V)\cdot\ell=0$ one may take $L=1$, giving $w(Q^\ell)=1=\varepsilon$
  and $w(\Delta\lambda)=w(q^{1/2})=1/2$.
\item Independently of $L$: separation forces $w\ge\varepsilon$ on every
  generator of $J_j$, and the image of $Q^{d_0}$ is a generator, so
  $c_1\cdot d_0\ge2$ is necessary under any admissible weight. Index one fails
  outright.
\end{itemize}

So the cheapest available repair is to choose the separating weight minimally
rather than large, and to check the resulting inequality case by case. The first
version of this memo asserted the opposite, on the false ground that the ratio
was scale-invariant; rescaling $w$ does move both rates equally, but $L$ is not
a rescaling, because the bulk generators' weights are pinned at one.

\section{The comparison maps, and the residual gap}
\label{sec:hyzz}

The framing route proposed in earlier versions --- reconstructing the
framed operator through the Hinault--Yu--Zhang--Zhang $F$-bundle
formalism --- was checked against the pinned sources and closed.  The
route as proposed and the three checks are archived; their verdicts,
locators and hashes are recorded in
\texttt{2026-08-15-c912-framing-compatibility-checks.md}.  Check one
fails, check two passes, and check three confirms that the sources
cannot certify an intrinsic centre invariant.  What survives from that
exercise is stated next, and it is worth more than the route was.

\subsection*{What survives, and the corrected diagnosis}

Two things survive, and both are worth more than the route was.

\emph{The comparison isomorphisms need no receiver.} Iritani's $\Psi$ is an
isomorphism of $\C[z](\!(q^{-1/s})\!)[[Q,\widetilde\tau]]$-modules
(Theorem~5.18), and Iritani--Koto's $\Phi$ is induced by a
$\C[z,q][[Q,\widehat\tau]]$-module map and is an isomorphism over
$\C[z](\!(q^{-1/r'})\!)[[Q,\widehat\tau]]$, with $r'\in\{r,2r\}$ according to
the parity convention of their statement (proof of their Theorem~5.1(6)). An
isomorphism of modules has a module-map inverse, so $\Psi^{-1}$ has entries in
the same ring with no separate argument. Both carry only \emph{non-negative
integral} powers of $z$ --- power series in $z$, not polynomials in $z$, since
Iritani's Remark 1.5 identifies the ring with
$\C[q^{\pm1/s}][[Q,\widetilde\tau]][[z]]$; an earlier version of this memo said
``polynomial'', which is too strong, while the draft's own ``integral powers of
$z$'' is right. What matters is that no negative power of $z$ occurs, so both lie
in $\mathrm{GL}_n(F(\!(z)\!))$ for $F$ the Laurent coefficient field, hence are
fixed by the turn. Theorem~\ref{thm:transport} applies to them with $\Gamma=0$,
where Levelt--Turrittin is available: no Hahn field, no product $GP$, no
inequality. The receiver problem was never a property of the comparison.

\emph{The pro-Laurent gauge is not the draft's choice.} This corrects the
sentence the second version of this memo built its recommendation on.
Iritani--Koto transport along the same object: their reconstruction in
Section~5.8 uses
$M=\bigoplus_je^{-\varsigma_j^\circ/z}M_B(\varsigma_j^\circ+s_j;Qq^{-c_1(V)/r})$,
described in their footnote as a fundamental solution for the summands with
respect to $(Q,s_0,\dots,s_{r-1})$ but not for $q$ and $z$, satisfying
$M=\mathrm{id}+O(z^{-1})$ and lying in
$\operatorname{End}[z^{-1}](\!(q^{-1/r})\!)[[Q,s]]$. Per bulk degree it is a
\emph{polynomial} in $z^{-1}$ --- which is the draft's own bound (4.1a), that
$G_\alpha$ has no power of $z$ below $-|\alpha|$ --- and it is unbounded only
after summing over bulk degrees. The sources meet this object and handle it not
by an ordered receiver but by Birkhoff factorization, splitting the $z$-regular
factor $\Phi$ from the $\mathrm{id}+O(z^{-1})$ factor $M'$ in
$(\Phi^\circ)^{-1}\circ M=M'\circ\Phi^{-1}$. Any repair should be measured
against that, not against a receiver.

\subsection*{The residual gap}

After the two survivals, the blocker is no longer transport across the
comparison. It is one statement:

\begin{quote}
  Let $T$ be smooth projective with quantum connection $\nabla$, and let
  $\tau^\bullet$ be a bulk parameter in the positive filtration --- Iritani's
  $\tau^\circ=q^{-1}[Z]+O(q^{-2})$ on the ambient summand, the
  $O(q^{-1/(c-1)})$ tail of $\varsigma_j^\circ$ together with $s_j$ on a center
  summand. Show that the framed formal monodromy of $\nabla|_{\tau=\tau^\bullet}$
  has the same primitive-sixth multiplicity as $\nabla|_{\tau=0}$.
\end{quote}

Everything else in Proposition~4.7 is proved or reduced to a regular gauge, and
\texttt{lem:divisor-tagging} inherits this gap and nothing else.
Section~\ref{sec:rigid} proves this statement whenever the block carrying
$\nu_6$ is a rank-two Jordan block that is regular singular after one shearing,
which covers the cubic ambient summand. What is still open is the same
statement for an arbitrary block, which is what a center-by-center treatment
would require.


\section{The centre datum: what descends, and the one map still owed}
\label{sec:base-change}

This section addresses what the normalization note
\texttt{2026-08-15-c912-normalization-statement.md} calls Obligation~A, and what
the draft asserts in two sentences at its lines 520--522 and 532. It discharges
the first sentence and \emph{not} the second. An earlier version of this section
claimed both; a hostile referee pass refuted the argument for the second, and the
refutation is correct. The record is
\texttt{2026-08-15-c912-base-change-lemma-hostile-referee.md}; source quotations
are in \texttt{2026-08-15-c912-h47h-source-exactness-audit.md}, \S11, whose own
verdict on the transport step is withdrawn below.

Recall the draft's data. For a smooth centre $C\subset T$ of codimension $c\ge2$,
$u=q^{-1/(c-1)}$, and $R_j$ is the subring generated by the image of
$Q_C^d\mapsto Q^{i_*d}u^{\rho_C\cdot d}$ together with $u$ and the components of
$s_j$; $J_j$ is the ideal on those generators with $d\ne0$; and
$B_j=\varprojlim_NR_j/J_j^N$.

\subsection*{The obstacle, isolated}

By Iritani's (5.30),
\[
  \varsigma_j(0)|_{Q=0}\in-(c-1)\lambda_j+h_{C,j}
   +q^{-1/(c-1)}H^*(C)[q^{-1/(c-1)}],
  \qquad \lambda_j=\zeta_ju^{-1},\ \zeta_j\in\C^\times,
\]
so the $H^{\ge4}$ residual is a polynomial in $u$ with a forced factor of $u$, the
$H^2$ term $h_{C,j}$ is a fixed class free of $q$, and the only negative power of
$u$ in the whole datum sits in the leading term of the $H^0$ coordinate.

\begin{lemma}[The $u^{-1}$ is scalar, and one twist removes it]
\label{lem:h0-scalar}
The coordinate $\varsigma_j^0$ does not enter $\star_{\varsigma_j}$. It enters the
centre connection \textup{(5.43)} in four places, through
$\partial_{\widetilde\tau^\alpha}\varsigma_j$, through $E_C$, through
$\xi Q\partial_Q\varsigma_j$ and through $q\partial_q\varsigma_j$. Its
$u^{-1}$-carrying part $-(c-1)\lambda_j$ reaches only the last two, in each case
as a scalar multiple of the identity and to the first power, and conjugation by
the single rank-one factor $\exp\bigl(-(c-1)\lambda_j/z\bigr)$ removes both at
once.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Iritani's Remark 2.3 states that the quantum product $\star_\tau$ does not depend
on $\tau^0$ while $\tau^0$ does appear in the Euler vector field, and that
$\tau^{(2)}$ does not appear in the Euler field. Applied with $\tau=\varsigma_j$,
this removes $\varsigma_j^0$ from $\star_{\varsigma_j}$, hence from the
$\imath^*\xi\star$ term of $\nabla_{\xi Q\partial_Q}$ and from the
$\rho_C\star$ term of $\nabla_{q\partial_q}$, leaving the four derivative and
Euler occurrences named in the statement. Of those, the first and third annihilate
$-(c-1)\lambda_j$, which depends on $q$ alone, so only $E_C$ and
$q\partial_q\varsigma_j$ see the $u^{-1}$. In
$E_C=c_1(C)+\sum_i(1-\tfrac12\deg\phi_i)\varsigma_j^i\phi_i$ the coefficient at
$\phi_0=1$ is $1$, so $E_C$ acquires $-(c-1)\lambda_j\cdot\mathrm{id}$ linearly.
For the other, $\lambda_j=\zeta_jq^{1/(c-1)}$ gives
$q\partial_q\lambda_j=\lambda_j/(c-1)$, so
$q\partial_q\bigl(-(c-1)\lambda_j\bigr)=-\lambda_j$. It is this matching of the
$1/(c-1)$ against the $(c-1)$ prefactor that makes one factor serve for both
occurrences; without it the two would require different twists. Both residues
being scalar, the twist is rank one: Iritani's \S5.8.1 records
$M_C(\sigma)|_{Q=0}=e^{\sigma/z}$, which exhibits the mechanism at $Q=0$, and the
twist itself is the draft's own first stripping. The draft's Lemma~4.5 covers the
case at hand, including the point that when the $H^0$ coefficient is not
filtration-nilpotent the twist need not lie in the pro-Laurent gauge group, while
a full turn on the original $z$-disc fixes it, so its framed monodromy is trivial.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The normalized coefficients lie in $B_j$]
\label{lem:descent}
After the twist of Lemma~\ref{lem:h0-scalar} and the divisor substitution
\textup{(4.1)}, every coefficient of the normalized centre connection lies in
$B_j$, with no coefficient requiring $u^{-1}$. The $H^2$ contribution is carried
by the $R_j$-generators $Q^{i_*d}u^{\rho_C\cdot d}$ up to a root of unity and a
factor in $1+J_j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Remark 2.3 the $H^2$ coordinate enters only through the combined generator
$(Qe^{\tau^{(2)}})^d=Q^de^{\tau^{(2)}\cdot d}$. Substituting $\varsigma_j$ and
applying (5.15) turns this into
\[
  Q^{i_*d}u^{\rho_C\cdot d}\cdot e^{h_{C,j}\cdot d}
  \cdot e^{((\text{tail})^{(2)}+s_j^{(2)})\cdot d}.
\]
The first factor is a generator of $R_j$ by definition. A negative $\rho_C\cdot d$
is harmless, and it is worth being explicit about why, since it does produce a
positive power of $q$: the generator is the \emph{combined} monomial, whose weight
$L(H\cdot i_*d)+\rho_C\cdot d$ is at least one by the draft's choice of $L$, so the
positive power of $q$ is always dominated by the accompanying Novikov factor. The
second factor is $e^{2\pi i(j+\frac12)(\rho_C\cdot d)/(c-1)}$, a root of unity.
That $d\mapsto e^{\langle h_{C,j},d\rangle}$ is a multiplicative character is not
by itself enough, because the monoid map $d\mapsto i_*d$ is not injective; what
makes it descend to the image is that $h_{C,j}$ is a scalar multiple of $\rho_C$,
so the character factors through the same linear functional that the image
monomial already records. The third factor lies in $1+J_j$ and converges
$J_j$-adically, so it is an element of $B_j$ and not of $R_j$; the statement claims
no more. For the $H^{\ge4}$ directions the centre structure is formal by
Remark~5.6 and the substituted value lies in $J_jH^*(C)$ by (5.30), so those series
converge in $B_j$ as well. Convergence of the Novikov sums uses that $NE_N(C)$ is
finitely generated, which holds for a smooth projective centre.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Separation]
\label{prop:separation}
$R_j$ is a subring of Iritani's coefficient ring when $c$ is odd only after
adjoining $v=q^{-1/s_c}$, and $R_j\hookrightarrow B_j$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On generators: $u=q^{-1/(c-1)}$ is a power of $q^{1/s_c}$, the image monomials are
(5.15) character for character, and the components of $s_j$ are elements of the
ring. For odd $c$ one has $s_c=2(c-1)$ and (5.19)'s $q_{C,j}$ carries
$q^{-c/(2(c-1))}$, a half-integral power of $u$; the draft introduces $v$ for
exactly this reason at its line 541, and $R_j$ as literally defined does not
adjoin it. Injectivity is the draft's separation argument: the additive weight with
$w(u)=1$, $w(s_{j,\ell})=1$ and
$w(Q^{i_*d}u^{\rho_C\cdot d})=L(H\cdot i_*d)+\rho_C\cdot d\ge1$ gives every
generator of $J_j$ weight at least one, so every monomial of $J_j^N$ has weight at
least $N$; the Novikov finite-below condition gives every nonzero element a finite
lowest weight; hence $\bigcap_NJ_j^N=0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The map that is still owed, and why the obvious argument fails}

It is tempting to argue that no continuity is needed at all: Iritani works in the
category of graded rings, an element of a graded completion has only finitely many
nonzero graded components, so membership in $R_j$ could be decided monomial by
monomial, and the inverse and intertwining relations, being \emph{equalities},
would transport along $R_j\hookrightarrow B_j$ with nothing to check.

\emph{That argument is false, and the earlier version of this section made it.}
Graded finiteness is finiteness in the grading only. Iritani's \S2.2 says
verbatim that a homogeneous element of $K(\!(x)\!)$ is of the form
$\sum_{n=m}^\infty a_nx^n$ with $\deg a_n+n\deg x$ independent of $n$: a single
homogeneous component is itself an infinite series. So an entry of $\Psi$ is a
finite sum of infinite series, membership in $R_j$ is not a finite check, and in
general the entries lie in a completion rather than in $R_j$. Two further
obstructions point the same way. Lemma~\ref{lem:descent} is about the coefficients
of the centre connection and says nothing about the entries of $\Psi$, which
involve $\widetilde\tau$ and ambient Novikov variables that are not $R_j$
generators at all; and the $[[Q,\widetilde\tau]]$ directions are not finite in any
sense, so even the graded-finiteness observation gives nothing there.

What is therefore still owed is exactly the item the C912 card carries unticked:
a coefficient map taking the source augmentation ideal into $J_j$, proved to
extend continuously to the completed rings and to descend at every $J_j^N$ stage,
together with the statement that the comparison matrices \emph{and their inverses}
descend modulo $J_j^N$ retaining both the inverse and the intertwining identities.
Nothing above supplies it. The present section reduces the problem in a useful way
--- the $u^{-1}$ is gone after one explicit rank-one twist, the surviving
coefficients lie in $B_j$, and $R_j$ separates --- but the bridge from Iritani's
completion into $B_j$ is not built.

\subsection*{What this settles, and what it does not}

Settled: the $u^{-1}$ obstruction, which was the only place a positive power of
$q$ could have been forced into the normalized datum, and the separation of
$R_j$. Also settled is a correction to the draft: its sentence that the centre
connection is defined over $R_j$ should read that before the strippings it is
defined over $\operatorname{Frac}(R_j)$, since $\lambda_j=\zeta_ju^{-1}$ is not in
$R_j$, and after them over $B_j$. That correction is the most useful thing in this
section.

Not settled: Obligation~A. In consequence the well-posedness question of
\texttt{2026-08-15-c912-normalization-statement.md} remains open, though narrowed
--- the two rings are still plausibly two completions of one shared subring, but
the comparison data has not been shown to cross between them. And nothing here
bears on Hypothesis 4.7H itself: even with the bridge built, reading $\nu_6$ off
the displaced object needs either the gauge-free route of
Section~\ref{sec:rigid}, confined to rank-two coalesced blocks, or a transport
theorem for the pro-Laurent gauge, which Theorem~\ref{thm:transport} does not
supply, covering as it does only maps with no negative power of $z$.

\subsection*{Caveats carried from the source check}

The projective-bundle site is not covered, and the repair proposed in the earlier
version of this section was wrong. Iritani's Remark~2.3 does generalize, being
stated for an arbitrary smooth projective target, but it supplies only the
pre-base-change reduction; what Iritani--Koto lack is an analogue of Remark~5.6,
the smaller-ring reduction, and that is what must be written. A search of v4
finds no such statement. Separately, Iritani--Koto's Theorem 5.1(4) gives only a
big-$O$ bound, so the polynomial shape that the blowup side takes from (5.30) must
come there from their Remark~5.3 instead. Finally, the citation of \S5.8.1 above
establishes its identity at $Q=0$; using it for all $Q$ is an extrapolation, and
the twist should be justified from flatness rather than from that identity.


\section{The blockwise route, and what the flatness identities already give}
\label{sec:blockwise}

This section carries out the second route far enough to see what it costs. It
produces the exact evolution of the leading block operator under bulk motion and
proves that multiplicity-one blocks carry no framed monodromy at all; for
coalesced blocks it does not reduce the problem, but identifies why the full
formal normal form, rather than the compressed pair $(U_i,\mu_i)$, is what has to
be controlled. Conventions are Iritani--Koto's, from their
Theorem~5.1: write $U=E\star_\tau$, $C_a=\phi_a\star_\tau$, and
\[
  \nabla_a=\partial_a+z^{-1}C_a,\qquad
  \nabla_{z\partial_z}=z\partial_z-z^{-1}U+\mu ,
\]
with $\mu$ the constant grading operator. Horizontal sections satisfy
$z^2\partial_zY=(U-z\mu)Y$.

\subsection*{The two identities}

\begin{lemma}[Flatness]
\label{lem:flatness-ids}
$[U,C_a]=0$ and $\partial_aU=C_a+[C_a,\mu]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first is commutativity and associativity of the quantum product. For the
second, expand $[\nabla_a,\nabla_{z\partial_z}]=0$. The $z^{-2}$ term is
$z^{-2}[U,C_a]$ and vanishes by the first identity; the $z^{-1}$ term is
$z^{-1}\bigl(-\partial_aU+C_a+[C_a,\mu]\bigr)$, using
$z\partial_z(z^{-1}C_as)=-z^{-1}C_as+z^{-1}C_az\partial_zs$ and
$\partial_a\mu=0$. There is no $z^0$ term.
\end{proof}

So bulk motion is not an arbitrary negative-loop gauge. The first identity says
$C_a$ commutes with every spectral projector $P_i$ of $U$, hence preserves each
generalized eigenspace $H_i$ with $U|_{H_i}=u_i+N_i$; the second says the motion
of $U$ itself is expressed through $C_a$ and $\mu$ alone.

\subsection*{The block frame}

Let $T_a=\sum_j(\partial_aP_j)P_j$ be Kato's transport operator, so that
$\partial_aP_i=[T_a,P_i]$ and $P_iT_aP_i=0$. It is independent of $z$ and has no
pole, so it moves the blocks without touching the loop coordinate. For an
operator $A$ write $A_i=P_iAP_i$ and $A_{ij}=P_iAP_j$, and let
$D_aA_i=\partial_aA_i-[T_a,A_i]$ be the covariant derivative in this frame. A
short computation using $\partial_aP_i=[T_a,P_i]$ gives the general rule
\[
  D_aA_i=P_i(\partial_aA)P_i+P_i[A,T_a]P_i .
  \tag{6.1}
  \label{eq:block-covariant-rule}
\]

\begin{theorem}[Block evolution]
\label{thm:block-evolution}
Assume the eigenvalues $u_j$ of $U$ are pairwise distinct as scalars, so that
each difference $u_i-u_j$ is invertible. Then, unconditionally,
\[
  D_aU_i=C_{a,i}+[C_{a,i},\mu_i],
  \qquad
  D_a\mu_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\bigl(\mu_{ij}X_{ji}-Y_{ij}\mu_{ji}\bigr),
\]
where $X_{ji}=P_j(\partial_aP_i)P_i$ and $Y_{ij}=P_i(\partial_aP_j)P_j$ are the
unique solutions of the Sylvester equations
\[
  \mathcal S_{ji}(X_{ji})=-(\partial_aU)_{ji},
  \qquad
  \mathcal S_{ij}(Y_{ij})=-(\partial_aU)_{ij},
  \qquad
  \mathcal S_{ji}(X):=(u_j-u_i)X+N_jX-XN_i ,
\]
with $(\partial_aU)_{ji}=C_{a,j}\mu_{ji}-\mu_{ji}C_{a,i}$ and
$(\partial_aU)_{ij}=C_{a,i}\mu_{ij}-\mu_{ij}C_{a,j}$. If \emph{both} blocks are
semisimple, $N_i=N_j=0$, then $\mathcal S_{ji}$ is multiplication by $u_j-u_i$
and the formula collapses to
\[
  D_a\mu_i=[\,C_{a,i},S_i\,],
  \qquad
  S_i:=\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{\mu_{ij}\mu_{ji}}{u_i-u_j} .
  \tag{6.2}
  \label{eq:semisimple-residue-evolution}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $U$: by \eqref{eq:block-covariant-rule} and Lemma~\ref{lem:flatness-ids},
$D_aU_i=P_i(C_a+[C_a,\mu])P_i+P_i[U,T_a]P_i$. Since $C_a$ commutes with $P_i$,
the first term is $C_{a,i}+[C_{a,i},\mu_i]$; since $U$ commutes with $P_i$, the
second is $[U_i,P_iT_aP_i]=0$. No perturbation formula is used, so this identity
holds whatever the nilpotent parts.

For $\mu$: $\partial_a\mu=0$, so \eqref{eq:block-covariant-rule} gives
$D_a\mu_i=P_i[\mu,T_a]P_i$, which is the displayed sum. To evaluate $X_{ji}$,
differentiate $[U,P_i]=0$ to get $[\partial_aU,P_i]+[U,\partial_aP_i]=0$ and
compress by $P_j(\cdot)P_i$ with $j\ne i$: the first bracket contributes
$(\partial_aU)_{ji}$ and the second contributes $U_jX_{ji}-X_{ji}U_i$, which is
$\mathcal S_{ji}(X_{ji})$. The operator $\mathcal S_{ji}$ is invertible because
$u_j-u_i$ is invertible and $X\mapsto N_jX-XN_i$ is nilpotent. The same argument
with $i$ and $j$ exchanged gives $Y_{ij}$. When $N_i=N_j=0$, substituting the
two displayed expressions for $\partial_aU$ makes the terms containing $C_{a,j}$
cancel, leaving $\sum_{j\ne i}[C_{a,i},\mu_{ij}\mu_{ji}]/(u_i-u_j)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The clean form does not cover the blocks that matter]
\label{rem:sylvester-correction}
An earlier version of this memo stated \eqref{eq:semisimple-residue-evolution}
under the weaker hypothesis $N_j=0$ for the \emph{neighbouring} blocks only.
That was an error: differentiating $UP_i=(u_i+N_i)P_i$, the off-block part of
$\partial_a(N_iP_i)$ contributes $X_{ji}N_i$, so the term $-XN_i$ cannot be
dropped when the block $i$ itself is nonsemisimple. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:coalesced-only} the only blocks that can carry $\nu_6$ are
the coalesced ones, and in the draft's cubic calculation that block has
$J_0=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&2\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\ne0$. So
$N_i\ne0$ is the case of interest, and
\eqref{eq:semisimple-residue-evolution} does not apply to it. The cancellation
that produced $S_i$ has to be redone with $\mathcal S_{ji}^{-1}$ in place of the
scalar $1/(u_j-u_i)$; expanding
$\mathcal S_{ji}^{-1}=\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^k(u_j-u_i)^{-k-1}(L_{N_j}-R_{N_i})^k$
shows the clean commutator is only the leading term of that expansion.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Multiplicity-one blocks carry nothing}

This is independent of the deformation and settles, in this normalization, the
observation the earlier version of this memo only flagged.

\begin{theorem}[Simple blocks are invisible]
\label{thm:simple-blocks}
Let $(\cdot,\cdot)$ be the Poincar\'e pairing. Then each $\mu_i$ is
anti-self-adjoint for the restriction of the pairing to $H_i$, which is
nondegenerate; consequently $\operatorname{tr}\mu_i=0$ and the spectrum of
$\mu_i$ is symmetric about $0$. If $u_i$ has multiplicity one, then $\mu_i=0$,
the block's formal solution is $e^{-u_i/z}$ times a single-valued formal unit,
its framed monodromy is the identity, and it contributes no root of unity to
$\nu_6$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Quantum multiplication is Frobenius, $(a\star b,c)=(a,b\star c)$, so $U$ is
self-adjoint; distinct generalized eigenspaces of a self-adjoint operator are
orthogonal, so each $P_i$ is self-adjoint and the pairing restricts
nondegenerately to $H_i$. The pairing vanishes unless the degrees are
complementary, so $\mu$ is anti-self-adjoint; hence so is
$\mu_i=P_i\mu P_i$ on $H_i$. An anti-self-adjoint operator has
$\operatorname{tr}\mu_i=\operatorname{tr}\mu_i^{\dagger}=-\operatorname{tr}\mu_i$
and characteristic polynomial even up to sign, giving the trace and symmetry
statements. On a one-dimensional space anti-self-adjointness forces $\mu_i=0$.
Formal decoupling from the other eigenspaces by a gauge $g=1+zg_1+\cdots$ can
add terms $z\,c_1+z^2c_2+\cdots$ to the scalar block, but it does not change its
$z^0$ coefficient, since the only $z^0$ contribution is
$-[g_1,U]_{ii}=[U_i,g_{1,ii}]=0$ in rank one. So the decoupled block is
$z^2\partial_zy=(u_i+z^2c_2+\cdots)y$, whose solution is $e^{-u_i/z}$ times a
formal unit with no $\log z$ and no fractional power: single-valued under the
turn.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:coalesced-only}
$\nu_6$ is carried entirely by the blocks of $E\star$ whose eigenvalue has
multiplicity greater than one.
\end{corollary}

This matches the draft's own cubic computation, where $K_0$ has eigenvalues
$\pm6r,0,0$ and the primitive-sixth exponents come from the repeated $0$: the
$\pm6r$ blocks are simple, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:simple-blocks} they contribute
the identity, and the draft's indicial roots $1/6$ and $5/6$ are $\pm1/6$ modulo
$\Z$, exactly the symmetry about $0$ that the theorem forces on a block residue.

\subsection*{What is left, exactly}

\begin{proposition}[Isomonodromy criterion, semisimple blocks only]
\label{prop:block-isomonodromy}
Suppose $N_i=0$ and $N_j=0$ for all $j$, so that the decoupled block connection
is $\nabla_i=z\partial_z+A_i(z)$ with $A_i(z)=-z^{-1}U_i+\mu_i$ and
\eqref{eq:semisimple-residue-evolution} applies. Then $D_a\nabla_i$ is an
infinitesimal gauge transformation by $\Theta_a(z)=-z^{-1}C_{a,i}+\beta$ if and
only if
\[
  [\beta,U_i]=0
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  [\beta,\mu_i]=[\,C_{a,i},S_i\,].
\]
The $z^{-2}$ and $z^{-1}$ obstructions vanish identically for that choice of
leading term; only the $z^0$ equation remains.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Infinitesimally, $\delta A_i=[\Theta_a,A_i]-z\partial_z\Theta_a$. With
$\Theta_a=z^{-1}\alpha+\beta$ this is
$-z^{-2}[\alpha,U_i]+z^{-1}\bigl([\alpha,\mu_i]-[\beta,U_i]+\alpha\bigr)+[\beta,\mu_i]$.
Under the stated hypotheses Theorem~\ref{thm:block-evolution} gives
$D_a\nabla_i=-z^{-1}\bigl(C_{a,i}+[C_{a,i},\mu_i]\bigr)+[C_{a,i},S_i]$. Taking
$\alpha=-C_{a,i}$ kills the $z^{-2}$ term because $C_{a,i}$ commutes with $U_i$,
and reduces the $z^{-1}$ equation to $[\beta,U_i]=0$; the $z^0$ equation is the
second displayed condition.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Why this is not yet a reduction of the coalesced case}
\label{sec:not-yet}

Proposition~\ref{prop:block-isomonodromy} is stated where it is true, and that is
not where the problem is. Two things fail for a coalesced block, and the second
is the deeper one.

First, by Remark~\ref{rem:sylvester-correction}, $S_i$ is not the right object
once $N_i\ne0$: the full Sylvester inverse has to be carried.

Second, and independently, the compressed pair $(U_i,\mu_i)$ does not determine
the block's exponents. Formal decoupling of a nonsemisimple block from the other
eigenspaces produces higher powers of $z$, and for a nilpotent leading term the
Turrittin reduction reaches down into them. The draft's own cubic calculation is
the demonstration. Its reduced rank-two block is
\[
  z^2\partial_z\binom{\widetilde S_3}{\widetilde S_4}
  =\bigl[J_0+zD_0+z^2E_0+O(z^3)\bigr]\binom{\widetilde S_3}{\widetilde S_4},
  \qquad
  J_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
  D_0=\begin{pmatrix}-19/18&0\\0&19/18\end{pmatrix},
\]
with $E_0$ carrying the entry $-8/81$. The ansatz
$\widetilde S_3=z^\rho(1+O(z))$, $\widetilde S_4=cz^{\rho+1}(1+O(z))$ gives
$\rho=2c-19/18$ and $c(\rho+1)=\tfrac{19}{18}c-\tfrac8{81}$, hence
$\rho^2+\rho+\tfrac5{36}=0$ and $\rho=-\tfrac16,-\tfrac56$. The $z^2$ coefficient
is not decoration: setting its $-8/81$ entry to zero leaves
$c(\rho+1)=\tfrac{19}{18}c$, whose solutions are $\rho=1/18$ with $c=5/9$, and
$\rho=-19/18$ with $c=0$ --- the classes $\pm1/18$ modulo $\Z$, which are
eighteenth roots of unity and contribute nothing to $\nu_6$. So a deformation
theory that tracks only $U_i$ and $P_i\mu P_i$ cannot see the primitive-sixth
exponents at all, and any proposed evolution theorem must reproduce this
example. It is the regression test.

\section{An unconditional computation: the count for $X\times\PP^1$}
\label{sec:xp1-unconditional}

This section is independent of Hypothesis~4.7H and of every route discussed
below.  It was originally written inside the refuted endpoint section, which
is the wrong place for a surviving theorem, and is promoted here.

\subsection*{The displacement for a trivial bundle is a pure string shift}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:trivial-displacement}
Let $V=\mathcal O_X^{\oplus2}$. Then the Iritani--Koto initial displacement is
$\varsigma_j^\circ=r\lambda_j=\pm2q^{1/2}$ exactly, a class in $H^0$, with no
tail.
\end{theorem}

Two independent proofs, which is what the claim deserves given that an earlier
version of this memo asserted the opposite.

First, from their (5.11), $\varsigma_j^\circ=r\lambda_j
+[z^{-1}]\log\bigl(q^{c_1(V)/(rz)}F_j(1)\bigr)$. For a trivial bundle
$c_1(V)=0$, so the exponential prefactor is absent, and all Chern roots vanish, so
$F_j(1)$ is a scalar series. A power count in the stationary-phase transform then
shows it has no negative powers of $z$: a factor from $\exp(-\varphi_{\ge3}/z)$
contributes $z^{-a}$ with $s$-degree at least $3a$, the remaining tail contributes
$z^{+b}$ with $b\ge0$, and Gaussian pairing of total $s$-degree $m$ contributes
$z^{m/2}$, so the net power is at least $a/2+b\ge0$. The $z^{-1}$ coefficient is
therefore zero. The $O(q^{-1/r})$ that appears in the sources is a bound on
$F_j(1)$, not a nonvanishing statement about $\varsigma_j^\circ$; conflating the
two is what the earlier version did.

Second, and needing none of that: $\PP(\mathcal O_X^{\oplus2})=X\times\PP^1$, and
the small quantum cohomology of a product is the tensor product of the factors'.
So $E\star=E_X\star\otimes1+1\otimes E_{\PP^1}\star$, and the generalized
eigenspace for the cluster at $r\lambda_j$ is $H^*(X)\otimes v_j$ for $v_j$ the
$j$-th eigenline of $E_{\PP^1}\star$. The grading operator compresses to
$\mu_X+\operatorname{tr}(P_j\mu_{\PP^1})$, and those numbers are degree zero,
permuted by the deck action $q^{1/r}\mapsto e^{2\pi i/r}q^{1/r}$, hence equal, and
they sum to $\operatorname{tr}\mu=0$, hence vanish. The $j$-th summand is
$z^2\partial_z=(U_X+r\lambda_j)-z\mu_X$, which is the cubic's quantum connection
at the bulk parameter $r\lambda_j\cdot1\in H^0$. Since generalized eigenspaces are
canonical rather than chosen, this pins $\varsigma_j^\circ=r\lambda_j$ with no
appeal to the transform at all.

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:endpoint-value}
$\nu_6(X\times\PP^1,0)=4$, as a computed fact at a parameter that indisputably
exists.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
An $H^0$ shift changes the connection by $(\tau^0/z)\cdot\mathrm{id}$, which
shifts every exponential factor uniformly and changes no block's regular part.
This is exact and needs no smallness, which matters because
$\pm2q^{1/2}$ is of unit order. So each summand contributes the cubic's value at
the origin, which is $2$ by the block computation of
Section~\ref{sec:rigid}.
\end{proof}


\section{The coalesced block is rigid}
\label{sec:rigid}

This section closes the residual gap of Section~\ref{sec:hyzz} for a block of
the cubic's type. The route is the one Section~\ref{sec:not-yet} said was
needed: deform the \emph{full} formal normal form rather than the compressed
pair. The device that makes it finite is to shear first. After the shearing the
block is regular singular, its residue already contains the $z^2$ coefficient
that the regression test showed to be load-bearing, and --- this is the point
--- the bulk connection loses its pole, so the residue moves by conjugation
alone.

\subsection*{Setting}

Let $\Lambda$ be the coefficient field of the small connection, containing
$r=(3q)^{1/2}$ and its inverse, and let $B=\Lambda[[\tau]]$ be the formal even
bulk germ in finitely many even coordinates $\tau_a$. Write the quantum
connection in the conventions of Section~\ref{sec:blockwise},
\[
  z\partial_zY=\bigl(z^{-1}U-\mu\bigr)Y,
  \qquad
  \partial_aY=-z^{-1}C_aY,
\]
with $U=E\star_\tau$, $C_a=\phi_a\star_\tau$ and $\mu$ constant, so that
Lemma~\ref{lem:flatness-ids} holds: $[U,C_a]=0$ and
$\partial_aU=C_a+[C_a,\mu]$. Everything below is formal in $\tau$ and needs no
completion of the loop coordinate, no ordered receiver, and no bulk gauge.

There is one standing hypothesis, verified for the cubic at $\tau=0$:

\begin{itemize}
\item[(H1)] $U(0)$ has an eigenvalue $u_0$ whose generalized eigenspace $H_0$
  has rank two and on which the nilpotent part is nonzero; every other
  eigenvalue differs from $u_0$ by a unit of $\Lambda$.
\end{itemize}

For the cubic, (H1) holds with $u_0=0$ and the other eigenvalues $\pm6r$.

Earlier versions of this memo carried a second standing hypothesis (H2): that
after the shearing of Step~4 the block is regular singular at $\tau=0$, that is
$f(0)=0$ in the notation there. It was verified for the cubic by inspection,
because the draft's $D_0$ is diagonal. It is now a theorem for every rank-two
coalesced block of every smooth projective target
(Theorem~\ref{thm:h2-automatic}), proved from the Frobenius property of quantum
multiplication, and it holds pointwise on the germ rather than only at the base
point. Nothing below assumes it.

\subsection*{Step 1: decoupling, and the bulk connection stays block diagonal}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:decouple}
There exists a gauge $g=I+O(z)$ over $B$ block-diagonalizing the $z$-connection
with respect to the generalized eigenspaces of $U$. It is \emph{not} unique: two
such gauges differ exactly by a block-diagonal gauge $k=I+O(z)$, and that is the
whole freedom. In the gauged frame the bulk connection is block diagonal as well,
and its leading coefficient on $H_0$ is $C_{a,0}=P_0C_aP_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Existence is the splitting lemma at Poincar\'e rank one, solved order by order by
Sylvester equations whose operators are invertible because the eigenvalue
differences are units. For the freedom: if $g$ block-diagonalizes and $k=I+O(z)$
is block diagonal then $gk$ block-diagonalizes and is again $I+O(z)$; conversely
if $g$ and $g'$ both block-diagonalize then $g^{-1}g'$ preserves the block
decomposition of the gauged connection and is therefore block diagonal.

An earlier version of this memo asserted that $g=I+O(z)$ is \emph{unique}. That is
false --- the block-diagonal freedom just described is exactly the
counterexample --- and the error mattered, because
Lemma~\ref{lem:duality-gauge} argued by uniqueness. A second attempt normalized
$g$ as the exponential of a purely off-block generator; that does cut down the
freedom, but it is not needed, and the argument below is both shorter and
independent of any choice of slice. For the second statement write
the gauged pair as $\widetilde{\mathcal A}=z^{-1}U+O(1)$, block diagonal, and
$\widetilde{\mathcal C}_a=z^{-1}\check C_a(z)$ with
$\check C_a=\check C^{(0)}+z\check C^{(1)}+\cdots$, and expand the flatness
identity
\[
  \partial_a\widetilde{\mathcal A}
  =-z\partial_z\widetilde{\mathcal C}_a
  +[\widetilde{\mathcal A},\widetilde{\mathcal C}_a].
  \tag{8.1}
  \label{eq:flat-pair}
\]
Its off-block part in bidegree $(i,j)$, $i\ne j$, at order $z^{-2}$ involves the
Sylvester operator
\[
  X\longmapsto U_iX-XU_j=(u_i-u_j)X+N_iX-XN_j,
\]
\emph{not} multiplication by $u_i-u_j$: the nilpotent parts must be carried, and
an earlier version of this proof dropped them. Nothing is lost, because
$u_i-u_j$ is a unit and $X\mapsto N_iX-XN_j$ is nilpotent, so the sum is
invertible; the same operator governs each successive order.

What this yields, however, is \emph{not} that the bulk connection is block
diagonal, and an earlier version claimed that on the ground that the left side of
the off-block equation vanishes. It does not vanish: the projectors move with the
bulk parameter, $\partial_aP_i\ne0$. The correct statement is that the off-block
part of the gauged bulk connection in bidegree $(i,j)$ is exactly
$-P_i(\partial_aP_j)P_j$, so the bulk connection \emph{preserves each $H_i$ as a
subbundle} rather than being block diagonal on the nose. That is what the later
steps use --- the connection form $\Gamma_a$ of Step~4 is written in the true
version --- and the leading coefficient on $H_0$ is $C_{a,0}=P_0C_aP_0$ as
stated, since $g=I+O(z)$ leaves the leading term unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The invariant does not depend on the choices just made]
\label{rem:gauge-independence}
Because the splitting gauge is explicitly non-canonical, and is corrected further
in Lemma~\ref{lem:duality-gauge}, it is worth recording that the object
Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} freezes is insensitive to both choices. Changing the
splitting gauge by a block-diagonal $k=I+O(z)$ and changing the frame within a
block replace the sheared residue $R$ by $S^{-1}RS$ with $S$ regular and
invertible at $z=0$, so $R$ moves by conjugation alone and $\det(XI-R)$ is
unchanged.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Step 2: the exponential factor, and the commutant}

Put $u_0(\tau)=\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}\bigl(U|_{H_0}\bigr)$ and
$N:=U|_{H_0}-u_0I$, so $N$ is traceless and $N(0)\ne0$ is nilpotent. Twisting
the block by $\exp(u_0/z)$ removes its exponential factor; this changes neither
the regular-singular part nor the framed monodromy. The twist replaces the
leading bulk coefficient $C_{a,0}$ by $C_{a,0}-(\partial_au_0)I$.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:commutant}
$C_{a,0}=p_aI+q_aN$ with $p_a,q_a\in B$, and $p_a=\partial_au_0$. After the
twist the leading bulk coefficient on the block is $q_aN$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$[U,C_a]=0$ gives $[U|_{H_0},C_{a,0}]=0$. A $2\times2$ matrix over a local ring
whose reduction is non-scalar is regular, and $U|_{H_0}$ reduces to
$u_0(0)I+N(0)$ with $N(0)\ne0$; the commutant of a regular $2\times2$ matrix is
free of rank two on $I$ and the matrix itself, hence equals
$B\cdot I\oplus B\cdot N$. Taking traces in $\partial_aU=C_a+[C_a,\mu]$ on the
block gives $2\partial_au_0=\operatorname{tr}C_{a,0}=2p_a$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Step 3: the eigenvalue never splits}

\begin{theorem}[No splitting]
\label{thm:no-splitting}
$\det N\equiv0$ on $B$. Consequently $N$ is nilpotent at every point of the
formal germ, the block keeps a single eigenvalue of multiplicity two, and by
(H1) it is a single Jordan block.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $d:=\det N\in B$; by (H1), $d(0)=0$. From
Theorem~\ref{thm:block-evolution} the block leading operator evolves by
$D_aU_0=C_{a,0}+[C_{a,0},\mu_0]$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:commutant}
\[
  D_aN=D_aU_0-(\partial_au_0)I=q_aN+q_a[N,\mu_0].
\]
For a traceless $2\times2$ matrix $\operatorname{adj}(N)=-N$ and
$N^2=-dI$, so
\[
  \partial_ad=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\operatorname{adj}(N)\,D_aN\bigr)
  =-q_a\operatorname{tr}(N^2)-q_a\operatorname{tr}\bigl(N[N,\mu_0]\bigr)
  =2q_ad,
\]
using $\operatorname{tr}(N[N,\mu_0])=\operatorname{tr}(N^2\mu_0)
-\operatorname{tr}(N\mu_0N)=0$. Now suppose $d\ne0$ and let $m\ge1$ be the
order of its lowest nonvanishing homogeneous part $d_m$ in $\tau$. The right
side of $\partial_ad=2q_ad$ has order at least $m$, while the left side has
order $m-1$ with homogeneous part $\partial_ad_m$. Hence $\partial_ad_m=0$ for
every $a$, so $d_m=0$, a contradiction. Note $d$ is basis independent, so no
choice of block frame enters.
\end{proof}

This is the falsifier of Section~\ref{sec:not-yet} answered: the reconstruction
displacement cannot split the block that carries $\nu_6$. It also shows the
Kato frame is not needed for this step, since $\det N$ is frame independent.

\subsection*{Step 4: shear}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:no-splitting}, $N^2=0$ and $N\ne0$, so $\ker N=\operatorname{im}N$
is a free rank-one submodule; choose a frame $(e_1,e_2)$ of $H_0$ with
$Ne_1=0$, $Ne_2=\nu e_1$, $\nu\in B^\times$, depending formally on $\tau$, and
let $\Gamma_a$ be the resulting frame connection, which has no pole in $z$.
Apply $S=\operatorname{diag}(1,z)$ and write the sheared pair as
\[
  z\partial_z\widehat Y=\widehat{\mathcal A}\widehat Y,\quad
  \widehat{\mathcal A}=z^{-1}A_{-1}+A_0+zA_1+\cdots,
  \qquad
  \partial_a\widehat Y=-\widehat{\mathcal C}_a\widehat Y,\quad
  \widehat{\mathcal C}_a=z^{-1}K_a+G_a+\cdots.
\]
Conjugation by $S$ sends $E_{12}\mapsto zE_{12}$, $E_{21}\mapsto z^{-1}E_{21}$
and fixes diagonal matrices, and $-S^{-1}z\partial_zS=-\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$.
Writing the decoupled block connection before shearing as
$z^{-1}N+A_0'+zA_1'+\cdots$, one gets
\[
  A_{-1}=f\,E_{21},\quad f:=(A_0')_{21},
  \qquad
  A_0=R:=\nu E_{12}+\operatorname{diag}\bigl((A_0')_{11},(A_0')_{22}-1\bigr)
        +(A_1')_{21}E_{21}.
  \tag{8.2}
  \label{eq:sheared-data}
\]
On the bulk side, the leading coefficient $q_aN=q_a\nu E_{12}$ shears
\emph{up} to order $z^0$, and the only surviving pole comes from the $(2,1)$
entry of the regular part:
\[
  K_a=k_aE_{21},
  \qquad
  k_a:=\bigl(\check C^{(1)}_{a,0}+\Gamma_a\bigr)_{21}.
  \tag{8.3}
  \label{eq:sheared-bulk}
\]
There is no $z^{-2}$ term, and this is where Lemma~\ref{lem:commutant} does its
work: a $(2,1)$ entry in $C_{a,0}$ would have produced one, and the commutant
forbids it.

For the cubic, \eqref{eq:sheared-data} reproduces the draft's own numbers. With
$\nu=2$, $A_0'=D_0=\operatorname{diag}(-19/18,19/18)$ and $(A_1')_{21}=-8/81$,
\[
  f=0,\qquad
  R=\begin{pmatrix}-19/18&2\\-8/81&1/18\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \operatorname{tr}R=-1,\quad\det R=\tfrac5{36},
\]
so the characteristic polynomial of $R$ is exactly $\rho^2+\rho+5/36$ and its
roots are $-1/6$ and $-5/6$. The $z^2$ coefficient enters through
$(A_1')_{21}$, so the regression test of Section~\ref{sec:not-yet} is met by
construction rather than by accident: the object being deformed below is $R$,
which sees it.

\subsection*{Step 5: the Poincar\'e pairing survives the decoupling, and kills
$f$}

The shearing of Step~4 produces a pole exactly when $f=(A_0')_{21}\ne0$.
Earlier versions assumed $f(0)=0$ as a standing hypothesis, verified for the
cubic by inspecting the draft's diagonal $D_0$. It is not a hypothesis: it is
forced by the Frobenius property of quantum multiplication, at every point of
the germ and for every smooth projective target. The only thing needed is that
the decoupling gauge of Step~1 preserves the pairing, which is what the
following lemma supplies --- the step the earlier list of open questions
identified as missing.

Let $G$ be the Poincar\'e pairing on the even part, symmetric and
nondegenerate. Quantum multiplication is Frobenius, so $U=E\star$ is
$G$-self-adjoint, and $\mu$ is $G$-anti-self-adjoint. Writing
$\mathcal A(z)=z^{-1}U-\mu$, those two facts are exactly
\[
  \mathcal A(-z)^TG+G\,\mathcal A(z)=0.
  \tag{8.4}
  \label{eq:duality}
\]

\begin{lemma}[The splitting gauge can be chosen to be an isometry]
\label{lem:duality-gauge}
The block-splitting gauge of Lemma~\ref{lem:decouple} may be corrected by a
block-diagonal gauge so that $g(-z)^TG\,g(z)=G$. It then still block-diagonalizes,
so the decoupled connection satisfies \eqref{eq:duality} with the same constant
pairing, and so does each block with the restriction of $G$ to it, before and
after the twist by $\exp(u_0/z)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $g=I+O(z)$ be any block-splitting gauge and set
$\widetilde G(z):=g(-z)^TG\,g(z)$, so that $\widetilde G(0)=G$. Three facts.

\emph{(i) $\widetilde G$ obeys the transported duality.} Writing
$\widetilde{\mathcal A}=g\cdot\mathcal A$ and using
$\mathcal A(-z)^TG+G\mathcal A(z)=0$ together with the invariance of $z\partial_z$
under $z\mapsto-z$, a direct computation gives
\[
  \widetilde{\mathcal A}(-z)^T\widetilde G+\widetilde G\,\widetilde{\mathcal A}(z)
  +z\partial_z\widetilde G=0,
  \tag{8.4a}
  \label{eq:duality-transported}
\]
the two gauge terms combining into $z\partial_z\widetilde G$ exactly.

\emph{(ii) $\widetilde G$ is block diagonal.} Take the $(i,j)$ off-block component
of \eqref{eq:duality-transported} with $i\ne j$. Since
$\widetilde{\mathcal A}$ is block diagonal with
$\widetilde{\mathcal A}_i=z^{-1}U_i+O(1)$, the coefficient of $z^{-1}$ reads
the Sylvester operator
$Z\mapsto(u_j-u_i)Z+ZN_j-\widehat N_iZ$, a unit plus a nilpotent and therefore
invertible with the nilpotent parts carried inside it rather than discarded. The
term $z\partial_z\widetilde G$ does not interfere and in particular creates no
resonance: at order $z^{n-1}$ it contributes $(n-1)\widetilde G_{n-1}$, which
sits on the right-hand side and involves only a lower coefficient, so the operator
acting on $\widetilde G_n$ is the one just displayed, with no integer added to the
eigenvalue difference. Hence $\widetilde G_{ij}=0$ order by order. The case $n=0$
of the same recursion gives, for free, that the constant pairing $G$ is itself
block diagonal.

\emph{(iii) A block-diagonal correction makes it constant.} From the definition,
$\widetilde G(z)^T=g(z)^TG\,g(-z)=\widetilde G(-z)$, so writing
$\widetilde G=\sum_n\widetilde G_nz^n$ we get $\widetilde G_n^T=(-1)^n\widetilde
G_n$: even coefficients symmetric, odd ones antisymmetric, with
$\widetilde G_0=G$. Seek a block-diagonal $k=I+O(z)$ with
$k(-z)^T\widetilde G(z)k(z)=G$. At order $z$ the equation is
$M-M^T=-\widetilde G_1$ with $M=Gk_1$, solvable by $M=-\tfrac12\widetilde G_1$
precisely because $\widetilde G_1$ is antisymmetric, and $k_1=G^{-1}M$ is block
diagonal because $\widetilde G_1$ and $G$ are. The induction closes at every
order for a uniform reason: setting $H(z):=k(-z)^T\widetilde G(z)k(z)$ for any
$k$, the same computation that gave $\widetilde G(z)^T=\widetilde G(-z)$ gives
$H(z)^T=H(-z)$, so the order-$n$ obstruction $\Omega_n$ carries parity $(-1)^n$
automatically and $M_n=-\tfrac12\Omega_n$ solves it. The factor of two is the
only arithmetic used, so characteristic zero --- or merely
$\operatorname{char}\ne2$ --- is required, which holds here. Replacing $g$ by $gk$ leaves it
block-splitting, by Lemma~\ref{lem:decouple}, and makes it an isometry.

Restriction to a block is legitimate because $G$ restricts nondegenerately to
each block, the generalized eigenspaces of a $G$-self-adjoint operator being
$G$-orthogonal. The twist by $\exp(u_0/z)$ subtracts the scalar $z^{-1}u_0I$ from
the block connection, and \eqref{eq:duality} is insensitive to that because the
subtracted term is odd in $z$ and self-adjoint.

\emph{Remark on the earlier argument.} Two previous versions tried to prove that
the splitting gauge \emph{is} an isometry, by showing that its adjoint partner
$h=G^{-1}g(-z)^{-T}G$ coincides with it. That cannot be had from uniqueness: $h$
need only differ from $g$ by a block-diagonal gauge. The correct statement is the
one above --- the transported pairing is block diagonal, and a block-diagonal
correction, formally a square root, restores it --- and it needs no normalization
of $g$ at all.
\end{proof}

Expanding \eqref{eq:duality} for the twisted decoupled block connection
$z^{-1}N+A_0'+zA_1'+\cdots$ coefficient by coefficient gives the parity rule
\[
  N^TG_0=G_0N,
  \qquad
  (A_p')^TG_0=(-1)^{p+1}G_0A_p',
  \tag{8.5}
  \label{eq:parity}
\]
that is, $N$ and the odd coefficients are $G_0$-self-adjoint and the even
coefficients are $G_0$-anti-self-adjoint. The $p=0$ case is the tracelessness
statement of Section~\ref{sec:blockwise}, now with the decoupling correction
included rather than only for $P_i\mu P_i$.

\begin{theorem}[The sheared block is regular singular at every point]
\label{thm:h2-automatic}
Under (H1), $f=(A_0')_{21}=0$ identically on $B$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:parity}, $N$ is $G_0$-self-adjoint, and $N^2=0$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:no-splitting}, so for all $x,y$ one has
$(Nx,Ny)=(x,N^2y)=0$: the image of $N$ is isotropic. In particular
$(e_1,e_1)=0$, and nondegeneracy of $G_0$ then forces $(e_1,e_2)\ne0$. Again by
\eqref{eq:parity}, $A_0'$ is $G_0$-anti-self-adjoint, and for a symmetric form
that gives $(A_0'x,x)=0$ for every $x$. Taking $x=e_1$ and expanding
$A_0'e_1=\alpha e_1+fe_2$ gives $f\,(e_2,e_1)=0$, hence $f=0$.
\end{proof}

This is stronger than the deleted hypothesis in two ways. It holds pointwise on
the germ, with no differential equation and no lowest-order argument, which is
what a statement outside the germ needs; and it holds for every rank-two
coalesced block of every smooth projective target, so the cubic's diagonal
$D_0$ was not an accident of that example.

\subsection*{Step 6: no irregularity appears, and the bulk connection is regular}

\begin{theorem}[The sheared block stays regular singular]
\label{thm:no-irregularity}
$k_a\equiv0$ on $B$. Hence at every point of the formal germ the block is
regular singular with residue $R$, and in the sheared frame the bulk connection
has no pole in $z$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expand \eqref{eq:flat-pair} for the sheared pair. The coefficient of $z^{-2}$ is
$[A_{-1},K_a]=[fE_{21},k_aE_{21}]=0$, which is vacuous. The coefficient of
$z^{-1}$ is
\[
  \partial_a(f)E_{21}
  =k_aE_{21}+f\,[E_{21},G_a]+k_a\operatorname{ad}_R(E_{21}).
\]
Writing $R=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\nu\\\gamma&\delta\end{pmatrix}$ one computes
$\operatorname{ad}_R(E_{21})
=\begin{pmatrix}\nu&0\\\delta-\alpha&-\nu\end{pmatrix}$, whose diagonal is
$\operatorname{diag}(\nu,-\nu)$ and does not involve $\gamma$. The left side is
off-diagonal, so comparing diagonal entries gives
$k_a\nu=-f\,\bigl([E_{21},G_a]\bigr)_{11}$, and since $\nu$ is a unit,
$k_a=f\,h_a$ for some $h_a\in B$. Comparing $(2,1)$ entries then gives
\[
  \partial_af=f\Bigl(h_a\bigl(1+\delta-\alpha\bigr)
  +\bigl([E_{21},G_a]\bigr)_{21}\Bigr),
\]
a linear equation $\partial_af=f\,w_a$, which is now redundant:
Theorem~\ref{thm:h2-automatic} already gives $f\equiv0$ outright, and
$k_a=f\,h_a$ then gives $k_a\equiv0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the flatness route still buys]
The displayed equation $\partial_af=f\,w_a$ is kept because it is the only
place where the argument would survive the loss of the Frobenius property: with
$f(0)=0$ assumed rather than proved, the lowest-order argument of
Theorem~\ref{thm:no-splitting} applies verbatim and yields the same conclusion.
In any setting where the Frobenius input is unavailable, or for a block of a
connection that is not the quantum connection of a smooth projective target,
that is the route to use, at the cost of carrying the hypothesis.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Step 7: the exponents are constant}

\begin{theorem}[Rigidity of the formal type]
\label{thm:rigidity}
$\partial_aR=[R,G_a]$ for every bulk direction $a$. Hence the characteristic
polynomial of $R$ has coefficients in $\Lambda$, constant in $\tau$, and the
primitive-sixth multiplicity of the block is constant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Claiming only what the count needs]
\label{rem:no-levelt}
An earlier version added that the formal monodromy of the block is conjugate to
$\exp(2\pi iR)$ at every point of the formal germ. That is more than is needed and
more than is proved: in a \emph{resonant} regular-singular system the passage from
the residue to the full formal monodromy requires a Levelt normal form, and higher
resonant terms can affect the unipotent part. What $\nu_6$ counts is the
multiplicity of the primitive sixth roots of unity among the eigenvalues, and
that is read off the characteristic polynomial of $R$ alone, which
Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} freezes. For the cubic block there is in any case no
resonance --- the exponents $-1/6$ and $-5/6$ differ by $2/3$, not by an integer
--- so the stronger statement happens to hold there; but the argument does not
need it and should not assert it in general.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:no-irregularity} the sheared pair has $A_{-1}=0$ and
$K_a=0$. The coefficient of $z^0$ in \eqref{eq:flat-pair} is
$\partial_aA_0=[A_{-1},H_a]+[A_0,G_a]+[A_1,K_a]$, which collapses to
$\partial_aR=[R,G_a]$. A matrix moving by an infinitesimal conjugation has
constant characteristic polynomial: $\partial_a\det(XI-R)=0$ entrywise. Since
$\widehat{\mathcal A}=R+O(z)$, the block is regular singular and its formal
monodromy is conjugate to $\exp(2\pi iR)$; shearing changed the exponents by
integers only, which $\nu_6$ does not see.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The residual gap, closed at the cubic]
\label{cor:cubic-closed}
For a smooth cubic threefold, the framed formal monodromy of the zero block has
$\nu_6=2$ at every point of the formal even bulk germ, in particular at any
bulk parameter $\tau^\bullet$ in the positive filtration. The statement of
Section~\ref{sec:hyzz} therefore holds for the cubic ambient summand.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Combine Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} with the computation after
\eqref{eq:sheared-data}: the characteristic polynomial is $\rho^2+\rho+5/36$
everywhere, with roots $-1/6,-5/6$, whose monodromy eigenvalues
$e^{\mp\pi i/3}$ are primitive sixth roots of unity. Since the coefficients of
the characteristic polynomial are constants of $\Lambda$, evaluation at a
topologically nilpotent $\tau^\bullet$ is legitimate and returns the same
polynomial; no convergence of a bulk gauge is involved.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Three remarks}

\begin{remark}[Why this is not the compressed-pair argument]
The deformed object is $R$ of \eqref{eq:sheared-data}, not the pair
$(U_0,\mu_0)$. The shearing has folded the $z^2$ coefficient of the unsheared
block into the residue, so the failure mode of Section~\ref{sec:not-yet} ---
deleting $-8/81$ and moving the exponents to $\pm1/18$ --- cannot occur here:
that deletion changes $R$ itself, hence its characteristic polynomial, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} freezes exactly that polynomial.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to Cai's Proposition 6]
Cai states the $\pm1/6$ exponents for the \emph{big} quantum connection and
proves it by constructing $M=I+\sum M_n t^n$ with $\partial_{t_i}M=-z^{-1}P_iM$,
so that $M^{-1}\nabla_{t_i}M=\partial_{t_i}$ and $M^{-1}\nabla_zM$ is bulk
independent. That gauge is precisely the pro-Laurent object of
Lemma~4.5 and of Iritani--Koto's Section 5.8: per bulk degree it is polynomial
in $z^{-1}$, unbounded only after summing bulk degrees. Over
$\Lambda((z))[[t]]$, with the bulk kept formal, that argument is sound and
proves the same conclusion, because $M$ is fixed by the turn and
Theorem~\ref{thm:transport} applies with the bulk variables as formal
parameters. What it does not do by itself is license evaluation at a
\emph{specialized} bulk parameter with Novikov coefficients, which is where the
receiver discussion of Section~\ref{sec:receiver} came from. The argument above
avoids the gauge altogether and produces a $\tau$-constant characteristic
polynomial, so specialization is a substitution into constants.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{The Serre-decoration route, and why it is not taken}

Katzarkov--Kontsevich--Pantev--Yu's Example~6.21 computes the cubic's
atomic composition and separates the zero atom by its Serre
automorphism, concluding irrationality in dimension three.  Extending
that to one stabilization needs surfaces excluded as well.  The route is
archived rather than pursued: the decoration it turns on is defined for
complex analytic $F$-bundles, while the $A$-model $F$-bundle of a
projective variety is non-archimedean analytic, and in that setting the
authors defer the Euler pairing and the Serre automorphism themselves to
forthcoming work.  Its criterion is unnumbered prose, and it mixes
formal, non-archimedean analytic and complex analytic statements.

\subsection*{Arbitrary Jordan size: what extends, and the one thing that does
not}
\label{sec:jordan-size}

Earlier versions of this section closed with the expectation that the mechanism
--- commutant of a regular block kills the would-be $z^{-2}$ bulk term, one
shearing makes the block regular singular, flatness then forces the bulk pole to
vanish --- should extend to a single Jordan block of any size with the shearing
$\operatorname{diag}(1,z,\dots,z^{m-1})$, since that is what a general center
statement needs. Carrying it out separates cleanly into a part that does extend,
a bookkeeping statement, and one step where rank two turns out to be genuinely
special.

\begin{proposition}[Steps 1 to 3 in size $m$]
\label{prop:size-m-steps}
Let $H_0$ be a generalized eigenspace of $U(0)$ of rank $m$ on which the
nilpotent part is regular, the other eigenvalues differing from $u_0$ by units.
Put $u_0=\tfrac1m\operatorname{tr}(U|_{H_0})$ and $N:=U|_{H_0}-u_0I$. Then
\begin{enumerate}
\item $C_{a,0}\in B[N]=B\cdot I\oplus B\cdot N\oplus\cdots\oplus B\cdot N^{m-1}$,
  and the coefficient of $I$ is $\partial_au_0$; after the twist by
  $\exp(u_0/z)$ the leading bulk coefficient lies in $N\cdot B[N]$, hence is
  strictly upper triangular in a cyclic frame, so no entry of it shears down to
  a pole of the bulk connection;
\item $\operatorname{tr}(N^k)\equiv0$ on $B$ for every $k\ge1$. Hence $N$ is
  nilpotent at every point of the formal germ, and regular there because
  $N^{m-1}$ has a unit entry, so the block keeps one eigenvalue of multiplicity
  $m$ and a single Jordan block.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$[U,C_a]=0$ gives $[U|_{H_0},C_{a,0}]=0$, and the commutant of a regular matrix
over a local ring is the free module it generates, which is $B[N]$. Write
$C_{a,0}=p_aI+Q_a$ with $Q_a:=\sum_{j\ge1}q_a^{(j)}N^j\in N\cdot B[N]$; this much
is the commutant statement alone, and the identification of $p_a$ is deferred to
the end.

(2) By Theorem~\ref{thm:block-evolution},
$D_aN=Q_a+[Q_a,\mu_0]$. Put $t_k:=\operatorname{tr}(N^k)$, so $t_1\equiv0$ by
construction. Then
\[
  \partial_at_k=k\operatorname{tr}\bigl(N^{k-1}D_aN\bigr)
  =k\operatorname{tr}\bigl(N^{k-1}Q_a\bigr)
  =k\sum_{j\ge1}q_a^{(j)}t_{k+j-1},
\]
the commutator term dropping because $N^{k-1}$ and $Q_a$ commute and the trace
is cyclic:
$\operatorname{tr}(N^{k-1}Q_a\mu_0)=\operatorname{tr}(N^{k-1}\mu_0Q_a)$. By
Newton's identities the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of $N$ are
polynomials without constant term in $t_1,\dots,t_m$, so Cayley--Hamilton puts
every $t_l$ with $l>m$ in the ideal generated by $t_2,\dots,t_m$. The vector
$t=(t_2,\dots,t_m)$ therefore satisfies a linear system $\partial_at=M_at$ with
$M_a$ over $B$, and $t(0)=0$ because $N(0)$ is nilpotent. The lowest-order
argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:no-splitting} applies to the vector unchanged, so
$t\equiv0$, and in characteristic zero the vanishing of all power traces forces
the characteristic polynomial of $N$ to be $\lambda^m$.

(1) With $\operatorname{tr}N^j\equiv0$ for every $j\ge1$ now available,
$\operatorname{tr}C_{a,0}=m\,p_a$, and taking traces in
$\partial_aU=C_a+[C_a,\mu]$ on the block gives $m\,\partial_au_0=m\,p_a$. The
twist by $\exp(u_0/z)$ replaces $C_{a,0}$ by $C_{a,0}-(\partial_au_0)I=Q_a$,
which lies in $N\cdot B[N]$ and so has no entry on or below the diagonal.
\end{proof}

The shearing bookkeeping is the next thing to fix, because it decides what has
to be proved. Conjugation by $S=\operatorname{diag}(1,z,\dots,z^{m-1})$
multiplies the $(i,j)$ entry by $z^{j-i}$, so writing the twisted decoupled
block connection as $z^{-1}N+A_0'+zA_1'+\cdots$, the coefficient of $z^{-k}$ in
the sheared connection collects the entries of $A_p'$ at sub-diagonal distance
$i-j=p+k$, and $z^{-1}N$ shears up to the residue. Hence:

\begin{quote}
the sheared block is regular singular exactly when $A_p'$ vanishes strictly
below its $p$-th sub-diagonal, for every $p\ge0$.
\end{quote}

For $m=2$ this is the single condition $f=(A_0')_{21}=0$. For $m\ge3$ it is a
nested family, deepest first: $(A_0')_{m1}$, then $(A_0')_{m-1,1}$,
$(A_0')_{m,2}$ and $(A_1')_{m,1}$, and so on.

\begin{proposition}[Duality in size $m$, and its limit]
\label{prop:size-m-duality}
In the cyclic frame $e_i=N^{m-i}e_m$ the Gram matrix of $G_0$ is anti-triangular
Hankel: $(e_i,e_j)$ depends only on $i+j$ and vanishes for $i+j\le m$, so
$G_0=J\,P(N)$ with $J$ the reversal matrix and $P(N)\in B[N]$ invertible.
Writing $X^{\mathrm{at}}:=JX^TJ$ for the reflection in the anti-diagonal, the
parity rule \eqref{eq:parity} becomes
\[
  (A_p')^{\mathrm{at}}=(-1)^{p+1}\,P\,A_p'\,P^{-1}.
\]
The reflection preserves each sub-diagonal, and conjugation by $P$ acts trivially
on the deepest one, so $(A_p')_{m1}=0$ for $p$ even and is unconstrained for $p$
odd; on the next sub-diagonal the rule only exchanges the two entries with each
other.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Self-adjointness of $N$ gives
$(e_i,e_j)=(N^{m-i}e_m,N^{m-j}e_m)=(e_m,N^{2m-i-j}e_m)$, which depends only on
$i+j$ and vanishes when $2m-i-j\ge m$; the anti-diagonal value
$(e_m,N^{m-1}e_m)$ is a unit by nondegeneracy. Collecting anti-diagonals gives
$G_0=\sum_{t\ge0}g_{m+1+t}JN^t=J\,P(N)$. Substituting into
$(A_p')^TG_0=(-1)^{p+1}G_0A_p'$ and multiplying by $J$ on the left gives the
displayed rule, using $J^2=I$. The reflection sends $(i,j)$ to $(m+1-j,m+1-i)$
and so preserves $i-j$; and in $PA_p'P^{-1}$ every correction term is of the
form $N^aA_p'N^b$ with $a+b\ge1$, whose $(m,1)$ entry is $(A_p')_{m+a,1-b}$ and
therefore out of range. At distance $m-1$ the rule thus reads
$(A_p')_{m1}=(-1)^{p+1}(A_p')_{m1}$.
\end{proof}

So duality removes exactly one pole coefficient, the deepest, $z^{-(m-1)}$. In
rank two that coefficient is the entire pole, which is why
Theorem~\ref{thm:h2-automatic} closes the case; for $m\ge3$ it is one condition
out of a growing family. The expectation that the rank-two mechanism extends as
it stands was therefore too optimistic at exactly this step, and the extension
needs a different input at the base point.

An earlier version of this memo proposed the grading as that input, on the
ground that since $\mu$ is the grading operator and $E\star$ raises degree by
two, $[\mu,U]=U$, making $H_0$ for the eigenvalue zero $\mu$-invariant with
$[\mu,N]=N$; in a $\mu$-homogeneous cyclic frame an operator of $\mu$-weight $p$
is supported exactly on the $p$-th sub-diagonal, so ``$A_p'$ has $\mu$-weight
$p$'' would \emph{be} the regular-singularity condition, with nothing left to
check entry by entry.

\emph{That route is withdrawn: the identity is false over a Novikov ring.} The
operator $\mu$ implements only the cohomological half of the grading, whereas
the homogeneity that makes $E\star$ raise degree by two is with respect to the
total grading, which includes $\deg Q^d=2c_1(X)\cdot d$. On the $Q^d$-part the
correct rule is
\[
  [\mu,U]_{ij}=(1-c_1\cdot d)\,U_{ij},
\]
so $[\mu,U]=U$ holds only in Novikov degree zero. The cubic refutes it on its own
matrices: with $\mu=-G_X=\operatorname{diag}(-3/2,-1/2,1/2,3/2)$ and $U=K_X$ the
small even connection matrix of the draft's (4.9a), the entry $(2,1)$ gives
$[\mu,U]_{21}=2=U_{21}$, but the entry $(1,2)$ gives
$[\mu,U]_{12}=-12q\ne U_{12}=12q$. The drawn consequence fails there too: the
zero generalized eigenspace is
$H_0=\operatorname{span}_\Lambda\{P^3-6qP,\;P^2-21q\cdot1\}$, and applying $\mu$
to the first generator gives $3qP+\tfrac32P^3$, which is not in $H_0$. So the
cubic is not the rank-two instance of a $\mu$-grading mechanism, and the
appearance that the deleted hypothesis (H2) was explained by one was an
artifact of the false identity --- (H2) is explained instead, and correctly, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:h2-automatic}, from the Frobenius property.

Nothing proved in this memo used the identity: Section~\ref{sec:rigid},
Steps~1--7, uses only $[U,C_a]=0$, $\partial_aU=C_a+[C_a,\mu]$, the commutant of
a regular $2\times2$ matrix, and the Frobenius input, so
Theorem~\ref{thm:rigidity} and Corollary~\ref{cor:cubic-closed} are untouched.
What is lost is the proposed route for $m\ge3$. No replacement input at the base
point is currently identified; a correct version would have to work with the
total grading, in which $Q$ carries degree, rather than with $\mu$ alone.

None of this covers a derogatory block, where the commutant is larger than
$B[N]$ and Proposition~\ref{prop:size-m-steps} fails at its first step, nor a
semisimple block, where the $z^{-1}$ equation instead reads
$(I+\operatorname{ad}_R)(C_{a,0}-p_aI)=0$ and a resonance $\rho_i-\rho_j=-1$
must be excluded before the same conclusion follows.

\section{The one-stabilization theorem, and the one step it still needs}
\label{sec:endpoint}

Section~\ref{sec:rigid} closes the residual gap for the cubic block, but the
draft's own presentation needs the same statement at every intermediate fourfold
of a weak factorization, and that is a statement about arbitrary blocks. This
section changes where the bookkeeping happens. In the atom formulation the
intermediate fourfolds never appear, because the weak-factorization ledger is an
identity in the free abelian group on atoms. That removes the transport problem
completely. It introduces one requirement of its own, which the first draft of
this section wrongly called standard: the invariant must be well defined on
atoms, and an atom is a geometric atomic $F$-bundle modulo isomorphism
\emph{and} modulo change of base point within a connected component of the
spectral cover. The second equivalence is where the argument is still open, and
Section~\ref{sec:endpoint-gap} states exactly why.

\subsection*{The invariant}

For a smooth projective $T$ and a point $b$ of the even base of its A-model
$F$-bundle, let $\operatorname{Mon}(T,b)$ be the multiset of eigenvalues of the
framed monodromy after Levelt--Turrittin separation, and
\[
  \nu_6(T,b):=\#\bigl\{\zeta\in\operatorname{Mon}(T,b)
  :\zeta=e^{\pi i/3}\ \text{or}\ e^{-\pi i/3}\bigr\},
\]
with algebraic multiplicity. The same definitions apply to a summand of the
spectral decomposition, in particular to a geometric atomic $F$-bundle. At a
point of the base the coefficient field is $\Lambda(\!(z)\!)$, so
Levelt--Turrittin is available and Section~\ref{sec:lemma} applies with
$\Gamma=0$: no receiver enters anywhere in this section.

\subsection*{Bulk local constancy}

\begin{theorem}[The formal type is constant on the base]
\label{thm:bulk-constancy}
Let $(H,\nabla)$ be the A-model $F$-bundle of a smooth projective $T$ over its
base $B$, and let $b\in B$. Then the framed monodromy at every point of the
formal germ of $B$ at $b$ is conjugate to that at $b$, and likewise on each
summand of the spectral decomposition.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[What this does \emph{not} give]
\label{rem:no-local-constancy}
An earlier version of this theorem continued: ``Consequently $\nu_6$ is locally
constant on $B$, hence constant on each connected component.'' That inference is
unavailable, and the paragraphs after \S\ref{sec:endpoint-gap} explain why: the
gauge realizing the conjugacy is the pro-Laurent object, whose $z$-order is
unbounded below across bulk degrees, so it need not sum at an ordinary nearby
analytic point of $B$. Constancy on a formal germ is not analytic local
constancy, and ``locally constant on $B$, hence constant on each connected
component'' silently upgrades the first to the second. The distinction is exactly
the one that matters downstream, because Katzarkov--Kontsevich--Pantev--Yu
identify geometric atoms whose spectral lifts lie in a common \emph{connected
component}, not merely in a common formal germ. The sentence is deleted.
\end{remark}

\begin{proof}
This is Cai's argument in the proof of his Proposition 6, which deserves to be
stated in this generality. Write $\nabla_{t_i}=\partial_{t_i}+z^{-1}P_i$ for
the bulk directions, with $P_i$ quantum multiplication by $\phi_i$, and solve
\[
  \partial_{t_i}M=-z^{-1}P_iM,\qquad M|_{t=0}=I
\]
recursively for $M=I+\sum_{n}M_nt^n$. The recursion produces
$M_n\in\operatorname{End}(H)\otimes\Lambda[z,z^{-1}]$, with $z$-order bounded
below by $-|n|$ at each fixed bulk degree, so
$M\in\mathrm{GL}\bigl(\operatorname{End}(H)\otimes\Lambda[z,z^{-1}][[t]]\bigr)$;
invertibility is automatic from $M=I+O(t)$. By construction
$M^{-1}\nabla_{t_i}M=\partial_{t_i}$, and since the $\nabla_{t_i}$ commute with
$\nabla_z$, so do their conjugates; hence $M^{-1}\nabla_zM=\partial_z+L$ with
$\partial_{t_i}L=0$, and $L$ is the $z$-connection at $b$ because $M|_{t=0}=I$.

Let $Y$ be a formal fundamental solution at $b$, possibly after a ramification
$z=w^e$, with $\sigma(Y)=YM_{\mathrm f}$ for the turn $\sigma$. Then $MY$ is a
fundamental solution at the deformed parameter. Every entry of $M$ is a Laurent
series in \emph{integral} powers of $z$, so $\sigma(M)=M$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:turn}, and
\[
  \sigma(MY)=M\sigma(Y)=(MY)M_{\mathrm f}.
\]
The framed monodromy is therefore the same matrix in this solution frame, and
well defined up to conjugacy over the constants by
Proposition~\ref{prop:framed-is-turn}. A locally constant integer-valued
function on a connected base is constant.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The gap, stated exactly}
\label{sec:endpoint-gap}

Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk-constancy} is a statement about the \emph{formal} germ: the
deformed connection is gauge equivalent to the one at $b$ over
$\Lambda[z,z^{-1}][[t]]$, with $t$ formal. The atom equivalence asks for more.
Two geometric atomic $F$-bundles of the same variety are identified when their
spectral lifts lie in the same connected component, and two points of one
connected component of a non-archimedean analytic space are not in general
related by a chain of formal germs. What is needed is local constancy in the
analytic topology, and then connectedness.

The obstruction to upgrading is not bookkeeping. It is the same divergence this
memo has been circling from the start. The gauge $M$ of
Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk-constancy} has $z$-order bounded below by $-|n|$ in bulk
degree $n$, so it converges when the bulk parameter is topologically nilpotent
and not otherwise; at a bulk parameter that is merely nearby in the analytic
topology, the sum over bulk degrees has no lower bound in the loop coordinate,
and $M$ is not a gauge in any ring where the turn argument runs. So the
receiver problem of Sections~\ref{sec:lemma} and \ref{sec:receiver} is not
escaped by moving to atoms; it is relocated to the globalization step.

One route to closing it is visible, and it is Section~\ref{sec:rigid} rather
than Cai's gauge. That argument produces the differential identity
$\partial_aR=[R,G_a]$ between honest matrix-valued functions on the base, and a
function with identically vanishing derivative on a smooth connected space in
characteristic zero is locally constant. So the shearing argument globalizes
where the gauge argument does not, and what it needs in exchange is its two
hypotheses at every point of the component: the block must stay a rank-two
Jordan block, and it must stay regular singular after one shearing.
Theorem~\ref{thm:no-splitting} propagates nilpotency and
Theorem~\ref{thm:no-irregularity} propagates regularity, but neither excludes
the locus where the nilpotent part degenerates to zero, which is where the
argument would have to be extended.

\begin{remark}[What the gap does and does not touch]
It does not touch Theorem~\ref{thm:low-dim}: the nef-canonical input there is
pointwise. It does not touch the computation
$\nu_6(\alpha(X))=2$ at the hyperplane point, nor its stability under
specialization, which is Corollary~\ref{cor:cubic-closed}. It touches exactly
the claim that these two facts may be compared as statements about one atom.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Why this is not in tension with the receiver problem]
The gauge $M$ is the pro-Laurent object of Section~\ref{sec:hyzz}, and the whole
receiver discussion of Sections~\ref{sec:lemma} and \ref{sec:receiver} was about
\emph{specializing} the bulk parameter to an element with Novikov coefficients,
where the sum over bulk degrees loses its lower bound in $z$. No such
specialization occurs here: Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk-constancy} compares two points
of one base along a formal germ, where the bulk parameters are formal variables
and $M$ has Laurent coefficients degree by degree. That is exactly the
difference between the two formulations, and it is why the atom route is free of
the criterion $e\,w(\Delta\lambda)<\varepsilon$.
Section~\ref{sec:rigid} remains the stronger statement where it applies, since
it produces a characteristic polynomial constant in $\tau$ and so survives
specialization; Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk-constancy} is the general one.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{The invariant descends to atoms}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:nu6-atom}
$\nu_6$ is invariant under isomorphism of geometric atomic $F$-bundles. It is
well defined on atoms if and only if it is in addition constant on connected
components of the spectral cover of each variety, which is open.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Atoms are geometric atomic $F$-bundles modulo the equivalence generated by
isomorphism of atomic $F$-bundles and by change of the base point within one
connected component of the spectral cover of one variety. An isomorphism carries
the connection, hence the Levelt--Turrittin separation and the framed monodromy,
so it preserves $\operatorname{Mon}$ and $\nu_6$. The second equivalence is
exactly the constancy statement, which Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk-constancy} supplies
along formal germs only.
\end{proof}

It is worth being precise about why the weaker statement does not suffice. One
might hope to compare the set of values of $\nu_6$ over the representatives of a
class, which is well defined without any constancy. It does not help: the class
one is trying to exclude is precisely a class whose value set would contain both
$2$, from the cubic representative, and $0$, from a surface representative, and
a non-constant value set is not by itself absurd. The contradiction needs
constancy, not a value set.

\subsection*{No atom in dimension at most two carries the invariant}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:low-dim}
Let $\alpha$ be an atom represented by a smooth projective complex variety of
dimension at most two. Then $\nu_6(\alpha)=0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Induction over the classification, using the two chemical-formula identities of
Katzarkov--Kontsevich--Pantev--Yu's Proposition 5.22, namely
$\operatorname{CFF}(\PP(V))=\operatorname{rank}(V)\operatorname{CFF}(T)$ and
$\operatorname{CFF}(\mathrm{Bl}_ZT)=\operatorname{CFF}(T)
+(\operatorname{codim}Z-1)\operatorname{CFF}(Z)$, both of which follow from their
Theorems 4.5 and 4.11.

A point has a one-dimensional cohomology with trivial connection, so its atom
has trivial framed monodromy and $\nu_6=0$.

If $K_T$ is nef, their Lemma 5.24 gives a single atom, and their Claim 6.15
gives a gauge, by $u^{g}$ with $g=\mathrm{Gr}+\tfrac12T_{\mathrm d}$, after which
the connection has a first-order pole with nilpotent residue. Undoing that gauge
shifts the exponents by the eigenvalues of $g$, which lie in $\tfrac12\Z$. So
the framed monodromy is unipotent on even cohomology and has eigenvalue $-1$ on
odd cohomology, and $\nu_6=0$. This input is pointwise and needs no constancy
statement: their proof shows that for $K_T$ nef, at any even base point with
vanishing $H^0$-coordinate, quantum multiplication by the Euler field moves the
grading eigenspace for $m$ into eigenspaces for values at least $m+1$, so its
block components $\kappa_{ij}$ vanish for $j\le0$ and the gauged operator has a
first-order pole. The normalization of the $H^0$-coordinate costs nothing, since
translation in the identity direction only twists by a scalar exponential.

Curves: $\PP^1=\PP(\mathcal O^{\oplus2})$ over a point, so its atoms are point
atoms; a curve of genus at least one has nef canonical class.

Surfaces: every smooth projective surface is obtained from a minimal one by
blowing up points, and a point has $\nu_6=0$, so by the blowup identity it
suffices to treat minimal surfaces. A minimal surface of Kodaira dimension at
least zero has nef canonical class. A minimal surface of Kodaira dimension
$-\infty$ is $\PP^2=\PP(\mathcal O^{\oplus3})$ over a point, or a geometrically
ruled surface $\PP(V)$ over a curve; in the first case the atoms are point
atoms, in the second they are curve atoms.

In every case the atoms are among those already shown to have $\nu_6=0$.
\end{proof}

Note that this argument is entirely at the level of atoms as classes, so no
bulk point is chosen and no transport is used; the only pointwise input is
Claim 6.15, which is a structural statement about a connection with nef
canonical class.

\subsection*{The theorem}

\begin{theorem}[One stabilization]
\label{thm:endpoint}
Assume $\nu_6$ is constant on connected components of the spectral cover, as
discussed in Section~\ref{sec:endpoint-gap}. Let $X\subset\PP^4_{\C}$ be a
smooth cubic threefold. Then $X\times\PP^1$ is irrational.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
At the hyperplane point of the base of $X$, the spectral decomposition has two
one-dimensional summands for the nonzero eigenvalues of the Euler operator and
one summand $\alpha(X)$ for the eigenvalue $0$; this is
Katzarkov--Kontsevich--Pantev--Yu's Example 6.21, and it is the draft's own
$K_0$ with eigenvalues $\pm6r,0,0$. The draft's computation, which is Cai's
Proposition 6, puts formal solutions with $z^{\rho}$, $\rho\equiv\pm1/6$, in the
rank-two block for the eigenvalue $0$; equivalently the sheared residue of
Section~\ref{sec:rigid} has characteristic polynomial $\rho^2+\rho+5/36$. Hence
$\nu_6(\alpha(X))\ge2$, and it is well defined by
Proposition~\ref{prop:nu6-atom}.

Since $X\times\PP^1=\PP_X(\mathcal O_X^{\oplus2})$, the projective-bundle
identity gives
$\operatorname{CFF}(X\times\PP^1)=2\operatorname{CFF}(X)$, so $\alpha(X)$ occurs
in the atomic composition of $X\times\PP^1$.

Suppose $X\times\PP^1$ were rational. By the non-rationality criterion, their
Proposition 5.17 in the equivariant form and Proposition 5.30 in the Hodge form,
every atom in its atomic composition would be represented by a smooth projective
variety of dimension at most $4-2=2$. Then $\alpha(X)$ would be such an atom,
and Theorem~\ref{thm:low-dim} would give $\nu_6(\alpha(X))=0$, contradicting
$\nu_6(\alpha(X))\ge2$.
\end{proof}

The invariant does not need to be computed exactly, and the odd cohomology
$H^3(X)$ may well lie in the same summand; only the presence of the two
primitive-sixth eigenvalues is used, and they cannot be cancelled because
$\nu_6$ counts with multiplicity in a free abelian group.

\subsection*{What is imported, and what is proved here}

Imported, and not reproved:

\begin{itemize}
\item The blowup decomposition of maximal $F$-bundles, and the projective-bundle
  decomposition. These are the theorems of Iritani and of Iritani--Koto,
  packaged as Theorems 4.5 and 4.11; reproving them would be far larger than
  this theorem, and no presentation should claim independence of them.
\item The chemical-formula identities and the non-rationality criterion, which
  are formal consequences of those two theorems together with weak
  factorization.
\item Claim 6.15 for a variety with nef canonical class, and Lemma 5.24.
\item Weak factorization in characteristic zero.
\end{itemize}

Proved here or in the draft: constancy of the formal type along formal germs
(Theorem~\ref{thm:bulk-constancy}), invariance of $\nu_6$ under isomorphism
(Proposition~\ref{prop:nu6-atom}), the low-dimensional vanishing
(Theorem~\ref{thm:low-dim}), the cubic block computation, and the
specialization-safe rigidity of Section~\ref{sec:rigid}.

Open: constancy of $\nu_6$ on connected components, which is
Section~\ref{sec:endpoint-gap}. Hypothesis 4.7H does not appear anywhere in the
chain --- no framed operator is transported across a comparison isomorphism ---
so the residual conditionality is of a different kind and a smaller one, but it
is conditionality and the theorem should be stated with it until it is removed.

\section{The endpoint: what is proved, and the one statement left}
\label{sec:unconditional}

The first version of this section claimed the endpoint unconditionally by
inverting Iritani's change of variables. That was refuted, and the refutation is
recorded here because the move is tempting and should not be made again.
Iritani--Koto's (5.13) sets $s_j=\varsigma_j-\varsigma_j^\circ$ and states that
the invertible change of variables is the one in those \emph{displaced}
coordinates, treated as independent formal variables; Iritani's Lemma 5.15 is a
normalization lemma about initial terms, and it is the unnumbered discussion
following it that draws the inverse-function conclusion, in the form that
$\vartheta\mapsto(\tau(\vartheta),\{\varsigma_j(\vartheta)\})$ is
\emph{formally} invertible over $\C(\!(q^{-1/(r-1)})\!)[[Q]]$ --- invertibility
of a formal power series map over a specified coefficient ring, with no germ in
sight, and Iritani's Remark 1.5 says explicitly that $\Psi$ is not expected to be
analytic in all the variables; and the well-definedness of the pullbacks along
the change of variables is argued by Iritani from the String and Divisor
equations together with the reduction of $QDM(X)^{\rm La}$ and $QDM(Z)^{\rm La}$
to the smaller rings of his Remark 5.6, not from any general substitution
principle. Every version of the
decomposition identity in the sources is an isomorphism of modules over a formal
germ, not a family of pointwise statements. So a parameter at which a prescribed
displaced coordinate vanishes need not exist, and "read the identity in either
direction" is false.

What replaces it is better, and it splits the endpoint cleanly into a computed
half and one remaining statement.

\subsection*{The remaining statement, and why it is small}

\emph{A defect, and it is this subsection's own.} An earlier version opened by
asserting that weak factorization ``can be arranged as a roof
$X\times\PP^1\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow V\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\PP^4$
with every arrow a blowdown'', and then observed that descending from such a roof
uses \emph{forward} substitutions only, so no inverse is evaluated and the
refuted move is not needed.

That is not weak factorization. Abramovich--Karu--Matsuki--W{\l}odarczyk
decompose a birational map into a \emph{zigzag} of blowups and blowdowns along
smooth centres; it does not produce a single smooth variety dominating both ends
through smooth blowups. A common resolution dominating both ends certainly
exists, by resolving the graph, but the assertion that each of the two
descending arrows factors into smooth blowups is precisely strong factorization,
which is open in the dimension at issue. So the roof is not available, and the
reduction built on it does not stand as stated.

The damage is confined but real. In a zigzag one must traverse some blowup in the
\emph{upward} direction, from base to blowup, and that is exactly where the
change of variables would have to be inverted --- the move refuted above. The
comparison identity is an isomorphism and can be read in either direction as an
identity of modules; what cannot be done freely is to \emph{prescribe} the
parameter on the base side and solve for the one upstairs. So the endpoint
reduction below should be read as conditional on either a strong factorization in
the relevant dimension, or an independent treatment of the upward steps. It is
not the unconditional reduction the earlier text claimed. Under a roof, were one
available, the centre terms would vanish by the low-dimensional lemma, giving
$\nu_6(X\times\PP^1,\widehat\tau_L)=\nu_6(\PP^4,\rho_R)=0$ for the two parameters
the two descents produce.

Against that stands Corollary~\ref{cor:endpoint-value}. So the endpoint reduces
to exactly one statement:
\begin{quote}
  $\nu_6(X\times\PP^1,\widehat\tau_L)=\nu_6(X\times\PP^1,0)$, or merely
  $\nu_6(X\times\PP^1,\widehat\tau_L)\ge1$.
\end{quote}
Three things make this much smaller than anything attempted above. It concerns
\emph{one} variety rather than arbitrary intermediate fourfolds. Only a lower
bound is needed, so no control of the other blocks is required. And the blocks
carrying $\nu_6$ on $X\times\PP^1$ are two copies of the cubic's rank-two Jordan
block, which are exactly the hypotheses of Section~\ref{sec:rigid}.

The engine to run is therefore Section~\ref{sec:rigid} along the pencil
$t\widehat\tau_L$, $t\in[0,1]$, and not Cai's gauge: Cai's gauge at $t=1$ still
has $z$-order unbounded below across Novikov degrees, so it is not a gauge in
$\Lambda(\!(z)\!)$ and the receiver criterion returns. Section~\ref{sec:rigid}
produces the differential identity $\partial R=[R,G]$ between $z$-regular
objects, so if the pencil's connection matrices are polynomial in $t$ per slot ---
which the dimension axiom gives, each insertion of an $H^{\ge4}$ class costing at
least two --- the identity holds over $\Lambda[t]$ and $t=1$ is inside the domain
rather than at its edge.

\subsection*{Owed lemmas}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Per-slot polynomiality in $t$ along the pencil, from the dimension axiom,
  stated for the accumulated parameter and not only for one step.
\item Nonvanishing of $\nu$ along the pencil. Section~\ref{sec:rigid} needs the
  nilpotent part nonzero, and over $\Lambda[t]$ that is nonvanishing of a
  polynomial with $\nu(0)=2$, not an open condition one may assume.
\item Legitimacy of the descent's substitutions past the first step, where the
  base parameter is evaluated at the accumulated parameter rather than at the
  origin. The accumulated parameter has only $H^{\ge4}$ components, so finitely
  many insertions survive per Novikov degree; this is the same finiteness as
  item 1 and should be proved once.
\item The low-dimensional lemma, restricted to the parameters that actually
  arise rather than stated for all of them, since the unrestricted version
  invokes the operation identity at arbitrary parameters and that is exactly what
  the refutation forbids.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Routes, in the order I would now try them}

The framing route has been removed from this list by the checks of
Section~\ref{sec:hyzz}, the blockwise route has been carried to its conclusion
for a block of the cubic's type in Section~\ref{sec:rigid}, and the endpoint
theorem is proved in Section~\ref{sec:endpoint}. So this list no longer ranks
attacks on an open problem; it records what each remaining route would still
buy, which is the general statement rather than the endpoint.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Blockwise, avoiding transport altogether.} Developed in
  Section~\ref{sec:blockwise} and completed in Section~\ref{sec:rigid} for a
  single Jordan block of rank two. What remains of this route is generalization,
  not repair: a single Jordan block of arbitrary size, by the same shearing with
  $\operatorname{diag}(1,z,\dots,z^{m-1})$, and the semisimple case with its
  resonance condition. That generalization is what a statement covering
  arbitrary centers would need; it is not needed for the cubic endpoint.
  Signs follow the displayed connection of Iritani--Koto's Theorem~5.1 and
  should be rechecked against the draft's normalization before anything is
  written into the manuscript.
\item \textbf{Birkhoff.} The identity $(\Phi^\circ)^{-1}\circ M=M'\circ\Phi^{-1}$
  of Iritani--Koto's Section~5.8, and its counterpart in Iritani's
  Section~5.8.2, already factors the bulk transport into a $z$-regular factor,
  which Theorem~\ref{thm:transport} handles, and a factor
  $M'=\mathrm{id}+O(z^{-1})$. What must be shown is that the second factor does
  not change the primitive-sixth multiplicity. This is a statement about a
  $z=\infty$-side gauge acting on a $z=0$ invariant, so it is not automatic, and
  it is close to the original difficulty in another form: any proof will have to
  use where $M'$ comes from, which is the flat quantum connection --- that is,
  the identities route~1 already exploits directly. Worth keeping as a
  cross-check rather than as the primary attack.
\item \textbf{The low-dimensional centers directly}: primitive-sixth vanishing
  after Iritani's specialization, using the classification the draft already
  proves, rather than through a general transport statement.
\item \textbf{Divisor tagging on its own terms}: whether the divisor equation
  gives a more explicit transport than the general positive-bulk gauge of
  Lemma~4.5. Their Lemma~2.24 is the same mechanism in print and is worth
  citing; it does not close the lemma, for the reasons under check three.
\item \textbf{Normalize, then verify.} Replace ``choose $L$ so large'' by the
  smallest weight satisfying separation, adopt the single-copy receiver of
  Section~\ref{sec:receiver}, and verify $e\,w(\Delta\lambda)<\varepsilon$ case
  by case. This is fallback machinery now.
\end{enumerate}

The iterated receiver of the archived iterated-receiver discussion is not on the list: it needs
a semipositivity hypothesis on the ambient variety that weak factorization does
not supply. It remains worth having where it applies. Restricting
Proposition~4.7 to centers satisfying the criterion is likewise available, at a
cost in generality that is the author's call.

The observation that multiplicity-one blocks contribute nothing, flagged in the
second version of this memo on the strength of Dubrovin's zero-diagonal fact in
canonical coordinates, is no longer a flag: Theorem~\ref{thm:simple-blocks}
proves it in this normalization, from self-adjointness of $E\star$ and
anti-self-adjointness of $\mu$ for the Poincar\'e pairing. So every route above
needs to control only the coalesced blocks.

A separate scope reduction stands on its own, unchanged: a direct quantum
K\"unneth computation for $X\times\PP^1$ together with a direct computation for
$\PP^4$ would leave the blowup step as the only operation formula the headline
theorem needs, with the genus-eight corollary waiting. Note that this cannot be
had by triviality of the bundle alone --- see the end of check one.
