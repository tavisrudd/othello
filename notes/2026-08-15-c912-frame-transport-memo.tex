\title{Frame transport for framed formal monodromy:\\
  the lemma, and why the present receiver cannot host it}
\author{Memo for a reader of \emph{Irrationality of Cubic Threefolds after One
    Stabilization}}
\date{15 August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Proposition~4.7 of the draft transports framed formal monodromy across the
comparison isomorphisms of Iritani and Iritani--Koto. The transport step itself
is asserted in one sentence. This memo proves it, in the form it should take,
over a differential field; then shows that the coefficient receiver the draft
constructs cannot be that field, for a reason that is quantitative rather than
cosmetic; and isolates the exact inequality any repair must establish. The
inequality holds at the cubic endpoint and is not supplied for a general
blow-up center. Section~\ref{sec:placement} says where each piece belongs in
the manuscript.
\end{abstract}

\section{The assertion at issue}

Everything below refers to Section~4 of the draft, \emph{Framed formal
monodromy}. The relevant statements are Definition~4.1 (the multiplicity
$\nu_6$, p.~15), Definition~4.2 (the pro-Laurent gauge group, p.~15),
Lemma~4.3 (coefficientwise base change, p.~16), Lemma~4.5 (formal base shift,
p.~16) and Proposition~4.7 (framed operation formulas, p.~18).

Lemma~4.3 ends with the sentence
\begin{quote}
  \emph{If the gauge uses only integral powers of $z$ at every finite level,
  this conjugacy preserves the original-$z$-disc frame,}
\end{quote}
and its proof discharges that clause in one sentence: ``the deck action fixes
every integral power of $z$ at each finite level, so the compatible gauge is
single-valued for the framed turn.'' Lemma~4.5 then \emph{defines}
\[
  M^{\mathrm{bulk}}=G\,M^{\mathrm{small,shifted}}_{\mathrm f,V}G^{-1}
  \tag{4.1c}
\]
and Proposition~4.7 concludes from it that ``each center target summand has the
framed monodromy of its specialized small connection.'' That conclusion is the
content of the proposition, and it does not follow from the displayed
definition alone: conjugating a matrix by an arbitrary invertible matrix
preserves its characteristic polynomial for trivial reasons, but says nothing
about whether the result is the framed operator of the other module. What is
missing is a statement that a gauge fixed by the turn transports framed
operators, together with an algebra in which the turn acts on solutions. The
draft constructs a common coefficient receiver, and the turn acts trivially on
it, so the content has to live on the solution side.

\section{The lemma, over a differential field}
\label{sec:lemma}

This section is self-contained and, I believe, complete. Notation for
$\operatorname{Exp}_V$, $\Omega_V$ and the complement $K=\C\oplus V$ is the
draft's, from (4.0) and (4.0a).

\subsection*{The ground field}

Let $\Gamma$ be a totally ordered abelian group and let
\[
  \mathcal H=\Omega_V(\!(\Gamma\oplus\Z e_z)\!)
\]
be the Hahn field of series $\sum_g c_gx^g$ with well-ordered support and
coefficients in $\Omega_V$, the value group ordered lexicographically with the
$e_z$-coordinate \emph{last}. Write $z=x^{e_z}$, and let $\theta$ be the
derivation multiplying the coefficient at $(\gamma,k)$ by $k$; Leibniz holds
because $k$ is additive on the value group. Then $\theta z=z$ and
\[
  \mathcal C:=\mathcal H^{\theta=0}=\Omega_V(\!(\Gamma)\!),
\]
a field. Taking $\Gamma=0$ gives $\mathcal H=\Omega_V(\!(z)\!)$, the classical
case. For $e\ge1$ put
$\mathcal H_e=\Omega_V(\!(\Gamma\oplus\tfrac1e\Z e_z)\!)$, a degree-$e$
extension of $\mathcal H$ containing $w=x^{e_z/e}$ with $w^e=z$, and
$\theta_w=e\theta$, so $\theta_ww=w$.

\subsection*{The solution algebra and the turn}

\begin{definition}
The \emph{universal formal solution algebra} $\mathcal U_e$ is the
$\mathcal H_e$-algebra generated by symbols
\[
  w^\rho\ (\rho\in\Omega_V),\qquad \ell,\qquad
  E(\varphi)\ \bigl(\varphi\in w^{-1}\Omega_V[w^{-1}]\bigr),
\]
subject to $w^\rho w^{\rho'}=w^{\rho+\rho'}$, $w^n=$ the element $w^n$ of
$\mathcal H_e$ for $n\in\Z$, $E(\varphi)E(\varphi')=E(\varphi+\varphi')$ and
$E(0)=1$, with the derivation extending $\theta_w$ by
\[
  \theta_w(w^\rho)=\rho\,w^\rho,\qquad
  \theta_w(\ell)=1,\qquad
  \theta_w\bigl(E(\varphi)\bigr)=\theta_w(\varphi)E(\varphi).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Structure]
\label{lem:structure}
Fix representatives $\Lambda\subset\Omega_V$ for $\Omega_V/\Z$ with
$0\in\Lambda$. Then $\mathcal U_e$ is free as an $\mathcal H_e$-module with
basis $\{w^\rho\ell^mE(\varphi):\rho\in\Lambda,\ m\ge0,\ \varphi\}$. In
particular $\mathcal U_e\neq0$ and $\mathcal H\subset\mathcal U_e$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The $w^\rho$ generate the group algebra of $\Omega_V/\Z$ over
$\mathcal H_e$, the $E(\varphi)$ that of the additive group
$w^{-1}\Omega_V[w^{-1}]$; both groups are torsion-free, so distinct monomials
are independent, and $\ell$ is a polynomial variable.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The turn]
\label{lem:turn}
There is a unique automorphism $\sigma$ of $\mathcal U_e$ with
\[
  \sigma(w)=e^{2\pi i/e}w,\qquad
  \sigma(w^\rho)=\operatorname{Exp}_V(\rho/e)\,w^\rho,\qquad
  \sigma(\ell)=\ell+\tfrac{2\pi i}{e},\qquad
  \sigma\bigl(E(\varphi)\bigr)=E(\sigma\varphi),
\]
acting on $\mathcal H_e$ by multiplying the coefficient at
$(\gamma,j/e)$ by $e^{2\pi ij/e}$. It fixes $\mathcal H$, hence $\mathcal C$,
pointwise; it commutes with $\theta_w$; and on a regular-singular block with
residue $R=R_s+R_n$, whose solution is $w^{R_s}e^{R_n\ell}$, the automorphism
$\sigma^e$ acts by
$\operatorname{Exp}_V(R_s)\exp(2\pi iR_n)=M_{\mathrm{RS},V}$, the operator of
the draft's construction.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The action on $\mathcal H_e$ multiplies coefficients by a character of the
value group, hence is a ring automorphism, and it is the identity on
$\mathcal H$ because there $j\in e\Z$. The two prescriptions agree on integral
exponents: $\operatorname{Exp}_V(n/e)=e^{2\pi in/e}$ by (4.0a), since
$n/e\in\Q$. Both $\sigma$ and $\theta_w$ are diagonal in the basis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:structure}, and $\theta_w$ commutes with $\varphi\mapsto
\sigma\varphi$, so they commute. The last claim is the computation
$\sigma^e(w^{R_s})=\operatorname{Exp}_V(R_s)w^{R_s}$ and
$\sigma^e(e^{R_n\ell})=e^{R_n\ell}\exp(2\pi iR_n)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Constants]
\label{lem:constants}
$\mathcal U_e^{\theta_w=0}=\mathcal C$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $v$ denote the Hahn valuation of $\mathcal H_e$, with values in the
lexicographically ordered group $\Gamma\oplus\frac1e\Z e_z$; it is additive,
and $\theta_w$ does not enlarge supports, so
$v(\theta_wc)\ge v(c)$.

Write $x=\sum c_{\rho,m,\varphi}\,w^\rho\ell^mE(\varphi)$ in the basis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:structure} and suppose $\theta_wx=0$. Since
$\theta_w(E(\varphi))=\theta_w(\varphi)E(\varphi)$, the equation splits over
$\varphi$.

Fix $\varphi\ne0$ and let $M$ be the top $\ell$-degree in that block. For each
$\rho$,
\[
  \theta_w(c_{\rho,M})+\rho\,c_{\rho,M}+\theta_w(\varphi)c_{\rho,M}=0 .
\]
Now $\theta_w(\varphi)\ne0$ and every monomial of $\theta_w(\varphi)$ has
$\Gamma$-part $0$ and strictly negative $e_z$-part, so
$v(\theta_w(\varphi)c)<v(c)\le v(\theta_w(c)+\rho c)$ for $c\ne0$; the lowest
term cannot cancel, so $c_{\rho,M}=0$. Descending in $M$ kills the block.

For $\varphi=0$ and $\rho\in\Lambda$, the coefficient equations are
$\theta_w(c_{\rho,m})+\rho\,c_{\rho,m}+(m+1)c_{\rho,m+1}=0$. On monomials
$\theta_w$ acts by the integer $j$ attached to the $e_z$-exponent $j/e$, so
$\theta_w(c)+\rho c=0$ forces $c=0$ unless $\rho\in\Z$, that is unless
$\rho=0$. For $\rho=0$: the top-degree coefficient $c_{0,M}$ is
$\theta_w$-constant, and the next equation gives $Mc_{0,M}=-\theta_w(c_{0,M-1})$,
whose right side has zero component in $e_z$-degree $0$; comparing that
component gives $c_{0,M}=0$ for $M\ge1$. So only $\rho=0$, $m=0$,
$\varphi=0$ survives, with $\theta_w$-constant coefficient, and the
$\theta_w$-constants of $\mathcal H_e$ are those supported in $e_z$-degree
zero, namely $\mathcal C$.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Framed operators and transport}

Let $D$ be a differential module over $\mathcal H$, free of rank $n$, with
connection matrix $\Omega$, so that $\nabla=\theta+\Omega$. A
\emph{fundamental solution matrix} is $Y\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal U_e)$ with
$\theta Y=-\Omega Y$; Levelt--Turrittin supplies one, after a suitable $e$, for
every module over $\Omega_V(\!(z)\!)$, and this is the algebra the draft's
phrase ``in the universal formal solution algebra'' refers to.

\begin{proposition}[The framed operator is the matrix of the turn]
\label{prop:framed-is-turn}
Let $Y$ be a fundamental solution matrix for $D$. Then
\[
  \operatorname{Sol}(D)
  =\{v\in\mathcal U_e^{\,n}:\theta v+\Omega v=0\}
  =Y\mathcal C^{\,n},
\]
free of rank $n$ over $\mathcal C$ and stable under $\sigma$. The matrix $M$
defined by $\sigma(Y)=YM$ lies in $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal C)$, and replacing
$Y$ by another fundamental solution matrix replaces $M$ by a
$\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal C)$-conjugate. For $D$ over $\Omega_V(\!(z)\!)$ in
Levelt--Turrittin form, $M$ is the framed operator $M_{\mathrm f,V}(D)$ of the
draft's construction.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
From $\theta(Y^{-1})=Y^{-1}\Omega$ one gets
$\theta(Y^{-1}v)=Y^{-1}(\theta v+\Omega v)$, so $v$ is horizontal exactly when
$Y^{-1}v$ has $\theta_w$-constant entries, and Lemma~\ref{lem:constants}
identifies those with $\mathcal C$. Since $\sigma$ commutes with $\theta$ and
fixes $\Omega$, whose entries lie in $\mathcal H$, it preserves
$\operatorname{Sol}(D)$; hence $M=Y^{-1}\sigma(Y)$ has entries in
$\mathcal C$, and it is invertible with inverse $Y^{-1}\sigma(Y^{-1})^{-1}$.
Two fundamental solution matrices differ by right multiplication by an element
of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal C)$, which conjugates $M$. The last sentence is the
definition: the draft's operator is by construction the action of the lifted
turn on the Levelt--Turrittin solution basis.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Frame transport]
\label{thm:transport}
Let $D,D'$ be differential modules over $\mathcal H$, free of rank $n$, with
connection matrices $\Omega,\Omega'$ and fundamental solution matrices
$Y,Y'\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal U_e)$. Suppose $G\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal H)$
carries $D$ to $D'$:
\[
  \Omega'=G\Omega G^{-1}-\theta(G)G^{-1}.
\]
Then $GY$ is a fundamental solution matrix for $D'$ and
$\sigma(GY)=(GY)M$, where $\sigma(Y)=YM$. Consequently
\[
  M_{\mathrm f,V}(D')=C\,M_{\mathrm f,V}(D)\,C^{-1}
  \qquad\text{for some }C\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal C),
\]
so the two framed operators have the same characteristic polynomial in
$\mathcal C[X]$ and the same multiplicity at every root of unity. Moreover
$GM_{\mathrm f,V}(D)G^{-1}$ is the matrix of the framed turn of $D'$ in the
transported frame $GY$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\theta(GY)=\theta(G)Y-G\Omega Y=-\Omega'(GY)$, using the displayed relation.
Since $\sigma$ fixes $\mathcal H$ pointwise, $\sigma(G)=G$, so
$\sigma(GY)=G\sigma(Y)=GYM$. Comparing $GY$ with $Y'$ through
Proposition~\ref{prop:framed-is-turn} gives $Y'=(GY)C_0$ with
$C_0\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal C)$, whence $M'=C_0^{-1}MC_0$. The final claim
is the identity $\sigma(GY)=(GY)M$ read in the frame $GY$, whose matrix in the
original frame of $D'$ is $GMG^{-1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The hypothesis is exactly ``no root of $z$, no symbol'']
Because $\sigma$ fixes $\mathcal H$ pointwise, the invariance $\sigma(G)=G$ is
automatic once $G$ has entries in $\mathcal H$; the whole content of the
hypothesis is that $G$ lies in $\mathcal H$ rather than in $\mathcal U_e$. No
hypothesis-free statement is available: $Y$ itself is a gauge over
$\mathcal U_e$ carrying $D$ to the trivial module, whose framed operator is
the identity. The rank-one case shows what survives. If $g\in\mathcal H^\times$
and $\theta(g)/g=\delta\in\Omega_V$, comparing coefficients gives
$(k-\delta)g_{(\gamma,k)}=0$ for every monomial, so $\delta\in\Z$ and
$g$ is a constant times $z^{\delta}$. A gauge over $\mathcal H$ shifts residues
only by integers, and $\operatorname{Exp}_V$ kills those. This is the precise
sense in which ``integral powers of $z$'' is the right hypothesis, and it is
what the draft's one-sentence proof was gesturing at.
\end{remark}

So the lemma the referee asked for is true and short. The difficulty is
elsewhere.

\section{The receiver cannot be that field}
\label{sec:obstruction}

Theorem~\ref{thm:transport} needs one ring containing both the comparison gauge
and the two modules' formal solutions. The draft's receiver, built in the proof
of Proposition~4.7, is
\[
  \mathscr R_j=\Omega_{V,j}\otimes_{H_{0,j}}H_{\mathrm{tot},j},
  \qquad
  H_{\mathrm{tot},j}=\C(\!(\Gamma^{\mathrm{coeff}}_j\oplus\Z e_z)\!) .
  \tag{4.4c}
\]
It is not such a ring, for three independent reasons.

\paragraph{Finite tensors.} An element of $\mathscr R_j$ is a \emph{finite}
sum $\sum b_\lambda\otimes h_\lambda$. A Levelt--Turrittin gauge is a series
$\sum_jP_jw^j$ whose coefficients $P_j$ span an infinite-dimensional
$H_{0,j}$-subspace of $\Omega_{V,j}$ in general. Such a series is not in
$\mathscr R_j$, and adjoining formal symbols to $\mathscr R_j$ does not add it.

\paragraph{The obvious enlargement is not well defined.} One would like to map
$\mathscr R_j$ into the Hahn field
$\Omega_{V,j}(\!(\Gamma_j^{\mathrm{coeff}}\oplus\Z e_z)\!)$ by
$a\otimes h\mapsto a\cdot h$. This is not well defined. The tensor product over
$H_{0,j}$ exists precisely to identify two copies of $H_{0,j}$: one sitting
inside $\Omega_{V,j}$ as coefficients, one inside $H_{\mathrm{tot},j}$ as
monomial series. In the Hahn field those two copies are different elements, so
the map does not respect the relation $ah_0\otimes h=a\otimes h_0h$. The
tensor product is doing real work and cannot be dissolved by hand.

\paragraph{No single Hahn field holds both families.} Order the value group
with the coefficient degree dominant, as the draft does. Then a pro-Laurent
element is legitimate: its $z$-order runs to $-\infty$ while its coefficient
degree grows, so its support is well ordered. But a formal solution whose
coefficient degrees run to $-\infty$ at fixed $z$-power is not. Order it with
$e_z$ dominant and the two verdicts swap. The two objects are well ordered for
opposite reasons.

\subsection*{The exact criterion}

A mixed order is the remaining possibility, and it turns the obstruction into
one inequality. Weight a monomial of coefficient degree $d$ and $z$-power $k$
by $a\,d+b\,k$ with $a,b>0$.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Pro-Laurent gauges gain one filtration unit per unit of $z$-pole.}
  The recursion (4.1a) in the proof of Lemma~4.5 gives the bulk-degree-$m$
  coefficient $z$-order at least $-m$ and places it in $F^m$. Its weight grows
  like $m(a-b)$, so these are well ordered exactly when $a>b$.
\item \emph{Splitting gauges lose $w(\Delta\lambda)$ per unit of $z$-power.}
  The formal decomposition divides once per order by differences of eigenvalues
  of the leading matrix $c_1\star$: writing $P=\sum_kP_kz^k$, the
  splitting equation at order $k$ is $[A_0,P_k]=R_k$ with $R_k$ built from
  earlier data. Hence $P_k$ has weight at least $-kC$ with
  $C=w(\Delta\lambda)$, and the weights grow like $k(b-aC)$, well ordered
  exactly when $b>aC$. Inside a block the regular-singular normalization
  divides only by residue differences offset by integers, which are nonzero
  constants and cost nothing.
\end{itemize}

Both hold together iff $C<1$, measured in units of the minimal weight
$\varepsilon$ of a generator of $J_j$; that is, iff
\[
  \boxed{\ w(\Delta\lambda)<\varepsilon\ }
\]
\emph{The ratio is scale-invariant}: rescaling the weight function $w$ moves
the pro-Laurent gain and the splitting loss by the same factor, so the choice
of the integer $L$ in the draft's separation argument is not a free parameter
here.

\subsection*{What the criterion depends on}

The eigenvalues of $c_1\star$ are homogeneous of quantum degree one, where
$Q^d$ has degree $c_1\cdot d$. When the relevant eigenvalue is a root of a
monomial, $\lambda^{c_1\cdot d_0}\sim Q^{d_0}$, the criterion reads
\[
  w(Q^{d_0})<\varepsilon\cdot(c_1\cdot d_0).
\]
Since $Q^{d_0}$ is itself among the generators of $J_j$, one has
$w(Q^{d_0})\ge\varepsilon$, so the criterion is a Fano-index condition:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{It holds at the cubic endpoint.} There $c_1=2H$ and the
  exponential factors are governed by $\lambda\sim q^{1/2}$, a square root of a
  Novikov generator, so $w(\Delta\lambda)=\varepsilon/2$. This is consistent
  with the draft's own finding that the two rank-one exponential blocks of the
  cubic are unramified in $z$, the square root being a coefficient extension.
\item \textbf{It fails as soon as $c_1\cdot d_0=1$}, since then the criterion
  demands $w(Q^{d_0})<\varepsilon$. Proposition~4.7 quantifies over arbitrary
  smooth centers arising in a weak factorization, and nothing in that
  hypothesis excludes index one.
\item \textbf{A center whose small connection is regular singular costs
  nothing}, $C=0$: no splitting divisions occur.
\end{itemize}

So the gap is confined to centers whose small even connection has at least two
distinct exponential factors separated by an eigenvalue difference of low
fractional index. That is smaller than ``all centers'', and it is not empty.

\subsection*{The levelwise route fails for the same reason}

It is natural to retreat to finite level, where $(B_j/F^NB_j)(\!(z)\!)$ is an
honest Laurent series ring hosting both a bounded-order gauge and a solution
algebra with no support conditions at all, and then to pass to the limit of
characteristic polynomials rather than of solution algebras. This does not
work. The splitting needs $\Delta\lambda$ invertible, and $\Delta\lambda$ lies
in $J_j$, hence is nilpotent at every finite level. It would also place framed
monodromy over the nonfield quotients, which the draft deliberately removed.

\section{Three routes, and what each must prove}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Mixed-order receiver plus a criterion.} Replace (4.4c) by the
  mixed-order Hahn receiver and prove $w(\Delta\lambda)<\varepsilon$ for the
  centers the theorem actually uses. The most promising form of this is to
  show that the centers surviving the draft's own low-dimensional analysis
  contribute either no distinct exponential factors or a high enough index.
\item \textbf{A solution-free transport theorem.} Prove that the
  Levelt--Turrittin data of a module over a field --- exponential factors,
  residues modulo $\Z$, deck permutation --- are determined by the module
  after base change along a $z$-integral isomorphism over $\mathscr R_j$,
  by a slope and valuation argument rather than by comparing solutions. The
  obstacle to check first: $\mathscr R_j$ is not a domain, being a tensor
  product of two fields, so the $e_z$-component of the Hahn valuation, which
  is additive on $H_{\mathrm{tot},j}$, must be shown to behave on the tensor
  product.
\item \textbf{Restrict the proposition.} State Proposition~4.7 for centers
  satisfying the criterion, and check that the weak factorization used in the
  main theorem can be run inside that class. This changes the shape of the
  result and is the author's call, not a referee's.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Placement in the manuscript}
\label{sec:placement}

If the mathematics is settled, the edits are localized.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The ground field and solution algebra} go immediately before the
  sentence ``Apply Levelt--Turrittin after a ramification $z=w^e$'' on p.~14.
  That sentence already speaks of ``the universal formal solution algebra''
  without having constructed one, so this repairs a forward reference as well.
\item \textbf{Lemma~\ref{lem:constants} and
  Proposition~\ref{prop:framed-is-turn}} go directly after Definition~4.1,
  where the framed operator has just been defined. They convert that
  definition from a recipe into a characterization: $M_{\mathrm f,V}$ is the
  matrix of the turn on the horizontal module.
\item \textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:transport}} becomes a new lemma, placed after
  the paragraph beginning ``The frame matters'' on p.~15 and before
  Definition~4.2. It must carry one semantic label,
  \texttt{lem:frame-transport}.
\item \textbf{Lemma~4.3} loses its final asserted sentence and the
  corresponding sentence of its proof; both become a reference to the new
  lemma.
\item \textbf{Lemma~4.5}, at (4.1c), gains one sentence: by the new lemma,
  $M^{\mathrm{bulk}}$ is the framed operator of the gauged module in the
  transported frame, not merely a conjugate of the small one.
\item \textbf{Proposition~4.7} cites the new lemma at its three load-bearing
  steps --- the appeal to Theorem~5.18 of Iritani's blow-up paper, the center
  summand conclusion, and the projective-bundle conclusion --- and must say
  explicitly which field plays the role of $\mathcal H$ at each, together with
  the criterion verified there.
\end{enumerate}

Two mechanical consequences: a new theorem-like environment must carry exactly
one semantic label and a matching row in the claim map consumed by the
manuscript's source-only gate, and the coverage snapshot recorded in the two
verification READMEs changes with it.

\section{Questions I would like answered}

\begin{enumerate}
\item Is there a receiver that holds both families that we have missed ---
  in particular, a completion of $\mathscr R_j$ in which the convolution
  defining a product of a pro-Laurent gauge with a formal solution converges
  for structural reasons rather than by a rate comparison?
\item Is route~2 available? A transport theorem that never compares solutions
  would make the receiver question moot.
\item Failing both: is $w(\Delta\lambda)<\varepsilon$ automatic for the centers
  that actually arise in a weak factorization between $X\times\PP^1$ and a
  rational model, given the draft's own vanishing result for centers of
  dimension at most two?
\item Is the criterion the right invariant at all, or is it an artifact of
  proving transport through an ordered receiver?
\end{enumerate}
