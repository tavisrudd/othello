## Fermi predictions, written before any code

Written from the C1170 census figures for the admission stage, which is the nearest measured
kernel: on the ASCII cohort admission costs 1,749,589 instructions over 43,008 source bytes,
15,489 tokens, 12,288 nodes and 3,520 name references — 40.68 instructions per source byte,
142.4 per node and 497 per resolved reference.

1. **What the lowering traversal does per node.** The build pass visits each node of a definition
   body once through the same explicit-stack shape admission uses, and its per-node work is a
   switch on `NodeKind` plus, at an application, a per-argument term emission. There is no name
   hashing and no symbol probe: the relation a name denotes is resolved by a linear scan over the
   relation pool, which is the definition count and not the reference count. I predict the build
   pass at **15 to 25 instructions per visited node**, against admission's 142.4, because
   admission hashes and probes at every reference and lowering does not.
2. **What the constants cost.** Interning one typed literal is an FNV hash over its spelling plus
   an open-addressed probe, the same shape as `admit::insert`, which C1170 measured at roughly
   110 instructions per insert including its call shape. I predict **80 to 130 instructions per
   distinct constant** and about 40 for a repeat.
3. **The per-rule passes.** Distribution, projection, range restriction, stratification and
   binarization are per-rule and per-literal, not per-byte. For a body of `b` literals with `v`
   variables each: projection and range restriction are `O(b·v)` with `v ≤ 8`, so about
   `30·b` instructions; Tarjan is `O(relations + edges)` at about 20 instructions per edge;
   binarization touches a rule only when it has more than two positive literals. For a cohort
   whose definitions average three literals and four variables I predict **150 to 300
   instructions per rule** for all five passes together.
4. **Stage total.** A positive-fragment cohort of 512 definitions at about 70 bytes each
   (36,000 bytes, roughly 9,000 nodes, 512 rules, about 1,500 literals, about 600 distinct
   constants) should cost roughly `9,000 × 20 + 512 × 220 + 600 × 110 + 900 × 40` ≈
   **395,000 instructions, or about 11 instructions per source byte** — between a quarter and a
   third of admission's per-byte cost. I will believe the model only if the measured stage lands
   within about 30 per cent of that; a larger gap means the per-node cost is wrong and the
   profile must be widened rather than the constants re-guessed.
5. **On the three frozen cohorts the stage is nearly free, and that is not a stub.** The ASCII,
   Unicode and comment-string cohorts are built from templates that use arithmetic, `not`,
   `implies`, `if/then/else`, aggregation and override, none of which is in the positive
   fragment. Lowering must therefore reject each of them at the first out-of-fragment construct
   in source order, which on the ASCII cohort is the second definition. I predict the measured
   lowering stage on those cohorts at **under 2,000 instructions**, dominated by the pool
   preparation and the walk up to the first rejection, and I predict that figure will be read as
   a rejection cost and not as a lowering cost. The per-byte lowering cost has to be measured on
   a cohort the fragment covers.
6. **The backend boundary allocates and is measured separately.** Building
   `rule_contract::Program` materializes `String` relation names, `Vec<Term>` atoms and the fact
   list; for a 512-rule program that is on the order of 10,000 allocations and I expect it to
   cost more than every pass before it. That is the boundary ADR 0004 names as the planned
   removal, and this milestone measures rather than hides it.

## Status

Milestone (a) is complete and every gate below passes. An admitted Rel program in the positive
fragment now becomes a relational IR, then a core `rule_contract::Program`, which the demand-driven
evaluator evaluates and whose derivation certificate the core's independent checker accepts, on

## What was built

### The relational IR

`src/rel_frontend/lower.rs` with `lower/build.rs` and `lower/passes.rs`, all of it dependent on
nothing outside the standard library. The pools are plain `#[repr(C)]` records with asserted
strides:

| Record         | Stride | Alignment | What it holds                                                                                                                         |
|----------------|-------:|----------:|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `Relation`     |     32 |         4 | spelling span, declaring site, name-part range, column-type range, owning module, parts, arity, kind, flags, stratum                  |
| `Rule`         |     32 |         4 | head literal, body literal range, origin definition, variable range, join-order range, disjunct index, stratum, variable count, flags |
| `Literal`      |     32 |         4 | relation, term range, source span, origin node, arity, sign, comparison operator, flags                                               |
| `Term`         |      8 |         4 | dictionary id or variable index, and the kind                                                                                         |
| `Value`        |     32 |         8 | integer payload, spelling span, hash, dense id, type                                                                                  |
| `Fact`         |     16 |         4 | relation and the dictionary-id range of one tuple                                                                                     |
| `Span`         |      8 |         4 | a source span or a half-open pool range                                                                                               |
| `Module`       |     16 |         4 | node id, name span, enclosing module                                                                                                  |
| `Edge`         |      8 |         4 | dependency target and the literal that induced it                                                                                     |
| `Component`    |     16 |         4 | Tarjan index, lowlink, component, flags                                                                                               |
| `Form`         |     16 |         4 | one node of a definition's formula tree, in post-order                                                                                |
| `Binding`      |     16 |         4 | one variable in scope while a body is built                                                                                           |

Capacity is reserved by `Workspace::new` from six new `Limits` fields — `relations`, `rules`,
`literals`, `terms`, `values` and `disjuncts` — which are the declared expansion bounds the
architecture note requires. Every pool push tests its capacity and returns a bounded `REL0503`
rather than growing, and the one pool that is written rather than pushed, the open-addressed value
index, is sized and cleared inside its reserved capacity by the first lowering on a workspace, so
`Workspace::new` still touches no page it reserves.

### The passes

**Flatten modules.** A definition owned by the module chain `M`, `N` becomes a relation whose name
parts are `M`, `N` and its own spelling; every module on the chain contributes its parameters as
leading columns, outermost module first, and those parameters are bound in every body the module
owns. A member reference `M[a]:f(x)` supplies the leading columns explicitly; a bare `f(x)` inside
the module fills them from the module parameters in scope; and `M:f(x)` from outside, where nothing
supplies them, is `REL0501` with the reference as the primary span and the module header as the
secondary.

**Distribute disjunction.** Each definition's body becomes a formula tree in a pool that is cleared
between definitions and is in post-order by construction, so one forward sweep computes each node's
disjunct set from its children's: a literal is one conjunct, `or` concatenates, `and` is the cross
product. Exceeding `Limits::disjuncts` is `REL0505` with the count and the bound.

**Project existentials.** Variables are numbered per definition while the body is built, because
that is the scope the binders live in; after distribution each rule carries only the subset its own
literals mention, so this pass renumbers them by first occurrence in head-then-body order and
records each one's source span. Every wildcard becomes a fresh rule-local variable here, which is
what makes an anonymous argument an existential projection and an anonymous head column an unbound
variable.

**Range restriction.** Every head variable, and every variable of a negative, comparison or
aggregate literal, must occur in a positive body literal of the same rule. A violation is `REL0501`
naming the variable's own span and the head as the secondary.

**Stratify.** Edges run from a rule's head relation to each relation its body reads, signed by the
literal's sign. Tarjan's strongly connected components run on an explicit frame stack with a
contiguous adjacency built by counting and prefix sum; a component closes only after everything it
depends on, so the emission order is a stratum order. A non-monotone edge inside one component is
`REL0502`.

**Binarize.** A body with more than two positive literals becomes a chain through auxiliary
relations, each carrying exactly the variables live in the remaining chain, computed from suffix
masks. The order is the variable-sharing heuristic — most already-bound variables first, then fewer
variables, then source order — factored into one function so a later cost-based order is a policy
swap. Each auxiliary belongs to the head's stratum.

**Close the domain.** The dictionary size becomes the program's domain; every relation gains an
ASCII identifier the core admits, by keeping the alphanumeric bytes of each name part, joining the
parts with `_`, prefixing `r` when the result would not start with a letter, naming an auxiliary for
its relation id, and breaking a collision with the relation's own id. Then every budget of the core
contract and its demand-driven evaluator is checked with its numbers, before anything is built.

### Backend v1

`src/rel_lowering.rs`, the one allocating step, builds `rule_contract::Program` with schema
`finite-min-plus-rules.v1`, carrier `boolean`, stability 0 and no symmetries, plus a readout map
carrying each relation's mangled name, its flattened source spelling, its column types, its stratum,
and the decoded value dictionary. Auxiliary relations are filtered from readout and kept in the
program, because they are part of what the certificate proves. It lives beside the frontend rather
than inside it because the native/WASM parity replay compiles the frontend source tree on its own
with `rustc` and no Cargo graph, and this module depends on the core `ergodis-verify` crate.

### The driver

`rel-frontend-bench --stage lower` is the measured stage: parse, then admit, then lower, with each
earlier boundary gating the next, and the receipt records what the lowering actually did on the
cohort. `rel-lower` is the end-to-end subcommand: source, parse, admit, lower, backend,
`Demand::new`, evaluation, derivation certificate, the independent checker, and the decoded closure,
with the demand evaluator's own row bound declared by `--max-rows` rather than defaulted. Both are
subcommands of the lane's one tier-2 binary; no new `src/bin` file exists.

## Semantics adopted

Written down as this stage's own contract, not as a claim about Rel, because no executable Rel
reference semantics is available to this lane. Figure 3 and Figure 4 of Addendum A of Aref et al.,
arXiv:2504.10323 (cache key `arxiv:2504.10323`, sha256
`6e1371160602b1df77d9e5a647369bcd747aec3e8a97e36d2e99f04c219194b6`) are the logical core the
fragment is measured against; the paper has no module construct, so module visibility, module
parameters as binders and `M:x` member resolution remain the frontend's own contract from
`2026-09-14-c1170-module-scopes.md`.

**Which programs are admitted.** A source is lowered when every top-level item is a `def` or a
`module … end`, and every definition is one of two shapes.

1. A **fact set**: `def r = {(c1, c2); (c3, c4)}`, a definition with no parameter list whose body is
   a `;`-chain of `,`-tuples of constants. It declares an input relation whose arity is the common
   tuple width and whose facts are those tuples. Every tuple must have the same width, and a fact
   set inside a parameterized module is rejected, because nothing would supply the module's leading
   columns.
2. A **rule**: `def r(p1, …, pk) = Body`, where `Body` is built from applications `q(t1, …, tn)`,
   conjunction (`and`, and `,` read as conjunction in a formula position), disjunction (`or` and
   `;`), `exists(x, y: F)`, parentheses, and the empty tuple `()` as the true formula. An argument
   is a variable in scope, `_`, or a constant. A parameter is a name, `_`, or `x in D`, which binds
   `x` and conjoins the positive literal `D(x)` with every rule of the definition.

Several `def` clauses may define one relation, as Rel allows; they must agree on arity and on
whether the relation is a fact set or a rule head. A free name is an external input relation, global
whatever module the use sits in, with its arity fixed by its first application — the same policy
admission adopted, extended to count the module's leading columns.

**Which fixed point is computed.** The least fixed point of the lowered positive Datalog program
over the finite domain the value dictionary defines, computed by the core demand-driven evaluator
and replayed independently by the core derivation checker. Auxiliary relations introduced by
binarization are part of that fixed point and part of the certificate; they are filtered from
readout because the source did not name them. A non-stratified program is rejected with `REL0502`,
never approximated, and in this milestone every edge is positive so every program that lowers has
one stratum per strongly connected component of its dependency graph.

**Deliberate readings, each of which could have gone the other way.**

- `,` in a formula position is conjunction. Figure 3 gives `(Expr1, Expr2)` the product, and the
  product of two nullary relations is their conjunction; reading it as a general product would need
  a tuple-typed IR that backend v1 has no carrier for.
- `()` is the true formula and `{}` is the false one, following Figure 4's `[[{()}]] = {<>}` and
  `[[{}]] = empty`. The true formula is absorbed by conjunction; a rule body that is false has no
  representation in the contract, so `{}` is `REL0504`.
- A body's own parameter shadows a module parameter of the same spelling. That is the direction the
  module-scope contract adopted and the one admission's binder scan already takes. Unlike at
  admission it is observable here, and the fixture below shows how.
- The direction of variable resolution inside the body is innermost binder first, so an `exists`
  binder shadows a parameter of the same spelling and the parameter is restored when the quantified
  formula closes.
- A relation name in a term position is higher-order and rejected (`REL0504`) rather than treated as
  a nullary value.

## Deviations from the architecture note

1. **The backend lives outside `src/rel_frontend/`.** The note's layering puts
   `backend::rule_contract` under the lowering. The native/WASM parity replay compiles
   `src/rel_frontend/mod.rs` and its submodules directly with `rustc` and no Cargo graph, so a
   module there cannot depend on `ergodis-verify`. The projection is therefore
   `src/rel_lowering.rs`, a sibling module of the private library; the layering and the one
   allocating boundary are unchanged, and the relational IR stays the only thing the frontend owns.
2. **Pool capacity is planned from `Limits`, and the `Admission` counts are checked against it.**
   The note says the pools are presized from the `Admission` counts plus declared expansion bounds.
   `Workspace::new` runs before any admission, so the capacity is reserved from the six new `Limits`
   fields, and the passes check every push against that capacity and return `REL0503` with the
   numbers. The reading that survives is the one that matters: the stage allocates nothing and its
   capacity is declared rather than grown.
3. **Binarization skips a rule that still carries a non-positive literal.** The note has
   binarization rewrite every body longer than two atoms. A body with a negative, comparison or
   aggregate literal is left alone, because backend v1 rejects it with `REL0504` before anything
   evaluates it and because fixing a join order across filters would commit the milestone that adds
   them to a choice it has not made. Every rule backend v1 accepts has at most two body atoms.
4. **`Rule::order` records the body in join order, and the chosen sequence of a binarized rule is
   the chain itself.** The note asks for the left-deep order recorded per rule. After binarization a
   rule's body is two atoms, so the recorded order is those two; the full chosen sequence is the
   chain of auxiliaries. The ordering policy is one function, `order_positives`, so a cost-based
   order remains a policy swap.
5. **`exists(x in D: F)` is rejected.** The note's projection pass treats `exists` binders
   uniformly. A restriction inside a quantified formula needs its literal conjoined with that
   formula's body, which the build pass does only for a definition parameter; the construct is
   `REL0504` and is a recorded coverage row.
6. **The value dictionary has no per-type sub-range.** The note asks for "a per-type sub-range so a
   column type can later select its own domain". Each `Value` carries its `kind` and each relation
   its column types, but ids are assigned in interning order, so one type's ids are not contiguous
   and no range structure exists. Backend v1 collapses every column into one domain and needs none;
   the ranges are derivable from the recorded kinds when per-column domains arrive. This deviation
   was missing from the first version of this list, found by the 2026-09-15 audit.
7. **The IR reserves no symmetry table.** The note asks the IR to reserve one per program so
   source-declared or discovered symmetries can be forwarded later. It does not; the backend emits
   an empty `symmetries` list. Nothing in milestone (a) reads or writes symmetries. Also found by
   the audit.

## Fixtures, and the Figure 3/4 equation table

`tests/rel_lowering.rs` holds the lowering fixtures; the existing
`tests/rel_frontend_portability.rs` carries the parity cases.

**End-to-end closures.** Eight small programs run the whole chain — source, parse, admit, lower,
backend, `Demand::new`, evaluation, derivation certificate, the independent checker, decoded readout
— and their expected closures are computed by an independent Python set evaluator,
`tests/support/rel_closure_oracle.py`, committed with its output in
`tests/support/rel-closure-expected.json`. The oracle has no parser, no relational IR and no
knowledge of the backend: it holds a hand-written transcription of each program into plain Python
sets and iterates to the least fixed point, so an agreement is evidence about the lowering rather
than about one implementation compared with itself. It is not the C1189 reference evaluator, which
remains a separate allocation. The eight are transitive closure, same generation, a three-atom body
that binarizes, a disjunctive rule, a module-owned definition with a parameter, an `x in D` domain
restriction, an anonymous projection, and a constant argument.

**The module flattening fixture the admission audit could not build.** The 2026-09-15
admission-chain audit recorded, as its correction to defect 8, that the direction of shadowing
between a module parameter and a body parameter of one spelling has no observable consequence at
admission: `admit::lookup` returns on the first non-stale binder match and reads no field of it, so
both directions give the same admission, the same summary and the same diagnostics. At the lowering
the direction decides acceptance. In `module M[x] def f(x) = e(x) end`, with the body's own
parameter winning, the module parameter is a head column that no body literal binds, so the rule is
not range-restricted and lowering rejects it with `REL0501` whose primary span is the module
parameter's own spelling at byte 9. Had the module parameter won, the two head columns would be one
variable and the rule would lower. The companion fixture, with the two spellings distinct, lowers
and shows the module parameter occupying the leading column of `M_f`.

**Every `REL05xx` with its exact code and spans.** `REL0501` for an unbound head variable, for an
anonymous head column, for a negated literal over an unbound variable and for a comparison over one;
`REL0502` is exercised only by the stratifier's own path, since no source in the fragment can build
a non-monotone cycle that admission accepts; `REL0503` for a five-column fact set, with the budget
named as "relation arity", `found` 5 and `limit` 4; `REL0504` for nine out-of-fragment constructs
with the span of the construct itself; `REL0505` for a disjunction expansion under a declared bound
of four, with `found` above it. Every rejection is rendered through `enrich_lowering` and the
rendered text is asserted to begin with the code.

rejected by backend v1; sixteen are rejected by the lowering with `REL0504`; two are rejected by
admission and two are not parsed at all. (The first version of this paragraph said twelve, two and
seventeen, which miscounted the table above; the table itself was and is right.) The two rows that
are not parsed are `[[E where F]]` and `[[{E1}[&{E2}]]]`, the higher-order application.

The `&` and `?` argument-disambiguation sigils have no token in the scanner, and the literal syntax
that uses either one is `REL0101` at the sigil, not `REL0504`. That affects three further rows,
which the table decides through a surrogate source rather than the paper's own spelling. The `?`
row, `[[{E1}[?{E2}]]]`, is exercised by a plain application, which the paper itself sanctions: it
says the annotations may be dropped when the engine can tell a first-order argument from a
higher-order one. The two aggregation rows, `[[reduce[&{E1},&{E2}]]]` and
`[[reduce(&{E1},&{E2},E3)]]`, are exercised by `sum[v: w(k, v)]` and by `s = sum[v: w(k, v)]`;
their recorded `REL0504` is the outcome of Rel's surface aggregation, and the literal `reduce` with
`&` would not scan. The sigil gap is a parser gap this milestone records rather than closes, and
three of the table's outcomes are about the unannotated spelling rather than the annotated one.

## Instructive negatives

1. **Four of the five first-run fixture failures were the fixtures, not the lowering.** The
   same-generation fixture asserted that `(4, 2)` is absent from the closure; with the base case
   written as "every node that has a child is same-generation with every such node", it is present
   and derivable, and the assertion was wrong. The module fixture's facts did not chain under one
   parameter value, so the empty closure it got was correct. The `not e(x, x)` case expected
   `REL0504` and got `REL0501`, because with the only body literal negated the head variable is
   unbound and range restriction fires first. The five-column fact set expected `REL0503` and got
   `REL0504`, which was a real defect: `fact_set` refused a tuple wider than the backend's arity and
   fell through to the nullary-relation rejection, so the budget and its numbers never reached the
   diagnostic. That one was fixed by letting the fact set report its width and letting the closing
   check report the budget.
2. **The shadowing fixture I set out to write could not exist in the form I planned.** I expected to
   distinguish the two directions by inspecting the lowered head's variable indices. The direction
   that loses does not produce a differently shaped rule that still lowers; it produces a rule that
   is not range-restricted. The fixture is therefore a rejection with the module parameter's own
   span, which is a stronger discriminator than the inspection would have been, and it is the reason
   the audit's own proposal for defect 8 was right about the location and wrong about the shape.
3. **The first `Literal` layout was 28 bytes and the build failed on its own stride assertion.**
   The record rules admit 8-, 16- and 32-byte compact records; the miscounted field list gave 28.
   Explicit padding to 32 is the fix, and the `const _: () = assert!(size_of … && align_of …)` is
   what caught it at compile time rather than in a profile.
4. **Resetting the definition-local variable map by re-walking the rule's terms is wrong once the
   terms are renumbered.** The first projection pass cleared the map by walking the same literals a
   second time and reading `term.var`, which by then held the rule-local index. The fix records the
   definition-local slot of each assignment in a fixed array of `MAX_VARIABLES` entries and resets
   exactly those, which also avoids a bulk clear of a pool sized to the binder limit and the libc
   `memset` a bulk clear would have lowered to.
5. **The three frozen cohorts cannot measure a lowering.** The ASCII, Unicode and comment-string
   templates are built from arithmetic, `not`, `implies`, conditionals, aggregation and override.
   Lowering rejects each at the first out-of-fragment construct, so the measured stage on them is a
   rejection cost. That was predicted in the Fermi and is why the `datalog` cohort exists.

## Remaining gaps

1. **Relation resolution is a linear scan, and the comment-string cohort is what proves it.**
   `lower::build::find_relation` walks the relation pool, and it is called from `declare`,
   `resolve_name` and `qualified`, so the declaring phase is quadratic in the relation count. The
   evidence is the comment-string cohort and only that cohort: it declares all 896 relations and
   pays 284.66 instructions per source byte, and its stage difference rises fourfold per doubling
   of the definition count (468,393 / 1,397,718 / 4,748,879 / 18,676,568 at 64 / 128 / 256 / 512),
   which is what a quadratic looks like. The ascii and unicode rows are *not* evidence for this —
   they reject inside `declare` at the second definition with one relation in the pool, and their
   cost is linear (see gap 2). The fix is the open-addressed spelling index admission already has,
   keyed by owner and spelling; it is a kernel candidate with a retained control and a Fermi the
   comment-string row already prices. This remains the first thing a successor should do, because
   it is the largest measured effect.
2. **Every lowering sweeps the whole node pool before its first declaration.**
   `build::declare_modules` walks every node looking for module nodes, at about 6.4 instructions
   per node. That is the entire cost of the ascii and unicode rows (79,208 over 12,288 nodes and
   79,977 over 12,416), and about 32,000 of the `datalog` cohort's 1,299,495 instructions. It is
   linear, so it is much smaller than gap 1 on a large program, but it is paid by every lowering
   including one that rejects immediately. The fix is a module index built while admission already
   walks the nodes, so the lowering reads a list instead of re-scanning. Second in order after
   gap 1, and the cheaper of the two to build.
3. **Stratified negation and aggregation (milestones b and c).** The relational IR represents
   negative, comparison and aggregate literals, range restriction checks them and the stratifier
   treats the non-monotone ones as non-monotone edges; only backend v1 rejects them. The routes are
   fixed in ADR 0004 and unchanged by this work: per-stratum projection with complement facts for
   negation, aggregation at stratum boundaries into the dictionary.
4. **`REL0502` has no source-level fixture, and `REL0505` is not under the allocation gate.** No
   program the positive fragment admits can build a non-monotone cycle, so the stratifier's
   rejection path is exercised by its own construction and not by a Rel source; it becomes testable
   with milestone (b), and until then the claim about it is about the code and not about an input.
   Separately, the disjunction-bound exit `REL0505` has a fixture but is not one of the four
   sources `the_lowering_stage_does_not_allocate` drives, so the allocation gate covers four of the
   stage's exits rather than all of them.
5. **Arity conflicts between clauses report `REL0504`, not a dedicated code.** Two `def` clauses of
   one relation that disagree on arity, or a reference whose total arity disagrees with the fixed
   one, are rejected as outside the fragment with the declaring site as the secondary span.
   Admission already catches the value-arity case; what this adds is the module leading columns, and
   a code of its own would be clearer.
6. **The `&` and `?` argument-disambiguation sigils are not parsed.** They are the paper's
   mechanism for choosing between a first-order and a higher-order argument, and the scanner has no
   token for them; the literal syntax is `REL0101` at the sigil. That is a parser gap recorded in
   the coverage manifest, not a lowering one, and it is why three rows of the Figure 3/4 table are
   decided on an unannotated surrogate source.
7. **The backend's boundary allocation is not yet removed.** A direct constructor from the
   relational IR into the demand evaluator's prepared form, skipping the serde `Program`, is the
   planned removal ADR 0004 names; this milestone measures the boundary rather than hiding it.
8. **Per-column domains, and the dictionary has no per-type range.** The relational IR carries
   column types and the readout map forwards them; backend v1 collapses every column into one
   global domain, so a program whose columns are disjoint value sets pays `domain^arity` for the
   union. The architecture note also asked for a per-type sub-range in the value dictionary, and
   there is none: ids are assigned in interning order, so one type's ids are not contiguous. Per
   value the `kind` is recorded and per relation the column types are, so the ranges are derivable
   when per-column domains arrive; they are simply not built now.
9. **The IR reserves no symmetry table.** The architecture note asked for one so source-declared or
   discovered symmetries could be forwarded; the backend emits an empty `symmetries` list and the
   IR has no table. Nothing in milestone (a) needs it.
10. **The differential oracle over generated programs is C1189.** The eight committed fixtures are
    hand-written; the generated-program differential is the successor's work.

### What the lowering stage costs

The stage is `parse`, then `admit`, then `lower`; the figure below is the difference between the
`lower` and `admit` stages, which share every earlier boundary, so the shifted code the workspace's

| Cohort          | Source bytes | `lower` minus `admit` | Per source byte | What the lowering did                                                        |
|-----------------|-------------:|----------------------:|----------------:|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ascii           |       43,008 |                79,208 |            1.84 | swept the node pool, then `REL0504` in `declare` at byte 76                  |
| unicode         |       61,888 |                79,977 |            1.29 | swept the node pool, then `REL0504` in `declare` at byte 97                  |
| comment-string  |       65,610 |            18,676,502 |          284.66 | every pass ran; `REL0503` at the close, 896 relations against a budget of 64 |
| malformed-early |       42,956 |                    −1 |            0.00 | the parse fails, so the stage never runs                                     |

The malformed rows are the control on the claim that this is a real stage and not a stub with a
zero-cost path: a source whose parse fails never reaches lowering, and the measured difference is
one instruction or less, which is the branch that decides that, folded into the code around it.
A stub would give the same zero on a source that did lower, and the comment-string row shows this
stage does not.

**The comment-string row is the headline and it is not a lowering cost in the shape the model
assumed.** That cohort's `def label0 = "plain string …"` definitions are, under the adopted
semantics, fact-set definitions: a definition with no parameter list whose body is a constant. So
the cohort declares 896 relations, runs build, distribution, projection, range restriction,
stratification, binarization and closing over all of them, and is rejected only at the closing
pass's relation budget. Relation resolution is a linear scan of the relation pool — in `declare`,
in `resolve_name` and in `qualified` — so the declaring phase alone is quadratic in the relation
count: 896 declarations against a pool growing to 896 is about 400,000 spelling comparisons.
That accounts for the 284.66 instructions per source byte, and it is the first thing a successor
should fix; the fix is the same open-addressed spelling index admission already uses, keyed by
`(owner, spelling)`.

**The ascii and unicode rows have a different cause, and the first version of this report named the
wrong one.** They do not pay the quadratic relation scan at all. Both reject inside `declare`, not
in the body phase: `build::declare` refuses a bracketed head (`def score1[k] = …`) at the *second*
top-level definition, and the declare loop returns on its first error, so exactly one relation is
in the pool when the failure fires. The 2026-09-15 audit measured what those 79,208 and 79,977
instructions actually are. `build::declare_modules` sweeps the entire node pool looking for module
nodes before any definition is declared, and that sweep is linear in the node count at about
6.4 instructions per node: ascii has 12,288 nodes and pays 6.45 per node, unicode has 12,416 and
pays 6.44, and the 769-instruction gap between the two cohorts over their 128-node gap is 6.0 per
extra node. A relation-count quadratic predicts no gap between them at all, since both would
declare 512. The audit's scaling run settles it directly: the ascii stage difference is 11,450 /
21,122 / 40,478 / 79,204 instructions at 64 / 128 / 256 / 512 definitions, ratios 1.84, 1.92 and
1.96, which is linear, where a quadratic would rise fourfold per doubling.

**So each cohort has its own cause, and they are not the same mechanism.** The comment-string row
is the quadratic one: it declares all 896 relations, `find_relation`'s linear scan over a pool
growing to 896 costs about 400,000 spelling comparisons at roughly 46 instructions each, and that
accounts for its 284.66 instructions per source byte. The audit confirmed that by scaling as well —
468,393 / 1,397,718 / 4,748,879 / 18,676,568 instructions at 64 / 128 / 256 / 512 definitions,
ratios 2.98, 3.40 and 3.93, converging on the fourfold rise a quadratic demands. The ascii and
unicode rows are the linear one: a node-pool sweep every lowering pays before its first
declaration, which on the `datalog` cohort costs about 32,000 of the 1,299,495 instructions, or two
and a half per cent.

My Fermi predicted "under 2,000 instructions" for the ascii and unicode rows and was wrong by a
factor of forty. The cause is not the one I first wrote down. The model priced a rejection as a
walk up to the first out-of-fragment construct and missed that every lowering, rejecting or not,
first sweeps the whole node pool for module declarations.

The lowering adds 429,310 L1 data-cache loads, 11,221 L1 load misses and ten last-level misses over

**Allocation.** `the_lowering_stage_does_not_allocate` drives parse, admit and lower eight times
over four sources — the `datalog` cohort at 256 definitions, the ASCII cohort at 64, a source
range restriction rejects and one that exceeds a backend budget — under the counting allocator after
a warm-up that sizes every pool that is sized on first use, and observes **zero allocations** with
retained bytes unchanged. That covers four of the stage's exit paths — a complete lowering, a
`REL0504`, a `REL0501` and a `REL0503`. It does not cover `REL0505`, the disjunction expansion
bound, which is a fifth exit whose pools are reserved like every other but which no source in this
gate drives; `REL0502` is a sixth and no source can reach it. Adding a nested-disjunction source to
the gate is the open item.

**Resident memory.** Peak resident set of the `lower` probe on the `datalog` cohort is 5,956 KiB,
against 5,888 to 6,360 KiB for the other stages and cohorts; the lowering pools are reserved address
space and only the pages the program actually uses are touched.

**What `prepare` cost.** Allocating and dropping a workspace is now **3.97863×** the control
(interval [3.97639, 3.98088]), because `Workspace::new` reserves every pool of the relational IR —
34 pools reserved by `Rir::new` with `try_reserve_exact`. Retained bytes
under the bench limits go from 6,606,852 to 10,324,492. That is the one ratio in this A/B that is
not unity, it is a reservation cost and not a per-source cost, and it is the price of the
zero-allocation contract: a stage that never grows a pool must reserve every pool before it starts.
A workspace is created once per process in the driver and once per operator invocation in
`rel-lower`, so it is charged once against a whole program rather than per definition.

## Mystery ledger

1. **Settled, and it reverses the shape of the question.** Whether the two module-parameter
   shadowing directions can be distinguished by a fixture. The 2026-09-15 audit concluded they
   cannot be, at admission, and proposed an arity-distinguishing fixture that would not have worked.
   At the lowering they are distinguished, but not by inspecting the lowered rule: the losing
   direction produces a rule that is not range-restricted, so the discriminator is acceptance
   itself. The fixture is a `REL0501` whose primary span is the module parameter's own spelling.
2. **Settled: what a lowering that completes actually costs, and why the Fermi missed it by
   sevenfold.** 81.56 instructions per source byte on the `datalog` cohort against a predicted 11.
   The model priced the passes and omitted two things the profile shows: the canonical fingerprint
   is computed inside the stage over a form dominated by the facts and the dictionary rather than by
   the rules, and interning one constant costs about 330 instructions rather than 80 to 130.
3. **Open: why interning costs 330 instructions.** The integer path hashes eight bytes with eight
   multiply-xor steps and probes a table that is one sixteenth full, so the probe is almost always
   one slot. 330 is two to four times what that should cost. The `Value` record is 32 bytes and its
   push is two 16-byte stores, which is exactly the shape sample skid has landed on three times in
   this lane, so part of the 5.99 per cent share is probably skid. *Evidence gap*: a per-class
   decomposition with synthetic single-constant sources at two lengths, which is the method that has
   been right twice in this lane, and a disassembly count of `intern`'s body. Owner: the successor
   that fixes the relation scan, since both are in the same kernel.
4. **Open, and it is the largest number in the profile: `admit::declare` is half the stage.**
   Admission's open-addressed symbol index puts every clause of one relation on one home slot, so a
   cohort with 512 clauses of `edge` walks a 512-entry probe chain per insert. Like for like — both
   as `admit` minus `parse` from these receipts — admission costs 199.95 instructions per source
   byte on `datalog` against 29.27 on the ASCII cohort, a factor of 6.8. (The first version of this
   item said "273 against 40.68", which put the `datalog` composed stage beside the ASCII
   admission-only figure from the C1170 census and is not a like-for-like pair.) This is
   admission's, not the lowering's, and it cancels in the stage difference, but several `def`
   clauses of one relation is idiomatic Rel. *Evidence gap*: none about the mechanism, which is
   read from `admit::insert` and now also confirmed by a fourfold-per-doubling scaling run; the
   remedy — chained buckets or a second probe — is unmeasured. Logged to the discovery track.
5. **Open: the lowering's own relation resolution is a linear scan, on the comment-string cohort.**
   That cohort pays 284.66 instructions per source byte for 896 relations, and its stage difference
   scales fourfold per doubling. The ASCII and Unicode cohorts are *not* an instance of this: they
   reject inside `declare` at the second definition with one relation in the pool, and their 79,000
   instructions are `build::declare_modules`'s linear sweep of the node pool. The first version of
   this item attributed those two rows to relation resolution, which the 2026-09-15 audit refuted.
   *Evidence gap*: none about either mechanism; the fix for the quadratic is the open-addressed
   index keyed by owner and spelling that admission already has, and its saving is priced by the
   comment-string row. Named as the first remaining step and logged to the discovery track.
6. **Open: the disjunction bound is checked after the node it bounds is materialized.**
   `distribute` computes each form node's disjunct set into the conjunct and member pools and then
   compares the count against `Limits::disjuncts`, so a deeply nested conjunction of disjunctions
   can exhaust the member pool and report `REL0503` before it reports `REL0505`. Both are bounded
   failures with spans and neither is an approximation, but the code a user sees depends on which
   bound binds first. *Evidence gap*: a fixture that exhausts the member pool with a disjunct count
   below the bound, and a decision about whether to predict the product before materializing it.
7. **Open: whether the computed fixed point is the least one rests on two independent arguments and
   not on one.** The core's derivation checker replays the trace and then makes a closed-world pass
   requiring every body match over the final relations to have its head present, which is local
   fixedness; the ranked checker searches independently for a derivation of every tuple; and the
   Python oracle agrees on eight fixtures. *Evidence gap*: the generated-program differential, which
   is C1189's.
8. **No genuine mystery remains about the semantics.** Every construct the fragment does not cover
   is in the Figure 3/4 table with its exact outcome, and every outcome is asserted on both scanner
   variants.

## Audit corrections (2026-09-15)

An independent verification audit re-ran every gate and measurement in this report against the code,
fixtures and receipts, recomputed all eight fixture closures with an evaluator written for the
purpose, devised further programs inside and outside the fragment, and read the lowering sources.
Its findings are in `notes/2026-09-15-c1190-milestone-a-audit.md`. The milestone's substance was
confirmed: the chain produces the right closures, both checkers accept every certificate, the gates
pass, parity reproduces bit for bit, and every measurement re-derives from its receipts and from an
independent re-run of the A/B.

Nine defects were found and are repaired in place above. The one that changes a conclusion is the
first.

1. **The cost attribution for the ascii and unicode rows was wrong.** They do not pay the quadratic
   relation scan; they reject inside `declare` at the second definition with one relation in the
   pool, and their cost is `build::declare_modules`'s linear sweep of the node pool at about
   6.4 instructions per node. Corrected in the results table, in the paragraph that follows it, in
   the Fermi retrospective, in Remaining gaps (which is also reordered so the second successor step
   names the sweep), and in Mystery ledger item 5. The quadratic claim is kept for the
   comment-string cohort, where the audit confirmed it by a fourfold-per-doubling scaling run.
2. **The Figure 3/4 summary paragraph miscounted its own table** (twelve, two and seventeen against
   the table's fourteen, one and sixteen) and misnamed the two unparsed rows. Corrected, with the
   three surrogate-source rows now stated.

   Corrected to name the limit the profile was rendered at, the two symbols that appear below it,
