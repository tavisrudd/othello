# Paper V title panel: algebraic combinatorics/coding theory

Cold-read basis: the abstract and the two introductory main theorem statements only.

## Five plausible titles

1. **Chordal and Conference Cubics: Reconstruction up to a Residual Involution**
2. **Six-Point Reconstruction from Chordal and Conference Cubics**
3. **Chordal Cubics, Conference Matrices, and the Golden Frobenius Torsor**
4. **An \(A_5\)-Equivariant Correspondence between Chordal and Conference Cubics**
5. **Conference Companions of Chordal Cubics and Their Binary Residues**

Scores run from 1 (weak/low) to 5 (strong/high). For overclaim risk, 1 is safest and 5 is riskiest.

A has the right searchable nouns, but “cubic shadows” makes it sound as though both cubics are shadows in the same technical sense. B is the strongest bibliographic title, but “exact” suppresses the selected-line hypothesis and the residual \(C_2\)-quotient. C memorably foregrounds information loss, yet hides “chordal,” “conference,” and \(A_5\), and “the icosahedral five-space” is not sufficiently self-defining. D is an effective internal name, not a portable submission title: a reader cannot infer the objects, theorem, or field.

## Recommendation

**Submission title:** *Chordal and Conference Cubics: Reconstruction up to a Residual Involution*

**Short running/slug form:** *Chordal--conference reconstruction*

This title carries the two most discoverable geometric objects, states the principal operation, and advertises the precise obstruction without claiming an unqualified equivalence. The lattice and Frobenius theorem remains a substantial secondary strand; forcing it into the title would make the title enumerative and obscure the reconstruction spine.

## Terminology flags

- **shadows:** potentially misleading in a title. It is accurate for the normalized metric chordal object in the theorem, but readers may take it to mean projection, degeneration, or a common construction applied to both cubics.
- **reconstructing:** basically accurate and useful. It becomes misleading only if the title suggests unconditional two-sided recovery; “up to a residual involution” cures that defect.
- **exact:** misleading unless the selected compatible chordal line is named. Unselected data retain a \(C_2\)-ambiguity, and algebraists may also hear “exact” as exact-sequence language.
- **icosahedral:** defensible shorthand for the \(A_5\)-setting, but weakly portable here. “The icosahedral five-space” may suggest a standard uniquely named space or a real Euclidean model not specified by the statements.
- **correspondence:** acceptable only with a marking/selection or quotient qualifier. Alone it blurs the distinction between the selected-line equivalence and the unselected residual torsor.
