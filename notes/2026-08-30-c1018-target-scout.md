## Ranked shortlist

### 1. Close the exact distance of the bivariate-bicycle code `[[756,16,d]]`

**Exact question.** Determine `d` exactly for the Bravyi et al. bivariate-bicycle code
published as `[[756,16,<=34]]`, or raise the certified lower bound above the current
`d >= 24`.

**Why it is open.** The published value 34 is an upper bound from heuristic decoding, not
a proof (Bravyi et al., arXiv:2308.07915, Table 3, explicitly marks it as an upper bound).
The snapshot records the non-closure in its own words: "a fourth, `[[756,16,.]]`, yields
only the lower bound `d>=24` and is not closed"
(`notes/2026-07-31-results-summary-snapshot.md`, quantum-code distance section, the same
paragraph that reports the three closed bivariate-bicycle codes). This is the single
explicitly unfinished exact computation named in the portfolio.

**Why Ergodis fits.** `P2` directly; the code is well inside the demonstrated reach, and
the sibling `[[784,24,24]]` at greater length was closed in 127 seconds. The compiled
`hx-gz-v3.ergocsl` artifact and the replay command already exist.

**Compute shape.** Multi-wave, with a useful partial result available in one wave. The
radius-22 stage exhausted 7,565,506,294 candidates in 32.8 s on 16 threads; radius 22 cost
9.21x radius 20, so radius 24 is minutes and radius 26 onward grows steeply. Weights 24–32
are the open band. Two levers named in the C985 spike change the shape: a memory-bounded
four-completion test, and recovering a replayable weight-34 incumbent from an
information-set decoder so pulse propagation can prune. Down-scoped win: certify `d >= 26`
or `d >= 28` and publish the narrowed interval.

**What a win looks like.** An exact-distance certificate with an independently replayed
minimum-weight witness, replacing a published heuristic table entry — the same class of
result as the two length-1496 lifted-product codes whose published upper bounds were
already replaced.

**Prior art.** `notes/2026-08-29-c985-bb756-large-code-spike.md` is the direct predecessor
and names this as its own next gate. This is a continuation of C985, not a new target;
coordinate with whoever owns the C985 line rather than re-spiking it.

### 2. Sweep the published qLDPC tables for distances that are only upper bounds

**Exact question.** For every code in the current bivariate-bicycle, lifted-product,
La-cross and related qLDPC literature with length under roughly 1500 whose published
distance is stated as an upper bound or as the output of randomized trials, compute the
exact minimum distance.

**Why it is open.** Published distances in this literature routinely come from heuristic
decoders and randomized search; the sources say so. The crosswalk already identifies this
as the *only* real contact between the repository and the famous `BIG-709` qLDPC frontier,
and identifies it as computational rather than structural: "exact distances for codes up to
length 1496, two of which replace published upper bounds obtained from randomized trials"
(`notes/open-problems/results-crosswalk.md`, `QI-2025-transversal-review` row).

**Why Ergodis fits.** `P2`, unchanged. This is the primitive's designed use.

**Compute shape.** One wave closes several small and medium codes; the large ones are
minutes to low hours each (3,089 s and 1,159 s for the two check sides of a length-1496
code). Trivially parallel across codes, and every code is independent, so the wave has no
all-or-nothing failure mode.

**What a win looks like.** A committed table of certified exact distances with decoded
witnesses and replay commands, each cross-checked by the independent second implementation
already used for the length-1496 witnesses. Every entry that lowers a published upper bound
is a directly citable correction to the qLDPC literature.

**Prior art.** Snapshot section on exact distances of large quantum codes; C985 reports for
`[[288,12,18]]`, `[[360,12,24]]`, `[[784,24,24]]`, R2Elite01/R2Elite02. The scope of this
target is *the codes not yet attempted*; the trust-tier language in the snapshot (exhaustive
enumeration by one implementation, witness replayed by a second, lower bound resting on the
enumerator alone) must be carried forward verbatim into any new claim.

### 3. Finite-size no-go census: transversal hierarchy level versus check weight

**Exact question.** Over an explicitly stated family class of CSS codes — say all CSS pairs
with `n <= N` and maximum stabilizer check weight `w <= W`, quotiented by coordinate
permutation — does any code carry a diagonal transversal gate at hierarchy level three or
higher acting nontrivially on the logicals? Equivalently: at what `(n, W)` does the observed
tension become a proved exclusion?

**Why it is open.** Last night's transversal wave found the tension exactly, not
heuristically, in nine codes: in the hypercube colour family `[[2^D,D,2]]` the minimum
possible `X`-check weight is exactly `2^D = n`, and in quantum Reed–Muller
`[[2^m-1,1,3]]` it is exactly `2^{m-1} = (n+1)/2`, while the transversal level rises with
the parameter in both. The report's own mystery ledger leaves it open —
"Whether the exact tension between rising transversal level and rising minimum check weight
is a theorem or an artifact of these two families ... **Open.** Nine codes are not a family
theorem" — and assigns it to a successor task
(`notes/2026-08-30-c1018-hunt-transversal-css.md`, section 8 and ledger row 4).

**Why Ergodis fits.** `P1` for the rank/span and Smith-normal-form layer, `P4` for the code
enumeration modulo permutation. The exact framework is already written and validated: the
driver `ergodis-private/src/bin/c1018_transversal_css.rs` computes the complete diagonal
transversal group over all real phases from the coset-constancy conditions, and the method
reproduced the known Steane, `[[15,1,3]]` and `[[8,3,2]]` facts before being used on new
codes.

**Compute shape.** Needs down-scoping before a wave. Enumerating all CSS codes at `n <= 64`
is out of reach; enumerate a structured subfamily instead — quasi-cyclic or bicycle CSS
pairs at fixed circulant size, or all CSS pairs generated by a bounded-weight seed under a
fixed permutation group. One wave per subfamily.

**What a win looks like.** A certificate table of the form "no code in family `F` with
`n <= N` and check weight `<= W` admits a level-3 diagonal transversal gate", with a
Hall-style or rank-style witness per excluded code. That is a finite-size no-go for exactly
the quantity the asymptotic `BIG-709` target is about, and it is the outcome the attack
portfolio explicitly prefers: "the best outcome of a census is often a reusable forbidden
spectrum, multiplier obstruction or preservation/no-go lemma, rather than a larger table"
(`plausible-bridges/independent-attack-portfolio.md`, `ej`+`tt` pressure test).

**Prior art.** The nine exact classifications from last night, including the three new ones
(`[[16,4,2]]` exactly CCCZ, `[[32,5,2]]` exactly C^4Z, `[[31,1,3]]` logical group exactly
Z_16 at level 4); the AME/MDS-CSS Clifford rigidity theorem
(`notes/2026-07-25-c649-stabilizer-ame-full-weyl-rigidity.md`) as the structural no-go
boundary this would extend computationally. `plausible-bridges/transversal-codes.md` route 3
is this target in the dossier's own language.

### 4. Projective Reed–Solomon redundancy ten and the fate of Conjecture B'

**Exact question.** At redundancy 10, is the deep locus strictly larger than the persistent
locus for some small `q` — that is, does a second exceptional orbit exist beyond the one
found at `(r,q) = (9,13)` — and does the replacement Conjecture B' (`q0(r) <= 23` for every
redundancy) survive?

**Why it is open.** Last night's follow-up wave falsified Conjecture B at redundancy nine by
exhaustive census of all 883,708,281 points of `PG(8,13)`: covering radius 8 as predicted,
but deep = 1638 against the persistent locus's 1274, exposing one new exceptional
`PGL_2(13)` orbit with stabilizer order 6, catalecticant rank 3 and a two-dimensional
non-split apolar pencil. That is the campaign's only genuinely new mathematical object, and
it currently has sample size one. The general classification problem is the recognized
specialist frontier `COD-PRS-deep-holes` (Zhang–Wan, arXiv:1605.02423), and the crosswalk
lists "complete PRS deep-hole classification beyond the proved redundancies" among the
locally important open programmes.

**Why Ergodis fits.** `P1` exactly, with the driver already written
(`ergodis-private/src/bin/c1018_prs_deephole.rs`) and the prime-field rank oracle validated
against 116 cross-checks and against the committed redundancy-5, -6 and -7 certificates.

**Compute shape.** One wave, probably the cheapest genuinely-new-mathematics wave available.
`PG(8,13)` at 8.8e8 points already ran inside the overnight window; `PG(9,13)` is roughly
13x that, which is inside `P4`'s demonstrated 1e11 range, and smaller `q` at `r = 10` is
cheaper still. The known gap is that the oracle is prime-field only, so extension fields must
be skipped or handled separately.

**What a win looks like.** Either a second exceptional orbit — which turns a one-off into a
phenomenon worth a structural conjecture about catalecticant rank and apolar pencils — or an
exhaustive negative at `r = 10` with the exact searched domain, which sharpens B' into a
statement with real evidential weight. Both outcomes are publishable inside the existing PRS
programme.

**Prior art.** `notes/2026-08-30-c1018-hunt-prs-deepholes.md` (three sharpened conjectures,
41 exhaustive cells, `X(8) ∩ [8,19] = {8,9,11}`, `X(4) = ∅` for `4 <= q <= 64`, and the
GF(16) field-labelling artifact logged as a standing certificate-recheck hazard). Also the
committed redundancy-5/6/7/9 literature audits under `notes/2026-07-2*`.

### 5. Order-12 planes: lift the order-13 collineation case from decomposition to incidence

**Exact question.** Does a projective plane of order 12 admit a collineation of order 13?
Concretely: is any of the 133 enumerated starters in `Z_13` jointly compatible with an
order-13 orbit matrix carrying a `(2^6)` row, and does any compatible pair lift to an actual
`157 x 157` incidence structure?

**Why it is open.** Last night's wave proved the reduction but explicitly stopped at the
lift: "the order-13 tactical decomposition is solvable ... so that case cannot die at
decomposition level", and the report's exact negatives section states "**No claim is made**
about the existence of a collineation of order 13 of a plane of order 12, about the existence
of an invariant hyperoval, or about the number of order-12 orbit matrices. Those are open at
the end of this wave" (`notes/2026-08-30-c1018-hunt-plane12.md`). The dossier
`plausible-bridges/projective-planes.md` names exactly this route — "eliminate structured
subclasses first ... plausibly tractable and scientifically useful even if the unrestricted
case is not" — and sets the promotion gate at "a certified *new* nonexistence class at order
12", which last night did not meet.

**Why Ergodis fits.** `P3` is already load-bearing here: all 10,395 pairings of `Z_13 \ {0}`
were screened with an explicit Hall-deficient set as the rejection witness for each of the
10,262 failures. `P4` handles the orbit-matrix and incidence-completion layers; the same wave
ran a 1.18e11-node exhaustion for the planar difference-set negative.

**Compute shape.** One wave completes the abandoned `n = 12` orbit-matrix profile census
(which is also the evidence gap for mystery-ledger row 3, the order-10 versus order-12
profile-rigidity question). The full incidence lift is multi-wave and its cost is genuinely
unknown, because the branching factor after fixing a starter and an orbit matrix has not been
measured.

**Mandatory gate before any compute.** A literature audit under
`notes/literature-audit-conventions.md`. The wave states plainly that "**No literature-audit
pass was run in this wave**" and that the collineation groups of hypothetical planes of orders
10 and 12 have a substantial literature. The order-13 case may already be settled in print;
that check is cheap and decides whether the target exists at all.

**What a win looks like.** Either a certified nonexistence for the order-13 collineation
class — which does meet the dossier's promotion gate — or the complete, replayable list of
surviving starter/orbit-matrix pairs as the input to a later incidence search. Note the
crosswalk discipline the wave itself insists on: any such result is conditional
("if a plane of order 12 exists, then it does not carry this symmetry") and bears on the
prime-power conjecture not at all.

### 6. The exact equiangular-line maximum `M(18) ∈ {57,58,59}`

**This target is already repo-queued. Ranking it here means executing queued work
`C1000(a)` / `C737`, not opening a new line.** `C1000` is queued as an
"INDEPENDENT OPEN-PROBLEM WIN" whose candidate (a) is precisely this census, with
instructions to absorb `C737` if chosen; `C737` is the standing task for the census itself.
The feasibility spike `notes/2026-08-29-c1000-feasibility-spike.md` already returned
**COMPLETE** with the recommendation to choose candidate (a) and absorb C737. Any wave here
must be coordinated with the owner of those rows and must not duplicate the spike.

**Exact question.** Decide whether the maximum number of equiangular lines in `R^18` is 57,
58 or 59.

**Why it is open.** The recorded range is `57 <= M(18) <= 59`
(`plausible-bridges/equiangular-lines.md`, with the bounds cross-checked against OEIS
A002853 on 2026-07-31 as a secondary status source). It is the smallest and narrowest open
value in the table, and the attack portfolio ranks it second overall among independent
projects.

**Why Ergodis fits.** Partially. There is no direct match to a proven primitive: this is
exact rational and algebraic-integer spectral arithmetic plus interlacing and Jacobi filters,
then a canonical graph search. `P4` covers the graph-existence half and the engine's exact
arithmetic covers the spectral half, but the fit is by analogy rather than by reuse of an
existing driver. Against that, the certificate story is the best of any target on this list:
per the spike, a construction is an integer matrix checkable in milliseconds and every
eliminated candidate is a rational Farkas vector verified by one exact matrix-vector product,
with no solver trust and no gigabyte proof traces.

**Compute shape.** Multi-wave. The spike measured rather than assumed the cost: the candidate
space at `n = 59` and `n = 58` is about five times the settled `n = 60` case, because two
trace identities confine the free spectrum to a sphere of radius under ten and a 2-adic
type-2 filter removes about 97 percent of the remainder.

**Main risk, from the spike.** Pre-emption. Greaves and Syatriadi published the enumeration
speed-up in April 2025 after naming `n = 59` as their blocker in 2021. The spike's mitigation
is to treat the enumeration as the entry ticket and aim at the elimination side.

**What a win looks like.** The machine-readable census of feasible 58/59-line spectra with a
stated reason each candidate dies or survives — valuable even before the last case closes —
and, at best, the elimination that fixes `M(18) = 57`.

### 7. Twisted Reed–Solomon deep-hole censuses

**Cross-lane: this is `C510`, pegged to the `reed-solomon` lane.** Scouting it here does not
claim it for gem-mining; running it requires coordination with that lane's owner.

**Exact question.** For the residual one-twist twisted Reed–Solomon families, determine the
covering radius and classify all deep holes at small `q` and low redundancy, in the ranges
left open by the published high-rate exclusions.

**Why it is open.** `COD-2025-twisted-deep-holes` (Fang, Xu and Zhu, arXiv:2403.11436,
published in Finite Fields and Their Applications in 2025) gives the covering radius and
standard deep holes for a general one-twist family, then excludes other deep holes only in a
high-rate range and completely determines only the full-length family in its top
redundancies. Gu, Wang and Zhang (arXiv:2509.08526) treat a different
multiplicative-evaluation one-twist family with the same shape of partial result. The gap
between "high-rate exclusion range" and "top redundancies" is where an exhaustive census
lands.

**Why Ergodis fits.** `P1` with a small change: the PRS driver's rank oracle needs only the
twisted parity-check columns substituted for the rational-normal-curve ones. Last night's
driver is the template, and the same exhaustive-projective-direction sweep applies.

**Compute shape.** One wave for small `q` at redundancy at most 6, assuming the twist keeps
the columns on a controlled projection. The anticipated obstruction, stated in the C510 card,
is loss of the full `PGL_2` symmetry, which costs the orbit quotient and therefore the
constant factor; a surviving affine or dihedral subgroup may be enough.

**What a win looks like.** An exhaustive deep-hole classification in a named open range,
with the same certificate format as the PRS censuses. The C510 card asks for a go/no-go
verdict first, and that spike is itself a legitimate one-wave deliverable.

**Prior art.** `notes/reed-solomon-tasks/c510-trs-deep-hole-pilot.md` (queued, gated behind
the C509 entry theorem); `notes/2026-07-22-c491-prs-redundancy-five-literature-audit.md` and
`notes/2026-07-23-c516-prs-redundancy-nine-literature-audit.md` for the citation streams.

### 8. Pseudo-ovals over small odd fields: is every pseudo-oval on `E(5,q)` a pseudo-conic?

**Exact question.** For `q = 5, 7, 9, 11` odd, enumerate all pseudo-ovals of `PG(5,q)` up to
equivalence and decide whether each is a pseudo-conic.

**Why it is open.** This is Problem 6 of the sixteen numbered problems in
`FG-2025-Hirschfeld-Thas` (Hirschfeld and Thas, *Arcs, Caps and Generalisations in a Finite
Projective Space*, DOI 10.3390/math13091489, arXiv:2503.06243): "decide whether every
pseudo-oval on `E(5,q)`, `q` odd, is a pseudo-conic". The catalogue records that all sixteen
numbered problem statements were inspected. Problem 6 is the one item in that list whose
fixed-`q` instance is a finite orbit computation rather than a statement about all fields.

**Why Ergodis fits.** `P4`: a pseudo-oval on `E(5,q)` is a set of `q+1` mutually disjoint
planes of `PG(5,q)` with the standard tangent condition, so the search is a canonical
augmentation over plane sets with an exact incidence oracle — the same shape as the arc and
cap searches the repository already runs, and squarely inside the finite-geometry results
family the crosswalk calls "the only dense object-level overlap".

**Compute shape.** One wave at `q = 5` and `q = 7`; `q = 9` needs an extension field, which
the current rank oracle does not cover, so it needs either the GF(p^h) gap closed or a
different incidence backend. Down-scope to `q = 5, 7` for the first wave.

**Gate before compute.** A literature audit. Small-`q` computer classifications of
pseudo-ovals exist in the finite-geometry literature and the low cases are the ones most
likely already done; the catalogue entry does not say which `q` are settled. Confirm the
first genuinely open `q` before spending a wave — this is exactly the failure mode the
attack portfolio warns about for order-12 planes ("the worst failure mode would be spending
months certifying a case already eliminated in older finite-geometry literature").

**Prior art.** None in the repository: the snapshot's arc, cap and conic results are in
`PG(2,q)` and `PG(3,q)`, and no pseudo-oval or egg computation appears in the local results
index. The crosswalk's `FG-2025-Hirschfeld-Thas` row is marked **weak** precisely because
degree bounds on uncovered loci "classify no hyperoval or ovoid, which is what these problems
ask for" — a direct exhaustive classification at fixed `q` is a different and cleaner kind of
contact.

## Rejected, with reasons

- **Everything Hadamard, Legendre-pair or conference-matrix adjacent** (`BIG-401`,
  `FD-2026-Hadamard-668`, `FD-2024-Hadamard-database`, `plausible-bridges/hadamard.md`,
  queue candidate `C1000(b)`) — owned by another agent; excluded by the scouting brief.
  Recorded here only so the exclusion is visible, not as a judgment on value.
- **Unrestricted order-12 plane search** — the dossier rates the full proof-carrying search
  "extreme with current evidence", and the order-10 oval precedent consumed terabyte
  certificates and thousands of core hours for a *restricted* subproblem.
- **Good qLDPC codes with linear distance and a transversal non-Clifford gate**
  (`BIG-709` asymptotic core, `QI-2026-almost-good-qLDPC-transversal`) — a construction or
  impossibility theorem, with no finite object to certify. The dossier's own reality check
  calls it a field-level problem. Target 3 above is the reachable finite shadow of it.
- **Complete MUBs in dimension six** (`BIG-706`) — the search space is continuous; the
  generalist crosswalk already marks the repository relation **negative**, and no exact finite
  census formulation exists.
- **SIC-POVM existence / Zauner** (`BIG-705`) — algebraic number theory over ray class fields
  of real quadratic fields, not a prime-field census. Crosswalk state: no present bridge.
- **Rank of the 3x3 matrix-multiplication tensor over `F_2`** (`BIG-607` neighbourhood) —
  genuinely finite-field-shaped and tempting for `P1`, but exhaustive nonexistence at rank 22
  is far beyond the 1e11-node envelope, and flip-graph methods yield only upper bounds against
  a well-funded field. Rejected on scale, not on fit.
- **Ryser and Brualdi–Stein Latin-square transversals** (`BIG-411`) — the best pure fit to
  `P3` on this entire list, and no repository prior art, but the exhaustive cases are done
  (order at most 9) and order 11 is out of reach by many orders of magnitude. It would need a
  down-scoping to a structured family, for example squares with a prescribed prime-order
  autotopism, plus a literature audit against the existing Latin-square computational
  literature. Revisit only if someone wants to design that restriction first.
- **Erdős–Szekeres convex-polygon thresholds, Hadwiger–Nelson** (`BIG-416`, `BIG-403`) —
  SAT-shaped rather than finite-field, matching or orbit shaped; no Ergodis primitive gives an
  advantage over the specialized solvers already applied.
- **K-stability of Fano varieties, Manin's conjecture, Rational Points 2025, two-`U(1)`
  anomaly geometry** (`AG-2026-K-stability`, `ARITH-*`, `PHYS-2026-two-U1`) — no exact finite
  certificate; the crosswalk states of these are "candidate after full extraction" and
  "priority audit", meaning literature work, not compute.
- **Hierarchical locally recoverable codes** (`LRC-2025-Haymaker-Malmskog-Matthews`) — marked
  `candidate` in the crosswalk, but the entry itself says "exact parameterization of that
  question is still to be extracted before crosswalk use". Not scoutable as a compute target
  until someone reads the paper and writes down the parameters.
- **Hirschfeld–Thas Problems 1–5 and 7–17** — all quantify over all `q` or over a
  characteristic class, so no fixed-`q` exhaustion settles them. Problem 6 (target 8) was
  kept because its fixed-`q` instance is a finite computation.
- **Combinatorial games** (`GAME-2025-Nowakowski`) — the mechanical screen of the complete
  20,940-word extraction found no occurrence of Nofil, cap game, Node-Kayles or finite
  geometry. The repository's game results are not on this canonical list, and the `kayles`
  lane is dormant.
