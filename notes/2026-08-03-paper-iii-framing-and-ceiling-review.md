# Paper III framing, unity, and ceiling review

**Date:** 2026-08-03
**Lane:** `clebsch` (Paper III stream; advisory, no manuscript edit)
**Author:** session review (Fable main agent) after a same-day fresh read of the

## Assessment baseline

Fresh-read grade for the released version: low nineties. With the current
route (C815 four-shadow recognition, C823 robustness/distance/moments,
C816/C824 integration) completed: mid-nineties. The gap to Paper I is not
rigor or care — the relative-marking discipline and the integral-boundary
disclosure are field-leading — it is unity and dependence: the paper reads as
a suite of strong results connected thematically by one golden sign, and its
geometric infrastructure is cited from Hitchin nearly wholesale.

## Framing upgrades

**1. Name the one theorem.** The missing "what is the single result" has a
cheap fix: a headline equivalence-of-torsors theorem. The marked bridge datum
identifies four orientation torsors relative to it — the incidence sheet
(`z = ±4√5 σ₃`), the conference source (`±C`), the oriented cubic line
(`±Z`), and the harmonic sign (`±` on the Gaunt cubic). Almost every piece of
this is already proved; what is absent is one displayed statement, early,
asserting the four-way identification and pointing to the sections that prove
each leg. That single theorem converts the suite into an arc and gives a
referee the sentence to quote.

**2. Re-center on recognition.** The forward version acquires, through the
C815/C823 route, exactly the genre the current version lacks: recognition and
rigidity theorems (four-shadow recognition, aligned-certificate distance and
robustness, the order-26 separator). Together with aligned-design faithfulness
— already the paper's most exportable theorem — the natural forward framing is
*descent, realization, and recognition of the golden operator*: the sign
descends from arithmetic (source), realizes classically (shadows), and is
recoverable from small local data (recognition). Faithfulness then stops
looking like an appendix-flavored extra and becomes the spine of the third
movement.

**3. Sell the conductor, not the constant.** The strongest arithmetic point is
underplayed in the abstract: the factor five is *forced* as the discriminant
of a residue-field pinching — a conductor, not a computed value. Leading with
"the descent datum of the two icosahedra is the golden quadratic algebra, and
the cover's square class is its discriminant" has a visibly higher ceiling
than "we determine the constant in Hitchin's cover."

**4. Elevate the Gaunt factorization.** The splitting of the restriction
coefficient into the universal angular-momentum coupling times the marked
Petersen restriction scalar — "the prime eleven belongs to angular momentum,
not to the golden descent" — is a memorable why-this-number result currently
living inside prose. Naming it as a proposition costs a paragraph and gives
the harmonic section its own quotable.

## Priority-ceiling judo moves

These follow the Paper II pattern: convert a dependence or a blocker into the
theorem.

**J1 — arithmetic completion of Hitchin.** Rather than positioning the paper
downstream of Hitchin, claim the completed arithmetic invariant package of his
construction: square class, Stein algebra, conductor/pinching local model, and
the spinor class of the exchanger, with the finite-field specialization
criterion. "The icosahedral incidence cover carries exactly two quadratic
characters, the descent class of five and the spinor class of two, and these
are its complete arithmetic obstruction data" is a claim Hitchin's papers do
not make and cannot be scooped on cheaply.

**J2 — the integral seam as a theorem-shaped problem.** The unspecified
inverted integer is the one seam a referee can poke. Two moves, by cost:
cheap — consolidate the seam into a single displayed open problem with the
precise missing assertions (already isolated in the cover section) and cite
the operator package's small minimal base from the C682 corpus as evidence for
the expected answer; expensive — actually prove the integral identification
(flatness, normality, Stein base change) and turn the bad-prime set into a
theorem. The expensive move is the single highest-rigor upgrade available to
the forward version and would merit its own allocated task; it should be
weighed against the golden lane's needs since both consume the same
infrastructure.

**J3 — extraction option on faithfulness.** Aligned-design faithfulness plus
the conference-signing corollary would stand alone as a short combinatorics
note aimed at a two-graph audience, cited back by the series. Gain: the
series' most general theorem reaches its natural readership and raises the
citation ceiling of every numbered paper. Cost: the suite loses its
third-movement spine exactly when the recognition reframing (upgrade 2) wants
it. These two moves are alternatives, not companions; my recommendation is
the reframing, not the extraction, unless a concrete venue plan for the note
exists. Caution first: the novelty of reconstruction-from-aligned-four-sets
against the switching-class literature has not been audited; run the bounded
literature check before either move.

**J4 — operational uniqueness of order six.** Balanced-exchange rigidity can
be restated operationally: order six is the unique nontrivial symmetric
conference order whose exchange statistics cannot detect the cut. This pairs
naturally with the golden quantum-statistics companion once that has a stable
public locator, and the operational phrasing raises the result from a spectral
curiosity to a characterization with an audience in quantum information.
Keep the companion citation gated on the locator, per the standing rule.

**J5 — golden integral cross-anchor with Paper I.** Today's C855 results give
Paper I a self-contained proof of the Dye package with an explicit hexagon
normal form and a polarity matrix over the golden integers; Paper III's
conference Gram lives in the same arithmetic. A short remark in the forward
version tying the two integral structures (and citing Paper I's formal
artifact) strengthens the series' claim that one golden order underlies
source, shadow, and return — at essentially zero cost. The literature audit
found the normal form is Dye's own, so the remark cites Dye through Paper I's
self-contained treatment, with no novelty language.

## Unity of mathematical level

The paper mixes three registers, and the register switches are where readers
fall off. Placing each result:

| register                       | results there |
|--------------------------------|---------------|
| research-grade / theorem       | Stein algebra and pinching; aligned faithfulness; balanced-exchange uniqueness |
| structural verification        | operator shadows; spinor class; golden fibre |
| exact evaluation / bookkeeping | harmonic restriction scalar; marked-datum comparisons |

The unity problem is that the middle and bottom rows sit beside the top row
without being raised to it. Editorial moves (appendix demotion, consolidated
marking subsection) help, but the real levelers are mathematical — for each
lower-register block there is a specific theorem that would lift it:

**L1 — an invariance theorem in place of the bookkeeping.** The
marked-datum apparatus is carried through every comparison because no
statement says how much of the datum the comparisons actually use. The
ambiguity ledger (C733) is the raw material for exactly such a statement:
determine the torsor of markings precisely, and prove that each composite
sign comparison (source to conference, source to harmonic) depends on the
datum only through a stated quotient — or exhibit the exact dependence where
it fails. One theorem of the form "the composite comparison is independent of
the chart lift and Petersen labels, and depends only on the ordered-axes
orbit" would replace the recurring not-claimed disclaimers with a positive
invariance result. This is the single highest-value unity move: it converts
the paper's largest reading tax into a theorem at the top register, and its
proof material is largely already in the ledger.

**L2 — the harmonic return as an isolation theorem.** The Gaunt computation
is an exact evaluation; its level-upgrade is already allocated as C813
(Petersen-channel eigenvalues, branching, restriction scalars, then a family
or isolation theorem). An isolation statement — degree six as the first or
the only channel where the invariant cubic restricts to the golden module
with a nonzero scalar, with the scalar as the eigenvalue of a canonical
Petersen-channel intertwiner — would make the harmonic section end on a
theorem rather than a number. Recommendation: treat C813 not as an optional
generalization but as the Paper III unity upgrade, sequenced so C824 can
integrate a positive outcome or state the boundary.

**L3 — shadows as functors, not coincidences.** The operator-shadows theorem
verifies that four constructions give one cubic; the level above it explains
why: the shadows are canonical functors of one torsor (Pfaffian, determinant,
exterior-cube, polar), and the commuting diagram exists intrinsically. The
mathematics for this exists in-house in the C704/C711 corpus (the functorial
bridge and the certificate-free identities), but that corpus is pegged to the
`golden` lane, which may cite but not be absorbed by Paper III. So the move
is available only as a boundary decision: either a short intrinsic-diagram
subsection with the golden lane's consent and citation, or leave the
verification register and accept the level gap. Flag for the C824
architecture selection; do not absorb silently.

**L4 — arithmetic seam.** The integral identification (J2's expensive
branch) is the top-register completion of the arithmetic section; without it
the section is a rational theorem with an integral remark. Ranked behind L1
and L2 because the seam is disclosed and cordoned, whereas L1/L2 tax every
reader.

Priority among levelers: L1 first (largest tax, cheapest proof material),
then L2 (allocated already), then L3 (boundary decision), then L4
(expensive). If only one lands in the forward version, L1 changes the
paper's read the most.

## Recommended order

1. Fold upgrades 1, 3, 4 and judo J1, J5 into the C816/C824 integration passes
   already routed — they are framing-level and cost prose, not mathematics.
2. Decide J3 (extraction versus reframing) before C824's architecture
   selection, since it changes the paper's third movement; default to
   reframing. Gate on the aligned-faithfulness literature check.
3. Treat J2's expensive branch (integral identification) as a candidate
   future allocation, not part of the current route.
