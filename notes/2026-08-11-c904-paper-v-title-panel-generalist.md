# Paper V title panel: cold generalist report

## Basis and scale

This assessment uses only the current abstract and the two introductory main
theorem statements. Scores run from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong), except that
**overclaim risk** runs from 1 (low risk) to 5 (high risk).

## Five plausible titles

1. **Chordal and Conference Cubics: Reconstruction and a Residual
   \(C_2\)-Torsor**
2. **Reconstructing Six-Point Data from Chordal and Conference Cubics**
3. **Chordal Cubics, Conference Matrices, and a Two-Fold Reconstruction
   Ambiguity**
4. **From Singular Quartics to Conference Companions**
5. **Six-Point Reconstruction and Frobenius Markings for
   \(A_5\)-Invariant Cubics**

The first is the strongest submission title. It names the two searchable
objects, states the mathematical action, and advertises the obstruction that
prevents an unqualified equivalence. The fifth reflects more of the second
main theorem, but is less immediate and makes the paper sound as though both
cubics carry Frobenius markings directly.

Candidate (b) has the best frame, but its unqualified “exact” correspondence
suppresses the selected-line hypothesis and residual \(C_2\)-quotient. A safer
revision is the recommended title above. Candidate (a) is accessible but
“shadows” is undefined in the title and does not clearly apply to both sides.
Candidate (c) is vivid yet sacrifices the standard search terms and gives the
ambient representation a name whose meaning is not evident. Candidate (d)
would work as an internal nickname, not as a portable journal title: neither
the cubic families nor the reconstruction problem can be recovered from it.

## Word-level cautions

- **shadows**: mildly misleading. It is defensible for the normalized metric
  chordal object, but sounds metaphorical to a new reader and may incorrectly
  classify the conference cubic as the same kind of shadow.
- **reconstructing**: sound if the reconstructed marked six-point carrier or
  bridge data is named. Unqualified, it may suggest recovery of all source
  data, which the theorem expressly does not claim.
- **exact**: risky without an immediate qualifier. Exact mutual inversion holds
  after selecting a chordal line; after forgetting it, the theorem gives a
  residual \(C_2\)-quotient rather than an equivalence.
- **icosahedral**: recognizable as an \(A_5\) cue but potentially misleading
  here. “Icosahedral five-space” is not self-defining and can be read as a
  geometric ambient space rather than a five-dimensional representation.
- **correspondence**: accurate only when its level is stated—selected-line
  equivalence or unselected residual quotient. Alone, it blurs that central
  distinction.

## Recommendation

**Submission title:** *Chordal and Conference Cubics: Reconstruction and a
Residual \(C_2\)-Torsor*

**Running title / slug:** *Chordal–conference reconstruction*
