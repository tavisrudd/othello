## The bottleneck lemma

Let an equivariant raising operator carry the \(\rho\)-multiplicity spaces
\[
M_{n-6}(\rho)\xrightarrow{B}M_n(\rho)
\xrightarrow{A}M_{n+6}(\rho),
\]
and let the lowering operator be its positive adjoint.  Suppose
\[
\dim M_{n-6}(\rho)=0,\qquad
\dim M_n(\rho)=2,\qquad
\dim M_{n+6}(\rho)=1.
\]
Then the degree-\(n\) corner generated by all closed words in the raising
operator and its adjoint acts on \(M_n(\rho)\) through
\[
\operatorname{span}\{I,A^\dagger A\}.
\]
In particular it is commutative and cannot be \(M_2\).

Indeed, split a closed word at its first returns to degree \(n\).  A primitive
excursion beginning downward vanishes because \(M_{n-6}(\rho)=0\).  A
primitive excursion beginning upward factors as
\[
M_n(\rho)\xrightarrow{A}M_{n+6}(\rho)
\xrightarrow{\lambda}M_{n+6}(\rho)
\xrightarrow{A^\dagger}M_n(\rho),
\]
where the middle endomorphism is scalar because the intermediate
multiplicity space at the endpoints has dimension one.  Thus it is a scalar
multiple of \(A^\dagger A\).  Products of primitive excursions remain in
the same two-space because \(A^\dagger A\) has rank at most one.

For the Klein operator the affine-\(E_8\) recurrence gives
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
n&\operatorname{Sym}^n|_{2.A_5}\\ \hline
16&5^{\oplus2}\oplus3'\oplus4,\\
22&3^{\oplus2}\oplus5^{\oplus2}\oplus3'\oplus4,\\
28&3\oplus5^{\oplus3}\oplus3'\oplus4^{\oplus2}.
\end{array}
\]
Hence \(\rho=3\) has the multiplicity pattern \(0,2,1\), exactly the lemma's
bottleneck.  The exact return calculation has dimension \(8\), rather than
at most \(7\), so \(A\ne0\): the \(3\)-corner is precisely
\(\mathbf C^2\), not merely the scalar line.

This argument proves failure for every closed word, including arbitrarily
long and nested returns.  It does not depend on the numerical normalization
of \(\Delta\).

## Why this is the first failure

The exact affine-\(E_8\) recurrence and rational matrix closure were checked
for every integer degree \(0\le n\le22\).  In degrees \(0\) through \(21\),
\(\langle C_n,D_n\rangle\) has dimension
\(\sum_\rho m_\rho(n)^2\), hence already equals the full commutant.  At
degree \(22\) the dimensions are \(8<10\).

The commutator \([C_{22},D_{22}]\) has rank \(10\).  A nonzero commutator on
a doubled irreducible has rank twice that irreducible's dimension.  Thus the
rank-ten part is exactly the doubled \(5\); the doubled \(3\) is the
unresolved block.  This independently matches the \(0,2,1\) neighbor
obstruction.

As a bounded word-level cross-check, all \(97\) nonempty balanced words of
length at most eight have a seven-dimensional nonunital span at degree
\(22\); adjoining the identity gives the same dimension eight.  Adding the
third straight outward return also leaves dimension eight.  These finite
checks support the structural lemma but are not used to extrapolate it.
