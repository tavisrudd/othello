## 1. Saturating the Fano curve lattice

Let (i:F\hookrightarrow J) be the Albanese embedding of the Fano surface of
a smooth cubic threefold, with principal polarization (\Theta).  The exact
source is Roulleau, *Fano surfaces with 12 or 30 elliptic curves*, Theorem 11,
not the earlier shorthand "Roulleau Theorem 1.17" and not *Elliptic curve
configurations on Fano surfaces*.  It proves

\[
0\longrightarrow \operatorname{NS}(J)
  \xrightarrow{i^*}\operatorname{NS}(F)
  \longrightarrow \mathbf Z/2\mathbf Z\longrightarrow0,
\]

and says that (\operatorname{NS}(F)) is generated by
(i^*\operatorname{NS}(J)) and an incidence divisor (C_s), with

\[
i^*\Theta=2C_s,
\qquad [F]=\frac{\Theta^3}{3!}.
\]

Thus there is exactly one saturation class to add to the pullback lattice.

Fix the cohomological Fourier convention used by the existing exotic-gluing
lattice certificate and write

\[
\alpha_C=\mathcal F(i_*[C])\in \operatorname{NS}(J).
\]

For (D\in\operatorname{NS}(J)), the standard divisor Fourier--Lefschetz
identity in dimension five gives

\[
\alpha_{i^*D}
 = \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbf C}
      (\phi_\Theta^{-1}\phi_D)\,\Theta-D.
\]

The normalization checks itself at (D=\Theta): the right side is (4\Theta),
while the left side is

\[
\mathcal F\!\left(\frac{\Theta^4}{3!}\right)
 =4\mathcal F\!\left(\frac{\Theta^4}{4!}\right)=4\Theta.
\]

For the missing incidence class,

\[
i_*C_s
 =\frac12\Theta\,[F]
 =\frac{\Theta^4}{2\cdot3!}
 =2\frac{\Theta^4}{4!},
\qquad
\alpha_{C_s}=2\Theta.
\]

Hence the full Fourier image of (\operatorname{NS}(F)) is generated by the
fifteen images coming from the saturated (\operatorname{NS}(J)) lattice and
(2\Theta).  The missing half-class on (F) becomes an even divisor class on
(J); it cannot supply an odd mixed degree.

## 3. What the Pontryagin calculation does and does not pre-empt

For actual source cycles, the difference constructions are signed
Pontryagin products:

\[
(d_{12})_*[C_1\times C_2]=[C_1]*[-C_2],
\qquad
(d_{3F})_*[C_3\times F]=[C_3]*[-F].
\]

Beauville's Fourier transform exchanges Pontryagin and intersection products
over (\mathbf Q).  Since
(\mathcal F([F])=\Theta^2/2) up to the harmless common sign convention,
the total source-correspondence intersection is the stated form (B).

This mechanism is classical.  Beauville's Proposition 3 prints the product
exchange, and Moonen--Polishchuk Theorem 1.6 constructs canonical divided
powers on the positive-dimensional Pontryagin ideal.  The latter paper also
warns that an integral Fourier transform has genuine two-primary defects: its
factor two cannot generally be removed even for elliptic curves.  Therefore
the architecture itself and an appeal to integral Fourier formalism are not
novelty surfaces.

The project-specific content is the exact integral lattice statement:

> On the actual exotic (A_5) principal gluing, the Fourier image of the
> saturated Fano Neron--Severi lattice has mixed degree ideal exactly
> (2\mathbf Z).

Even this should remain a project-internal theorem until the full priority
audit required for manuscript novelty wording is run.

### Geometric normalization gate

If (d_{12}) and (d_{3F}) are generically finite of degrees
(e_{12}) and (e_{3F}), then the classes of their reduced images are the
Pontryagin pushforwards divided by these degrees.  Thus an even total degree
can become odd after division by an even (e_{12}e_{3F}).  Before using this
parity result as a no-go for reduced image subvarieties, prove for each chosen
architecture that:

1. both difference maps have the expected dimensions and are generically
   finite;
2. their generic degrees are odd (preferably one);
3. no factor-exchange quotient or component multiplicity introduces a hidden
   factor two; and
4. the intersection with the Abel--Jacobi fibre is proper, with all
   multiplicities retained.

Absent these checks, the safe conclusion concerns natural correspondence
cycles, not every reduced geometric image.

## 4. Localization torsion no-go

Let

\[
U=J\setminus\Theta,
\qquad
a=\left.\frac{\Theta^4}{4!}\right|_U\in CH^4(U)[2],
\]

where the two-torsion statement comes from the known cycle supporting twice
the minimal class on (\Theta).  Since (U) is a smooth affine complex
fivefold, it has the homotopy type of a CW complex of real dimension at most
five, so (H^7(U,\mathbf Q/\mathbf Z(4))=0).  The tempting implication

\[
H^7=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad CH^4(U)[2]=0
\]

is invalid.

The motivic coefficient Bockstein gives a surjection

\[
H^7_M(U,\mathbf Z/2(4))
 \twoheadrightarrow
H^8_M(U,\mathbf Z(4))[2]
=CH^4(U)[2].
\]

Voevodsky's Beilinson--Lichtenbaum comparison is an isomorphism in
bidegrees (p\le q) and only a monomorphism at (p=q+1).  Here
((p,q)=(7,4)), three degrees beyond the isomorphism range.  Equivalently,
the comparison identifies only the truncation
(\tau_{\le4}R\epsilon_*\mu_2^{\otimes4}); it says nothing that kills the
degree-seven motivic source above.

Nor is there a general torsion-cycle injection to replace it.  Alexandrou--
Schreieder's introduction records the exact boundary: over algebraically
closed fields Bloch's map is injective in codimension at most two and for
zero-cycles by Roitman, but for (2<i<\dim X) it is in general not injective.
Our class has (i=4) on a fivefold, exactly in that failure range.  Their
Theorem 1.1 applies to (i=1,2), not to this case.  Ma's Theorem 1.1 further
shows that torsion one-cycle Abel--Jacobi kernels are controlled by coniveau
and unramified-type invariants; topological cohomology vanishing alone is not
the needed input.

Therefore closure needs a theorem special to this (U) or this class, for
example:

- compute (CH^4(U)[2]) from localization;
- prove that this particular class has vanishing refined-unramified/coniveau
  obstruction; or
- construct an explicit primitive cycle supported on (\Theta).

None of those statements follows from affineness or the current general
torsion theorems.
