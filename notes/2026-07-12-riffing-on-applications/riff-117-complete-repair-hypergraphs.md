## Draft theorem package with prose proofs

### Theorem A — the certified q=9 inner seed

Let `C₀` be the row code over `𝔽₉` generated by the nine finite twisted-cubic columns

```text
c(t) = (1,t,t²,t³)ᵀ,  t ∈ 𝔽₉,
```

together with the axis column `x=e₂`. Then `C₀` has parameters `[10,4,6]₉`, dual distance four,
and all-symbol locality three.

#### Prose proof

The first four finite columns form a Vandermonde minor, so the four generator rows are independent
and the code has dimension four. A nonzero row-code word is obtained by evaluating a polynomial of
degree at most three at all nine field elements, with the axis coordinate recording its quadratic
coefficient. Such a polynomial vanishes at no more than three finite points unless it is zero. If
the axis coordinate also vanishes, the polynomial has no quadratic term; this does not increase the
number of possible roots. Hence every nonzero word has weight at least six. The polynomial
`T³-T` attains three finite roots and has zero quadratic coefficient, so the distance is exactly six.

Any three columns are independent, while the columns at parameters `0,1,-1` together with the axis
column satisfy an explicit four-column relation. Thus the dual distance is exactly four. Every
minimum dual support containing a target coordinate supplies a three-helper repair, giving locality
three; the exact all-symbol assertion uses the geometric circuit classification developed in the
coding ledger.

### Theorem B — the axis repair hypergraph is the affine zero-sum triple system

Let `H_x` be the complete three-helper repair hypergraph of the axis coordinate. Under the natural
identification of the nine finite coordinates with the additive plane `𝔽₉ ≅ 𝔽₃²`, its edges are
exactly the three-element sets whose parameters sum to zero. Consequently

```text
ν(H_x)=3,    τ(H_x)=5.
```

#### Prose proof

A repair edge is the support away from `x` of a dual word whose coefficient at `x` is nonzero.
Equivalently, the axis column lies in the span of the three helper columns. Expanding the relevant
generalized Vandermonde determinant shows that this happens exactly when the three distinct field
parameters sum to zero. In characteristic three, such triples are precisely the affine lines of
`AG(2,3)`.

The three parallel lines in any affine direction form a matching, so `ν≥3`; no matching can contain
more than three triples on nine vertices, so `ν=3`. A five-point set meeting every affine line gives
`τ≤5`. Conversely, the complement of a four-point transversal candidate has five points; the exact
finite affine-plane argument shows that some affine line avoids the candidate, so four points cannot
hit every edge. Hence `τ=5`.

### Theorem C — universal matching/transversal bounds for bounded repairs

For every nonempty repair hypergraph `H`,

```text
ν(H) ≤ τ(H).
```

If every repair edge has at most `p` helpers, then

```text
τ(H) ≤ p ν(H).
```

#### Prose proof

A transversal must meet each edge in a matching, and matching edges are pairwise disjoint, so the
transversal contains at least one distinct vertex per matching edge. This proves `ν≤τ`. For the
upper bound, take a maximum matching. Its union is a transversal: any edge disjoint from that union
could be appended to the matching, contradicting maximality. The union has at most `p` vertices per
matching edge, hence size at most `pν`.

#### Lean support

[`FiniteGeom/Hypergraph.lean`](../../lean/FiniteGeom/Hypergraph.lean): `matching_card_le_transversal_card`,
`nu_le_tau`, and `transversalNumber_le_mul_matchingNumber`.

This theorem is fully formalized and is mechanism, not headline novelty.

### Theorem D — bounded dual words are confined to one inner block

Let `C=O∘I` be an ordinary concatenation. Suppose a concatenated dual word has q-ary weight at most
`s`, where

```text
s < d(O⊥)    and    s < 2 d(I⊥).
```

Then every block is inner-dual and at most one block is nonzero.

#### Prose proof

Represent the linear functional induced by each block through a trace coefficient `β_j` over the
outer alphabet. Orthogonality to the concatenated code makes `β=(β_j)` an outer-dual word. Every
nonzero coefficient requires a nonzero block and therefore contributes at least one to the q-ary
weight, so `wt(β)≤s<d(O⊥)`. Thus `β=0`, and trace nondegeneracy says every block annihilates the
inner code.

Every nonzero inner-dual block has weight at least `d(I⊥)`. Two nonzero blocks would therefore give
total weight at least `2d(I⊥)>s`, a contradiction. Hence the support is confined to one block.

### Theorem E — the general characteristic-matched family

For odd prime `p`, `q=p^h`, `h≥2`, the degree-`p` normal-rational-curve columns together with
`e_{p-1}` generate an optimal `[q+1,p+1,q-p]_q` NMDS-LRC. The minimum repairs of the distinguished
coordinate are the zero-sum `p`-subsets of `𝔽_q`, with

```text
ν=q/p,    τ=q-Z_p(𝔽_q).
```

#### Prose proof

Vandermonde independence gives rank `p+1` and independence of every set of at most `p` columns. A
degree-`p` polynomial has at most `p` roots; an affine `𝔽_p`-line supplies an equality polynomial
whose `T^{p-1}` coefficient vanishes, proving distance `q-p`. Expanding the generalized Vandermonde
determinant with the axis column shows that a minimum circuit occurs exactly when its `p` finite
parameters sum to zero.

Parallel affine `𝔽_p`-lines partition `𝔽_q`, giving a matching of size `q/p`, which is maximal by
cardinality. A failure set hits every repair exactly when its complement contains no zero-sum
`p`-subset. If `Z_p(𝔽_q)` is the largest such complement, the minimum hitting-set size is
`q-Z_p(𝔽_q)`.

and imported additive-combinatorial asymptotics are **paper-proved but not yet Lean-formalized**.

## Thesis

Locality, advertised repair-set count, and disjoint availability do not determine the resilience
of a code's bounded repairs. The complete bounded-radius repair hypergraph is the right object: its
matching number `ν`, transversal number `τ`, coordinatewise variation, and behavior under code
composition expose shared dependencies that conventional summaries miss.

## Minimum publishable contribution

1. Define the complete radius-`r` repair hypergraph in a coding-theoretically natural way.
2. Prove at least one infinite family with a nontrivial `τ/ν` separation or coordinatewise
   phenomenon not recoverable from locality and repair count.
3. Prove a transfer/composition theorem preserving the complete bounded-radius repair hypergraph.
4. Demonstrate on small exact instances that representation count or `ν` alone can rank repair
   resilience incorrectly.

The existing `τ>ν` constructions and bounded-repair transfer lemma appear to cover most of this
core, subject to a specialist citation-chain audit and statement review.

- Give one synthetic rack/region example showing why repair count exaggerates resilience.
- Avoid claims about deployed storage performance without an implementation benchmark.

## Shallow literature and novelty check

Closest precedents found:

- Kim and Song construct LRCs from hypergraphs and use disjoint repair sets as availability:
  [Hypergraph-Based Binary Locally Repairable Codes with Availability](https://doi.org/10.1109/LCOMM.2017.2730183).
- The wider LRC literature already treats recovery/repair sets, availability, and hypergraph-based
  constructions; those ingredients are not independently novel.

Preliminary verdict: **promising but terminology-sensitive**. The likely distinct contribution is
not “use a hypergraph for an LRC.” It is to take *all* bounded-weight repairs at each coordinate as
the authoritative hypergraph, study transversal rather than only disjoint availability, exhibit
`τ/ν` separations and non-monotonicity, and prove preservation of that complete object under the
transfer construction.

Required deeper audit:

- stopping-set, recovery-set, availability, cooperative-repair, and batch-code terminology;
- prior use of hitting/transversal numbers on complete repair families;
- whether concatenation or locality-preserving constructions already imply the claimed complete
  bounded-repair preservation theorem.
