# C907 v1: framed formal-monodromy proof

Date: 2026-08-11

Status: theorem spine; direct framed-QDM replacement passes the coefficient,
specialization, directionality, and low-dimensional red-team gates

## 1. Why this formulation

The one-step proof needs neither formal-monodromy constancy on KKPYY
geometric-atom components nor a generic HLT theorem.  It needs a numerical
support that is preserved by the **specific formal quantum-D-module
isomorphisms** attached to blow-ups and projective bundles.

The natural support is the formal-monodromy action on a coefficientwise
Turrittin solution ring.  This is the structure actually used in Cai's cubic
calculation.  It must be framed over the original `z`-disc and defined
without algebraically adjoining roots of `z`: otherwise a ramified `z`-gauge
can change the descended monodromy.

## 2. The framed solution category

Fix an algebraically closed field `k` of characteristic zero containing all
Novikov coefficients and the required roots of Novikov monomials, but not an
algebraic root of the loop coordinate `z`.  Let `PV_k` be the standard
Turrittin solution ring over `k((z))`, generated by

\[
 z^\rho\quad(\rho\in\mathbf Q),\qquad
 e^Q\quad
 (Q\in\bigcup_m z^{-1/m}k[z^{-1/m}]),\qquad
 \log z,
\]

with the usual relations and no new differential constants.  Formal
monodromy `T_f` is induced by one counterclockwise turn on the original
`z`-disc: it acts on `z^rho` by `exp(2 pi i rho)`, shifts `log z` by
`2 pi i`, and applies the deck action to ramified exponential factors.
Thus it combines regular-singular monodromy with the permutation of HLT
exponential factors.

For formal bulk/Novikov parameters use the coefficientwise completion

\[
 \widehat{PV}_k=PV_k\widehat\otimes k[[Q,\tau]].
\]

The completion permits the negative `z`-orders of successive parameter
coefficients to be unbounded.  Formal monodromy acts coefficientwise.  All
allowed changes of frame have entries with integral `z`-powers and therefore
commute with `T_f`.  They may lie in the coefficientwise completion but may
not use a ramified power of `z`.

For a smooth projective variety `Y`, let `k_Y` be an algebraic closure of the
fraction field of its Novikov ring; algebraic closure is taken in the
coefficient direction only, before adjoining the independent loop variable
`z`.  Apply the construction to the **small even quantum connection** over
`k_Y((z))`.  Let

\[
 \operatorname{FM}(Y)
\]

be the eigenvalue multiset of `T_f` on its full Turrittin horizontal-solution
space, counted with multiplicity.  Put

\[
 \nu_6(Y)=
 \operatorname{mult}_{e^{\pi i/3}}\operatorname{FM}(Y)+
 \operatorname{mult}_{e^{-\pi i/3}}\operatorname{FM}(Y).
\]

This is an intrinsic framed invariant of the small even quantum connection,
not an invariant of a KKPYY geometric atom.  Coefficient-field extensions
that are independent of `z` do not change it.  Integral-`z` connection
isomorphisms preserve it; external direct sum makes it additive.

### Framed PV base-change lemma

Let `PV_k` be a Picard--Vessiot/Turrittin solution algebra with differential
constants exactly `k`, equipped with the framed formal-monodromy operator
`T_f`.  Let `B` be an `I`-adically complete and separated `k`-algebra on which
`d/dz=0`.  Define

\[
 PV_{k,B}:=\varprojlim_N PV_k\otimes_k B/I^N.
\]

Then the differential constants of `PV_{k,B}` are exactly `B`.  Indeed, at
every finite level, expand in a `k`-basis of `B/I^N`: coefficientwise
differentiation and `PV_k^{d/dz}=k` show that a constant lies in `B/I^N`.
Taking the inverse limit gives `B`.

If `D` is a rank-`n` formal differential module over `k((z))` with solution
space `Sol(D)` in `PV_k`, then the horizontal module after scalar extension
is

\[
 Sol(D_B)=Sol(D)\widehat\otimes_k B.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

To see this, choose a fundamental solution matrix in `PV_k`; multiplication
by its inverse identifies horizontals with the constant vectors, and the
previous paragraph identifies those with `B^n`.  The operator `T_f` on
(2.1) is `T_f\otimes1`.  Therefore its characteristic polynomial is the
scalar extension of the characteristic polynomial over `k`.

More generally, an invertible connection gauge over `PV_{k,B}` whose entries
have only integral powers of `z` commutes with `T_f` and conjugates the two
formal-monodromy operators.  We define `nu_6` by the algebraic multiplicity
of the two primitive-sixth roots in that characteristic polynomial.  This
makes scalar-extension and gauge invariance literal over an arbitrary
complete coefficient ring; no eigenbasis over `B` is required.

## 3. Bulk and operation lemmas

### Bulk lemma

For every smooth projective `Y`, the flat bulk equations

\[
 \partial_{\tau_i}M=-z^{-1}(\phi_i\star_\tau)M,
 \qquad M|_{\tau=0}=I
\]

have an invertible solution in the coefficientwise completion.  Every
coefficient of `M` uses only integral powers of `z`; hence `M` commutes with
formal monodromy.  Flatness gives

\[
 M^{-1}\nabla_zM=\nabla_z|_{\tau=0}.
\]

Therefore a formal bulk pullback has the scalar extension of the same
formal-monodromy spectrum as the intrinsic small module.  This statement is
coefficientwise formal.  It makes no claim that `M` specializes to a
meromorphic gauge at arbitrary analytic bulk points.

Unit-direction translations multiply solutions by a scalar exponential
`exp(a/z)` and add no regular-singular monodromy.  Divisor-direction translations
are absorbed into the Novikov coefficients by the divisor equation.  After
these two normalizations, the mirror coordinates in the formal comparison
theorems are topologically nilpotent and may be substituted into `M`.

The last sentence is an assertion in the complete coefficientwise category,
not an evaluation in an ordinary Laurent field.  The precise statement is:

### Admissible base-shift lemma

Let `A` be a characteristic-zero coefficient field and let `B` be a complete
separated filtered `A`-algebra with multiplicative descending filtration
`F^pB`, so `F^pB F^qB` is contained in `F^(p+q)B`.  Let an admissible even
bulk pullback of the quantum connection of `Y` have coordinate

\[
 \gamma=a_0\mathbf1+a_2+\eta,
 \qquad a_2\in A\otimes H^2(Y),\quad
 \eta\in F^1B\widehat\otimes H^{\rm ev}(Y).
\]

Suppose the pullback is defined through the reduced quantum-D-module ring,
so its connection matrices lie in `B((z))`.  Then its framed
formal-monodromy characteristic polynomial over `B` is the scalar extension
of that of the small module after the divisor automorphism

\[
 Q^d\longmapsto e^{\langle a_2,d\rangle}Q^d.
 \tag{3.0}
\]

Indeed, the string equation removes `a_0` by the scalar exponential
`exp(-a_0/z)`, which has trivial formal monodromy, and the divisor equation
gives (3.0).  It remains to treat `eta`.  For every `N`, the normalized flat
bulk equations over `B/F^NB` have an invertible solution

\[
 M_N\in GL(H^{\rm ev}(Y)\otimes(B/F^NB)((z))),
 \qquad M_N\equiv I\pmod {F^1B}.
\]

Only finitely many positive-filtered degrees survive, so the negative `z`-order of `M_N`
is bounded.  The compatible inverse limit is an invertible matrix `M` in the
coefficientwise completion.  Both `M` and `M^{-1}` use only integral powers
of `z`, and therefore commute with framed formal monodromy.

Let `Sol_0` be the finite-dimensional Turrittin horizontal-solution space of
the small module.  Over every `B/I^N`, horizontal sections of the pulled
connection are exactly

\[
 M_N\bigl(Sol_0\otimes_A B/F^NB\bigr).
\]

Here the convention is `M_N^{-1} nabla M_N=nabla_0`.  The formula follows
in both directions because `M_N` is an invertible connection gauge.  Taking
the compatible inverse limit gives a finite free horizontal
module over `B`, not a larger uncontrolled solution space.  Its formal-
monodromy operator is conjugate to `T_f\otimes1`.  Hence its characteristic
polynomial, and in particular `nu_6`, is unchanged.

The filtered formulation is necessary because the final `tilde tau` Laurent
chart mixes positive and negative powers of
`u=q^{-1/(r-1)}` with bulk variables.  We avoid that chart by using the
reconstruction coordinates in Iritani Section 5.8.2.  Write

\[
 \tau=\tau^\circ+t,
 \qquad \varsigma_j=\varsigma_j^\circ+s_j.
\]

Iritani proves that `(t,s_0,...,s_(r-2))` are formal invertible coordinates
over the Laurent coefficient field and, crucially, writes the fundamental
solution in these coordinates over
`C[z^{-1}]((u))[[Q,t,s]]`; see (5.47)--(5.48) and the displayed matrix `M`
in Section 5.8.2.  The initial conditions satisfy

\[
 \tau^\circ=O(u^{r-1}),\qquad
 \varsigma_j^\circ+(r-1)\lambda_j=h_{Z,j}+O(u).
\]

Remove the identity term `-(r-1)lambda_j` by the string equation and the
divisor term `h_(Z,j)` by the divisor equation.  Before extending
coefficients to `C((u))`, the remaining shifts and the independent variables
belong to the honest complete ideal

\[
 I=(u,Q,t,s_0,\ldots,s_{r-2})
\]

in the corresponding power-series subring.  Apply the lemma there, and only
then extend scalars in the `z`-constant coefficient direction to the Laurent
field where `Psi` and `Psi^{-1}` live.  The mixed terms
`u^{-(l+1)}tilde tau_(l,m)` never enter this argument.

This lemma applies to Iritani's pullbacks even when pullback of the full
fundamental solution as a function is ill-defined.  Equations (5.16)--(5.19)
reduce the connection to the admissible ring; the constant term
`h_{Z,j}=(j+1/2)rho_Z/(r-1)` is a divisor shift, and the identity shift
`-(r-1)lambda_j` is handled by the string equation.  The remaining mirror
coordinate is positive-filtered in the reconstruction coordinates.  Thus no specialization of
`exp(t/z)` at a nonnilpotent `t` is used.

### Blow-up lemma

Let `Bl_C Y -> Y` have smooth center of codimension `r>=2`.  Iritani,
Theorem 5.18, gives a formal invertible mirror map and a quantum-D-module
isomorphism

\[
 QDM(\operatorname{Bl}_C Y)\cong
 QDM(Y)\oplus QDM(C)^{\oplus(r-1)}.
\]

The map and its inverse are defined over
`C[z]((q^{-1/s}))[[Q,t]]`; the mirror variables are independent of `z`.
Thus the comparison commutes with formal monodromy.  Composing it with the bulk
lemma on every summand gives

\[
 \nu_6(\operatorname{Bl}_C Y)
 =\nu_6(Y)+(r-1)\nu_6(C).
 \tag{3.1}
\]

The equality is between the intrinsic small invariants: every pulled-back
summand is returned to its own small module by the bulk lemma.  The
completions and coefficient fields may therefore be enlarged separately at
each factorization step by roots of Novikov monomials.  Such extensions are
unramified in `z` and preserve formal monodromy.  No solution or
certificate is transported from one exceptional Laurent cusp to the next,
and no global comparison field is required.

There is one asymmetry in the coefficient maps which must be recorded.  The
ambient map used in Iritani Section 5.5.1 is an inclusion, but the center map

\[
 Q_C^d\longmapsto Q^{i_*d}q^{-\rho_C\cdot d/(r-1)}
 \tag{3.1a}
\]

in (5.15) need not be injective.  Accordingly, the center term in (3.1) is
not interpreted as a scalar extension of the generic coefficient field of
`C`.  What the one-step proof uses is the stronger uniform statement

\[
 \nu_6(C;\chi)=0
 \tag{3.1b}
\]

for every point, curve, or surface `C` and every strictly Novikov-admissible
monomial specialization `chi` of its Novikov ring occurring in (3.1a).  The
coefficientwise proof in Section 4 below proves exactly (3.1b): its degree
filtration is valid curve-class by curve-class and therefore survives
quotients and monomial identifications.  Projective-bundle and point-blowup
reductions are likewise identities after the same specialization.

Thus the operation statement needed later is more precisely

\[
 \nu_6(\operatorname{Bl}_C Y)
 =\nu_6(Y)+(r-1)\nu_6(C;\chi),
 \tag{3.1c}
\]

where the ambient term is transported through the injective map and the
center term is specialized by (3.1a).  In dimensions at most two the second
term is zero for every `chi`, so a blow-up or blow-down preserves the ambient
primitive-sixth multiplicity.  This stepwise zero-center statement, rather
than an equality of generic center invariants across unrelated fields, is
what is iterated along weak factorization.

Here and below `chi` is **strictly Novikov-admissible**.  More precisely, its
target is a complete separated valued domain `A` with domain associated
graded, every monomial has nonzero image, and

\[
 v(\chi(Q^d))=\ell(d),
 \tag{3.1d}
\]

where `ell` is positive and proper on the effective numerical cone.  Iritani's
center map has this property: use the valuation induced by an ample divisor
on `Y`; its restriction to `C` is ample, while the exceptional Laurent
factor is coefficient data of valuation zero.  This condition is stronger
than continuity and is exactly what makes completed Novikov series and their
specializations well-defined.

### Divisor-tagging lemma

Although Iritani writes the completed monoid ring of homology classes, first
base-change his entire comparison diagram along the continuous quotient from
the effective homology monoid to its image in the numerical curve lattice.
This image, rather than all integral points of the numerical cone, is the
numerical Novikov monoid used here.  The quotient on completed rings is
well-defined: effective homology classes of bounded ample degree form a finite
set.  One way to see this is to embed the variety projectively and use the
finite-type Chow varieties of cycles of bounded degree; the homology class is
constant on a connected component.  Quantum products descend by summing the
coefficients of homology classes with the same numerical class.
The completed monoid maps, mirror maps, `Psi`, and `Psi^{-1}` all base-change;
the last two remain inverse matrices.  Push-forward of curves and pairing with
`rho_C` respect numerical equivalence.  The endpoint maps remain embeddings
because

\[
 N_1(\operatorname{Bl}_C Y)=N_1(Y)\oplus\mathbf Z[\text{exceptional line}].
 \tag{3.1e}
\]

Thus the numerical-Novikov comparison is a direct base change of Iritani's
theorem, not a new Gromov--Witten identity.

Index the resulting Novikov ring of `C` by numerical curve classes.  Choose integral
divisors `D_1,...,D_rho` whose pairing separates `N_1(C)`, and define

\[
 \chi_{\boldsymbol t}(Q^d)=\chi(Q^d)
 \exp\!\left(\sum_i t_i\langle D_i,d\rangle\right)
 \in A[[t_1,\ldots,t_\rho]].
 \tag{3.1f}
\]

Then `chi_t` is injective on the completed numerical Novikov ring.  For a
nonzero series, properness of `ell` makes its lowest-valuation support finite.
The initial image is a nonzero finite combination of distinct exponential
characters.  After a generic one-parameter restriction `t_i=a_i s`, their
exponents are distinct, and the Vandermonde matrix of the derivatives at
`s=0` proves their linear independence.  Since the associated graded of `A`
is a domain, the initial image cannot cancel.

After taking fraction fields and algebraic closures, the
`chi_t`-specialized module is therefore a scalar extension of the intrinsic
small module of `C`.  On the other hand, the divisor equation identifies it
with the formal divisor-bulk pullback of the `chi`-specialized module:

\[
 Q^d\longmapsto Q^d e^{\langle\boldsymbol t,d\rangle}.
 \tag{3.1g}
\]

The coefficientwise bulk gauge at every quotient by
`(t_1,...,t_rho)^N` is Laurent in integral powers of `z`, and its compatible
inverse limit commutes with framed formal monodromy.  Consequently

\[
 \operatorname{char}_{T_f}(C;\chi)
 =\operatorname{char}_{T_f}(C;\chi_{\boldsymbol t}).
 \tag{3.1h}
\]

This is a formal identity over `A[[t]]`; it does not evaluate a bulk gauge at
a nonnilpotent parameter.  Thus generic vanishing for `C` implies vanishing
after every specialization that actually occurs in (3.1a), despite the
noninjectivity of that map.

### Projective-bundle lemma

Iritani--Koto, Theorem 5.1, similarly gives over
`C[z]((q^{-1/r'}))[[Q]]`

\[
 QDM(\mathbf P_Y(V))\cong QDM(Y)^{\oplus\operatorname{rk}V}.
\]

Again the map commutes with formal monodromy, so

\[
 \nu_6(\mathbf P_Y(V))=(\operatorname{rk}V)\nu_6(Y).
 \tag{3.2}
\]

Here too use the reconstruction coordinates of Iritani--Koto Section 5.8:
`s_j=varsigma_j-varsigma_j^circ`.  Their equation (5.13) makes the `s_j`
independent formal variables, and the following displayed matrix gives the
block fundamental solution

\[
 \bigoplus_j e^{-\varsigma_j^\circ/z}
 M_Y(\varsigma_j^\circ+s_j).
\]

The initial condition in (5.11) has leading identity term `r lambda_j`, a
divisor term, and `O(q^{-1/r})`.  String/divisor normalization and the
`(q^{-1/r},Q,s)`-adic base-shift lemma therefore identify each block with
the intrinsic small module before scalar extension to the Laurent field.

### Parity restriction

Both decompositions restrict to the even quantum differential modules.  In
Iritani's conventions the quantum rings are supercommutative, the Novikov
variables and their roots have even parity, and the quantum connection with
all odd bulk variables set to zero preserves cohomological parity.  The
Fourier-transform, push-forward, pull-back, and characteristic-class
operators used to construct `Psi` and `Phi` are even maps: their geometric
degree shifts are multiples of two.  Hence the odd ideal is stable throughout
the constructions, and reduction modulo that ideal gives connection
isomorphisms

\[
 QDM^{\rm ev}(\operatorname{Bl}_C Y)
 \cong QDM^{\rm ev}(Y)\oplus
 QDM^{\rm ev}(C)^{\oplus(r-1)},
\]

and

\[
 QDM^{\rm ev}(\mathbf P_Y(V))
 \cong QDM^{\rm ev}(Y)^{\oplus\operatorname{rk}V}.
\]

Equivalently, apply Iritani's super-QDM theorems and take their even direct
summands.  Although the displayed maps carry nonzero ordinary graded degree,
their coefficient factors compensate that degree and have even super parity;
no parity shift occurs.

## 4. Endpoint calculations

### Cubic

Cai's small-connection calculation gives indicial roots

\[
 -\frac16,\qquad-\frac56
\]

in the rank-two even zero block of every smooth cubic threefold.  His bulk
matrix has fractional degree zero, exactly as in the bulk lemma.  Hence

\[
 \nu_6(X)\ge2.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Only Cai's explicitly constructed solutions are used.  No algebraic closure
in `z` and no specialization of the bulk matrix is invoked.

### Dimension at most two

For every smooth projective `T` of dimension at most two and every strictly
Novikov-admissible monomial specialization `chi` of its numerical Novikov ring, its
specialized small even module satisfies

\[
 \nu_6(T;\chi)=0.
 \tag{4.2}
\]

A point has trivial formal monodromy.  For a curve with nef canonical class and
for a minimal surface with nef canonical class, the degree gauge in KKPYY
Claim 6.15 is integral after the half-parity correction and leaves a
nilpotent residue.  Thus the only formal-monodromy eigenvalues of the small
even connection are `1` and `-1`.  The remaining curves and
minimal surfaces are `P^1`, `P^2`, or ruled and reduce by (3.2) to points or
curves.  Every nonminimal surface reduces by (3.1) through point blow-ups.
These reductions first prove vanishing over the intrinsic generic numerical
Novikov field.  In the point-blowup case the center has no Novikov variables,
and both endpoint maps are injective.  The divisor-tagging lemma then promotes
generic vanishing to every strictly admissible specialization, without
specializing those decompositions at an unrelated Laurent cusp.

For completeness, the load-bearing coefficientwise argument is as follows.
Write

\[
 \operatorname{Gr}=\frac{\operatorname{Deg}-d}{2},
 \qquad g=\operatorname{Gr}+\frac12T,
\]

where `T` is the identity on degrees whose difference from `d` is odd and is
zero otherwise.  The operator `g` has integral eigenvalues.  At the small
even point the unit coordinate vanishes.  If `K_T` is nef, every coefficient
of quantum multiplication by the Euler field sends the `Gr`-eigenspace of
weight `m` to weights at least `m+1`; equivalently its degree blocks satisfy

\[
 \kappa_{ij}=0\qquad(j\le0).
 \tag{4.3}
\]

This is coefficientwise over the small Novikov ring: nefness gives (4.3) for
each effective curve class separately.  Conjugating

\[
 \nabla+\frac12T\frac{dz}{z}
\]

by `z^g`, the block `kappa_ij` acquires the factor `z^{j-1}`.  Equation
(4.3) therefore leaves a first-order pole with strictly degree-raising,
hence nilpotent, residue.  The gauge `z^g` is integral and does not alter
formal-monodromy eigenvalues.  Undoing the half-parity correction can only
multiply them by `-1`.  For surfaces `T=0`, so the eigenvalues are `1`; for
curves on the even module `T=1`, so they are `-1`.  Neither case contains a
primitive sixth root.  This reproduces the proof of KKPYY Claim 6.15 over the
small-even coefficient ring rather than importing a statement at one rigid
point.

## 5. One-step theorem

Let `X` be a smooth cubic threefold.  Equation (3.2) and (4.1) give

\[
 \nu_6(X\times\mathbf P^1)=2\nu_6(X)>0.
\]

If this fourfold were rational, weak factorization of a birational map to
`P^4` would use only smooth centers of dimension at most two.  Apply (3.1)
at every step.  Equation (4.2) makes every center contribution zero, so

\[
 \nu_6(X\times\mathbf P^1)=\nu_6(\mathbf P^4)=0,
\]

where the last equality follows from (3.2) over a point.  This is a
contradiction.

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{X\times\mathbf P^1\text{ is irrational for every smooth cubic
 threefold }X.}
\]

## 6. Exact boundary

For `X x P^2`, a fivefold factorization permits threefold centers.  Such a
center can itself have nonzero primitive-sixth support; `X` already does.
Hence the framed formal-monodromy count is exhausted at one stabilization.
The `m=2` theorem requires the operation-framed Stokes/Rees placement being
developed in C907, not a refinement of this scalar multiplicity.

## 7. Closed audit gates

1. The coefficientwise Turrittin solution category has no new constants after
   finite quotient and inverse limit, and its framed monodromy is functorial.
2. The comparison maps use only integral powers of `z`; their even restrictions
   and the string/divisor normalizations preserve the framed action.
3. Both endpoint Novikov maps in a blow-up comparison are injective.  The
   possibly noninjective center map is handled by strict admissibility and the
   divisor-tagging lemma.
4. Equality of the two endpoint integers is reversible, so separate comparison
   fields telescope through either orientation of a weak factorization.
