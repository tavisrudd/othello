# C919 cold read — de-branded fronts and programme codas, five manuscripts

**Lane:** `clebsch` · **Date:** 2026-08-19 · **Type:** read-only verification pass. No
file under `papers/` was edited.

Scope: commits `1142b4bad`, `5e38a3675`, `5d54c316d`, judged against the author's
implementation packet (`2026-08-18-c919-author-implementation-packet.md`), the revised
plan (`2026-08-18-c919-series-apparatus-and-epigraph-plan.md`, which wins where the two
differ), the task card (`clebsch-tasks/c919-series-apparatus-and-epigraph.md`), the
completion report (`2026-08-19-c919-series-apparatus-completion.md`), and
`papers/style-guide.md`. Evidence is the committed `.tex` for what moved and the tracked
PDFs for what a reader sees.

## Verdict

The structural refactor is done and done well. Page 1 of every paper is clean, the coda
lands in the right place in all five, and the poem, map, caption, and independence
sentence are byte-identical across the five apart from the emphasized line and the shaded
box — the mechanical consistency requirement is fully met.

The failure is at the seams, and it is systematic rather than scattered. Sentences were
moved into the introduction *and* also written into the coda, so every paper now states
the same claim twice, sometimes on consecutive pages. There is one hard defect: a stale
"the series map" reference survives in Paper V's body, pointing at a figure that no
longer carries that name and now sits two pages later. Three transplanted sentences lost
their antecedent. None of this is a mathematical error; all of it is visible to a cold
reader on a first pass.

Counts: one hard reference defect, five instances of on-page or near-page duplication
(one per paper), three dangling referents, two front-matter/back-matter inconsistencies,
and a stale label list in the task card. All are cheap to fix; none requires re-deciding
anything the author settled.

## Ranked defects

Ordered by how much a first-time reader is hurt.

### D1 — Paper V still refers to "the series map" in its body

`papers/chordal-conference-reconstruction/chordal_conference_reconstruction.tex:1469`,
rendered on **page 19** of 22:

> This completes the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:normalization-residue-intro}
> and explains the last arrow in the series map: Paper~V does not merge the
> upper and lower branches; it identifies the common reason that their final
> ambiguity is a Frobenius orbit.

Three things are wrong at once. The reader has not seen any map — the map used to sit in
the introduction and now sits on page 21. The map is no longer called a series map. And
"the last arrow" is a positional reference into a figure the reader cannot see. The
completion report's claim that "No source retains the strings ... 'Series map'" is a
case-sensitivity artifact: the surviving occurrence is lowercase.

The prior grep that cleared this checked `Series map`; the case-insensitive grep over the
five roots returns exactly this one hit.

**Fix.** Either forward-reference the coda figure — "explains the lower branch of the
programme map in the coda (Figure~\ref{fig:programme-map})" — or, better, drop the figure
reference and name the mathematics: "...and explains why the upper and lower branches are
not merged here: Paper~V identifies the common reason that their final ambiguity is a
Frobenius orbit." The second avoids a forward reference across two pages into an
unnumbered section.

### D2 — Every coda states the independence claim twice, back to back

The standard sentence required by plan §4 is followed immediately, in all five papers, by
a paper-specific sentence making the same point. On one page the reader gets:

| paper | boilerplate | paper-specific restatement |
|---|---|---|
| I   | "The five papers are logically independent; the map records reconstruction correspondences and thematic relations, not proof dependencies." | "...the later papers study other shadows of related sources, but none is used in the proofs here." |
| II  | same | "...and no companion construction is used in the proof." |
| III | same | "...that comparison supplies no hypothesis here." |
| IV  | same | "The theorem is logically independent of the other Clebsch papers..." |
| V   | same | (none — Paper V is the exception and reads better for it) |

Paper IV is the worst: two paragraphs apart, "The five papers are logically independent"
and "The theorem is logically independent of the other Clebsch papers"
(`papers/q13-passant-code/passant_code_q13.tex:1105` and `:1113`). A cold reader
registers this as the author protesting.

This is not an implementation error — the plan prescribed both the boilerplate sentence
(§4) and the paper-specific clauses (§5, inherited from the old interludes). But the
combination was never read end to end.

**Fix.** Keep the boilerplate sentence, which is the one that must be identical across the
five, and delete the redundant clause from each paper-specific paragraph. For Paper IV
that means deleting the whole first clause of the second coda paragraph, leaving "Its
place in the programme is methodological." For Paper I, cut "but none is used in the
proofs here". For Paper II, cut "and no companion construction is used in the proof". For
Paper III, cut "that comparison supplies no hypothesis here" — the sentence still says
"Paper V *later* compares", which already conveys it.

### D3 — Sentences merged into the introduction are repeated verbatim in the coda

The plan's §5 sentence dispositions send several sentences into the introduction, and the
plan's own coda prose for the same paper then contains those sentences again. The
implementer followed both instructions faithfully; nobody checked the two lists against
each other.

**Paper I**, exact verbatim repeat of a full sentence, page 1 and page 27
(`clebsch_rigidity.tex:93` and `:2033`):

> The order-eleven Clebsch code is the rigid exceptional answer, while the chord-defect
> identity and conic-filling bounds are field-uniform.

**Paper II**, two consecutive sentences repeated with only "here" and "one"→"an" changed,
page 1 and page 27 (`clebsch_factorization.tex:86` and `:2112`):

> The classification ranges over every odd prime power.  The Clebsch-related
> $H_3/\F_{11}$ case is one exceptional output, not an input.

versus

> The classification here ranges over every odd prime power.  The Clebsch-related
> \(H_3/\F_{11}\) case is an exceptional output, not an input, ...

**Paper IV**, the paper's signature claim, now stated four times — abstract
("reconstruction therefore has exact arity two"), introduction
(`passant_code_q13.tex:90`), conclusion (page 15, `:1054`), and coda (page 16,
`passant_code_q13.tex:1109`):

> Unary support data are constant, while weighted pair concurrences recover the marked
> conic plane and its polarity, so the exact reconstruction arity is two.

The conclusion and the coda are on facing pages; the reader turns one page and reads the
same sentence.

**Fix.** In each case the coda copy is the one to cut or reduce, because the introduction
copy is doing load-bearing work for a standalone reader. Paper I's coda paragraph loses
its third sentence. Paper II's coda paragraph loses its first two and keeps only "On the
two surviving matching orbits, quadratic data recover the unordered sheets and the first
surviving signed cubic restores their orientation" — but see D4, which says that sentence
is itself a repeat. Paper IV's coda keeps "Here the sparse shadow is literal: the 364
minimum words of the binary conic code are the starting data" (which is the sentence poem
line IV requires) and drops the arity sentence after it.

### D4 — Paper II's coda repeats Paper II's conclusion one page later

`clebsch_factorization.tex:2114–2116`, page 27, third sentence of the coda paragraph:

> On the two surviving matching orbits, quadratic data recover the unordered sheets and
> the first surviving signed cubic restores their orientation.

The conclusion's own final sentence, page 26, immediately before the page break:

> ...quadratic data recognize and recover the unordered factorization, while the first odd
> degree both restores orientation and supplies the Macaulay dual generator.

Same claim, adjacent pages, and the coda's version is strictly weaker (it drops the
Macaulay generator). Combined with D3, Paper II's entire paper-specific coda paragraph is
a restatement of material the reader has already met twice.

**Fix.** Replace the paragraph with prose that says where Paper II sits rather than what
it proves — that is what a coda is for. Something on the order of: "The shadow here is the
conic quotient of a secant product: the pairing is deliberately forgotten and recovered
from low-degree algebra alone. The other papers recover cubic shadows of related sources
from different sparse data." That also repairs D6 below.

### D5 — Paper I's coda points at a "q = 13 consequence recorded above" that is not above

`clebsch_rigidity.tex:2035`, page 27:

> Paper~IV gives the structural and reproducible account of the \(q=13\) consequence
> recorded above~\cite{RuddPassant2026}.

`q=13` occurs exactly once in Paper I outside this sentence: at line 226, on page 2, in a
list of things a *separate computational companion* established ("...and the original
computational form of an exact $q=13$ binary incidence-code theorem"). Twenty-five pages
separate the deictic from its antecedent, the antecedent is a subordinate clause in a list,
and no section of Paper I proves a q = 13 consequence. On the coda page itself, the string
"q = 13" appears nowhere else.

In the pre-refactor source this sentence sat directly after its antecedent and read
correctly. The move broke it, and the added words "recorded above" advertise a back
reference the reader cannot follow.

**Fix.** Name the thing: "Paper~IV gives the structural and reproducible account of the
exact \(q=13\) binary incidence-code theorem noted in the introduction~\cite{RuddPassant2026}."

### D6 — Paper II's own vocabulary does not contain "chordal" or "shadow"

Mechanical check over the five sources: in `clebsch_factorization.tex` the word "chordal"
occurs at lines 2089, 2092, and 2102 only — the map node, the map's right-hand package
box, and the map caption. The word "shadow" occurs at lines 2073 and 2102 only — the poem
and the same caption. Neither word appears in Paper II's 46 pages of mathematics.

So a Paper II reader reaching the coda finds their own paper labelled "matching-quotient
chordal cubic" using a term the paper never defines, described by a caption about "the
chordal and conference shadows", and given a paper-specific paragraph (see D4) that never
connects any of it to the secant-product cubic they just read about. This is the
weakest-integrating coda of the five.

The plan settled these labels deliberately (§4), and they are right for the programme as
a whole, and the other three papers use their label vocabulary natively: Paper I proves
that its orbital operator is "a symmetric conference matrix of order six"
(`clebsch_rigidity.tex:1525`) and uses the word four more times outside the map,
"conference" runs throughout Paper III, and "minimum-word" occurs a dozen times in Paper
IV. Paper II is the single outlier, and the fix
is local to Paper II, not to the shared map.

**Fix.** One gloss in Paper II's coda prose tying its object to the label, e.g. "...the
secant-product cubic that Paper~V calls the chordal shadow." That satisfies the packet's
"do not invent terminology" constraint by attributing the term rather than adopting it.

### D7 — Three transplanted sentences lost their antecedent

**Paper III**, `sections/01-introduction.tex:25`. With the "Reconstruction perspective."
label stripped, the three-question roadmap paragraph now opens cold:

> The first inverse question is arithmetic: what is lost when Hitchin's two-sheeted
> incidence construction is remembered only through its branch sextic?

The preceding paragraph ends with a figure pointer and never mentions questions. "*The*
first inverse question" reads as a back reference to an enumeration that was never opened.
The bolded label used to do that framing work. This is the only place in the five papers
where removing the wrapper left the paragraph unable to stand.

*Fix:* one lead-in clause. "Three inverse questions organize what follows. The first is
arithmetic: ..." — or simply change "The first inverse question is arithmetic" to "A first
inverse question is arithmetic".

**Paper I**, `clebsch_rigidity.tex:86`, second sentence of the first body paragraph:

> The motivating question is not specific to the Clebsch configuration: when can a sparse
> invariant determine the object that produced it?

Two problems. It disclaims specificity to "the Clebsch configuration" before the
introduction has named the Clebsch configuration — the specialization arrives two
sentences later. And it restates the sentence it now follows: "determine how much of a
projective system can be reconstructed from coarse metric data" and "when can a sparse
invariant determine the object that produced it?" are the same question in two registers.
In the interlude, this sentence came *after* the specialization and the disclaimer landed.

*Fix:* move it down two sentences, so it follows "The exceptional configuration is
therefore the output of a recognition theorem, not part of its input." There it reads as a
widening of scope rather than a pre-emptive denial.

**Paper V**, `chordal_conference_reconstruction.tex:123`:

> The classification itself is formulated on the fixed metric carrier.

"itself" is contrastive and its foil was the clause that stayed behind: the interlude read
"Papers~I--III supply concrete sources realizing the two shadow types, **but** the
classification itself is formulated on the fixed metric carrier." With the first half gone,
"itself" contrasts with nothing. Separately, "the fixed metric carrier" is a definite
description whose term is not introduced until line 233. As placed, the sentence also
blunts a strong paragraph closer — "it does not identify the cubics" — by appending a
technical aside after it.

*Fix:* drop "itself", or make the contrast explicit and local: "The classification is
formulated on the fixed metric carrier rather than on any particular realizing source."

### D8 — Paper V refers to itself in the third person in its own coda

`chordal_conference_reconstruction.tex:1582`, page 21:

> Paper~V shows that these distinct cubic shadows recover the same marked six-axis
> carrier, with a residual \(C_2\)-torsor when the selected chordal line is forgotten.

The other four codas use first-person or deictic self-reference — "The sparse datum here
is...", "The classification here ranges...", "This paper follows a marked conference
source...", "Here the sparse shadow is literal". Paper V alone names itself by series
number in running prose. The plan fixed this wording in §6.2, so it is faithfully
implemented; it is still the wrong register for the paper's own voice.

*Fix:* "This paper shows that these distinct cubic shadows recover...". The map box and
the poem line already establish that this is Paper V.

*Related, pre-existing:* the same third-person self-reference occurs at line 1470 in the
body ("Paper~V does not merge the upper and lower branches"). If D1 is being fixed anyway,
fix both in one edit.

### D9 — Paper IV's title sits 12 mm lower than the other four

Measured first-word `yMin` on page 1 of the tracked PDFs: 124.95 pt for Papers I, II, III,
and V; **158.97 pt** for Paper IV. The 34 pt difference is exactly the
`\par\vspace*{12mm}\noindent` retained inside Paper IV's surviving title-redefinition
group (`passant_code_q13.tex:41`).

The plan (§1) read that spacing as "page-1 spacing rather than branding" and asked for it
to be preserved. Provenance says otherwise: `git log -S'vspace*{12mm}'` returns exactly one
commit, `b3012f18c` ("Land proved five-paper companion architecture"), and its diff shows
the `\vspace*{12mm}` was added *in the same hunk* that introduced the banner line, to push
the banner clear of the top margin. It is banner scaffolding. Keeping it leaves precisely
the "awkward vertical whitespace" the packet's §12 front-matter checklist asks about, and
it is the only respect in which the five title pages are not uniform.

*Fix:* delete the `\begingroup ... \makeatother` title group in Paper IV entirely, leaving
a bare `\maketitle` as in the other four.

### Paper V — `chordal-conference-reconstruction`

Page 1 clean. Order is Conclusion (§12, page 20) → coda (page 21) → AI-assistance
disclosure (same page) → references.

The conclusion/coda split is the strongest single result of this task. The new §12
concludes Paper V and only Paper V — the marked correspondence, the constant double

Defects: D1 (stale "series map" reference, page 19 — the one hard defect in the set),

Paper V is also the only paper whose coda does not restate the independence claim a second

## Cross-paper consistency

Mechanically diffed the five coda blocks from `\section*{Clebsch: Rigidity from Sparse
Shadows}` through the standard independence sentence. **No drift whatsoever.** The only
differences between any two of the five are:

- which poem line carries `\textbf{...}`; and
- which TikZ node carries the `active` style.

Everything else — the `\clearpage`, the heading, the `tabular` preamble
`{@{}r@{\hskip 1.4em}l@{}}`, all five `{\scriptsize N}` numerals, all five poem lines
character for character including whitespace, the `figure[H]`, every TikZ style
definition, every node coordinate and label, every arrow, the caption, the figure label
`fig:programme-map`, and the two-line independence sentence — is byte-identical in all
five files. This includes the papers where the coda lives in a separate file
(`clebsch-passages/sections/09-programme-coda.tex`) and where it precedes an `\appendix`.

Confirmed against the settled specifications:

- Poem wording matches plan §3 exactly, including the three author amendments to lines II,
  III, and IV. No line wraps in any of the five rendered PDFs, so the 8/7/9/10/13-syllable
  shape survives.
- Upper node labels are the plan §4 set: `deep-hole conference cubic`,
  `matching-quotient chordal cubic`, `golden-descent conference cubic`,
  `marked cubic correspondence`. Papers I and III are no longer duplicate labels.
- Lower target is the common two-line `marked conic plane and polarity / with an
  \(\F_8\)-marking` in all five, as §4 decided.
- Caption is the plan §4 wording verbatim, adapted only for `Paper~V`/`Paper~IV` ties and
  `\(C_2\)`.
- Independence sentence is the plan §4 wording verbatim in all five.
- Emphasis is one mechanism throughout: bold whole line plus `active` box (`very thick`,
  `fill=black!12`). No phrase-level bolding anywhere. The poem reads normally with the
  emphasis ignored.
- Cluster separation: node III at y = −1.2, node IV at y = −3.6, a 2.4 cm gap against the
  1.55 cm spacing inside the upper cluster. Visually unambiguous in the rendered PDFs.

Two consistency notes that are not drift:

1. Paper V's V-node uses `active` where Papers I–IV use `hinge`; `active` is strictly
   stronger (`very thick` + shading vs `thick`), so V remains the most emphasized node in
   every paper. Correct. But the `hinge` distinction is close to invisible at print size —
   at 100 dpi I could not reliably tell a `hinge` box from a `paper` box. The packet said
   "do not over-style", so this is acceptable; just note that the hinge signal is not
   actually carrying information.
2. The poem block's horizontal position shifts by a few points between papers, because
   bolding a different line changes the centred `tabular`'s width. Inherent to the design,
   not a defect.
