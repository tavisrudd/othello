# From coarse data through Clebsch to operator shadows

**Lane:** `golden`

**Date:** 2026-07-31

## Framing decision

The strongest cross-paper account is not that the Clebsch configuration has
many unrelated exceptional appearances.  It is a recovery--propagation
program:

\[
 \text{coarse nearest-codeword data}
 \longrightarrow \text{Clebsch geometry}
 \longrightarrow \text{golden conference operator}
 \longrightarrow \text{functorial shadows beyond the original problem}.
\]

Paper I supplies the inverse direction.  Its projective deep-hole syndrome
locus reconstructs the Clebsch hexagon, conic, polarity, orientation torsor,
support cubic, and signed conference switching class.  The Golden paper
supplies the forward direction.  Exterior power, golden compression,
commutator, Pfaffian/determinant, adjugation, and centered squaring carry the
operator to cubic, polar, determinantal, exceptional, fermionic, and anomaly
shadows.

The Clebsch configuration is therefore a recognition stage rather than an
endpoint.  Its finite field is exceptional; the structure recovered there
propagates outside the finite-geometric problem.

## Three-stage paper narrative

1. **Recognition.**  Coarse syndrome data force the Clebsch configuration
   rather than merely distinguish it inside a stored census.
2. **Extraction.**  Decoder ambiguity and the orientation two-graph recover
   the golden conference operator up to its natural equivalences.
3. **Propagation.**  Functorial constructions on that operator produce the
   later algebraic and physical shadows.  These are consequences of one
   operator identity, not a list of formula matches.

This framing makes Paper I the entrance theorem to the broader program.  It
does not enlarge Paper I's field-uniform theorem, import Golden proofs into
Paper I, or make Paper I depend on later work for correctness.

## Candidate composite theorem

The investigation should test the following statement rather than assume it.

> **Candidate recovery--propagation theorem.**  The unlabelled projective
> deep-hole syndrome locus of the Clebsch code determines, up to switching,
> permutation, outer orientation, and golden conjugation, the golden
> conference operator and the complete functorial family of paper-owned
> shadows constructed from it.

The phrase "determines" must be resolved separately for every target.  Some
outputs may be canonical objects; others may be unordered pairs, projective
lines, signed six-sets, gauge classes, or conjugate families.  A theorem that
forgets these residual torsors would be false or overstated.

## Stronger possibility: a recovery cycle

The current results suggest more than one-way propagation.  Paper I already
identifies the support cubic and the conference switching class as two
presentations of the same signed two-graph: triangle products recover the
switching class, while the cubic's six nodes recover the unlabelled axis
carrier.  The commutator Pfaffian in the Golden paper then returns that same
cubic line.  C727 should therefore test the cycle

\[
 \text{coarse data}\longrightarrow[C]\longrightarrow[Z_C]
 \longrightarrow[C],
\]

not merely the forward chain from \(C\) to its shadows.  If every arrow
descends through the same natural equivalence relation, the result is an
information-preservation theorem: a selected low-degree shadow retains the
complete oriented switching class from which it arose.

The strongest plausible statement is an equivalence of reconstruction
classes among the syndrome datum, the oriented Clebsch cubic line, and the
golden conference switching class.  The full shadow family would then be a
functorial consequence of any one of these equivalent presentations.  This
claim must fail cleanly if the syndrome locus needs decoder multiplicities or
if the cubic retains only a quotient of the orientation torsor.

The C720 post-freeze corollaries now settle the marked reverse tests.  Over
characteristic zero, the full determinant polynomial
\(\det[D_x,C]=16Z_C(x)^2\) determines the cubic line \([Z_C]\), because a
square in the polynomial ring has only the expected scalar/sign ambiguity.
Its squarefree coefficients recover the triangle two-graph up to common
negation and hence recover the unoriented switching line.  Independently,
the ten-cut dimer syndrome recovers the same line through four-cycle
holonomies.  By contrast, centered squaring vanishes exactly at the ten Segre
nodes: the twenty balanced (3+3) amplitude signs pair into those ten
projective base points.  C727 still owns descent of these reverse maps from
Paper I's input hierarchy and the classification of nonzero polar fibres.

## Descent hierarchy

C727 should organize outputs by the amount of recovered marking they retain:

1. **Unmarked invariants:** objects fixed by switching, permutation,
   orientation exchange, and golden conjugation, such as an appropriate
   determinant square or unlabelled projective locus.
2. **Orientation-cover objects:** cubic or Pfaffian lines and paired outputs
   that descend only after retaining the orientation torsor.
3. **Golden-marked objects:** cross-eigenspace determinants, resolutions, or
   modules that require a choice of golden embedding or occur as a conjugate
   pair.
4. **Fully marked realizations:** indexed outer coordinates, mode-labelled
   Hamiltonians, or anomaly vectors that descend only up to a stated gauge
   and permutation action.

This hierarchy is itself a possible theorem: it measures exactly how much of
the original coarse datum survives in each shadow and where an additional
choice first becomes unavoidable.

## Required ambiguity ledger

The composite statement must follow each ambiguity from the Paper I input to
every Golden output:

| source ambiguity | operator effect | downstream question |
|---|---|---|
| relabelling of the six axes | permutation conjugacy | which outputs descend as unlabelled objects? |
| switching of axis representatives | diagonal sign conjugacy | which cubic, Pfaffian, and physical systems descend only up to gauge? |
| exchange of the two orientation classes | (C\mapsto-C) or the corresponding outer exchange | which outputs change sign, exchange sheets, or remain fixed? |
| golden conjugation | exchange of the two golden eigenspaces | which determinant, resolution, and MCM objects form a conjugate pair? |
| projective scaling of the cubic | scalar change in an invariant line | which claims concern a cubic line and which require a normalization? |

The first acceptance question is whether the deep-hole locus alone recovers
all input needed for this ledger, or whether decoder multiplicities, a marked
code realization, or an orientation choice must be added.  The theorem should
state the coarsest sufficient input, not silently strengthen it.

## Downstream targets

The primary audit should cover only exact Golden-paper constructions:

- the middle-exterior cubic line and its six outer conjugates;
- the Segre point and centered-square Segre--Igusa polar image;
- the commutator Pfaffian and determinant sextic;
- the cross-golden determinant/adjugate and paired small resolutions;
- the literal Cartan restriction;
- the six-mode Majorana family and its Pfaffian parity wall;
- the balanced Slater/interferometric optimum and exceptional outer transform;
- the anomaly-amplitude family, with no claim that the finite model
  constructs a gauge theory.

The (E_8)--Hamming obstruction, Clifford/doily refinements, and hyperbolic
lattice repair belong in a secondary audit.  Their inclusion in a final
composite theorem depends on whether they are genuinely functorial outputs of
the recovered equivalence class rather than contextual relatives.

## Editorial use

Before a stable Golden preprint exists, Paper I should make no vague promise
of future physics.  After the theorem and its covariance statement are
public, Paper I may use one restrained conclusion-level pointer.  The best
interface is the literal identity

\[
 \operatorname{Pf}[D_x,C]=4Z_C(x),
\]

which says that the orientation cubic reconstructed from coding data becomes
the zero-mode and fermion-parity wall of the associated six-mode free-fermion
family.  A catalogue of anomaly, quantum, lattice, and exceptional-group
connections would dilute the inverse theorem.

The Golden paper should state the forward functorial theorem on an abstract
marked switching class.  It may then cite Paper I as a source that recovers
that class from coarse data.  This preserves both papers' logical
independence while making their composition mathematically explicit.

## Honest boundary

The program does not yet prove a general principle that arbitrary codes or
finite configurations yield physical systems.  It proves, or may prove after
the covariance audit, that this particular coarse-data reconstruction
determines a distinguished operator equivalence class and that exact
functorial constructions on that class descend to specified outputs.

The desired conclusion is stronger than a broad analogy:

> Coarse nearest-codeword data recover the Clebsch geometry; its orientation
> recovers the golden conference operator; and that operator propagates the
> recovered structure beyond the Clebsch cubic.

Whether this is a theorem, a theorem plus marked variants, or only a framing
sentence is the subject of C727.
