# Series reconstruction-framing proposals

Date: 2026-08-11

Status: full-paper reread complete; decisions frozen before manuscript edits.

This note records the two framing packets supplied by the author during the
Paper V closeout: (A) twenty-four introduction/conclusion/trust-boundary
changes across Papers I--V and the computational companion, and (B) six
replacement abstracts.  Every item remains subject to a complete reread of
the affected paper.  The final audit will mark each item `KEEP`, `MERGE`,
`REPAIR`, or `REJECT` and will state why.

The governing editorial idea is:

> Lead with reconstruction from lossy invariants and the quantified theorem;
> present the exceptional Clebsch object as a forced answer rather than an
> assumption; preserve each paper's actual scope and voice.

## Audit questions to answer after the reread

1. Does the proposed opening improve the existing one, or merely repeat a
   reconstruction framing already present?
2. Does a replacement delete attribution, scope, or proof-mode information
   that the current paragraph deliberately carries?
3. Are the displayed quantifiers exactly those of the theorem?
4. Does the proposal conflate an intrinsic reconstruction with one relative
   to retained marking data?
5. Does it claim that a structure is reconstructed when the manuscript only
   reconstructs an orbit, quotient, line, or unordered pair?
6. Does it make a fixed-field theorem look uniform, or make a uniform theorem
   look exceptional?
7. Is the series map helping navigation, or delaying the standalone theorem?
8. Can one common series vocabulary be used without flattening the papers'
   genuinely different proof genres?

## Cross-paper decision

The common vocabulary is reconstruction from sparse or lossy invariants, but
the papers keep different proof genres and theorem scales:

- Paper I is the series entry point and may retain the series map.
- Paper II is an all-odd-field classification and should reach that theorem
  before series architecture.
- Paper III has its own source--shadow--return diagram; a second series diagram
  competes with the proof architecture and is removed.
- Paper IV is a fixed-field maximal-reconstruction theorem and should foreground
  exact arity two, not the upper cubic branch.
- Paper V is the companion classification that earns the full cross-series map.

This is a deliberate asymmetry.  Repeating the same four-line paragraph and
diagram in every introduction makes the programme look templated and delays the
standalone theorem.  Shared framing should identify the inverse question, not
force identical openings.
