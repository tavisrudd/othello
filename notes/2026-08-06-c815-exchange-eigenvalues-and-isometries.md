## How the pieces go

**The eigenvalue reading.**  The spectral theorem writes `A = V D Vᵀ` with `V`
unitary and `D` the diagonal of eigenvalues, so `1 - q⁻¹ • (A * A)` is the
conjugate of `1 - q⁻¹ • (D * D)`, which is the diagonal of `1 - αᵢ²/q`.
Conjugation by a unitary leaves the characteristic polynomial alone — one
application of the commutation of characteristic polynomials, not a similarity
theory — and the characteristic polynomial of a diagonal matrix is the product
of its linear factors.

**The isometries.**  The eigenvalues of a real symmetric `Q` with `Q * Q = 1`
are `±1`: conjugating the relation back through the eigenvector unitary gives
`D * D = 1`, hence `μᵢ² = 1` entrywise.  The columns of the eigenvector unitary
belonging to eigenvalue `1`, assembled by a bijection from the index type, are
the isometry: orthonormality of the columns gives `Uᵀ * U = 1`, and
`U * Uᵀ = V E Vᵀ` for `E` the zero-one diagonal selecting those columns, which
is `(1 + Q)/2` because `(1 + μᵢ)/2` is `1` exactly on them.  The antifixed case
is the fixed case for `-Q`.

The bijection needs the two eigenspaces to be equally large, which is where the
trace enters: the trace of `Q` is the sum of its eigenvalues, so a vanishing
trace makes the numbers of `+1` and `-1` eigenvalues equal, and each is half the
label count.  For a conference matrix the vanishing diagonal supplies exactly
that.

## What row OPER-3 still needs

One mechanical item.  The cut-dependence statement is proved on the invariant
the second moment sees — the number of aligned four-sets of a half — rather than
on the exchange operator of the cut that a half defines.  Turning a balanced
half `Y` of a matrix on one label set into cut coordinates is the equivalence
`Equiv.sumCompl`, under which the principal block is the submatrix on `Y`; what
must then be transported is the fourth trace of that block, which is a sum over
`Y`, and the count of its aligned four-sets, which is a bijection between the
four-subsets of `Y` and the four-subsets of the block's own label set.  Neither
step has mathematical content and neither is started.
