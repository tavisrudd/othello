# The Diagonal Rigidity Phase Boundary for Stabilizer States

## Smith normal form classification, the Schur-cube obstruction, and a 2n^{1/4}-uniform non-Clifford family

**Status:** proved (diagonal sector), numerically verified.
**Scope caveat, stated up front:** all theorems below concern *diagonal* product symmetries
(each local factor diagonal in the computational basis) of CSS coset states. The reduction of
general product symmetries to this sector is the known hard step in LU–LC-type problems and is
**not** proved here; it is the standing imported assumption wherever "all product symmetries"
would be wanted. Within the diagonal sector the classification below is complete.

---

## 1. Setup

Let C ⊆ F₂ⁿ be a linear code and

```
|C⟩  =  |C|^{−1/2} Σ_{x ∈ C} |x⟩
```

the associated CSS coset (stabilizer) state, with stabilizer generated by X(c), c ∈ C, and
Z(c′), c′ ∈ C⊥. Standard facts used freely:

- **Uniformity.** |C⟩ is k-uniform iff every nontrivial stabilizer element has weight ≥ k+1,
  i.e. k = min( d(C), d(C⊥) ) − 1.
- **Diagonal product operators.** Up to a global phase, U = ⊗ⱼ diag(1, e^{iαⱼ}), α ∈ (R/2πZ)ⁿ.
  Its action: U|x⟩ = e^{i α·x}|x⟩, where α·x uses the 0/1 integer lift of x.
- **Clifford criterion.** diag(1, e^{iα}) is proportional to a Clifford gate iff α ∈ (π/2)Z
  (then D X D† = cos α · X + sin α · Y must be a single Pauli). Hence the diagonal product
  operator U(α) is a local Clifford operator iff 4α = 0 in (R/2πZ)ⁿ.

Write Λ_C ⊆ Zⁿ for the **lift lattice**: the Z-span of the 0/1 lifts of all codewords of C.

**The lift identity.** For x₁,…,x_m ∈ {0,1}ⁿ, coordinatewise XOR satisfies

```
lift( x₁ ⊕ ⋯ ⊕ x_m )  =  Σ_{∅ ≠ T ⊆ [m]}  (−2)^{|T|−1}  ∧_{i∈T} x_i ,          (LI)
```

where ∧ is the coordinatewise product. (Per coordinate: ⊕aᵢ = (1 − ∏(1−2aᵢ))/2; expand the
product.) In particular 2(x∧y) = x + y − lift(x⊕y) ∈ Λ_C for x, y ∈ C, and inductively
2^{m−1}·(m-fold products) ∈ Λ_C.

Write C^{∘m} ⊆ F₂ⁿ for the span of all m-fold coordinatewise products of codewords (the m-th
Schur power). Since x∧x = x, the chain C = C^{∘1} ⊆ C^{∘2} ⊆ C^{∘3} ⊆ ⋯ is increasing.

## 2. Theorem 1 — complete classification of diagonal symmetries, and decidability

> **Theorem 1.** The group A of diagonal product symmetries of |C⟩ (mod global phase) is
> canonically
>
> ```
> A  =  { α ∈ (R/2πZ)ⁿ :  α·x ∈ 2πZ  ∀ x ∈ C }  =  Hom( Zⁿ / Λ_C ,  R/2πZ )
>    ≅  T^{\,n−r}  ×  ∏_i  Z/d_i ,
> ```
>
> where r = rank_Q Λ_C and d₁ | d₂ | ⋯ are the elementary divisors of Λ_C (Smith normal form
> of the codeword-lift matrix over Z). A symmetry α is a local Clifford operator iff 4α = 0.
> Consequently:
>
> ```
> every diagonal product symmetry of |C⟩ is local Clifford
>       ⟺   r = n   and   every  d_i ∈ {1, 2, 4} ,
> ```
>
> which is decidable in polynomial time from any generator matrix of C.

**Proof.** U(α)|C⟩ = |C|^{−1/2} Σ e^{iα·x}|x⟩ equals e^{iθ}|C⟩ iff α·x ≡ θ (mod 2π) for all
x ∈ C; taking x = 0 forces θ ≡ 0, and the condition α·x ∈ 2πZ is Z-linear in x, hence
equivalent to α ∈ Hom(Zⁿ/Λ_C, R/2πZ). The structure decomposition is the Smith normal form of
the finitely generated abelian group Zⁿ/Λ_C ≅ Z^{n−r} ⊕ ⊕ᵢ Z/dᵢ and Pontryagin duality of its
finite part; the Clifford criterion is the local computation above, and 4-torsion elements are
exactly the α with every αⱼ ∈ (π/2)Z. ∎

The rigidity question for the diagonal sector is thus *entirely* a statement about the 2-adic
and Q-rank structure of the lift lattice: **the phase boundary object is a Smith normal form.**

## 3. Theorem 2 — the Schur-cube obstruction (one hypothesis)

> **Cascade Lemma.** Let t ∈ Zⁿ and N ≥ 1 with t·x ≡ 0 (mod N) for every x ∈ C.
> Then for all x₁,…,x_m ∈ C:
>
> (i) if N = 2^ℓ and m ≤ ℓ:  t·(x₁ ∧ ⋯ ∧ x_m) ≡ 0 (mod 2^{ℓ−m+1});
> (ii) if N = q is odd:      t·(x₁ ∧ ⋯ ∧ x_m) ≡ 0 (mod q) for every m ≥ 1.

**Proof.** Strong induction on m; m = 1 is the hypothesis. Apply t·(−) to (LI) for the
codeword x₁⊕⋯⊕x_m ∈ C: the left side is ≡ 0 (mod N). Every proper term T ⊊ [m], |T| = j < m,
contributes (−2)^{j−1} t·(∧_T x). In case (i) the inductive hypothesis makes each such term
divisible by 2^{j−1}·2^{ℓ−j+1} = 2^ℓ; the surviving term (−2)^{m−1} t·(x₁∧⋯∧x_m) is therefore
≡ 0 (mod 2^ℓ), giving (i). In case (ii) proper terms vanish mod q by induction, and 2 is
invertible mod q, giving (ii). ∎

> **Theorem 2 (Schur-cube rigidity).** If C^{∘3} = F₂ⁿ, then **every** diagonal product
> symmetry of |C⟩ is local Clifford — in fact of level ≤ 2 (Z- and S-type gates). No further
> hypotheses are needed.

**Proof.** Three parts, each killed by fullness of the cube.

*No continuous part.* Fullness mod 2 of the set of triple products means some n of them have an
odd determinant, so their integer lifts are Q-independent; each triple product lies in
Λ_C ⊗ Z[1/2] by the lift identity, hence rank_Q Λ_C = n and A is finite (Theorem 1).

*No odd torsion.* Let α ∈ A have odd order q, α = (2π/q)t. Cascade (ii) gives
t·v ≡ 0 (mod q) for every product v. Choosing n products whose mod-2 reductions are
independent, the coefficient matrix has odd — hence mod-q invertible? not necessarily, but its
determinant is odd; if q ∤ det we conclude t ≡ 0 (mod q). To handle q | det: the reductions of
*all* products span F₂ⁿ, so the lattice Λ' they generate has odd index in Zⁿ (its mod-2
reduction is everything, so det of some generating n-subset is odd — det odd suffices only for
that subset; instead: Λ' ⊗ F₂ = F₂ⁿ implies [Zⁿ : Λ'] is odd, because an even index would give
a proper subgroup containing 2Zⁿ… cleanest: [Zⁿ:Λ'] equals the product of the elementary
divisors of Λ', and if any divisor were even, Λ' mod 2 would lie in a proper subspace).
So [Zⁿ : Λ'] is odd; since t annihilates Λ' mod q and gcd([Zⁿ:Λ'], q) = 1 forces q | [Zⁿ:Λ']
or t ≡ 0 — precisely: [Zⁿ:Λ']·eⱼ ∈ Λ' for every j, so [Zⁿ:Λ']·tⱼ ≡ 0 (mod q); if
gcd(q, [Zⁿ:Λ']) = 1 then t ≡ 0 (mod q) and α = 0. If instead some odd prime p | q divides
[Zⁿ:Λ'], repeat the argument with the p-part — but [Zⁿ:Λ'] is odd yet could share p; however
the elementary-divisor form of Λ' mod p: Λ' ⊗ F₂ full does not control p. So argue directly at
p: the products span F₂ⁿ; pick n of them with odd determinant D; the system V t ≡ 0 (mod p)
with det V = D. If p | D this particular subset fails, but fullness mod 2 gives many subsets;
the correct invariant is [Zⁿ:Λ'] itself: it divides D for every choice of n products with
nonzero determinant, and D can be taken odd, so [Zⁿ:Λ'] is odd — but p is odd too, so this
does not yet separate them. The clean closing step: [Zⁿ:Λ'] · tⱼ ≡ 0 (mod p) for all j; if
p | [Zⁿ:Λ'] we instead localize: over Z_(p), the identity 2(x∧y) = x+y−lift(x⊕y) shows
Λ' ⊗ Z_(p) = Λ_C ⊗ Z_(p) (2 is a unit), and Λ_C ⊗ Z_(p) contains, for each pair j ≠ k, a 0/1
idempotent separating j from k (C^{∘3} full ⇒ C separates coordinates and omits none, and with
2 invertible all Boolean operations on supports are available inside the algebra generated:
products give ∧, and 1 ∈ C^{∘3}-span mod 2 lifts to an element ≡ 1 mod 2, hence a unit
adjustment yields complements). The unital separating idempotent algebra over the local ring
Z_(p) contains every eⱼ, so Λ_C ⊗ Z_(p) = Z_(p)ⁿ and A has no p-torsion. 

*No 2-power torsion of order ≥ 8.* Let α ∈ A have order 2^ℓ, ℓ ≥ 3, α = (2π/2^ℓ)t with some
tⱼ odd. Cascade (i) with m = 3 gives t·(x∧y∧z) ≡ 0 (mod 2^{ℓ−2}), in particular mod 2, for
all triples; by linearity t mod 2 ∈ (C^{∘3})⊥ = 0, so t is even — contradicting the odd
coordinate. Hence A is 4-torsion, i.e. local Clifford. ∎

*(Remark on the odd-torsion step: the fully airtight write-up is the localization argument in
the last four lines; the determinant detour above is retained to show where the naive route
needs care. A Lean pass should formalize the localization version only.)*

**Interpretation.** The third Schur power is exactly the obstruction: level-ℓ non-Clifford
diagonal symmetries pair against C^{∘ℓ}, the chain is increasing, and fullness at ℓ = 3 kills
every level at once. This is the precise sense in which **triorthogonality is the unique enemy
of diagonal rigidity** — and it is the same trilinear object as the Gorenstein socle pairing
G₁⊗G₁⊗G₁ → G_s in the reconstruction corpus.

## 4. Theorem 3 — the converse and the boundary family

> **Theorem 3 (existence).** |C⟩ admits a non-Clifford diagonal product symmetry of order 8
> iff there is t ∈ Zⁿ with at least one odd coordinate and t·x ≡ 0 (mod 8) for all x ∈ C.
> A necessary condition is (C^{∘3})⊥ ≠ 0. If C is triply even (all weights ≡ 0 mod 8), then
> t = (1,…,1) works and the symmetry is **transversal T**: T^{⊗n}|C⟩ = |C⟩.

**Proof.** The order-8 elements of A are α = (2π/8)t with t odd somewhere; Theorem 1 gives the
iff. Cascade (i) with ℓ = 3, m = 3 gives the necessary condition. Triply even is the case
t = 1: 1·x = wt(x) ≡ 0 (mod 8), and T = diag(1, e^{iπ/4}) is non-Clifford. ∎

> **Theorem 4 (a 2n^{1/4}-uniform non-rigid family).** For r ≥ 1 let C = RM(r, 4r),
> n = 2^{4r}. Then C is triply even (Ax–McEliece divisibility: weights of RM(r,m) are
> divisible by 2^{⌈m/r⌉−1} = 8), so T^{⊗n} is an exact non-Clifford product symmetry of |C⟩,
> and |C⟩ is k-uniform with
>
> ```
> k  =  min( 2^{3r}, 2^{r+1} ) − 1  =  2^{r+1} − 1  ≈  2 n^{1/4} .
> ```
>
> Hence **diagonal rigidity fails at uniformity ~ 2n^{1/4}** on infinitely many n. The
> smallest member (r = 1) is a 16-qubit, 3-uniform state with T^{⊗16} symmetry.

**Proof.** d(RM(r,m)) = 2^{m−r} and RM(r,m)⊥ = RM(m−r−1, m) with distance 2^{r+1}; uniformity
is the min minus one. Divisibility is Ax–McEliece with m/r = 4. ∎

Combined with Theorem A of the companion note (2-uniformity ⇒ the full product-symmetry group
is finite), the RM family has a *finite* symmetry group containing a non-Clifford element of
order exactly 8 — discrete non-rigidity, the precise failure mode the phase diagram needed a
witness for.

## 5. Corollary — why AME is rigid: Schur saturation of MDS codes

For a q-ary [n, k] MDS code, dim C^{∘3} = min(n, 3k − 2) (with equality exact for GRS — the
Schur-flag computation R_a ⋆ R_b = R_{a+b} of the portfolio's spine). Hence:

```
k ≥ (n+2)/3   ⟹   C^{∘3} full.
```

AME-type inputs have k = n/2 ≥ (n+2)/3 for all n ≥ 4. So — conditional on the qudit extension
of Theorem 2 (the F_p carry analysis replacing the lift identity; open, and exactly where the
Ax–Katz divisibility theory of the Schur flag applies) — **the diagonal sector of the AME
rigidity theorem follows from Schur-power saturation**, and the mechanism is explanatory:
maximal distance forces maximal Schur growth, and the cube saturates with room to spare. The
phase boundary picture, current best:

```
uniformity ~ 2 n^{1/4}          :  rigidity FAILS   (Theorem 4, constructive, qubits)
MDS input at rate ≥ 1/3          :  diagonal rigidity HOLDS (qubit proof; qudit conditional)
AME  (rate 1/2)                  :  full rigidity HOLDS (portfolio theorem)
```

The open strip is genuinely open in both parameters: whether non-Clifford symmetries survive
at uniformity n^{β} for β ∈ (1/4, 1/2), and whether the diagonal proof lifts to all product
symmetries below AME.

## 7. Honest sizing

**What is proved here.** A complete, polynomial-time-decidable classification of the diagonal
product symmetries of CSS coset states by the Smith normal form of the lift lattice
(Theorem 1); a one-hypothesis rigidity criterion — full Schur cube — killing continuous, odd,
and high 2-power symmetries simultaneously (Theorem 2); the exact order-8 existence criterion
(Theorem 3); and an explicit ~2n^{1/4}-uniform family with transversal-T non-Clifford
symmetry, verified at n = 16 (Theorem 4), improving the uniformity of known non-rigid examples
from constant to polynomial in n *for the symmetry problem*.

**What is imported or at risk.** (1) The reduction from general product symmetries to the
diagonal sector is not proved and is the known hard step; every "rigidity holds" statement here
is diagonal-sector only. (2) Cui–Gottesman–Krishna (2017) classified diagonal gates in the
Clifford hierarchy and their transversal action on stabilizer codes; the CSS-T /
generalized-triorthogonality literature (Rengaswamy–Calderbank–Pfister line) covers adjacent
divisibility conditions; the T-on-triply-even mechanism is textbook in magic-state
distillation. The lattice-SNF classification for *states*, the single-hypothesis cube theorem,
and the uniformity-boundary framing appear new, but a targeted novelty pass against those two
literatures is mandatory before any firstness claim. (3) The LU–LC *equivalence* numbers in
the literature (27-qubit minimal counterexample; distance-two for n ≥ 28) concern pairs of
states, a different problem from the symmetry rigidity treated here; the two must not be
conflated in the write-up. (4) The odd-torsion step of Theorem 2 should be formalized via the
localization argument only.

**Where it goes.** As the classical-mechanism section of the phase-boundary paper, or merged
with the approximate-rigidity note into a single "rigidity of maximally entangled stabilizer
states: stability and boundary" manuscript — which would then contain the exact theorem
(portfolio), its stability (companion note), and its boundary (this note).
