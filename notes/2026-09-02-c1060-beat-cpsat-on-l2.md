# C1060 — beating CP-SAT on the L2 negative-control row

**Lane**: `complete-ports` · branch `c1060-l2`, branched from the C1049 dominance-pruning
commit `e4705ff63`.

**Verdict.** The goal is met with room to spare. A certified exact solve of L2 now runs in
**0.41 ms** warm (0.65 ms single-shot including first touch) against the CP-SAT control's
**4.6 ms** solve time excluding process startup, and it emits a **128-byte** optimality
certificate that an independent checker replays in **0.8 µs**. CP-SAT emits no certificate at
all. Against the C1049 incumbent on the same row (9.29 s, 20.2 MB of dominance witnesses) this
is about a 22,000-fold reduction in solve time and a 158,000-fold reduction in certificate size.

The mechanism is not a faster dynamic program. It is a different proof: the layered Pareto
frontier is replaced by a branch-and-bound search under a Lagrangian dual bound, and the
optimality proof collapses from "here are 632,666 justified state deletions" to "here are six
integers and ten scheduled demands".

The whole of CP-SAT's advantage on this row is one number. The linear-programming relaxation of
L2 has value **10.944009**, computed independently with HiGHS. Its floor is 10, and 10 is
attainable. So a single root LP bound proves optimality, and CP-SAT spends its 162 branches
only on *finding* a feasible schedule of value 10, never on proving that none is better. Zero
conflicts is the signature: the instance has no combinatorial hardness that a bound cannot see.

This also settles the symmetry question the task raised. The six resources carry independently
drawn loads, so no two are interchangeable and there is no resource-permutation group to quotient
by. CP-SAT builds the symmetry graph and finds nothing to use. Route (b) is dead on this
instance, not merely unattractive.

## 3. The certified route

New module `src/scheduler_bound.rs`; `src/scheduler.rs` is untouched, so the dynamic-programming
route and its C1049 dominance certificate are unchanged.

### 3.1 The bound

Relax to the linear program: `x[d][i] >= 0`, `sum_i x[d][i] <= 1` per demand,
`sum_{d,i} load[d][i][r] x[d][i] <= capacity[r]` per resource, maximizing `sum x`. For any
nonnegative resource multipliers `y`,

```
g(y) = sum_r capacity[r] * y[r] + sum_d max(0, max_i (1 - load[d][i] . y))
```

upper-bounds the relaxation and hence the integer optimum.

The design turn that makes the certificate cheap is that the per-demand multiplier `u[d]` is
*defined* by the inner maximum rather than supplied. Dual feasibility therefore holds by
construction, and **every** nonnegative `y` yields a valid bound. Bound quality depends on how
well `y` was fitted; bound validity does not. A checker never has to reason about the fitting
procedure — it only has to see that `y >= 0`, which is one comparison per resource.

Multipliers are carried as integer numerators over `DUAL_DENOMINATOR = 4096`, so the bound and
its floor are computed in exact integer arithmetic on both sides. No floating-point value enters
the certificate or the proof. Floating point is confined to `LagrangianDual::fit`, a projected
subgradient descent whose output is clamped nonnegative and snapped to the dyadic grid; whatever
it returns is sound.

Restricted to demands `d..` with a residual capacity vector, the same expression bounds the
optimal completion of any partial schedule, which is exactly the quantity §1.3 measured and the
quantity the dynamic program could never compute.

### 3.2 The search

Iterative depth-first branch and bound over demands — no recursion over the search tree, per the
Ergodis architecture gate. The stack (residual capacities, cursor, repairs, chosen option) lives
entirely in a presized `BoundWorkspace`, so the traversal is allocation-free; the dual fit and
the answer assembly are cold and sit outside the guarded region. Each demand branches over its
options plus a skip. Options are tried cheapest-first in the dual metric so the first dive
reaches a strong incumbent before any bound is tested; this is a heuristic and exactness does not
depend on it. That ordering alone cut the L2 node count from 75,058 to 4,825.

The dual-fit budget escalates rather than being tuned to the instance: the solve starts at 500
subgradient iterations and doubles up to `MAX_DUAL_FIT_ROUNDS = 4` times while the root bound
fails to meet the primal value. The search returns the exact optimum on every round, so
escalation buys the *proof*, never the answer. Starting budgets of 100, 200 and 500 all certify
L2; 500 does it in one round.

### 3.3 The certificate

A certified answer is the pair (dual multipliers, primal assignment). It proves optimality
exactly when the assignment is feasible — each demand used at most once, each chosen load vector
genuinely an option of its demand, every capacity respected — and `floor(g(y))` equals the number
of demands the assignment repairs. Then nothing repairs more and something repairs exactly that
many.

For L2: `y = (165, 94, 218, 127, 252, 98)/4096`, root bound numerator 44845, `g(y) = 10.948486`,
floor 10, assignment of size 10. Total 128 bytes. `verify_certificate` rebuilds every per-demand
slack from the multipliers alone, never re-solves, and never sees the search.

When the root bound does not close, the solve returns the exact optimum with
`certified: false` and the caller falls back to the C1049 frontier certificate. This scheme
deliberately has no compact proof for an instance with a genuine integrality gap, and says so
rather than pretending otherwise.

## 6. Accepted and rejected variants

**Accepted.** Lagrangian dual with the per-demand multiplier derived rather than supplied;
integer numerators over a power-of-two denominator; subgradient fit with escalation on failure to
certify; dual-metric option ordering; iterative traversal in a presized workspace.

**Rejected.**

- *Exact rational simplex on the dual.* Would guarantee the sharpest bound, but derived `u[d]`
  makes any nonnegative `y` sound, so the extra machinery buys nothing L2 needs and costs a
  session.
- *Resource-symmetry quotient.* No symmetry exists; confirmed by CP-SAT's detector.
- *Bound-pruning the existing Pareto frontier in place.* Would have left the quadratic dominance
  scan as the cost driver. The frontier is the problem, not its size.
- *A fixed 1500-iteration fit.* Certifies L2 but at 1.12 ms, and it is a constant tuned to one
  instance. Escalation from 500 is both faster and principled.
- *Emitting a branch-and-bound tree certificate.* Unnecessary when the root bound closes, and the
  fallback for when it does not is the existing C1049 frontier certificate.

## 6a. Where the certified route does *not* apply

A closeout pass ran the new route on the other scheduler rows of the tier. **W2 does not finish
within 120 s** and was killed; W3 was not run after that. This is a real limit, not a tuning
problem, and it is the reason `src/scheduler.rs` was left untouched rather than replaced.

W2 has 400 demands over 2 resources with a positive grading, and W3 has 4,000 demands over 2
resources with a repeated interface. Both are rows where the dynamic program is *already* the
right shape: the frontier stays tiny (2,461 states on W2, 22,801 on W3) because the grading and
the repeated interface collapse it, and the solve is milliseconds. Depth-first branch and bound
over 400 or 4,000 demands has a search tree those instances give it no way to prune, since the
Lagrangian bound is weakest exactly where many demands each contribute a little.

So the two routes are complementary and the correct integration is a shape gate, not a
replacement:

- **Few demands, wide generic loads, near-integral relaxation** — L2's shape — take the certified
  bound.
- **Many demands, graded or repeated interface, small frontier** — W2 and W3's shape — keep the
  layered dynamic program and its C1049 dominance certificate.

Choosing between them from the instance alone is the natural successor task, and the classifier
in `docs/ergodis-shape-classifier.md` is where the discriminator belongs. Nothing in this task
wired an automatic gate; `certify` is a separate entry point that the caller selects.

## 7. Mystery ledger

1. **Why does the root bound close at all?** The integrality gap on L2 is 0.944, just under one
   unit — the loosest it can be while still rounding down to the optimum. *Settled by the `ej`
   pass only as a description, not a cause:* the instance has 18 demands, mean total load 30 per
   demand against 240 units of capacity, so roughly eight demands' worth of slack is spread over
   six resources, and the relaxation can fractionally fill each one. Whether generic instances of
   this shape reliably land under one unit of gap is **open**; the owning successor is a sweep
   over the L2 generator's seed.
2. **The 500/800/1500-iteration non-monotonicity.** More subgradient iterations gave *more* nodes
   at 1500 (8,752) than at 800 (4,824), on a bound that certified in both cases. The fitted `y`
   moves the option ordering as well as the bound, so a marginally better bound can produce a
   worse dive. Not settled; harmless here because escalation starts low, but it means "more
   iterations is better" is false and should not be assumed by a future tuner.
3. **Whether L2 was ever a fair negative control.** C1038 predicted L2 would lose to the
   incumbent because the Pareto frontier is the state and grows with the load range. That
   prediction was correct about the dynamic program and wrong about the *problem*: a bound the
   dynamic program had no way to express collapses the instance to under half a millisecond. The
   classifier entries N1 and N2 attribute the loss to load-range genericity; the actual
   discriminator on this row is the integrality gap. Whether the other loss rows share that
   diagnosis is **open** and is the natural successor task.
4. **W2 does not finish under the certified route.** Killed at 120 s, against a dynamic-programming
   solve of the same row in milliseconds on a 2,461-state frontier. *Settled by the closeout pass
   as to mechanism:* the Lagrangian bound is weakest where many demands each contribute a little,
   and W2 has 400 demands over 2 resources, so depth-first branch and bound gets no purchase. The
   consequence is recorded as a routing decision in §6a — the two routes are complementary and
   `src/scheduler.rs` keeps every row it already wins. What is **open** is the automatic
   discriminator: no shape gate was wired, and `certify` remains a caller-selected entry point.
   The owning successor is a classifier entry in `docs/ergodis-shape-classifier.md`.
5. **No mystery in the counter A/B.** The 35,000× instruction ratio is fully accounted for by
   35.4 billion dominance comparisons giving way to 4,825 search nodes. Nothing there is
   unexplained.
