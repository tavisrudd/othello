conics in the classical projective plane*, Designs, Codes and Cryptography 40
(2006), 343--356, DOI `10.1007/s10623-006-0022-6`.

- **Read depth:** full text, 17 pages; Sections 4.1--4.3, Table 8, and the

  `minimum words`, `minimum-weight`, `reconstruct`, `association scheme`,
  `Gram`, `endomorphism`, or `F_8`.  Nothing in the read sections classifies

### Madison--Wu

Adonus L. Madison and Junhua Wu, *On binary codes from conics in
\(\mathrm{PG}(2,q)\)*, European Journal of Combinatorics 33 (2012), 33--48,
DOI `10.1016/j.ejc.2011.08.001`; preprint arXiv:1104.0324.

- **Read depth:** full preprint; introduction and the main module theorem read
  directly.  The publisher's primary full-text page was searched to verify the
  published numbering and statement.
- **Load-bearing result:** published Theorem 5.1(i) decomposes the kernel over
  the algebraic closure into \((q-1)/4\) pairwise non-isomorphic simple modules
  of dimension \(q-1\); published Corollary 5.4 gives the binary dimension.
  These are Theorem 6.1(i) and Corollary 6.3 in the arXiv preprint.
- **Negative check:** the preprint text contains no discussion of minimum
  weight, support reconstruction, Gram matrices, \(A_9\), or an \(\mathbf F_8\)
  marking.  The descent to one binary irreducible and its endomorphism field is
  an inference using the present paper's Frobenius-conjugate scalars, not a
  statement attributed to Madison--Wu.
- **Cache:** key `arXiv:1104.0324`; SHA-256
  `f3edf20a2b63286164b3aced06a04a9039d7bbba2eb955a6461b7f7e793f6343`;
  23 pages; extracted text 12883 words.
- **Citation defect found:** the manuscript bibliography names the published
  article but currently uses the preprint pinpoints “Theorem 6.1(i)” and
  “Corollary 6.3”.  For the cited published version these must be Theorem
  5.1(i) and Corollary 5.4, or the citation must explicitly say “arXiv
  version”.

## Search record

### Claim discriminator

A result passed screening only if it did at least one of the following for the
passant-line/internal-point binary code:

1. classified the weight-12 words, including their number or orbit structure;
2. reconstructed the incidence structure, elliptic scheme, or plane from
   pairwise data on those words;
3. selected a specific elliptic relation operator among the Frobenius-conjugate
   scalar actions; or
4. supplied orbit-specific Gram certificates for the minimum-word families.

Dimension-only, distance-bound-only, other-conic-code, generic
minimum-codeword, generic association-scheme, and generic weighted-pair-graph
results failed the discriminator.
