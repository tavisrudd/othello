### Repair 3 — the domain is part of the workspace shape

`DemandWorkspace` gains a `domain: u32` field, `Demand::workspace` sets it, and `Demand::shape`
compares it, so a workspace handed between two same-shape programs over different domains is
refused with `Error::Source` on the ordinary result path — the error `evaluate_into` already
raises for every other shape mismatch.

| What | Before the repair | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C1199's probe, domain 4,096 then 2,048 then 4,096 into one workspace | third `evaluate_into` does not terminate; killed at 60 s (exit 124) after printing steps 1 and 2 | refused at step 2 in milliseconds |
| `a_same_shape_program_over_a_different_domain_is_refused_rather_than_evaluated` | — | passes; with the domain comparison removed it fails by name in 0.38 s with `called `Result::unwrap_err()` on an `Ok` value: Evaluation { rounds: 2, derived: 19, probes: 38, candidates: 19 }` |

The unpatched hang was re-measured rather than taken from the audit: `git archive HEAD` of core
`2be1e68` into `~/.cache/ergodis/c1200/mutate/`, the audit's own probe file dropped in as
`crates/rules/tests/domain_probe.rs`, `timeout 60` on the test binary. Log at

### Repair 4 — a test that binds the fill-versus-walk rule

`a_bucket_head_large_against_its_rows_is_never_returned_to_empty_by_a_fill` adds a cohort with a
third relation whose rule joins **both** columns of the growing relation, so the index over it has
a key space of `domain^2`: at a domain of 4,096 that is a bucket head of 2^24 slots, 64 MiB, over
209 rows. The existing commit gate cannot see the rule because its whole workspace-table footprint
is a 2 MiB bitmap and a 16 KiB head.

| Rule | First evaluation commits | Second evaluation commits | Test |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| shipped, `bytes <= 64 · rows` | 180,224 B | 0 B | passes |
| inverted, `bytes > 64 · rows` | 71,409,664 B | — | **fails**: `the first evaluation committed 71409664 bytes against a 67108864-byte bucket head, so the reset filled the head rather than walking the rows` |

The assertion threshold is a quarter of the head, 16,777,216 bytes, so the shipped rule clears it
by a factor of 93 and the inversion misses it by a factor of 4.3.

**One correction to the audit's prescription.** C1199 asks for the *second* evaluation's commit
delta, and the second evaluation cannot see the inversion. A fresh workspace's row count is zero,
so `bytes > 64 · 0` is true for every non-empty table and the inverted rule fills on the **first**
reset; by the second there is nothing left to commit and both rules give zero. The first
evaluation's delta is therefore the discriminator, and the test asserts the second as well because
that is the steady-state property the rule is for. The test comment says so.

### Repair 5 — a test that binds the stagger offset in `Drop`

`a_dropped_reservation_releases_the_whole_mapping_and_not_the_staggered_slice` reserves 200 × 256
MiB in a loop and reads `VmSize` from `/proc/self/status`. `VmSize` and not `VmRSS`: a leaked
`MAP_NORESERVE` reservation costs address space and commits nothing, so a resident-set counter
would not see it.

| `Drop` | `VmSize` over the loop | Test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| shipped, releases `base` | 73,556 → 73,556 KiB, a movement of **0 KiB** | passes |
| releases `ptr.as_ptr()` | 415,648 → 56,395,840 KiB, a growth of **55,980,192 KiB** | **fails**: `address space grew from 415648 KiB to 56395840 KiB over 200 reservations, so a dropped reservation is not being released` |

The slack is eight gibibytes, which is generous against a fifty-gibibyte leak and tolerates the
sibling test in the same binary that reserves four gibibytes concurrently — the audit measured
51,381,008 KiB for the leak alone and this run measures 55,980,192 KiB with that sibling included.
The test is `#[cfg(target_os = "linux")]` because `/proc/self/status` is, which is a subset of the
`unix` gate the `Pages` backing carries.

### Repair 13 — the `SAFETY` premise

`Pages::zeroed`'s comment claimed `reserve` returns storage "aligned to at least a cache line",
which only the Unix backing gives; the portable backing is handed `align_of::<T>()` and returns
that. **The comment was repaired rather than the call**, and the reason is that passing 64 to
`reserve` on both paths would change the portable backing's `Layout` — the shape the change is
measured against, and the shape the reservation is released with — to buy an alignment nothing
needs. The comment now says what each backing provides and draws the same conclusion from the
premise that holds: `stagger` is a multiple of sixty-four, which is a multiple of both element
alignments the module admits, so the offset pointer is still aligned for `T`.

`RESET_FILL_BYTES_PER_ROW`'s doc comment said four structures sit between one and sixteen times the
boundary; against the shipped boundary of 64 the C1198 table's structures are seven, between a
quarter and eight times it, five of those within a factor of two. The comment now says that.

### The verdict

**The stagger commit is the repair and the runtime offset is not the mechanism.** The two probes
give the same answer to five decimal places in instructions and to within one percentage point in
cycles, and they differ in the one thing the C1198 report says does the work: `c1198-stagger-probe`
staggers every reservation and `c1198-nostagger-probe` has that expression multiplied out to a zero
offset. What they share is the compiled body. So what removes the 9 per cent is the recompilation
the stagger commit's source change causes, not the cache-line offset it computes.

Three further things this settles or removes:

1. **C1199's defect 1 is right that the stagger's *offset* is not the repair and wrong about what
   is.** It names the private `--cold` driver edit; the measurement above has no driver edit in it.
2. **C1198's mystery ledger item 3 is wrong in the same direction.** The 0.8 per cent of
   instructions it attributes to an untimed harness mode is the stagger commit's, by both the
   arithmetic above and the direct measurement.
3. **The store-to-load-forward counters do not support the 4 KiB-aliasing story.** `ls_stlf` moves
   by one per cent on every row; the conflict counter is unreadable on three rows and says the
   *faster* arm has 1.39 and 2.53 times as many conflicts on dense 512, on counts three orders of
   magnitude below the cycles they would have to buy. C1198's mystery ledger item 2 asserts the
   counter that would show the mechanism is not in the playbook's supplementary set; it is on this
   PMU, it runs at 100.00 per cent enabled, and it runs against the claim.

What is **not** settled is why a source change to a module the derivation loop does not call
recompiles that loop into something 0.8 per cent cheaper in instructions and 9 per cent cheaper in
cycles. That is an open item.

## The `ej` and `tt` closeout

**The first evidence about the recompilation, and it is free.** The opcode histograms of the two
bodies differ by 40 instructions and no new opcode: 27 fewer `mov`, 11 fewer `int3`, 8 fewer `lea`,
4 fewer `jmp`, 4 more `je`, and nothing else above 3. Twenty-seven fewer register moves in a body
that runs the same opcode vocabulary is the signature of register allocation, not of a different
algorithm, and it is consistent with 0.8 per cent fewer instructions buying 8.7 per cent of cycles —
a removed spill on a dependency chain costs far more than its instruction count. This is a lead and
not a conclusion; the measurement that would settle it is named in the mystery ledger.

**What is worth saying plainly about the stagger.** On the evidence now available, the rotating
offset does nothing measurable, costs up to 4,032 bytes of address space per table, and is the one
term `workspace_bytes()` excludes from the ceiling it checks (remaining gap 7). Its whole measured
value is that the source change which introduced it happens to make the compiler produce a faster
loop. That is a fragile thing to ship deliberately, and a successor that touches `pages.rs` should
know the offset is not load bearing for the result the report credits it with.

**The lever this exposes, which is larger than anything the lane has been chasing.** Three
independent, semantically irrelevant source changes have now moved `Demand::evaluate_into` by
0.8 to 1.7 per cent of instructions and up to 9 per cent of cycles: C1170's bench-driver dump path,
C1198's stagger commit, and C1200's workspace field. The free variable in all three is the
compiler's inlining and register allocation over a `lto = "thin"`, `codegen-units = 1` workspace,
and the lane has been spending tasks on changes worth one to six per cent while an unexamined build
configuration moves the same kernel by as much in either direction at random. A deliberate search —
profile-guided optimization on the retained cohorts, `codegen-units`, inline thresholds, and a
`-C llvm-args` inliner sweep, each measured against a retained control on the six direct-path
cohorts — is a cheap task with a plausible several-per-cent win and, more importantly, would turn a
source of noise that has now confounded three reports into something measured.

## Mystery ledger

2. **Is the rotating cache-line offset the mechanism?** No. A probe whose offset is multiplied out
