## Propagation through every strict peak

The eventual peak residues are
\[
\begin{aligned}
1&:\{0,12,20,32,40,52\},\\
2&:\{1,11,21,31,41,51\},\\
3&:\{2,10,22,30,42,50\},\\
3'&:\{6,26,46\}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Let
\[
 L=\operatorname{im}D_{\rho,n-6},\qquad
 U=\operatorname{im}D_{\rho,n}^{\dagger}.
\]
The all-weight two-sided-defect theorem gives \(L+U=M_{\rho,n}\) for
\(n>52\).  For every displayed residue the checker selects one fixed
global-Weyl matrix entry of
\[
 D_{\rho,n}D_{\rho,n-6}.
\]
All twenty-one entries are distinct degree-four polynomials in \(q\);
after writing \(q=1+s\), every one has nonzero coefficients of one sign.
Thus the composition is nonzero for every \(q\ge1\), so
\(L\not\perp U\).

The phase quotients anchor every formerly unanchored equal-rank plateau
component.  Multiplicity induction therefore supplies the two smaller
neighboring path corners at a strict peak.  They push the full supported
algebras on \(L\) and \(U\) into the peak corner.  Since those subspaces
span and are nonorthogonal, the supported-two-subspace lemma generates
\(\operatorname{End}(M_{\rho,n})\).  Combining this with the earlier
\(q=0\) certificates closes all eventual strict peaks.

## `ej` + `tt` closeout

The cheap strengthening is the peak witness.  Dimension counting is no
longer carrying nonorthogonality: all twenty-one families have explicit
degree-four two-step compositions, already coefficientwise one-signed
from \(q=1\).  This is stronger and cleaner than a phase-by-phase finite
rank check.

The phase determinants are genuinely distinct.  No two become
proportional after normalization, even within one Kostant module.  Thus
the sixteen-phase audit did not conceal another degree-\(20\) or
degree-\(60\) intertwining shortcut.

The `tt` object is the denominator-cleared quotient connection, not the
large ambient transvectant matrix.  Its exact degree drops
\[

\]
from the formal bounds are the associated-graded data left by elimination.
They should be carried into the off-peak step rather than replaced by
larger generated-algebra determinants.
