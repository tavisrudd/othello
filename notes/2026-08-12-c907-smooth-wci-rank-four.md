# C907 smooth weighted-CI rank-four theorem

**Lane:** `clebsch`

**Status:** theorem-grade; supersedes the Cartier-degree subclass in
`2026-08-12-c907-cartier-wci-rank-four.md` without altering its history.

## Theorem

Let

\[
 X=X_{d_1,\ldots,d_c}\subset W=\mathbf P(w_0,\ldots,w_{c+3})
\]

be a quasismooth weighted complete-intersection threefold with smooth coarse
space, strongly well-formed in the sense that
`codim_X(X intersect Sing(W_coarse))>=2`.  Suppose `Pic(X)=Z[H]`, with
`H=O_X(1)` primitive, and suppose `X` is Fano of index

\[
 r=\sum_iw_i-\sum_jd_j>0.
\]

Then the full ordinary small-even quantum D-module of `X` is cyclic rank
four.  Its reduced scalar operator has the form

\[
 \theta^4-C s\prod_{a\in A}(\theta+a),\qquad s=q/z^r,
 \tag{1}
\]

where `C!=0`, the multiset `A` is invariant under `a mapsto1-a`, and
`|A|=4-r`.  Hence

\[
 \nu_6(X)\le2.
\]

## 1. Geometric cyclotomic cancellation

Strong well-formedness plus smoothness of the coarse threefold implies that
the stack `X` has trivial inertia.  At a nontrivial stabilizer chart, a smooth
coarse quotient must be generated by pseudoreflections; their fixed divisor
would lie in `X intersect Sing(W_coarse)`, contrary to strong
well-formedness.  A trivial effective action would put the whole chart in the
same locus.

For each `m>1`, let

\[
 n_m=\#\{i:m\mid w_i\},\qquad h_m=\#\{j:m\mid d_j\}.
\]

The `m`-inertia stratum is the weighted projective space on the `n_m`
coordinates with weights divisible by `m`.  Equations with `m` not dividing
their degree restrict to zero there.  If `h_m<n_m`, the remaining at most
`h_m` positive-degree homogeneous equations have a common projective zero,
contradicting inertia avoidance.  Thus

\[
 h_m\ge n_m \quad(m>1). \tag{2}
\]

The principal-sector factorial period is

\[
 \Phi(s)=\sum_{n\ge0}
 \frac{\prod_j(d_jn)!}{\prod_i(w_in)!}s^n.
 \tag{3}
\]

At every reduced fraction of denominator `m`, the numerator occurs `h_m`
times and the denominator `n_m` times.  Equation (2) cancels every fractional
denominator.  With `F_N={1/N,\ldots,(N-1)/N}`, the residual numerator
multiset is

\[
 A=\left(\bigsqcup_jF_{d_j}\right)\setminus
   \left(\bigsqcup_iF_{w_i}\right),\qquad
 |A|=\sum_j(d_j-1)-\sum_i(w_i-1)=4-r.
\tag{4}
\]

It is nonnegative by (2), and is symmetric under `a mapsto1-a`.  The
`c+4` denominator `(n+1)` factors and `c` numerator factors leave
`(n+1)^4`, proving the formal operator (1).

## 2. Non-Cartier QDM bridge

The preceding cancellation is not itself a quantum-Lefschetz theorem.  Apply
Wang's nonconvex toric complete-intersection mirror theorem
(arXiv:1910.14440, Theorem 1.1) directly to the weighted-projective GIT
presentation.  It puts his explicit big `I`-function (formula (1.1)) on the
ordinary Givental cone of `X`, with no Cartier or convexity hypothesis.

The term of degree `beta` in that formula lies in the sector labelled by
`g_beta^(-1)`.  Since `X` has trivial inertia it vanishes unless
`g_beta=1`.  For `W=P(w)`, primitivity of `H=O_X(1)` says exactly that this
means `beta=n` is an integer.  Then every `n d_j` is nonnegative integral,
so Wang Lemma 3.2 and Corollary 3.3(1) identify the localized class with the
fundamental class.  Formula (1.1) is consequently the cohomology-valued
hypergeometric lift of (3), not merely a raw stacky series.

This is the sector argument that removes the Cartesian-degree hypothesis:
the nonintegral ambient degrees are excluded by *target* inertia rather than
by convexity.  It also gives the required Birkhoff equality
`I=J(q,mu,z)` as part of Wang Theorem 1.1.

The Fano degree estimate gives a small, not big, normalization: the degree
`n` principal term begins in `z^(1-rn)`.  Thus for `r>=2` the principal
series is the small `J`-slice; for `r=1` its sole extra nonnegative
positive-degree term is a scalar unit `q`-term.  The string gauge removes
that scalar, changing the irregular exponential but not formal residues.

Finally `Pic(X)=Z[H]` and Poincare duality give
`H^even(X)=span(1,H,H^2,H^3)`.  The cohomology-valued lift and its three
cyclic derivatives have these classical leading terms, so (1) is the full
small-even QDM.  The rank-four hypergeometric support bound then gives
`nu_6<=2`.

## Exact boundary

The conclusion does not cover a non-strongly-well-formed WCI, a singular
coarse target, nonprimitive `O_X(1)`, higher Picard rank, odd quantum sectors,
or the operation-framed carrier length.  It does cover non-Cartier degrees:
  their source bridge is Wang's nonconvex big-`I` theorem, not convex orbifold
  quantum Lefschetz.  The singular `(3,6)` false positive remains outside the
  theorem because it is not quasismooth.
