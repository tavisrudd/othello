## 53.1 The saturated closure of a generically invisible span

Let (R) be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer (s), fraction field
(K), and residue field (k).  Let (V_R) be finite free and write
(V_K=V_R\otimes_RK).  Suppose that

\[
             r_R:V_R\longrightarrow R                         \tag{53.1}
\]

is a row and that (C_K\subset V_K) is a (K)-subspace on which its generic
fibre vanishes.  Define the closure intrinsically by

\[
 C_R:=V_R\cap C_K
     =\{v\in V_R:v\otimes1\in C_K\},
 \qquad Q_R:=V_R/C_R.                                           \tag{53.2}
\]

The intersection is taken inside (V_K); it is not an arbitrary lattice in
(C_K).

### Proposition 53.1 -- saturated row-null closure

With the preceding notation:

1. (C_R\subset V_R) is saturated and (Q_R) is finite free;
2. (C_R\otimes_RK=C_K) whenever (C_K) is generated by vectors from
   (V_R) after clearing denominators;
3. (r_R(C_R)=0), so there is a unique descended row
   \[
                  \bar r_R:Q_R\longrightarrow R;               \tag{53.3}
   \]
4. the sequence
   \[
       0\longrightarrow C_R\otimes_Rk
        \longrightarrow V_R\otimes_Rk
        \longrightarrow Q_R\otimes_Rk\longrightarrow0          \tag{53.4}
   \]
   is exact.

#### Proof

If (av\in C_R) for nonzero (a\in R), then (av\in C_K), hence
(v\in C_K) because (C_K) is a (K)-space.  Thus (v\in C_R), proving
saturation.  The quotient is therefore torsion-free, hence free over the
DVR.  Clearing denominators proves item 2.  For (v\in C_R), the scalar
(r_R(v)) maps to zero in (K); injectivity (R\hookrightarrow K) gives
(r_R(v)=0).  This proves item 3.  Finally, (Q_R) is flat, so tensoring the
defining short exact sequence with (k) remains exact.  \(\square\)

This proposition is the exact anti-confluence law needed by the rank
consumer.  A row-null generic supported span cannot acquire a row-visible
closed-fibre vector after taking its saturated closure.  Any such vector must
come from a different integral lattice, a nonexact closed-packet
identification, or parameter torsion outside (Q_R).

### Corollary 53.1A -- one common quotient replaces overlap Stokes matrices

Let (C_K\) be the span of all generic exceptional/supported blocks occurring
in two incident sectorial charts.  Assume each block is (r_K)-null.  Let
(C_R) and (Q_R) be (53.2).  Suppose the actual closed-fibre ambient packet
in each chart is identified, compatibly with its row and primitive projector,
with the same subquotient (P_i\subset Q_R\otimes_Rk).  If the two packet
identifications differ by a lawful row-compatible reindexing, then their
zero/nonzero row-coimage markers agree.

#### Proof

All generic supported blocks disappear in the single quotient (Q_R), and
Proposition 53.1 gives one descended closed row.  The two markers are
therefore evaluations of the same row on lawfully reindexed versions of the
same packet subquotient.  Row-compatible reindexing preserves whether the
coimage is zero.  \(\square\)

No comparison of two Stokes splittings appears in this proof.  The
load-bearing input is instead the typed identification of both actual closed
packets with the one saturated quotient.

## 53.2 Why torsion is exactly the hostile alternative

Saturation cannot be deleted.  The finite (R)-module

\[
                         T=R/(s)                                \tag{53.5}
\]

has zero generic fibre and nonzero closed fibre.  Thus a generically
invisible supported class can reappear at resonance if it is stored as
parameter torsion rather than in a flat quotient.  The perfect complex
([R\xrightarrow{s}R]) gives the derived version: it is generically acyclic
and has two nonzero closed-fibre cohomology groups.

Conversely, if a finite (R)-module (M) is torsion-free and
(M\otimes_RK=0), then (M=0).  Applied to the kernel and cokernel of a
comparison, this recovers Module 40's saturation theorem.  Proposition 53.1
adds the useful constructive direction: once a common free receiver is
known, quotienting by the saturated generic supported span manufactures the
flat row receiver canonically.

## 53.3 Normalized primary coimages

The quantum-Serre and canonical-completion maps have a universal zero on the
rank row.  The correct special object is their normalized row coimage, not
their raw closed fibre.

Let (a\in R) be nonzero, let (P_R^\pm) be finite free modules with rows
(r_R^\pm:P_R^\pm\to R), and define

\[
 \mathcal L_R^\pm
  :=P_R^\pm/(P_R^\pm\cap\ker r_R^\pm).                           \tag{53.6}
\]

Assume the two coimages are nonzero free rank one and that their induced rows
identify them with (R).  When a phase restriction is desired, first pass to
a named finite extension of fraction fields and choose one DVR component
(R'/R) of the integral closure which contains (\chi).  Replace (R) by
(R'), include its ramification index in the parameter valuation, and then
assume (\chi\in R).  Let (T_\pm) be endomorphisms and take
(P_R^\pm) to be the resulting saturated generalized-(\chi) packets.

### Theorem 53.2 -- normalized primary-coimage transport

Suppose (F:P_R^-\to P_R^+) satisfies

\[
       F T_-=T_+F,
 \qquad
       r_R^+F=u\,a^d r_R^- ,
 \qquad u\in R^\times,\quad d\ge0,                              \tag{53.7}
\]

and carries the named saturated generalized-(\chi) packet into its target,
becoming an isomorphism on the named packets after inverting (a).
Then (F) induces

\[
 \bar F_\chi:\mathcal L^-_{R,\chi}\longrightarrow
                  \mathcal L^+_{R,\chi},                       \tag{53.8}
\]

and, under the row identifications, (\bar F_\chi) is multiplication by
(u a^d).  Hence the normalized map

\[
       \bar F^{\mathrm{res}}_\chi:=a^{-d}\bar F_\chi           \tag{53.9}
\]

is an integral isomorphism and its closed fibre is multiplication by the
unit (\bar u).

If (a=v s^e) with (v\in R^\times), then the raw valuation is (ed), not
(d).  Thus ramification is recorded exactly and cannot be removed by
renaming the parameter.

#### Proof

Equation (53.7) sends the row kernel into the row kernel and therefore
induces (53.8).  The two induced rows are isomorphisms to (R), so the same
equation identifies the induced scalar with (ua^d).  Dividing this scalar,
not either packet, gives the unit (u).  Intertwining carries generalized
eigenspaces to generalized eigenspaces, and saturation makes the restriction
integral.  The valuation statement follows from additivity of the DVR
valuation.  \(\square\)

If the coimage rows are not saturated isomorphisms to (R), their two lattice
indices contribute an additional valuation.  Exact nonzero closed-coimage
base change rules this out: an injective map from a free rank-one coimage to
(R) whose closed row is nonzero has unit image ideal.

### Corollary 53.2A -- composition law

For composable normalized primary-coimage maps, the raw orders add and the
residual units multiply.  A path of (n) simple conormal bridges has raw
leading term in ((s/s^2)^{\otimes n}), while the residual maps compose as
ordinary line isomorphisms.  This is the precise jet/Writer distinction from
Modules 42--43.

## 53.4 Extension of the quantum-Serre source theorem

For a rank-(d) bundle (E), Iritani--Mann--Mignon write

\[
 e_\lambda(E)=\sum_{i=0}^d\lambda^{d-i}c_i(E).                  \tag{53.10}
\]

Let (\rho_0) take the degree-zero cohomological coefficient.  Then, without
any convergence or semisimplicity assumption,

\[
              \rho_0(e_\lambda(E)\alpha)
                   =\lambda^d\rho_0(\alpha).                   \tag{53.11}
\]

Their Theorem 2.11 proves formally, with its nonlinear base pullback (f),
that multiplication by the invertible multiplicative class intertwines the
two twisted quantum connections and their fundamental solutions before the
non-equivariant specialization.  In a convergent, single-valued analytic
realization on a domain carried through (f), this intertwining also carries
the monodromy operator of a named common based loop.  Combining that
additional analytic realization with Theorem 53.2 gives:

### Corollary 53.2B -- residual quantum-Serre primary row

Assume a trait through (\lambda=0) supplies saturated generalized-(\chi)
packet lattices, exact nonzero row-coimage base change, a convergent
single-valued realization with a common loop under (f), and identifies the
generic quantum-Serre comparison with the actual occurrence map.  Then the
induced primary row-coimage map is divisible by (\lambda^d), and its
normalized residual induces the identity on the special primary row coimage,
up to the named unit from a change of primitive uniformizer.

This conclusion is new only at the sparse coimage level.  It does **not**
assert that the full operator (e_\lambda(E)) is divisible by
(\lambda^d), or that (\lambda^{-d}e_\lambda(E)) acts on the full QDM at
(\lambda=0), and it does not manufacture the trait lattice or identify an
ordinary QDM occurrence with the required Euler-twisted one.  The
non-equivariant map of their Theorem 3.14 remains the honest full-object
limit; its rank row is zero before this coimage normalization.

The (K)-theoretic formula of Modules 42 and 46 is the same theorem with
(a=1-q^{-1}):

\[
 \operatorname{rk}\lambda_{-1}(q^{-1}E)
                 =(1-q^{-1})^d.                                \tag{53.12}
\]

## 53.5 The exact two-arrow extension of the one-arrow receiver

The formal-Novikov sectorial receiver proves, for one blowup arrow, that the
Gamma rank row vanishes on every generic center block and is the base rank
row on the ambient block.  It does not compare two incident Stokes
embeddings at a confluence point.

Let the two incident generic receivers be embedded in one (V_K), and let
(C_K) be the sum of their generic center/supported blocks.  The one-arrow
theorem gives (r_K(C_K)=0).  Therefore Proposition 53.1 proves the following
conditional extension.

### Theorem 53.3 -- saturated-confluence overlap theorem

For every consecutive-discrepant overlap, assume:

1. the two incident sectorial systems and their common Gamma rank row have a
   canonical finite-free trait receiver (V_R);
2. their generic center/supported blocks lie in a common (K)-subspace
   (C_K\subset V_K), and the source one-arrow theorem identifies the row as
   zero on (C_K);
3. the actual closed fixed-phase ambient packets, projectors, and rows are
   identified with the corresponding subquotients of
   \[
                         Q_R=V_R/(V_R\cap C_K);                 \tag{53.13}
   \]
4. those packet/coimage identifications commute with closed base change and
   adjacent deck/phase reindexing.

Then the induced primitive row-coimage marker agrees across the overlap.
If these hypotheses hold at every consecutive-discrepant overlap of the
chosen (m=2) weak factorization, the Module 34 quotient-line telescope
closes and (X\times\mathbf P^2) is irrational.

#### Proof

Proposition 53.1 makes (Q_R) finite free and gives it one descended row.
Item 3 puts both actual closed ambient packets in this common quotient;
item 4 makes the two readings lawful.  Corollary 53.1A therefore gives the
local marker law.  Module 39 already supplies ordinary and isolated-wall
transitions, so the local laws cover the remaining edges.  The Module 34
source marker is nonzero and its projective endpoint marker is zero, a
contradiction.  \(\square\)

This is strictly weaker than a two-arrow Stokes-matrix theorem.  It asks for
no equality of sectorial splittings and computes no mutation coefficient.
It asks only that both charts live in one integral receiver and that their
actual consumed packets are faithfully read in its saturated ambient
quotient.

## 53.6 What has and has not moved

The earlier analytic gate was:

\[
 \text{construct a coherent two-arrow Stokes/Gamma comparison}.           \tag{53.14}
\]

The proved reduction replaces it by:

\[
 \boxed{\text{common canonical trait receiver}}
 \quad+\quad
 \boxed{\text{closed packet quotient-fidelity and exact base change}}.     \tag{53.15}
\]

Saturation of the generic row-null supported span is no longer a separate
conjecture: (53.2) constructs it and Proposition 53.1 proves its flatness.
Likewise, the universal quantum-Serre/conormal scalar is no longer an open
calculation: Theorem 53.2 and (53.11)--(53.12) compute its normalized primary
row exactly.

What remains genuinely analytic/geometric is the existence of one canonical
trait lattice containing both incident receivers and the identification of
its quotient with the actual fixed-phase QDM packets.  A lattice chosen after
seeing the answer is not lawful; rescaling changes valuations.  An abstract
associated-graded identification is also insufficient because it need not
commute with the closed primitive projector or row coimage.

This target is uniform in codimension.  If proved occurrence-uniformly, it is
compatible with the all-(m) rank-row route; no new scalar calculation is
introduced as (m) grows.

## Boundary

The source extension proves that generic exceptional row-nullity survives
every saturated confluence and that the normalized primary quantum-Serre row
is a unit.  It thereby removes a full Stokes-mutation calculation and a
separate scalar computation from the local target.  It does not construct
the common trait receiver or identify its saturated quotient with the actual
fixed-phase primitive QDM packet.  Those two occurrence-level statements are
now the complete local gate for this route; no unconditional (m=2) theorem
is claimed.
