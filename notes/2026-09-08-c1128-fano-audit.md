## 1. Quantum input and normalization

Przyjalkowski, arXiv:math/0507232v3, Section 2.6, defines the counting matrix
using classical powers of (K=-K_V), with row (i), column (j) coefficient
of (K^i) in (K\star K^j). The curve degree attached to that entry is

\[
K\cdot\beta=j-i+1.
\]

For index two write (K=2H) and let (q) record (H\cdot\beta=1).
An entry of anticanonical degree (2d) therefore receives (q^d).
If a variable (t) records anticanonical degree, then (q=t^2).
This is a Novikov convention, not a change or ramification of (z).

Theorem 2.6.6 gives ((a,b)=(240,1248)) and ((48,160)), respectively, in

\[
U_{a,b}(q)=\begin{pmatrix}
0&aq&0&a^2q^2\\1&0&bq&0\\0&1&0&aq\\0&0&1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The upper-right coefficients printed in the source are 57600 and 2304.
Both matrices and Proposition 2.6.4 were also inspected on a rendered image
of PDF page 9. The theorem number here is for arXiv v3; the source metadata
explicitly warns that journal numbering differs.

Source (V_1,V_2) have anticanonical volumes 8 and 16, hence (H^3=1,2).
Proposition 2.6.4 identifies their models as sextics in
\(\mathbf P(1,1,1,2,3)\) and quartics in \(\mathbf P(1,1,1,1,2)\).
The source's third family (V'_2), of anticanonical volume 2 and index one,
is a different sextic-double-solid family and plays no role here.
The source proves the matrix theorem using its weighted-complete-intersection
formula and reconstruction. This audit checks that application and its
normalizations; it does not reprove the mirror theorem. Theorem 1.1's
index-at-least-two case has no index-one exponential correction.

The source statements concern these smooth types, rather than a chosen
special defining equation. Their use for every smooth member is consistent
with genus-zero deformation invariance. The corroborating family descriptions
and period formulas in Coates–Corti–Galkin–Kasprzyk, arXiv:1303.3288v3,
Sections 3–5, use (B_1,B_2,B_3) for the same three families. We do **not**
substitute a period sequence for a quantum connection.

For the manuscript's cubic matrix in the classical (H)-power basis,

\[
S=\operatorname{diag}(1,2,4,8),\qquad
S^{-1}(2U_{6,15})S=U_{24,60}.
\]

The grading operator (D=\tfrac12\operatorname{diag}(3,1,-1,-3)) commutes
with (S). Thus the equation remains (z^2\partial_z y=(U+zD)y).
There is no extra factor two to attach to the source's anticanonical matrix.

## 2. Rational calculation, with the derivative term retained

Work over \(\mathbf Q(a,b,q)\), (s=2a+b), on (sq\ne0), with (q)
constant under (\partial_z). Put

\[
C=\begin{pmatrix}
aq&0&0&-(a+b)q\\
0&(a+b)q&-aq&0\\
1&0&0&1\\
0&1&1&0
\end{pmatrix},\qquad \det C=-s^2q^2.
\]

Its first two columns span \(\operatorname{im}U^2\), the last two
\(\ker U^2\). Direct multiplication gives

\[
C^{-1}UC=J_+\oplus N,quad
J_+=\begin{pmatrix}0&sq\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
N=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det(TI-U)=T^2(T^2-sq).
\]

No square root is needed to isolate the zero-primary block. The complementary
block need not be split into its two eigenlines for this computation.

Let (B=C^{-1}DC). For the normalized formal gauge

\[
G=I+zX+z^2Y+\cdots,
\]

take diagonal blocks of (X,Y) zero. The off-diagonal equations are

\[
J_jX_{ji}-X_{ji}J_i=-B_{ji}.
\]

They have unique solutions because the spectra of (J_+) and (N) are
disjoint. More generally, allow an original (z^2F) coefficient. Expanding

\[
z^2G'=(J+zB+z^2F)G-G(J+zB_1+z^2B_2)
\]

gives

\[
B_1=B+[J,X],\qquad
B_2=F+BX-XB_1-X+[J,Y].
\]

The (-X) term is the derivative contribution. Its diagonal blocks vanish,
as do those of (XB_1) and ([J,Y]). Consequently

\[
(B_2)_{ii}=F_{ii}+\sum_{j\ne i}B_{ij}X_{ji}.
\]

This formula has a specified gauge normalization; it is not a statement
about arbitrary diagonal gauge choices. For (F=0), the elementary
modification (\operatorname{diag}(1,z)) on the zero block yields

\[
R_{a,b}=\begin{pmatrix}
-\dfrac{2a+3b}{2s}&1\\
-\dfrac{4a^2}{s^2}&\dfrac{b-2a}{2s}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det(TI-R)=T^2+T+\frac{10a-3b}{4s}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\delta^\sharp=\frac{4(b-2a)}{b+2a}.
\]

For the cubic, conjugating this residue by \(\operatorname{diag}(2,1)\)
gives exactly the manuscript's printed matrix with upper-right entry 2 and
lower-left entry (-8/81). Both the four-dimensional normalization and the
two-dimensional residue normalization are checked.

## 3. Independent derivation in the original basis

For a zero-exponential formal solution write

\[
y=z^\rho\sum_{n\ge0}y_nz^n,
\qquad Uy_{n+1}=(\rho+n-D)y_n.
\]

Choose (y_0=(0,-aq,0,1)^t). A first lift is

\[
y_1=\left(-q\left((a+b)\rho+\frac{-a+3b}{2}\right),
0,\rho+\frac32,0\right)^t.
\]

The left null vector (l=(1,0,-aq,0)) gives the next compatibility condition

\[
l(\rho+1-D)y_1
=-q\left(s\rho^2+s\rho+\frac{10a-3b}{4}\right)=0.
\]

Its monic polynomial agrees with the residue characteristic polynomial.
Adding a multiple of (y_0) to (y_1) does not affect the compatibility
condition. This is an independent finite indicial calculation; it does not
assert absence of logarithms or construct complete solutions in resonant cases.

The second executable uses only Python's exact `Fraction` arithmetic and

the quadratic from three evaluations for each of four parameter pairs and
four nonzero rational (q) values. It imports neither SymPy nor the first
generator. The main generator also verifies the displayed recurrence
symbolically for arbitrary (a,b,q,\rho).

## 4. From the matrix to the whole generic connection

For a smooth complex Fano threefold of Picard rank one, Kodaira vanishing
gives (H^{2,0}=0); the Lefschetz ((1,1)) theorem then gives (b_2=1).
Poincaré duality gives (b_4=1), and (b_0=b_6=1). Thus

\[
H^{\mathrm{ev}}(V,\mathbf Q)
=\langle1,H,H^2,H^3\rangle.
\]

There is no extra primitive even cohomology omitted by the counting matrix.
Odd middle cohomology is outside the invariant's even connection. This is
why an ambient-versus-whole-primary objection of the cubic-fourfold kind
does not apply to these threefolds.

At nonzero (q), the rank-two zero-primary summand is separated and cyclic
with nonzero square-zero leading term. The already-audited persistence
statement carries it through the formal even bulk germ and keeps its
canonical discriminant constant. The other two eigenvalues are distinct
and nonzero after algebraic extension; their continued clusters have rank
one. Hence there is exactly one possible counted block.

## 5. Accepted deductions, conditional on the audited geometric inputs

For every smooth complex Picard-rank-one, index-two Fano threefold of degree

\[
d=H^3\in\{1,2,3\},
\qquad V\times\mathbf P^1\text{ is irrational}.
\]

Indeed, the applicable count is one on (V), two on its trivial rank-two
projectivization, and zero on \(\mathbf P^4\). Low-dimensional vanishing
and the blowup formula give birational invariance in dimension four. Degree
one and the cubic use (I_{\rm exp}); degree two uses (I_{\rm lat}).

Kuznetsov–Prokhorov, arXiv:1911.08949v3, Theorem 2.7 gives the degree range
1–5. Their Theorems 3.3 and 3.5 give rationality in degrees five and four
over \(\mathbf C\). In degree four, the line condition is automatic over
\(\mathbf C\). Combining these positive source results with the audited
deduction gives the proposed complete statement **over \(\mathbf C\)**:

\[
V\times\mathbf P^1\text{ rational}
\iff V\text{ rational}\iff d\in\{4,5\}.
\]

This is our deduction, not a theorem attributed to either quantum source
or to Kuznetsov–Prokhorov. It classifies one stabilization, not stable
rationality or the exact finite levels of degree-one and degree-two varieties.

The negative conclusion in degrees 1–3 extends to characteristic-zero forms
of these geometric types. A hypothetical rationalization and inverse descend,
together with the variety and the geometric type data after finite extension
if necessary, to a finitely generated characteristic-zero field. Embedding
that field into \(\mathbf C\) contradicts the complex result. This does not
promote the displayed degree-four rationality equivalence to arbitrary fields:
the cited theorem there imposes arithmetic point/line conditions.
