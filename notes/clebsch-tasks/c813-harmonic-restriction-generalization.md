# C813 — Generalizing the harmonic restriction mechanism

**Lane:** `clebsch`  
**Status:** queued; mathematics only; cheap falsifiers first; paper promotion excluded

## Objective

Determine whether the Petersen/face-axis zonal embedding and invariant-cubic restriction mechanism extends beyond harmonic degree six, either through other even degrees or through other $A_5$-irreducible channels, and explain the restriction scalar structurally.

## Cheap gates first

1. Compute the $A_5$-character decomposition of spherical harmonics $\mathcal H_\ell$ for a bounded range of small degrees and tabulate the relevant irreducible multiplicities and invariant cubics.
2. Compute exact Gram or reproducing-kernel eigenvalues on the ten face axes as polynomials in $\ell$, identifying which Petersen eigenspace each channel occupies.
3. Evaluate one exact test vector per surviving channel to obtain the restriction scalar and detect zeros, repetitions, or a recurrence.
4. Only if a pattern survives, make a bounded literature check under icosahedral harmonics, zonal spherical functions, association schemes, Petersen eigenspaces, Gaunt coefficients, and $A_5$ harmonic branching.

## Mathematical continuation if the gate passes

Prove the recurrence, generating function, or representation-theoretic selection rule governing the nonzero degrees and scalars. If degree six is isolated, prove the isolation criterion rather than calling the search negative.

## Stop conditions

Stop broad computation once the bounded character and scalar tables force either a clean family or a clean isolation mechanism. Do not promote a numerical pattern without an exact representation-theoretic explanation.

## Acceptance

An exact branching/scalar table plus a theorem establishing a family, recurrence, selection rule, or degree-six isolation, with reproducible calculations and a human proof route.

## Promotion boundary

Do not edit a paper source or public package. Freeze any positive result in the task report; manuscript integration, public positioning, and a new cold read require a separately allocated follow-up.
