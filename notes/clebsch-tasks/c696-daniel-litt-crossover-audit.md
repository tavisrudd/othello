# C696 — Daniel Litt crossover audit

**Lane:** `clebsch`

**Opened:** 2026-07-29

**Status:** queued; bounded literature and expert-outreach preparation.

## Objective

Identify the strongest mathematically honest connections between Daniel
Litt's research and the three Clebsch papers, with special attention to
Paper III.  Produce a technically specific basis on which to ask him for
review.

## Work package

1. Build a bounded primary-source bibliography of Litt's work on:
   reconstruction from period/Higgs data, monodromy and exceptional
   groups, cubic/Fano geometry, arithmetic local systems and trace fields,
   and adjacent AI-assisted mathematics.
2. Triage by abstracts, then read in full the highest-overlap papers.
   Start with Krämer--Litt--Maculan, arXiv:2604.20970; do not infer
   crossover from titles or shared exceptional-group labels.
3. Compare each serious candidate against the exact theorem surfaces of
   Papers I--III and C682.  Classify the relationship as:
   direct reusable theorem, common geometric object, transferable method,
   informed analogy, or false friend.
4. Rank concrete upgrades, citations, terminology corrections, and
   questions on which Litt would be an unusually valuable reviewer.
5. Produce a one-page technical briefing and a concise draft review
   invitation.  The invitation should foreground one or two exact
   connections, state the paper's scope honestly, and make a bounded ask.
   Do not send or post anything.

## Acceptance

- Every claimed connection cites the primary source at theorem/section
  level and states both the common structure and the mismatch.
- The audit identifies at least one credible review angle or concludes
  sharply that no sufficiently specific angle exists.
- Any novelty or pre-emption conclusion follows the repository literature
  audit conventions and records the searched domain and stopping rule.
- The final ranking distinguishes what belongs in Paper III from material
  that should remain an outreach briefing or future project.

## Boundaries

- Do not use social-media prominence as mathematical evidence.
- Do not contact Litt, his coauthors, or anyone else.
- Do not bulk-download an undelimited publication list.  Triage first and
  use the shared literature cache.
- Do not reshape a paper merely to manufacture an external connection.
