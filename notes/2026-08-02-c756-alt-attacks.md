# C756 — alternative attacks after the tensor stop

**Lane**: clebsch · **Date**: 2026-08-02 · **Scope**: proof-route reset

## Verdict

The failed Ball--Lavrauw interface does not leave one cofactor attack with several
names.  There are four genuinely different saturated-internal attacks and one
independent nonsaturated attack.  They differ in both input object and desired output,
and each has a bounded falsifier.

The highest-EV next move remains the prime-field lacunary route, but it should target
the cleared first angle coefficient rather than the weaker master-polynomial
divisibility.  If that coefficient does not acquire bounded support or a bounded-order
Cartier recurrence, switch to the four-point row-transition attack.  Do not fall back
to another ordinary-rank or spectral bound: both are already tight on negative data.

## 1. Route L — lacunary first cofactor

**Input.**  The full first row moment in cleared form

\[
 C_i=\sum_{j\ne i} f_j(z_i)
       \prod_{\substack{r\ne i\\r\ne j}}f_r(z_i^q)=0,
\]

together with the squarefree rational master polynomial
$G=\prod_i f_i$ of degree $q+3$.  The older condition
$G\mid G'^q-(-1)^{t+1}G'$ uses only the constant angle coefficient and is
strictly weaker.

**Desired output.**  Over prime fields, express the section $i\mapsto C_i$ as a
bounded-support polynomial, Hasse derivative, or Cartier eigenvector on the root
divisor of $G$.  A degree/support theorem should force the four-point endpoint,
not merely recover $C_i=0$ row by row.

**Cheap discriminator.**  Canonically remove $f_i$, reduce the resulting coefficient
modulo $G$, and measure its monomial support and Cartier orbit in the existing prime
fields.  Continue only if the normalized support or recurrence order is bounded
independently of $q$.  Stop if it grows linearly with $q$, or if the construction
requires choosing a tangent direction at every root.

**Why distinct.**  This is a one-variable lacunary-polynomial theorem.  It neither
uses the angle matrix's ordinary rank nor asks a tangent tensor to couple rows.

## 2. Route Q — four-point row-transition rigidity

**Input.**  For every base point $i$, the map

\[
 \rho_i:j\longmapsto\alpha_{ij}
\]

is a bijection from $A\setminus\{i\}$ to the odd norm-one coset.  Compare two
rows on their common $K-2$ indices and retain the cross-ratio identities among
four conjugate pairs.

**Desired output.**  Show that a transition $\rho_\ell\rho_i^{-1}$, with its one
deleted and one inserted label, has bounded rational degree on the norm-one torus.
If simultaneous binomiality forces a Möbius or quadratic transition, agreement on
$K-O(1)$ labels turns the local four-point identity into a global one; the residual
classification can then force $q=5$.

**Cheap discriminator.**  From the existing angle matrices, interpolate each row
transition on the torus and record its least rational degree, pole divisor, and
four-point cross-ratio defect.  Continue only if the positive frames have a bounded
profile which every negative candidate violates, or if all candidates share a bounded
profile susceptible to classification.  Stop if the minimum degree grows with $q$
and the radial coordinate remains unconstrained.

**Why distinct.**  This route uses the complete family of row permutations and local
four-point compatibility.  Its output is a low-degree transition law, not a global
cofactor section.

## 3. Route F — sparse integral Paley trade

**Input.**  The conjugation-odd signed indicator
$x=\mathbf1_Z-\mathbf1_{Z^q}$, with coefficients in $\{0,\pm1\}$, support
$q+3$, and exact Paley eigenvalue $(q-1)/2$.  General position and the matched
conjugate support are retained as extra constraints.

**Desired output.**  Classify the support-$q+3$ integral vectors in this Paley
eigenspace which are anti-invariant under Frobenius and whose positive support is an
arc.  A successful theorem identifies the $q=5$ frame without mentioning angle
cofactors.

**Cheap discriminator.**  Compute the Fourier value distribution, additive-energy
profile, and short autocorrelations of the bounded coherent candidates.  Continue
only if the frame is isolated by an integral or valuation profile that is invisible
to the already-tight second moment.  Stop on another norm, entropy, or interlacing
inequality: those mechanisms are already known to attain equality for the whole
coherent family.

**Why distinct.**  This is a sparse Fourier/trade classification on the additive
group of $\mathbb F_{q^2}$; its output is support rigidity rather than polynomial
lacunarity or row-transition geometry.

## 4. Route D — coupled higher moments and second compounds

**Input.**  The displacement identity for the first angle matrix, the possible
nullity-two branch seen at $q=23$, and the full family of moment equations

\[
 \mathsf A^{\circ r}\mathbf1=0,
 \qquad 1\le r<t,
\]

for a genuine saturated arc.

**Desired output.**  Use two adjacent Hadamard powers simultaneously to prove that
the first matrix has nullity one, or derive a second-compound recurrence whose
Plücker coordinates contradict the next moment.  The point is to let full
binomiality remove the $\operatorname{rank}\le K-2$ branch before computing an adjugate.

**Cheap discriminator.**  On the $q=23$ nullity-two candidates, compute the exterior
kernel coordinates and their compatibility with the next row moment on every already
vanishing row.  Continue only if one bounded compound identity separates the seven-row
texture from the $q=5$ frames.  Stop if the compound degree grows with $K$ or merely
restates all $t-1$ row moments.

**Why distinct.**  This route attacks the exceptional rank branch directly with two
moment levels.  It does not attempt to globalize the nullity-one adjugate line.

## 5. Route R — masked Rédei gap on the nonsaturated branch

**Input.**  After deleting an arc point on a spare external line, the remaining affine
set has a quadratic-character half-alphabet mask and misses $h$ directions.  The
complete audit has $h\ge2$ through $q\le43$, while ordinary Rédei divisibility
alone permits $h=0$.

**Desired output.**  Prove directly that the character-masked Rédei polynomial has a
nontrivial Frobenius-fixed defect, hence $h\ge1$.  This single missing direction
closes every nonsaturated conic-filling arc, independently of the saturated crown.

**Cheap discriminator.**  On the committed 234,188-instance audit, test only canonical
trace, norm, or Cartier defects of the Moore quotient.  Continue if one defect is
uniformly nonzero and survives extension fields.  Stop if it needs an ordinary global
Cartier divisor on the varying pencil: that carrier is already proved not to exist.

**Why distinct.**  This changes branch and theorem output.  It proves one missing
direction rather than classifying saturated coherent systems.

## 6. Attack order and non-routes

Recommended order:

1. Route L for one bounded prime-field pass;
2. Route Q if L has no bounded support or Cartier recurrence;
3. Route F if the transition degree is unbounded;
4. Route D only as a targeted nullity-two repair, not as a general determinant project;
5. Route R as the independent full-theorem lottery ticket.

The prefix-extension groupoid remains a useful scouting instrument for Route R, but it
is not itself a proof attack until a bounded intrinsic state catalogue exists.  The
missing-set kernel curves, raw entropy, degree-two Lloyd moments, local cumulants,
ordinary rank, and a further spectral bound remain closed routes.

## 7. EJ + TT closeout

**EJ.**  The free upgrade is the row-transition falsifier in Route Q.  It converts the
vague request for a four-point identity into three exact bounded outputs: least rational
degree, pole divisor, and cross-ratio defect.  Those can be extracted from the existing
angle matrices without a new enumeration.

**TT.**  The current obstruction is an order mismatch.  Pairwise structures
(resultant signs, spectra, bilinear tangent tensors) are saturated by negative
candidates, while the unused information is genuinely multi-neighbour: a cofactor,
a row-transition system, or higher Hadamard moments.  Future attacks should be rejected
unless their output retains one of those higher-order shapes.
