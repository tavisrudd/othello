# C909 — integral Hodge lattice versus divisor products for finite-etale graph quotients

Date: 2026-08-11

Status: bounded literature/math audit; no manuscript, PDF, mirror, Lean, or
commit edit

This note is restricted to complex abelian varieties isogenous to a power of
one non-CM elliptic curve, the integral `SL_2` invariant lattice in their
exterior cohomology, and the marked finite-etale graph presentation. It does
not audit Chow groups, non-etale slope classification, or C907. Four named
sources are used below, all at **partial** read depth; no citation-graph
closure was attempted.

## Verdict

The rational statement is classical, but the integral statement is a
different theorem.

Let `E` be non-CM and let `A` be isogenous to `E^g`. Then

\[
 \operatorname{End}^0(A)=M_g(\mathbf Q),
 \qquad \operatorname{Hg}(A)^{\rm der}=SL_2,
\]

on the rank-two elliptic factor. Consequently the rational Hodge ring is the
`SL_2`-invariant algebra in the exterior algebra of `H^1(A,Q)`, and it is
generated by divisor classes. Milne's Theorem 3.2 proves the corresponding
Lefschetz invariant theorem over a coefficient field; Abdulali records the
product-of-elliptic-curves consequence. Neither theorem identifies the
integral lattice.

For the integral lattice put

\[
 I_A^k:=H^{2k}(A,\mathbf Z)\cap H^{k,k}(A),\qquad
 P_A^k:=\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}^kNS(A)
       \longrightarrow H^{2k}(A,\mathbf Z)\bigr).
\]

The rational theorem only says `I_A^k tensor Q=P_A^k tensor Q`. The finite
cokernel

\[
 \Delta_A^k:=I_A^k/P_A^k
\tag{1}
\]

is an integral-lattice question. The C909 equal/arbitrary-depth graph notes
prove, under their marked finite-etale hypotheses,

\[
 \operatorname{PD}\langle NS(A)\rangle^k=P_A^k,
\tag{2}
\]

but (2) does **not** by itself prove `Delta_A^k=0`. It says that divided
powers of the named divisor lattice have no extra factorial defect; it does
not say that every integral `SL_2` invariant is such a divided power.

The bounded audit found no exact predecessor for the stronger, marked
integral equality `I_A^k=P_A^k` for all degrees under finite-etale graph
gluing, and no in-scope counterexample. The smallest possible obstruction is

\[
 \Delta_A^2=I_A^2/P_A^2
 \subset H^4(A,\mathbf Z),
\tag{3}
\]

because degree two is exactly `NS(A)`. For dimension two the degree-four
group is top degree and the principal polarization already supplies the
primitive generator; the first nontrivial test is therefore dimension at
least three, codimension two. A dyadic class is the first factorial-risk
candidate, but finite-etale rank-one identities introduce no forced factor of
two.

## Exact algebraic formulation of the missing comparison

Write `V=H^1(E,Q)` with its standard `SL_2` action and let `L=H^1(A,Z)` be
the integral Hodge lattice inside `V^{\oplus g}`. Define

\[
 I^k(L)=\bigl(\Lambda^{2k}L\otimes Q\bigr)^{SL_2}\cap\Lambda^{2k}L,
\]

and let `N(L)=I^1(L)=NS(A)` under the Lefschetz `(1,1)` identification.
The required integral invariant-theory lemma is the surjectivity of

\[
 \Gamma^kN(L)\longrightarrow I^k(L),
\tag{4}
\]

or, after C909's square-zero/rank-one argument,

\[
 P_A^k\longrightarrow I^k(L).
\tag{5}
\]

Over `Q`, (4) is the usual first-fundamental-theorem/Schur-module statement.
Over `Z`, invariants need not be an exact functor under arbitrary lattice
changes, so rational generation does not imply (4). The missing C909 lemma
must control the integral `SL_2` invariant lattice after the graph-overlattice
change, not merely the symmetric degree-two coefficient lattice.

After an unramified splitting of a finite-etale graph, the cohomology lattice
is a sum of rank-two elliptic lattices indexed by spectral blocks. Let
`q_ij` denote the rational contraction/divisor invariant between blocks `i`
and `j`. The graph calculation gives coefficient ideals

\[
 q_{ii}:p^{a_i},qquad q_{ij}:p^{e_{ij}}\quad(i\ne j),
\tag{6}
\]

with `e_ij` as in the arbitrary-depth graph theorem. The C909 DVR criterion
and the finite-etale inequalities show that `N(L)` is generated by rank-one
forms, so every divided power of an element of `N(L)` lies in `P_A^k`.
To conclude (4), one still needs an integral straightening statement saying
that the weighted `SL_2` invariant lattice is generated by the corresponding
weighted standard monomials in the `q_ij` (with divided powers where a
repeated contraction occurs). This is not supplied by the rank-one criterion
alone and no source was found that states it in this weighted graph form.

## Smallest obstruction and an exact test

For each bad prime `p`, define the local defect

\[
 \Delta^k_{A,p}:=
 \frac{I^k(L_p)}{P_A^k\otimes\mathbf Z_p}.
\tag{7}
\]

The first test is `k=2`. After finite unramified splitting, compute the
weighted degree-four invariant module and compare it with products of the
weighted degree-two generators:

\[
 \operatorname{coker}\!left(
 \operatorname{Sym}^2 N(L_p)
   \longrightarrow
   \bigl(\Lambda^4L_p\otimes\mathbf Q_p\bigr)^{SL_2}
          \cap\Lambda^4L_p\right).
\tag{8}
\]

Any nonzero `p`-primary Smith factor in (8) is an actual integral Hodge
obstruction, not a failure of rational Hodge generation. For an equal-depth
finite-etale split chart, the candidate basis terms are the usual contractions
`q_ii q_jj`, `q_ii q_jk`, and four-block matchings, with weights prescribed by
(6). The signed rank-one identity handles the repeated-contraction terms
without division by two. Thus the finite-etale theorem strongly suggests
that (8) is zero, but that conclusion requires the weighted straightening
lemma, not only equation (2).

The generic rank-one warning is real outside the C909 branch: a principally
polarized abelian surface with `NS=Z Theta` has
`Theta^2/2=[pt]` in top integral cohomology while the ordinary product image
in degree four is `2Z[pt]`. This shows why rational divisor generation and
ordinary integral products are logically distinct. It is not an example in
the non-CM elliptic-power finite-etale locus, whose larger NS lattice is the
reason C909 can avoid this defect.

## What the literature actually supplies

### Milne: Lefschetz classes are a rational/coefficient-field theorem

Milne defines Lefschetz classes as the `Q`-algebra generated by divisor
classes. Theorem 3.2 states that the invariant cohomology algebra fixed by
the Lefschetz group is generated by degree-two divisor classes, and the
application to Hodge classes is phrased in Betti cohomology over `Q`.
The source never asserts an integral invariant-lattice equality. In the
notation above it identifies the rational Lefschetz subalgebra, not the
cokernel (1).

### Abdulali: product elliptic curves have divisor-generated rational Hodge ring

Theorem 6.1 states that for a product of elliptic curves the group-theoretic,
arithmetic, and rational Hodge filtrations agree and recalls that its Hodge
ring is generated by divisors (Tate's result, with detailed proofs cited
there). The statement is over `Q`; it is not a theorem that the integral
Hodge lattice is the ordinary integral divisor-product lattice after an
isogeny quotient.

### Beckmann--de Gaay Fortman: integral one-cycle classes are a different test

Their Theorem 1.1 characterizes the integral Hodge conjecture for one-cycles
through integral Fourier/Poincare classes, and Theorem 1.3 gives density
results involving prime-power isogenies to products of elliptic curves or
Jacobians. This is relevant evidence that integral isogeny denominators are
genuine, but it neither computes (8) nor identifies all integral Hodge classes
with divisor products for the C909 graph quotient.

### Jordan--Keeton--Poonen--Rains--Shepherd-Barron--Tate: integral module
classification is not an integral Hodge-ring theorem

Their categorical description of abelian varieties isogenous to a power of an
elliptic curve classifies torsion-free modules and kernel subgroups. It is
useful for the underlying isogeny class, but it does not calculate the
`SL_2`-fixed sublattice in exterior cohomology or prove (4).

## Safe conclusion for C909

Use the following hierarchy.

1. **Established/source-backed:** rational Hodge classes on a non-CM
   elliptic power, and on its rational isogeny class, are divisor-generated.

2. **Established in the marked C909 theorem:** for finite-etale graph data,
   the named integral NS lattice has no divided-power factorial defect:
   `PD<NS>=ordinary products` in the stated cohomological presentation.

3. **Not yet established by those results:** the full integral Hodge lattice
   equals the ordinary divisor-product lattice. The exact missing assertion
   is the weighted integral invariant comparison (4), with (8) its smallest
   degree-two-product test.

4. **No bounded in-scope counterexample found:** finite-etale splitting and
   the C909 rank-one inequalities are compatible with vanishing of (8), but
   this is evidence for a theorem, not its proof.

The safe manuscript/handoff sentence is therefore:

> The finite-etale graph argument proves ordinary integral products for the
> divided-power envelope of the marked Néron--Severi lattice. Rational Hodge
> generation by divisors is classical for non-CM elliptic powers. Equality
> with the full integral Hodge lattice requires a separate weighted integral
> `SL_2`-invariant-lattice lemma and is not claimed here.

## Mystery ledger / next proof gate

* **Settled:** all cited Hodge-ring predecessors are rational/coefficient-
  field statements; none closes the integral lattice gap.
* **Settled:** the first possible strict quotient is the codimension-two
  defect (8); degree two is tautological and surface top degree is too small
  to test the issue.
* **Open:** prove the weighted integral `SL_2` straightening lemma for the
  finite-etale graph lattices, or exhibit a nonzero Smith factor in (8).
* **Open:** if (8) vanishes, verify the analogous weighted comparison in all
  degrees before promoting `PD(NS)=P` to `I_A=P`.
