## Summary and contribution

The manuscript asks which local recovery information remains sufficient when coded recovery is composed, subjected to failures, or allocated under shared helper capacities. It begins with scalar linear recovery and then parameterizes simultaneous recovery by a nonzero subspace of target functionals. The canonical helper pair is the puncturing and shortening of the inner dual. Its relative generalized Hamming weights give the minimum union of helpers when the recovered subspace is allowed to vary at fixed dimension.

The main finite formula (`thm:main`, Theorem 1.1, p. 2; proved in `thm:ungated-ranked-confinement`, Theorem 4.1) partitions global recovery systems according to their induced block-functional labels. Nonzero compatible labels permit independent minimization in the block fibres. The zero-label sector consists of a local normalized recovery with an external inner-dual perturbation, whose minimum additional cost is the inner-dual distance. This gives an exact first cost of leaving a designated inner block, including attaining coefficient witnesses.

The strongest operational statement is `thm:minimal-support-confinement`, Theorem 4.2, p. 12. A lower bound on the least outer-dual weight touching the target block forces the target-block part of every bounded recovery to recover the target by itself. Consequently all bounded inclusion-minimal supports remain local, even when equations with dispensable external terms exist. This transfers availability events pointwise, hence probabilities under arbitrary global laws with the same local marginal, as well as the specified fractional and integral support-allocation regions.

The remaining development identifies exact interfaces for additional questions: quotient-labelled lifting and min--sum composition; support antichains for future failure and price queries; finite tests for numerical contextual equivalence at a fixed original leaf boundary; and a fixed-query dynamic program whose states are affine boundary syndromes. Examples separate unlabelled recovery data from encoder alignment, relative-weight minima from reliability, and an abstract helper pair from its ambient coefficient presentation. Distance collapse, MDS bounds, and a projective-simplex family supply concrete consequences.

## Significance and scope

The mathematical value lies in specifying exactly what is being transported and exactly what information suffices. The proof of the escape formula is short once the correct fibres are written down, but the target normalization and the separate nonzero-kernel cost are essential. Equally useful is the recognition that equation escape and a new usable repair are different events. The full-outer-code running example makes this distinction tangible: equation escape costs three helpers, while no new external minimal support appears at any radius.

The result is naturally a contribution to finite scalar recovery and its exact compositional optimization. It does not solve bandwidth-optimal array repair, arbitrary nonlinear reconstruction, general integral scheduling, or efficient compilation for all codes. The text normally observes these boundaries. In particular, the connected adjacent-pair example in Section 5.4 proves an economical supplied-interface family, not a general tractability result; its exponential comparison is expressly against ambient-state enumeration.

The novelty framing in Section 5.7 is substantially more informative than merely calling the formulas new. The partial source checks confirm that executable dual-word recovery, prescribed-tuple union-support costs, min--sum elimination, and linear cut-state compression have direct predecessors. The recovery-specific claims should therefore continue to be framed around exact reverse confinement, normalization, resource-sensitive interfaces, and the stated finite separator bounds. Whether every such specialized statement is absent from the wider literature remains outside this read's coverage. No broad absence claim is made here.

## Correctness assessment

### Core definitions and confinement

The distinction between target-coordinate dimension and recovered functional dimension is handled correctly. A normalized helper map is injective because its image under the helper generator map is minus the identity on the requested space. This proves the support-preserving correspondence in `thm:relative-weight-recovery`. Choosing a complement to the lower code justifies minimizing over disjoint subspaces without changing the relative-weight minimum. Dependent target columns are not silently counted as independent information.

The use of the represented encoder is also necessary and correctly retained. The trace argument identifying the functional dual with the extension-field dual uses extension-field linearity of the outer code, not merely base-field linearity. Nonzero target projection then means surjective projection. This rules out nonzero functional-dual words supported only at the target block and is the exact hypothesis needed to make every nonzero-label lift nonconfined.

In the zero sector, an external map has nonzero image in the inner dual and therefore costs at least its minimum distance. A rank-one map onto a minimum word attains this cost, independently of the local recovery. The block supports are disjoint, so their costs add without an overlap assumption. These observations establish both inequalities in the formula, including attainment. The rank-one restriction argument in `cor:all-rank-bottleneck` preserves nonconfinement by choosing a parameter on which an external map is nonzero; it does not incorrectly infer confinement merely from minimum cardinalities.

The proof of Theorem 4.2 has the correct direction and quantifiers. If the target label were nonzero on one parameter, the induced outer-dual word would touch at most the target block plus one block per contacted helper. Its weight would be at most r+1. Once the target label is zero, deleting external coefficients gives a local recovery with the same normalization. Minimal-support equality follows in both directions. Taking expectations after equality of indicators justifies the arbitrary-law assertion; independence is unnecessary.

### Composition, algorithms, and certificates

`thm:quotient-lifting-composition` minimizes over genuine quotient lifts and uses disjoint physical coordinate sets. Surjectivity of finite-dimensional quotient maps supplies linear sections and attainment. The target-normalized composition formula in `prop:prescribed-coset-composition` correctly separates the target contribution U from the total label X, so helpers must realize X−U. Trace transitivity supplies the field-tower identification. Storing a single numerical minimum is rightly distinguished from storing one witness or every alternative.

The support-antichain substitution is valid because replacement by a contained support occurs within the same functional fibre. It therefore preserves compatibility. Nonnegative prices are important: they justify ignoring strict supersets. The price/availability equivalence handles both the empty family and the family containing the empty support. Its use of zero/one prices and deterministic survival vectors proves an information equivalence, not an efficient conversion procedure.

The sequential dynamic program has a clear invariant and charges transitions and witness storage separately. In `prop:boundary-width-compilation`, Proposition 5.3, p. 20, the cut states are an affine compatibility space, not an assertion that every state is locally realizable. The proof explicitly preserves this distinction with infinite costs. The sibling-plus-outside-completion argument is sufficient for the converse invariant at an internal node. The quoted complexity counts pair combinations, with local scans, polynomial arithmetic, bit costs, budgets, and output alternatives charged separately.

The tree-potential certificate has the right inequality direction: induction makes a potential a lower bound on every feasible subtree realization. A root witness attaining it proves optimality. Completeness of local choices and transitions remains an explicit requirement. Likewise, absence of improving columns certifies the full fractional LP only when the pricing oracle ranges over the full admitted repair family. The manuscript does not confuse that certificate with an integral optimum.

### Secondary results and examples

The encoder-alignment example in `prop:functional-label-separation` is effective and its displayed binary coset costs agree with the three nonzero column labels. The two representations have the same image code but different labelled access to a fixed outer relation, precisely as claimed. The three-level priced repair example supplies both compatible top branches and reconstructs the changed equation after failure.

The reliability-separation proof supplies a general rational construction and reduction to a common finite prime field, independently of the two displayed numerical matrices. The generic lifting argument has a genuine nonzero cofactor for each helper quadruple because its projected vectors span dimension three. The direct-sum extension to every quotient rank uses a sound lower bound: a t-dimensional quotient image has at least t active one-dimensional components, each incurring its component cost. The polynomial difference factors as s^6(1−s)^3.

The Singer-cycle construction uses disjointness of the weight-one projective label set from its multiplier translate. Hence a nonzero outer label cannot be realized with one coefficient in every block, while the zero sector is too costly. This gives the stated nonvacuous improvement over the distance-only sufficient condition.

The MDS staircase follows from the uniform-column rank count. The best-target generalized-weight statement separately treats identity-normalized parameter spaces when target columns are dependent; this avoids losing target-only relations. The projective-simplex reliability law clearly allows the recovered subspace to depend on availability. Its failure-rank criterion, subspace-lattice inversion, and endpoint counts are consistent with that event.

Finally, `prop:rank-bounded-outer-tests`, Theorem A.3, pp. 40–41, correctly uses the extension-field span of at most t image vectors. In the witness clause it retains blocks with nonzero coefficient maps, not only nonzero functional labels, which is essential for zero-label perturbations. Restricting the dual to a subspace cannot improve the competing inner problem, so the small separating-context argument is valid. Contextual closure is stated only for the original leaf confinement region, not for a newly enlarged macroblock boundary.

## Exposition and organization

The opening example is unusually effective: one symbol with three minimal repairs simultaneously demonstrates why cardinality, supports, and supplied functionals are different data. The explanation immediately after Theorem 1.1 states why its two sectors exist. The three-level example is also worth retaining in full; it translates abstract labels into physical coefficients and makes reuse under failure credible.

The paper is nevertheless broad for its central mechanism. Its 43 pages include an extensive opening overview, several repeated descriptions of the same two sectors, a substantial algorithm section, three families of separation/application results, and a contextual appendix. The central theorem is easy to locate on page 2, but a first-time reader may still be uncertain which subsequent results carry the main conceptual burden. Section 4.2's operational theorem deserves at least equal narrative prominence with the equation formula.

The rendered pages sampled are legible, including theorem statements and the software pipeline. Figure 1's dashed arrows crowd the intervening boxes, and its source object could be named more precisely as discussed below. The software discussion is generally restrained about what checking establishes, although its repeated presence in the abstract, introduction, Section 5, and conclusion gives it more rhetorical weight than the actual verification scope supports.

## Major comments: highest-priority revisions

These are substantive presentation requests, not demonstrated theorem failures.

1. **Make the operational theorem the other main result in the reader's hierarchy.** In the introduction and Section 4, explicitly pair exact equation escape with the minimal-support transfer theorem, distinguishing the finite exact equation threshold from the sufficient target-touching condition for operational transfer. The present text proves this distinction correctly. A compact two-row comparison of the retained object, hypothesis, and consequence would reduce the repeated prose and make the stronger operational applicability easier to find. Do not describe the distance condition as an exact characterization of operational escape; the text correctly leaves the exact operational threshold epsilon separate.

2. **Make the normalization/interface convention explicit at the field-language change.** The early generator columns live in the dual of a chosen message coordinate space, while later restriction maps use trace coefficients. Add one sentence stating the identification used to transport T and its normalization between these descriptions. Also make explicit that fixing the normalization convention G_P alpha = id does not fix a particular section alpha unless a particular coefficient witness is being discussed. I can reconcile the definitions, and the proofs use them consistently, but this bridge is presently left for the reader to reconstruct.

3. **Keep the implementation claims tied to an inspectable contract.** Section 10 correctly limits the Lean coverage to the associated-pair exact sequence, and correctly says that Ergodis checks specified lowered presentations. Preserve that precision. A short explicit cross-reference from each asserted implemented capability in Section 5.6 to its public pinned interface/checker documentation would improve reviewability. The theorem proofs do not need software execution; conversely, those proofs alone do not establish that a particular implementation performs sound lowering, complete pricing, or all described state reductions. This report has not independently established those implementation facts. No additional benchmark campaign or expanded Lean formalization is requested as a condition of mathematical acceptance.

4. **Figure 1, p. 3: specify the ambient collection.** The top box says “recovery equations” with induced block functionals. The verification section's statement that every mathematical result has a human proof is reasonable for the theorem statements, but can sound broader than the evidence for every numerical assertion. “Every theorem has a human proof; the displayed finite instances additionally have the documented arithmetic checks” would be more exact.

6. **Optional compression.** The conclusion can focus on the operational distinction and the open interface-construction question. Repeating the software embodiment there adds little after Sections 5 and 10. Similarly, the secondary contextual quotient is correctly placed in an appendix; its introduction paragraph can be shorter without losing a load-bearing hypothesis.

## Final editorial judgment

The main theorem is correct on this examination, and the operational confinement theorem gives the paper a clear reason to exist beyond a repackaging of a min--sum identity. The examples demonstrate the losses in coarser invariants rather than merely asserting that more information is useful. The algorithms have appropriately qualified complexity and certificate claims. Acceptance after minor revision is therefore justified in the stated subject scope. The listed citation and roadmap errors should be corrected, and the interface and contribution hierarchy should be clarified, without adding new theorem requirements or implying that this one review completes any broader release gate.
