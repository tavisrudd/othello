Rains recovered the three Pauli axes from a diagonal correlation tensor,
and Van den Nest, Dehaene, and De Moor used this mechanism in a
minimal-support LU-to-LC criterion for qubit stabilizer states
\cite[Theorem~13]{Rains1999}
\cite[Theorem~1 and Corollary~1]{VanDenNestDehaeneDeMoor2005}.
Corollary~\ref{cor:recognition-generation} is a criterion of the same kind
at every local dimension.  It asks that both states have diagonal marginals
on a family of party sets through each party, and that the Weyl labels
recovered there generate the local label group; no stabilizer, CSS, or
linearity hypothesis enters, the states are arbitrary, and the coefficients
on the marginals are unconstrained.
For fully entangled qubit stabilizer states on at least three parties it
unifies their theorem rather than strengthening it.  Their hypothesis, that
all three Pauli matrices occur at each qubit inside the subgroup generated
by minimal elements, is our generation hypothesis: the projection at a qubit
of that subgroup is generated by the labels our marginals recover there.
Their two proof branches are the two index sizes available at \(q=2\), the
four-element case being Rains's theorem and the two-element case being
Proposition~\ref{prop:partial-weyl-marginal} at \(N=2\); full entanglement
on at least three parties is what excludes a two-party minimal support,
leaving the three parties the axis argument needs.
What is new is the move away from that dimension.  Their Lemma~1 finds only
two possible sizes for the label group of a minimal support, and this is not
a feature of the mathematics: that group embeds in the local label group
under the coordinate projection at every party of the support, so
intermediate subgroups occur as soon as the local dimension exceeds two.
Those intermediate sizes, which their argument cannot see and which
Proposition~\ref{prop:full-weyl-marginal} also misses, are what a CSS state
typically presents: a minimum-weight \(X\)-support carrying no \(Z\)-word
contributes a line of \(q\) labels.
Proposition~\ref{prop:stabilizer-ame-support} is what verifies the
hypothesis for stabilizer AME states at every prime power.
Only the additive structure of the labels is used, so the criterion applies
verbatim to the integer-modulus Weyl system on \(\mathbb C^d\) for arbitrary
\(d\), a setting with no field and no arc geometry.  It is not vacuous
there, and it does not prove too much.  In that system Wong and Jiang
exhibit a three-party stabilizer state at \(d=9\) that is LU but not LC
equivalent to the GHZ state \cite{WongJiang2025}; computing from the state
itself, its minimal supports are the three two-party sets, each carrying
nine labels, and each projects at every party into the index-nine subgroup
\(3\mathbb Z_9\times3\mathbb Z_9\), so the recovered labels do not generate
and the criterion correctly does not apply.
The linear algebra behind the axis recovery is classical
\cite{Harshman1970,Kruskal1977}, and the general machinery of constraining
local unitaries by tensor-decomposition uniqueness applied to a coefficient
tensor in a discrete operator basis is due to Chang and Jing
\cite{ChangJing2022}.  They treat generic states, whose Pauli coefficient
tensors are not diagonal in the operator basis, so uniqueness there pins a
rotation up to scaling and yields invariants separating LU classes; they
draw no Clifford or stabilizer conclusion.  At \(q=2\) that conclusion for
stabilizer states is Van den Nest, Dehaene, and De Moor's, and above \(q=2\)
we have not located it anywhere.
