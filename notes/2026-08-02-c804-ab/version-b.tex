Rains recovered the three Pauli axes from a diagonal correlation tensor,
and Van den Nest, Dehaene, and De Moor used this mechanism in a
minimal-support LU-to-LC criterion for qubit stabilizer states
\cite[Theorem~13]{Rains1999}
\cite[Theorem~1 and Corollary~1]{VanDenNestDehaeneDeMoor2005}.
Corollary~\ref{cor:recognition-generation} is a criterion of the same kind
at every local dimension.  It asks that the Weyl labels recovered from the
diagonal marginals through a party generate the local label group; no
stabilizer, CSS, or linearity hypothesis enters, the states are arbitrary,
and the coefficients on the marginals are unconstrained.
For fully entangled qubit stabilizer states it unifies their theorem rather
than strengthening it.  Their hypothesis, that all three Pauli matrices
occur at each qubit inside the subgroup generated by minimal elements, is
our generation hypothesis: the projection at a qubit of that subgroup is
generated by the labels our marginals recover there.  Their two proof
branches are the two index sizes available at \(q=2\), the four-element case
being Rains's theorem and the two-element case being
Proposition~\ref{prop:partial-weyl-marginal} at \(N=2\); full entanglement
is what puts at least four parties under a four-element support, as the
axis argument requires.
What is new is the move away from that dimension.  Their Lemma~1 finds only
two possible sizes for the label group of a minimal support, and this is not
a feature of the mathematics: that group embeds in the local label group
under the coordinate projection at every party of the support, so its size
is unrestricted once the local dimension exceeds two.  The intermediate
sizes their argument cannot see, and which
Proposition~\ref{prop:full-weyl-marginal} also misses, are the generic case
for CSS states.  Proposition~\ref{prop:stabilizer-ame-support} is what
verifies the hypothesis for stabilizer AME states at every prime power.
Only the additive structure of the labels is used, so the criterion applies
verbatim to the integer-modulus Weyl system on \(\mathbb C^d\) for arbitrary
\(d\), a setting with no field and no arc geometry.  It is not vacuous
there, and it does not prove too much.  In that system Wong and Jiang
exhibit a three-party stabilizer state at \(d=9\) that is LU but not LC
equivalent to the GHZ state \cite{WongJiang2025}; its minimal supports are
the three two-party sets, each carrying nine labels, and each projects at
every party into the index-nine subgroup \(3\mathbb Z_9\times3\mathbb Z_9\), so the
recovered labels do not generate and the criterion correctly does not apply.
The linear algebra behind the axis recovery is classical
\cite{Harshman1970,Kruskal1977}, and constraining local unitaries by
tensor-decomposition uniqueness applied to a coefficient tensor in a
discrete operator basis is due to Chang and Jing \cite{ChangJing2022}.  Their
setting is the qubit one, where that method cannot give the conclusion drawn
here: every single-qubit unitary preserves the span of the Pauli operators,
so uniqueness yields invariants separating LU classes rather than a
restriction on the local factors.  Above local dimension two a local unitary
need not preserve the Weyl span at all, and it is there that recovering the
axes becomes a Clifford condition on the factor.
