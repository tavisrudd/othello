Rains recovered the three Pauli axes from a diagonal correlation tensor,
and Van den Nest, Dehaene, and De Moor used this mechanism in a
minimal-support LU-to-LC criterion for qubit stabilizer states
\cite[Theorem~13]{Rains1999}
\cite[Theorem~1 and Corollary~1]{VanDenNestDehaeneDeMoor2005}.
Corollary~\ref{cor:recognition-generation} is a criterion of the same kind
at every local dimension, prime power or not.  It asks that the Weyl
labels recovered from the diagonal marginals through a party generate the
local label group; no stabilizer, CSS, or linearity hypothesis enters, the
states are arbitrary, and the coefficients on the marginals are
unconstrained.
At local dimension two it unifies rather than strengthens: their
four-element branch is Rains's theorem, their two-element branch is the
\(N=2\) case of Proposition~\ref{prop:partial-weyl-marginal}, and the step
that closes their proof is the two-label case of the generation criterion.
What is new is the move away from that dimension.  Their Lemma~1 finds only
two possible sizes for the label group of a minimal support, and this is
not a feature of the mathematics: the group embeds in the local label group
under the coordinate projection at every party of the support, so its size
is unrestricted once the local dimension exceeds two.  The intermediate
sizes their argument cannot see, and which our full-Weyl marginal
proposition also misses, are the generic case for CSS states.
Proposition~\ref{prop:stabilizer-ame-support} is what verifies the
hypothesis for stabilizer AME states at every prime power.
The criterion is not vacuous above local dimension two and does not prove
too much.  Wong and Jiang exhibit a three-party stabilizer state at local
dimension nine that is LU but not LC equivalent to the GHZ state
\cite{WongJiang2025}; its minimal supports have order nine and project at
each party into the three-torsion subgroup, so the recovered labels
generate a subgroup of index nine and the criterion correctly does not
apply.
The linear algebra behind the axis recovery is classical, and the use of
tensor-decomposition uniqueness to constrain local unitaries through a
coefficient tensor in a discrete operator basis is due to Chang and Jing
\cite{Harshman1970,Kruskal1977,ChangJing2022}.  The conclusion drawn here,
that the local factors must permute the projective Weyl axes, has no
content at local dimension two, where every local unitary preserves the
Pauli span; it is a restriction on the local factors only above it.
