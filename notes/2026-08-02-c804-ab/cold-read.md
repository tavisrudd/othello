# Cold read: two candidate positioning paragraphs against Van den Nest–Dehaene–De Moor and Rains

Date: 2026-08-02. Referee-style read of two candidate versions of one introduction paragraph in
`papers/ame_lu`. Sources consulted: the manuscript's `sections/01-introduction.tex`,
`sections/03-lu-rigidity.tex`, `refs.bib`; Van den Nest, Dehaene and De Moor,
*Local unitary versus local Clifford equivalence of stabilizer states* (arXiv:quant-ph/0411115,
Phys. Rev. A 71, 062323); Rains, *Quantum codes of minimum distance two* (arXiv:quant-ph/9704043,
IEEE Trans. Inform. Theory 45, 266–271); Wong and Jiang, arXiv:2507.09416; Chang and Jing,
arXiv:2205.06422. Working notes under `notes/` were not read.

Throughout, "their Theorem 1" and "their Corollary 1" mean Van den Nest, Dehaene and De Moor's;
"the generation criterion" means Corollary REF-A of the manuscript's own numbering as supplied.

## 0. What the prior work actually says

Reproduced so the argument below is checkable.

- **Their Lemma 1.** For a minimal support `omega` of a stabilizer state, `A_omega` (the number of
  stabilizer elements with support exactly `omega`) is 1 or 3, and 3 forces `|omega|` even.
- **Their Lemma 2.** Two mixed states on 2m qubits, `m >= 2`, both local-Clifford equivalent to
  `rho[2m,2m-2,2]`, related by a product unitary, force that product unitary to be local Clifford.
  They state explicitly that this is "a variant of a result in Ref. [11]", and Ref. [11] is Rains,
  *Quantum codes of minimum distance two*. So the Rains attribution in both passages points at the
  right paper, and Rains's Theorem 13 is indeed the diagonal-correlation-tensor argument: he writes
  `4 P_Q - 1` as the vector `|00...> + (-1)^m |11...> + |22...>` in `(R^3)^{tensor 2m}` and asks
  which elements of `SO(3)^{tensor 2m}` fix it, concluding each factor is monomial. Rains's version
  is a one-state (automorphism) statement; their Lemma 2 is the two-state variant.
- **Their Theorem 1.** Let `psi` be a fully entangled n-qubit stabilizer state, `n >= 3`, such that
  `sigma_x`, `sigma_y`, `sigma_z` all occur on every qubit in `M(psi)`, the subgroup **generated by**
  the minimal elements. Then `LU(psi) = LC(psi)`, where `LU(psi)` is by their definition the set of
  **stabilizer** states locally-unitarily equivalent to `psi`.
- **Their Corollary 1(iv).** If there are minimal supports `omega_1, omega_2, ...` with
  `A_{omega_j} = 3` for each, together covering every qubit, then `LU(psi) = LC(psi)`.

The distinction that decides the second question: Theorem 1 asks that the three Paulis occur at each
qubit somewhere in the *group generated by* minimal elements, so they may be assembled from several
different minimal supports each contributing only one axis. Corollary 1(iv) asks that a *single*
minimal support through each qubit already carry all three. Corollary 1(iv) is strictly stronger as
a hypothesis, hence a weaker theorem.

## 1. Does the generation criterion have their Theorem 1, or only Corollary 1(iv), as its q=2 case?

**It has Theorem 1, exactly — not merely Corollary 1(iv).** The identification is clean and I could
not break it.

Projection is a group homomorphism, so the image of `M(psi)` at qubit `i` is the subgroup of `F_2^2`
generated by the qubit-`i` labels of the minimal elements. Minimal elements not through `i`
contribute 0. Therefore

> "sigma_x, sigma_y, sigma_z occur on qubit i in M(psi)" is equivalent to
> "the qubit-i labels carried by the minimal supports through i generate the full local label group
> F_2^2".

That is verbatim the generation criterion's hypothesis, with the family of partial-Weyl diagonal
marginals taken to be the minimal-support marginals. The supporting pieces all line up:

- The minimal-supports lemma (projection injective at every party of a minimal support) makes
  `rho_omega = 2^{-|omega|} sum_{M supported in omega} alpha_M M` partial-Weyl diagonal with
  `P = L(omega)`, `f_i = p_i`, all coefficients nonzero and `f_i(0) = 0`. Their Lemma 1 is the q=2
  shadow of this same injectivity: `L(omega)` embeds in `F_2^2`, whose subgroups have order 1, 2, 4,
  giving `A_omega` in {0, 1, 3}.
- Their `A_omega = 3` branch is `N = 4`, the full-Weyl case at q=2, and is closed by their Lemma 2,
  i.e. by Rains's mechanism.
- Their `A_omega = 1` branch is `N = 2`: `rho_omega` is proportional to `I + M_omega`, conjugation
  gives `U_1 M_1 U_1^dagger = ± M_1'` factorwise, which is the `N = 2` case of the partial-Weyl
  criterion and needs no size hypothesis on `omega`.
- Their closing step — `M_1 = sigma_x` from one minimal support, `N_1 = sigma_z` from another,
  therefore `U_1` Clifford — is the two-generator case of the recognition-group lemma.

So Passage One's structural reading of their proof is correct in every part. Passage Two's weaker
positioning is also correct, but for a different comparison: the **AME application** needs only
Corollary 1(iv), because Proposition REF-C hands each minimum support the whole order-`q^2` label
group at once, which at q=2 is all three Paulis on one support. Both statements can be true
simultaneously; they are about different objects (the criterion versus its AME instance).

### Both directions, checked

**Does the criterion supply everything their theorem assumes?** Almost, with one hypothesis that has
to be imported and that Passage One does not mention.

- *Second state.* Their Theorem 1 puts no condition on `psi'` beyond `psi'` being a stabilizer state
  — the rest follows from local-unitary invariance of `A_omega`. The criterion instead assumes both
  operators are partial-Weyl diagonal. In the stabilizer application this is free (`psi'` stabilizer
  plus invariance of `A_omega` gives it). In the "arbitrary states" generality that Passage One
  advertises, it is not free: it is a real structural assumption on the *second* state that nothing
  about the first state implies. Passage One's phrase "the states are arbitrary" glosses over this.
  Not false — the corollary's own hypothesis carries the marginal condition — but loose.
- *Full entanglement.* Their Theorem 1 uses full entanglement exactly once, to force `|omega| >= 4`
  in the `A_omega = 3` branch (a size-2 minimal support with `A = 3` gives a pure EPR marginal and a
  product state). The partial-Weyl criterion needs `r >= 3` when `N > 2`. This is the same content,
  but it is a hypothesis of the criterion rather than a consequence. So the criterion does **not**
  imply their Theorem 1 as a black box: to recover Theorem 1 one must reinstate their argument that
  full entanglement excludes `|omega| = 2` on the four-element supports. The exclusion is not
  cosmetic — for `psi = EPR ⊗ chi` the local factor on an EPR party genuinely need not be Clifford,
  since `U ⊗ conj(U)` fixes the EPR pair. Passage One says nothing about `r >= 3` or full
  entanglement anywhere; a referee will ask for that clause.

**Does their theorem conclude anything stronger on its own domain?** No. Their stated conclusion is
the orbit equality `LU(psi) = LC(psi)`; their proof establishes per-factor Cliffordness, which is
what the criterion concludes. Per-factor Cliffordness implies the orbit equality, and the reverse
inclusion is trivial. The criterion's conclusion is at least as strong, and matches what their proof
actually delivers.

**Verdict on "at local dimension two it unifies rather than strengthens".** Accurate, and if
anything slightly too modest. It is right that on qubit *stabilizer* states nothing is gained. It
understates in one direction the paragraph itself flags earlier: at q=2 the criterion also applies
to non-stabilizer states whose relevant marginals are partial-Weyl diagonal with unconstrained
nonzero coefficients, which Theorem 1 does not cover. Since the sentence is explicitly scoped to
"their" setting, I would not call this an error — it is a defensible, deliberately conservative
framing, and the right one for a referee's peace of mind.

## 2. Attacks on the mathematical claims

### Passage One

**(a) The Wong–Jiang example is in the wrong Pauli group. This is the serious defect.**

Wong and Jiang's `D = 9`, `N = 3` example is real: `S = <X1X2X3, X1^3X2^6, Z1Z2Z3, Z1^3Z2^6>` versus
the GHZ stabilizer `S' = <X1X2X3, Z1Z2^8, Z1Z3^8>`, not Clifford-connected but locally-unitarily
connected, both being products of two three-level GHZ states in different groupings. But their
qudit Pauli group is the **integer-modulus** group over `Z_9`, not the field Weyl group over `F_9`.
Their stated obstruction — "Clifford unitaries cannot alter the dimension of a subgroup of the Pauli
group", exhibited first for `D = 4` with `<X^2, Z^2>` — is precisely a `Z_{p^n}` phenomenon: it needs
elements of additive order 9 and a proper 3-torsion subgroup.

The manuscript's Weyl group is `W_v = X(a)Z(b)` with `v` in `F_q^2`. For `q = 9` that group is
elementary abelian of exponent 3: **every** nonzero element has additive order 3, so "the
three-torsion subgroup" of `F_9^2` is the whole group, of index 1, not index 9. Passage One's
sentence — "its minimal supports have order nine and project at each party into the three-torsion
subgroup, so the recovered labels generate a subgroup of index nine" — is arithmetic in `Z_9^2`
(where the 3-torsion subgroup `{0,3,6}^2` does have order 9 and index 9). Read in the paper's own
labelling it is false.

Worse for the rhetorical purpose: the `F_9` Weyl group on a 9-dimensional party is isomorphic
modulo phases to the two-qutrit Pauli group, so the `F_9` Clifford group on that party contains the
full two-qutrit local Clifford group, including the recoupling that turns one grouping of two
three-level GHZ states into the other. In the paper's framework the Wong–Jiang pair is therefore
plausibly local-Clifford equivalent, and so cannot serve as the example showing "the criterion is
not vacuous above local dimension two and does not prove too much". I have not carried out that
equivalence in detail and do not assert it as proved; what I do assert is that the sentence as
written computes in `Z_9` while the criterion lives in `F_9`, and that the gap must be closed
explicitly before the example can be cited for this purpose. As it stands this is the one claim in
either passage that a referee would return.

**(b) The final sentence is wrong.** "The conclusion drawn here, that the local factors must permute
the projective Weyl axes, has no content at local dimension two, where every local unitary preserves
the Pauli span; it is a restriction on the local factors only above it."

Permuting the projective Weyl axes *is* the Clifford condition at q=2, and the single-qubit Clifford
group is a finite proper subgroup of `PSU(2) = SO(3)`. It is an entirely nonvacuous restriction —
proving it is the whole content of Van den Nest, Dehaene and De Moor's Theorem 1, which the same
paragraph correctly says the criterion reproduces. The sentence conflates two different statements:
"preserves the span of the Weyl operators", which is vacuous at every `q` because the Weyl operators
are a basis of the full matrix algebra, and "permutes the projective Weyl axes", which is never
vacuous. As written the paragraph contradicts itself, and it hands a referee a reason to doubt the
author understands the q=2 case. Cut or rewrite.

**(c) "so its size is unrestricted once the local dimension exceeds two" overstates.** The label
group of a minimal support embeds in `F_q^2`, so its order is a subgroup order, not arbitrary; the
correct claim is that intermediate subgroups exist as soon as `q > 2`, which is all the argument
needs. Relatedly, "this is not a feature of the mathematics" is the wrong phrase for a fact that is
a feature of the mathematics of `q = 2` — the intended point, that the dichotomy is an artifact of
`F_2^2` having no intermediate subgroups rather than of minimality, is correct and should be said
that way.

**(d) Cross-reference slip.** "their two-element branch is the `N = 2` case of Proposition REF-B" is
inconsistent with Proposition REF-B being the *full*-Weyl criterion, where `N = q^2` is forced and
the maps `f_i` are bijections; the same paragraph later calls REF-B "our full-Weyl marginal
proposition". The `N = 2` case belongs to the partial-Weyl criterion. Fix the pointer.

**(e) Dependency, not an error, but load-bearing.** I grepped the manuscript: `sections/` currently
contains the diagonal-tensor axes lemma, the full-Weyl definition, Proposition
`prop:full-weyl-marginal`, Corollary `cor:full-weyl-cover` and Proposition
`prop:stabilizer-ame-support`. There is **no** partial-Weyl definition, no recognition-group lemma,
no generation criterion and no minimal-supports lemma anywhere under `sections/` or `supplement/`.
Every distinctive claim in Passage One rests on results the manuscript does not yet contain. If
they are added and correct, the paragraph is supported; if they are not, it is unsupported in full.

**(f) Attribution, checked and broadly sound.** Rains's Theorem 13 is correctly characterized and
is correctly the source their Lemma 2 credits. Chang and Jing, *Local Unitary Equivalence of Generic
Multi-qubits Based on the CP Decomposition*, does exactly what the passage says — it introduces
CANDECOMP/PARAFAC decomposition and Kruskal's uniqueness condition into the local-unitary
equivalence problem for the Pauli coefficient tensor. Two cautions. First, "due to Chang and Jing"
is a strong word given that Rains in 1999 and Van den Nest, Dehaene and De Moor in 2005 already
constrained local unitaries through a diagonal Pauli correlation tensor; what is due to Chang and
Jing is the explicit importation of the *general* tensor-uniqueness machinery, and the sentence
should say that. Second, `sections/03-lu-rigidity.tex` already attributes the lemma to Jennrich via
Harshman and to Kruskal, and cites Sidiropoulos–Bro and Kolda–Bader; Passage One's shorter citation
list should not silently drop those. Note also that Chang and Jing is not currently cited in
`sections/`, though the bib entry exists.

**(g) One scope question a referee will raise.** Passage One concedes that Proposition REF-C
verifies the hypothesis for stabilizer AME states, and Proposition REF-C delivers the *full* local
label group at each party — the full-Weyl case. So the partial-Weyl generalization is never needed
for this paper's own theorem; it exists to position against prior work. That is a legitimate thing
to do, but the paragraph should not leave the reader thinking the generality is used.

### Passage Two

**(a) Wrong cross-reference, and it is in the live manuscript.** `sections/01-introduction.tex`
line 113 reads "For \(q=2\), Proposition~\ref{prop:full-weyl-marginal} supplies all three Pauli axes
on each minimum support". The full-Weyl marginal criterion concludes that local factors are
Clifford; it does not supply Pauli axes on a support. The proposition that does is
`prop:stabilizer-ame-support` (REF-C), whose `|L(A)| = q^2` with bijective coordinate projections
gives, at `q = 2`, exactly the three Paulis at each party of each minimum support. This is a
straightforward citation error to fix regardless of which paragraph survives.

**(b) The concession is mathematically correct but nearly empty, and the passage does not say so.**
With REF-C substituted for REF-B, the reasoning is right: each minimum support of a stabilizer
AME(2m,2) state has `A_omega = 3`, these supports cover all parties, the state is fully entangled
and `n = 2m >= 4`, so Corollary 1(iv) — and a fortiori Theorem 1 — applies. But AME(n,2) states
exist only for `n = 2, 3, 5, 6`, so under `m >= 2` the entire q=2 case of Theorem REF-D is the
single family AME(6,2). Conceding "already covered by their result" is therefore conceding one
state's worth of ground, and a reader who does not know the nonexistence results will badly
overestimate what was given away. If this framing is kept, say which case it is.

**(c) No overclaim, no miscredit otherwise.** "The new scope here is the uniform prime-power theorem
for arbitrary additive stabilizers, together with the minimum-support atlas and its exact
symmetry-group sequence" is supported by the theorems as stated. The shared Rains and Van den Nest
sentence is accurate in both passages.

## 3. Which passage presents the stronger contribution

**Passage One, decisively, on comprehension and position — but only after the two defects in §2(a)
and §2(b) are repaired, and only if the results it cites are actually added to the manuscript.**

What a referee takes away from Passage One: the paper has isolated the mechanism behind the qubit
result, generalized it to every local dimension and to states with no stabilizer, CSS or linearity
structure, identified precisely why the prior work's minimal-support dichotomy is an artifact of
`F_2^2` having no intermediate subgroups, named the regime the generalization is for (intermediate
label-group sizes, the generic CSS situation), and delimited the criterion with an example where it
correctly fails. It also states plainly that at q=2 nothing is gained. That combination — a strictly
larger theorem, an exact account of how the known case sits inside it, and a self-imposed limit — is
what a strong positioning paragraph looks like, and the exactness is verified: the q=2 stabilizer
case is Theorem 1 itself, not the weaker Corollary 1(iv).

What a referee takes away from Passage Two: the authors know the prior work covers their q=2 case
and claim novelty only in the prime-power generality plus the atlas and symmetry sequence. It is
safe and short. Its cost is that it explains nothing about the mechanism, so a referee cannot tell
whether the prime-power extension is routine or substantial, and it volunteers a concession that is
much larger-sounding than the single AME(6,2) case it actually covers. It also contains a live
citation error.

Passage One is not safe as written. Its Wong–Jiang paragraph computes in the wrong group and its
final sentence asserts something false about q=2. A referee who catches either will discount the
rest. The right outcome is Passage One's position with those two sentences fixed, the `r >= 3` /
full-entanglement clause added where it claims to contain Theorem 1, "the states are arbitrary"
softened to name the marginal hypothesis on both states, and the REF-B pointer corrected — with
Passage Two's REF-C fix applied in either case.

## 4. What I could not verify

- That the Wong–Jiang pair is or is not local-Clifford equivalent in the `F_9` Weyl framework. I
  showed the passage's stated reason is `Z_9` arithmetic that fails in `F_9^2`; I did not compute
  the `F_9` answer.
- That intermediate label-group sizes are "the generic case for CSS states". The reasoning is
  plausible — a minimal support of a CSS code typically carries a single `F_q`-line of codewords, so
  `N = q`, which is intermediate exactly when `q > 2` — but I did not verify genericity.
- Proposition REF-C and Theorem REF-D themselves; I read REF-C's statement and the start of its
  proof in `sections/03-lu-rigidity.tex` and took them as given, as instructed.
