# Synthesis

## Earliest clear thesis

The thesis was first inferable in the abstract (0002), but not comfortably absorbable there. It became genuinely clear at 0004–0007: scalar minima discard the functional label an outer code can force, so exact concatenation needs labelled costs; support-overlap data form a separate branch for reliability. The decisive intuitive explanation was 0018’s zero/nonzero-sector mechanism, and 0019 supplied the concrete controlled separation that should appear earlier. By 0052–0054, after the classical nested-pair layer had been built, the motivation and two-stage plan were fully clear.

## Strongest exposition sequences

- 0027–0032: recovery equations, normalized coefficients, exact supports, reliability, and multi-target helper unions are introduced concretely and in a useful order.
- 0034–0048: the exact sequence, canonical puncture/shorten pair, recovery interpretation of RGHWs/RDLPs, and proofs form a coherent classical foundation. Definitions occasionally arrive later than the introduction needs, but locally this sequence is strong.
- 0054 and 0058–0063: the two-sector roadmap, central formula, and proof classification are conceptually clean; the zero-sector rank-one perturbation is especially illuminating.
- 0080–0087: projective line probes and rank-bounded outer tests have short, separating constructions and readable proofs once the notation is in place.
- 0088–0090: the all-rank bottleneck has an excellent one-line idea—restrict a nonconfined system to a witnessing line.
- 0131–0135: the exact dynamic program, invariant, complexity, and witness backtracking are among the most accessible technical passages.
- 0172–0175: the bounded service-rate region and its transfer are self-contained and show cleanly how the support bijection is used.
- 0182–0186: the information-level table and projective reliability interpretation give a strong unifying example, though the Möbius formula needs more notation support.
- 0192–0195: the verification boundary is precise, transparent, and appropriately scoped.
- 0197–0202: the conclusion reconstructs the paper’s logic well and draws honest boundaries around both software evidence and future extensions.

## Weakest exposition sequences

- 0002 and 0012–0017: the abstract/main-results tour introduces too many bespoke terms before a concrete recovery/block example. The plain claims are present but buried under “prescribed coset,” “complete outer functional dual,” “nonconfined,” “contextual quotient,” and witness-state terminology.
- 0055–0058: one oversized setup moves through represented codes, field extensions, trace duality, functional duals, confinement, and two fibre-cost functions. This should be decomposed into definitions/lemmas with a map diagram and a notation dictionary.
- 0067–0077, especially 0072–0073: the composition theorem changes notation and bundles ordinary closure, envelopes, target-normalized closure, dual distance, associativity, and tower propagation. The theorem at 0073 is the principal blocker for cold absorption; split it and introduce the roles of $U$, $X$, and $X-U$ before the formula.
- 0070–0071: the key label-separation example is too compressed to audit. A label-by-label cost table would turn it into the paper’s best motivating example.
- 0113–0117: the rank-one reliability separation moves from digit-coded hyperedges to projective arrangements, generic lifts, reduction modulo a prime, and code distance without diagrams or intermediate lemmas.
- 0124–0126: the ambient-presentation separation needs an explicit table of target coefficients, helper words, and total dual weights, plus a clear reminder of the outer-distance regime.
- 0143–0147: benchmark speedups precede sufficient methodology and mix heterogeneous controls, censored sub-microsecond values, single runs, and precise ratios. The chart magnifies comparability problems. Put protocol/hardware/repetitions first; report absolute times and uncertainty; specify the exact output checked per row.
- 0161–0164: the MDS ceiling theorem packs several parameter regimes and a rigidity converse without a diagram or narrative. Split the universal ceiling from the MDS characterization.

## Cross-field knowledge assumed too heavily

- Coding theory: puncturing versus shortening conventions, represented code versus image code, generalized/relative weights and RDLPs, information sets, MDS bracket notation, dual-distance gates, and entropy/random-code existence. Local definitions often come only after their first use in the introduction.
- Finite algebra/geometry: trace-pairing identification $L\cong L^*$, field-tower compatibility, Singer cycles, projective spaces/frames, Gaussian binomial coefficients, subspace-lattice Möbius inversion, and point–hyperplane design parameters. These are usually mathematically correct-looking but frequently unglossed.
- Combinatorial optimization/probability: clutters, upward closures, reliability inclusion–exclusion, ZDDs, CP-SAT, Pareto pruning, graded shells, and fractional service-rate polytopes. The service-rate definition is good; the compiler figure and benchmark section assume more terminology than they explain.
- The manuscript is most friendly when it translates between fields operationally (helper union, label, block cost, backpointer) and least friendly when it uses one field’s compact noun phrase as though universal.

7. Repair benchmark exposition before emphasizing ratios: define every baseline, hardware, compilation inclusion, run/interleaving protocol, sample counts, uncertainty, and exact checked output. Avoid ratios derived from “$<1\,\mu$s,” six-digit speedups, and a single log-bar chart across heterogeneous tasks.
8. Add local glosses for cross-field terms at first use: puncture/shorten, clutter, projective point/frame, Gaussian coefficient, Singer cycle, CP-SAT, ZDD, information set, and $Q$-ary entropy. Do not make citations carry definitional load.
9. Consolidate repeated confinement formulations. Present the ungated $\Gamma$ theorem as the master result, then give the outer-distance, rank-minimized, and one-coordinate forms as clearly labeled corollaries with an explicit convention dictionary ($Z=\Gamma+1$).
10. Add small conceptual diagrams where they materially lower load: target/helper recovery equation; two-sector $\Gamma$ formula; two-level min–sum composition; projective incidence constructions. Avoid adding more feature-list figures.

## Overall verdict

The paper has a strong and genuinely coherent core: exact composition requires functional labels, support overlap is a separate information axis, and both insights lead to precise transfer, separation, and optimization results. Once the foundational definitions are established, many proofs are short and elegant. However, the intended cross-field expert reader currently pays a high front-loaded and recurrent notation tax. Too many central notions are announced before being made operational, the composition theorem is overbundled, and several decisive examples are harder to audit than they should be. The software methodology section also needs more disciplined experimental framing.

Overall expert-reader friendliness: **promising but presently demanding (major revision for exposition)**. A coding theorist can reconstruct most of the paper; a finite algebraist or optimization expert will repeatedly stall on imported conventions, while a reader expert in only one of the three areas is unlikely to absorb the first pass without backtracking. The highest-value repair is not more background in bulk, but earlier concrete examples, stable notation, theorem decomposition, and one-sentence operational glosses exactly where disciplines meet.
