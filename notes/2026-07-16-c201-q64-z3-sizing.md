# C201 Gate 2 — split-`Z3` union-family sizing at `q=64`

**Lane:** `relconic`

## Shape

After the single order-13 orbit family fails because it leaves the conic
nucleus uncovered, the next symmetry-aligned size decomposition is

```text
A = {nucleus} union O1 union O2 union O3 union O4,
```

where the `Oi` are length-three orbits of a split order-three subgroup of the
standard conic stabilizer.  Including the nucleus clears the preceding
family's fixed-point obstruction and gives size thirteen exactly.

## Sizing result

The action has three fixed points and 1,386 length-three orbits.  Of the 1,365
three-orbits disjoint from the conic, 1,302 form a four-arc together with the
nucleus.  Their pair-compatibility graph has

```text
vertices = 1302,
edges = 730380,
degree range = 1120..1124.
```

The graph is too dense for a full four-orbit enumeration.  If `delta=1120`,
the common neighborhood of every edge has at least
`2 delta-(1302-2)=940` vertices.  Since every vertex has at most 181
nonneighbors, that common-neighbor graph has minimum degree at least 758 and
at least 356,260 edges.  Double-counting over the six edges of a four-clique
gives at least

```text
43,367,529,800 pairwise-compatible quadruples.
```

These are not asserted to be thirteen-arcs: triples using points from three
or four distinct `Z3` orbits still require checks.  The count is a sizing
lower bound on inputs reaching those mixed-orbit checks.  Even division by
the order-756 split-torus semilinear normalizer leaves more than 57 million
classes.  Full enumeration is therefore rejected.

## Consequence

The `Z3` union route remains mathematically possible, but only after a
symbolic mixed-orbit collinearity reduction.  A future probe must express
that constraint in orbit parameters and use it before constructing
quadruples.  Blind clique traversal, coverage evaluation, and quadratic-rank
work are outside the C201 bounded gate.
