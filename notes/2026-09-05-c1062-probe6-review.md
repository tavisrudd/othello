## 2. Findings

### 2.1 The separator arm does carry a teaching signal; it is small, and it is consistent on every seed. Defect in the reported conclusion.

This is the one substantive reversal.

§ 5 of the report concludes: "choosing an experiment because it separates a sampled confused pair
carries no measurable teaching signal once the baseline is restricted to experiments that make
progress", and calls this "the second independent measurement" of probe 5's finding and "the reason to
stop treating the separating witness as the valuable part of the machinery". The evidence offered is
one run of `causal_design_report` at one seed, `0x5eed1062`, with 256 repeats per candidate truth, and
a table of five ratios between `1.000x` and `1.103x`. There is no variance, no second seed, and no
paired statistic anywhere in the module or the binary.

Running the same binary at forty seeds (`--seed 101..140`, defaults otherwise) gives the ratio of the
uniform-splitting arm's mean to the separator arm's mean, per family, one value per seed:

| family                  | mean ratio | seeds where separator wins | ties | losses | two-sided sign test |
|-------------------------|------------|----------------------------|------|--------|---------------------|
| priced-probes           | `1.0814`   | 40                         | 0    | 0      | `p ≈ 2e-12`         |
| faulty-component        | `1.0218`   | 37                         | 0    | 3      | `p ≈ 2e-8`          |
| split-repair            | `1.0215`   | 40                         | 0    | 0      | `p ≈ 2e-12`         |
| dominant-repair         | `1.0058`   | 36                         | 0    | 4      | `p ≈ 2e-7`          |
| quaternary-probe        | `1.0000`   | 0                          | 40   | 0      | degenerate          |

An independent twelve-seed sweep (`--seed 1..12`) reproduces the same picture: means `1.078`,
`1.024`, `1.022`, `1.007`, `1.000`, with per-seed standard deviations of `0.015`, `0.013`, `0.009`,
`0.006` and `0.000`.

So the separator arm is better than uniform sampling among splitting experiments, on four families,
by two to eight percent, on essentially every seed. That is measurable — overwhelmingly so by the
only statistic anyone would apply to it — and the report says it is not.

The size of the effect is better read against the headroom that exists at all, because the exact
optimum is in the same table. Taking the fraction of the gap between the random-splitting baseline
and the exact optimal arm that the separator arm recovers, over the twelve-seed sweep:

| family           | fraction of the random-to-optimal gap the separator recovers (mean, min..max) |
|------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| split-repair     | `0.45` (`0.25..0.71`)                                                          |
| dominant-repair  | `0.26` (`-0.12..0.48`)                                                         |
| priced-probes    | `0.20` (`0.15..0.25`)                                                          |
| faulty-component | `0.16` (`0.03..0.27`)                                                          |

A rule that recovers a sixth to a half of the distance from random to optimal is a weak heuristic, not
a null one. The correct statement is: *the separator rule is consistently but only marginally better
than uniform sampling among splitting experiments, recovering a minority of the available headroom,
and it misses the predeclared `1.10x` informativeness bar on every family.* That is a different
sentence from "no measurable teaching signal", and it does not transfer probe 5's result.

The transfer claim is the part that most needs withdrawing. After its review repairs, probe 5's
separator-pair arm sat at `p = 0.180` on ten paired seeds with per-seed ratios spanning `0.519` to
`1.800` — direction without effect, and the direction was *against* the separator. Probe 6's separator
arm is positive with a near-perfect sign record. These are not the same finding measured twice; one is
a null with a negative lean and the other is a small positive effect. Calling probe 6 "the second
independent measurement of it" reports agreement that the numbers do not show.

**Effect on the reported verdict**: the *predeclared* verdict is unaffected and in fact comes out
cleaner (see § 2.2). The narrative verdict of § 5, and the exploration log's summary sentence "the
separating experiment again carries no teaching signal once the baseline is uniform sampling among
experiments that actually split the survivors", must be restated.

### 2.2 The `1.103x` miss is a single-seed artifact, and the predeclared threshold is actually met on every family. Wording problem, in the conservative direction.

The mystery ledger records `priced-probes` at `1.103x` against a `1.10x` threshold as "a miss by three
thousandths rather than rounded into a pass", and closes it as noise "with the number stated". Under
the seed sweep the published seed's `1.103` is above every one of the twelve seeds in the first sweep
(range `1.057..1.101`) and the forty-seed mean is `1.0814`. So the published value is a high draw, and
on the seed-averaged number the family passes the predeclared band comfortably rather than missing it
by three thousandths.

Recording the number rather than rounding it was the right call, and the direction of the error is the
safe one. But "a miss by three thousandths" invites the reader to treat the threshold as knife-edge on
this family when it is not; the replacement it should carry is "`1.08 ± 0.015` across seeds, inside the band,
with the published single seed drawing `1.103`". The ledger's own reasoning ("the family has only four
candidates and two useful experiments, so the arm has almost no room to choose badly") is not the
explanation — `priced-probes` has the *largest* separator effect of any family, not the smallest.

### 2.3 One of the five families in the arm table is degenerate and carries no information. Wording problem.

On `quaternary-probe` every arm — optimal, greedy, uniform-splitting, separator — returns exactly
`2.000` mean and `2` worst case, on every seed, with zero variance. Every splitting experiment there
resolves the family in two steps, so the arm comparison has nothing to measure. The report presents
"Four families land inside the predeclared `1.10x` band and the fifth misses it by `0.003`" as five
data points; it is four, one of which (`quaternary-probe`) is a tautology. This does not change the
direction of anything, but the band is being tested on fewer families than the sentence implies.

### 2.4 The `uniform` straw arm's reported range is wrong. Wording problem.

§ 5 says "Against that weak baseline the separator arm looks excellent, at `2.12x` to `4.00x`." From
the published run the separator-to-uniform ratios are `3.997` (dominant-repair), `3.033`
(split-repair), `2.120` (faulty-component), `3.392` (quaternary-probe) and `1.949` (priced-probes).
The range is `1.95x` to `4.00x`; the stated lower end silently drops the `priced-probes` row, which is
the same family the report singles out elsewhere. Small, but it is a number in a report that claims
every number was checked.

### 2.5 The predeclared loss loses only under the strongest of several reasonable decision criteria, and the probe never says which criterion it chose or measures another. Defect in the claim, not in the code.

`ActionSets::build` computes, per candidate, the set of experiments that reach the target observation
*at that candidate's own minimum edit cost*, and `share_an_action` stops when the survivors' sets
intersect. So "decision-sufficient" here means: there is a single action that is simultaneously
**cost-optimal** for every survivor. That is the strongest of the criteria a caller might use, and the
probe reports its numbers as though "learning enough to act" had one meaning.

The natural weaker criterion is the one the objective's own wording suggests — a repair that is
guaranteed to reach the target under every survivor, priced at what it costs. Recomputing the same
dynamic program from the exported tables with that stopping rule (`scratchpad probe2.py`, stop when
some single column reaches the target under every survivor):

| family                  | reported decision experiments | under "one action that works for every survivor" |
|-------------------------|-------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| dominant-repair         | 0                             | 0                                                |
| split-repair            | 2                             | 1                                                |
| faulty-component        | 3                             | **2**                                            |
| quaternary-probe        | 1                             | 0                                                |
| indistinguishable-twins | 1                             | 0                                                |

Every family's decision count falls, and the predeclared loss stops being a loss: `faulty-component`
goes from `3/3 = 1.00x` to `3/2 = 1.50x`. The mechanism is visible in the family: with two survivors
`{i, j}`, the arity-two intervention that repairs both components reaches the target under both, at
cost `2` where the informed repair costs `1`. Under the declared probe price (`probe_base = 1`,
`probe_cost` all zero) that trade is an exact tie — two probes plus a cost-two hedged repair, or three
probes plus a cost-one informed repair, both total four. So the report's sentence "knowing enough to
act is precisely knowing everything" is not a property of the family; it is a property of insisting on
simultaneous *optimality*, and at the declared prices the hedging caller does exactly as well.

The same criterion choice cuts the other way on two families, which is why this is a defect in the
claim rather than a correction to a number. On `quaternary-probe` a single cost-two action reaches the
target under all eight candidates and equals the worst case of the informed action, so a worst-case
cost minimizer needs **zero** experiments where the report says one; the reported `2.00x` gap
understates that family. On `indistinguishable-twins` the same thing happens: a cost-two action works
for all four candidates, so the "decision still takes one probe" headline is again criterion-specific.

What is missing structurally is that the probe carries two price scales — `probe_costs` for
experiments and `edit_costs` for actions — and never puts them in one objective. `priced-probes` shows
the author knew price changes plans, but only for identification. Until probe price and repair price
are traded against each other, "decision-sufficient" is one point on a family of stopping rules and
the reported gaps are neither upper nor lower bounds on what a decision actually needs.

**Effect on the reported verdict**: the § 2 table's numbers are correct for the criterion implemented,
and the code and oracle agree on them. What must change is the report's framing: § 2's "the gap is
exactly one, as entered" needs "under the simultaneous-optimality stopping rule", and the closeout
sentence in the exploration log that the predeclared loss "lost at exactly `1.00x`" inherits the same
qualification.

### 2.6 The whole gap result is true by construction of six hand-built families, and the report does not say so. Wording problem, but a load-bearing one.

The plan warned about exactly this: "Include a predeclared family where the decision needs full
identification and expect a gap near zero; without it, 'decision-sufficient is much cheaper' is true by
construction." Probe 6 supplied that family, which is the guard the plan asked for and it works.

But the rest of § 2 reads as though the range of gaps were discovered. It was built:
`dominant-repair` forces actuator zero into every candidate's support so that one repair is optimal
everywhere; `indistinguishable-twins` adds a constant mechanism on a variable that is deliberately
excluded from the observed set; `faulty-component` gives each candidate a private singleton repair.
Each family is three or four lines of fixture code whose comment states the intended verdict before
the model is built. Nothing here measures how often the gap is large in any population of models.

The correct reading, which the report supports and does not state: **the gap between identification and
decision-sufficiency is unbounded in both directions, demonstrated by construction, and the
implementation computes each constructed case correctly.** That is a legitimate and useful result — it
is what kills the quotient idea in § 3 — but it is an existence result, not a measurement, and the
verdict line "the gap is real and ranges from nothing to unbounded" invites the measurement reading.

### 2.7 The oracle is independent of the dynamic program and not of the causal layer, and it checks fewer entries than the report claims. Wording problem.

`python/c1062_design_oracle.py` reads `outcomes`, `probe_costs`, `edit_costs` and `target_output` from
the exported JSON and rebuilds, from those alone, the optimal-action sets, both stopping predicates,
and a memoized top-down minimax over frozen hypothesis sets. That part of the report's claim holds:
the recursion structure is genuinely different from the Rust flat-array sweep, and it agrees on every
family (replayed; output in § 4).

Two qualifications the report does not make.

- **The outcome table itself is imported wholesale.** The rows come out of `OutcomeTable::build`,
  which calls `CausalModel::solve_into` and `observation_of` for every candidate under every
  admissible intervention, and `admissible_experiments` which enumerates the vocabulary. None of that
  is re-derived. This is the design analogue of the failure mode the plan names for probe 1's oracle —
  importing the compiler's own view of the problem — and it means an error in mechanism solving,
  observation packing, or support enumeration would be invisible to the oracle. The independent check
  that *does* bear on that layer is the exhaustive nonadaptive search inside the Rust module (§ 4), and
  it also runs on the same table.
- **The oracle compares only three entries per family.** `full`, `priced` and `decision` are compared
  against the predeclared values. The `binary` column is computed and printed but never entered into
  the agreement test; the `gap`, `action classes` and `all share` columns of § 2 and every number in
  § 4, § 5 and § 6 are not exported and not checked. The report's "An independent Python oracle
  reproduces every entry" and the exploration log's "an independent Python oracle reproduces all of
  them from the exported tables by a different algorithm" both overstate the coverage. Also, for
  `priced-probes` the oracle's line `if family["target_output"] is None: measured["decision"] =
  expected["decision"]` forces agreement on that cell rather than skipping it, which is a tautology in
  the middle of an agreement table.

### 2.8 The certificate corruption tests are real mutations, and the verifier is a real re-derivation. No issue, with one caveat.

`a_corrupted_certificate_is_rejected` builds the real certificate for `split-repair`, then mutates it
two ways — `swapped.nodes[0].experiment += 1` and `truncated.nodes[0].branches.pop()` — and asserts
`verify_certificate(...).is_err()` in both cases. These are actual mutations of an actual certificate
observed to be rejected, not assertions about a constant. The report's "that is a test rather than a
claim" is accurate.

`verify_certificate` recomputes every branch's block from the outcome table, rejects a node whose mask
is already a stopping set, rejects overlapping or uncovered blocks, rejects fewer than two branches,
checks each child node's stored mask equals the recomputed block, and then walks every hypothesis from
the root, measuring depth and cost during the walk with a cycle guard. So the § 1 sentence "the
reported depth and cost are measured during replay rather than copied from the plan" is exactly right,
and the report binary additionally asserts the replayed depth equals the plan's.

Caveat, for the record rather than for repair: the verifier checks that the certificate is a *valid*
design meeting the stopping rule, never that it is *optimal*. Optimality rests entirely on the Rust
dynamic program, cross-checked by the Python oracle on the three exported counts. The corruption
coverage is also thin — one family, node zero only, two mutation kinds — so it demonstrates that the
verifier is not vacuous rather than that it catches every corruption. Rerouting a branch to a
different existing node, a mutation the code does handle via the child-mask check, is not tested.

### 2.9 `ArmMetrics::mean` divides a censored sum by the uncensored run count. Defect, numerically negligible here.

In `run_arm`, `metrics.runs` is incremented for every run, but `metrics.total_experiments` is
incremented only when the run reached a stopping set; a run that hits `ARM_STEP_LIMIT` contributes
nothing to the numerator and one to the denominator. `mean()` is therefore neither the mean over
completed runs nor a censored estimator: it silently pulls the mean **down** for exactly the arms that
fail to finish.

Only the straw `uniform` arm ever stalls in the published run, once, on `dominant-repair` (the
`stalled` column reads `1` out of 2,048 runs), so the published `12.199` should read about `12.205` —
immaterial. It matters because the bias runs in the direction that flatters the worst arm, and because
any future family where a randomized arm stalls more often would report that arm as better than it is.
Patch in § 3.1.

### 2.10 The two `2.00x` binarization rows have different causes, and the report says they have the same one. Defect in the mechanism claim.

§ 4 gives the mechanism as: "the binary encoding available from a `d`-ary experiment is the family of
per-value indicators 'did the answer equal `v`', and those peel one block at a time, so a `b`-way split
becomes `b - 1` questions in the worst branch rather than `log2 b`", and concludes "Both families with
a four-valued answer land at exactly `2.00x`, which is that mechanism and not a coincidence."

For `quaternary-probe` the mechanism is right: the k-ary plan asks the four-valued probe then the
binary one, two questions; peeling the four-valued answer costs three, plus the binary one, giving the
measured four, against an information lower bound of three.

For `priced-probes` it is wrong, and demonstrably so. The k-ary plan asks the single four-valued
expensive probe and finishes in one. The named mechanism predicts three binary questions. The measured
binary count is **two**. Recomputing the binarized plan from the exported table and recording which
original experiments its chosen tests come from (`scratchpad probe2.py`) shows the optimum draws on two
*different* source columns — the two cheap one-bit probes, whose indicators are `{index even}` and
`{index < 2}` — rather than peeling the four-way column at all. So the ratio there is `2/1`, where `2`
is simply the information content of four candidates in bits and `1` is the k-ary count. The
binarization escapes the peel mechanism entirely on that family. The two rows agree numerically by
coincidence, which is the opposite of what the report asserts.

This does not touch the § 4 table, which reproduces, nor the conditional headline ("binarizing costs
nothing when the observation is already binary, and costs the full branching factor when it is not"),
which survives for `quaternary-probe`. The sentence claiming both rows share one mechanism should go.

### 2.11 "The exhaustive nonadaptive optimum" is exhaustive over a truncated vocabulary. Wording problem; verified not to change any number.

`nonadaptive_optimum(&table, 24)` searches subsets of the **first 24 columns only**, and the pairwise
"never separated" early-out is also evaluated on that prefix. `faulty-component` has 73 columns and
`dominant-repair` has 33, so on those two families the reported nonadaptive number is an exhaustive
search over a prefix, not over the vocabulary. The report's mystery ledger calls it "the exhaustive
nonadaptive optimum".

Recomputing the true nonadaptive optimum over the full column set from the exported tables
(`scratchpad nonad.py`) gives 3, 3, 4, 2, inseparable, 1 — identical to the truncated search on every
family. So the reported numbers are correct and the "adaptivity buys at most one experiment here"
ledger entry stands; the word "exhaustive" needs the truncation stated, because on a family whose
useful experiments sorted late it would silently report an inflated nonadaptive count and therefore an
inflated adaptivity benefit.

### 2.12 The mystery ledger's explanation for greedy never losing contradicts its own arity-bound entry. Wording problem.

The ledger says greedy never lost because "with at most eight candidates and a balanced-split
experiment always available, the greedy tree hits the information lower bound". Two entries later it
says "With six components and arity two, no experiment can split three against three" for
`faulty-component` — that is, a balanced split is precisely *not* available on that family. The
accurate statement covers both: on every family the optimum equals the information lower bound
attainable from the branching factors the vocabulary actually offers (three for six or eight
candidates under binary splits, two for eight candidates under one four-way plus one binary split, one
for four candidates under a four-way split), and max-split greedy attains that bound. The conclusion —
that these families are too small to separate greedy from optimal — is unaffected.

### 2.13 "Greedy matched the exact optimum ... in mean" compares against a tree that does not optimize the mean. Wording problem, with a latent test fragility.

`DesignPlan::compile` is a minimax: `worst = worst.max(value)` over blocks. It minimizes worst-case
cost, breaking ties towards fewer experiments. The `optimal` arm in § 5 therefore walks the
*worst-case-optimal* tree, and its reported `mean` column is the mean of that tree, not the minimum
achievable mean. A tree with a worse worst case can have a better mean, so "greedy maximum-split
matched the exact optimum on every family, in mean and in worst case" is only meaningful in worst case;
in mean, greedy tied a tree that was not trying to win that comparison.

The same assumption is baked into the library test `the_optimal_arm_is_never_beaten`, which asserts
`metrics.mean() >= optimal.mean()` for every other arm. That invariant is not mathematically
guaranteed for a minimax tree, and a future family where a heuristic beats the minimax tree in mean
would fail the test for a correct reason. Patch in § 3.2 restates it as a worst-case assertion.

### 2.14 Predeclaration is recorded in code, but the code, the evidence and the numbers landed in one commit. Process finding.

The plan's process rules require predeclared numeric thresholds and shape verdicts "entered before the
run", with at least one predicted loss per measurement probe.

What exists: `DesignFixture` carries `predicted_unit_experiments`, `predicted_weighted_cost` and
`predicted_decision_experiments` per family, with a `note` stating the intended shape; the library
test `every_family_meets_its_predeclared_numbers` asserts all three; the report binary re-asserts the
first two at run time so a report run cannot diverge quietly; the blame fixture carries `predicted` and
the binary asserts it; and the `1.10x` separator band is printed by the binary as "Predeclared before
the run". The predicted loss (`faulty-component`) is present, as the plan demanded. This is good
practice as far as it goes.

What does not exist is any git-visible ordering. In `ergodis-private`, `4bdc713` adds
`src/causal_design.rs`, the fixtures, the report binary, the oracle **and**
`evidence/c1062-design-tables.json` in a single commit; `ec5a294` adds only the downward-closure and
non-transitivity test a minute later; the report itself is one commit in `othello` two minutes after
that. In `notes/`, there is exactly one commit touching the probe 6 report. So nothing on disk
distinguishes "entered before the run" from "entered after the numbers were known", for any of the
three predeclared columns, the `1.10x` band, or the blame rationals. The claim in § 2 that "Every
number below was predeclared before the run and every one held" is unverifiable from the record.

This is the same shape as the predeclaration discipline elsewhere in C1062 and is not specific to
probe 6, but the plan asks for it explicitly and it costs one extra commit to make it checkable.

Two smaller overstatements in the same sentence: the § 2 table's `gap`, `action classes` and `all
share` columns are not predeclared anywhere, and of the six rows in the § 4 binarization table only
`quaternary-probe` is (in `binarizing_the_quaternary_probe_doubles_the_questions`, and in that
family's fixture note).

### 2.15 The arm table — the only measured content in the probe — has no retained evidence. Defect against the reproducibility convention.

`evidence/c1062-design-tables.json` is the only file probe 6 retains, and it contains outcome rows,
prices, targets and the three predeclared counts. From it, § 2 and § 4 are recomputable, and I did
recompute them. Nothing in it bears on § 5: the arm means, worst cases, stall counts and every ratio in
the § 5 table exist only as stdout from a run that was never captured to a file. Probe 5, by contrast,
retained `evidence/2026-09-04-causal-abstraction-arms.txt` for exactly this table, and its review
retained a second one.

The workspace convention is that a claimed run is never sole evidence for a paper-facing result. The
arm table reproduces today because the binary is deterministic given its default seed — I verified
that — but it is the part of probe 6 that a code change could move silently, and it is the part with
no committed artifact. Redirecting the report's stdout into `evidence/` at the published seed would
close this.

### 3.1 The censored mean (finding 2.9)

```diff
--- a/src/causal_design.rs
+++ b/src/causal_design.rs
@@ impl ArmMetrics {
+    /// The mean over runs that reached a stopping set.  A run that hits the
+    /// step limit contributes no steps, so dividing by every run would bias the
+    /// mean *down* for exactly the arms that fail to finish; `unresolved` is
+    /// reported beside this so a censored mean is never read as a complete one.
     pub fn mean(&self) -> f64 {
-        if self.runs == 0 {
+        let completed = self.runs - self.unresolved;
+        if completed == 0 {
             0.0
         } else {
-            self.total_experiments as f64 / self.runs as f64
+            self.total_experiments as f64 / completed as f64
         }
```

## 4. What was checked and found sound

**Every published number reproduces.** The report binary at defaults on `256959b` prints the § 2
family table, the § 4 binarization table, the § 5 arm table and the § 6 blame lines exactly as the
report gives them, and rewrites `evidence/c1062-design-tables.json` byte-identically to the committed
file. `git status --short` is empty afterwards.

**The mask-ordering argument holds for every experiment the code can enumerate.** `DesignPlan::compile`
skips any column producing fewer than two blocks on the current mask (`if blocks.len() < 2 { continue }`),
so every experiment it evaluates yields at least two nonempty blocks, each a proper subset of the mask
and therefore numerically smaller, and every entry it reads is final. Degenerate and no-split
experiments are excluded by that guard rather than by an assumption; `d`-ary blocks are handled by
collecting distinct values into a vector rather than by a binary split, so a four-way column produces
four blocks and the argument is unchanged; the binarized table runs through the identical code with a
two-valued column. The strongest confirmation is the oracle: its memoized top-down recursion over
frozen sets imposes no numeric order at all, and it produces the same counts on every family, which is
exactly the check that the flat order is valid.

**The oracle agrees on every family**, replayed at `256959b`: `dominant-repair` 3/3/0,
`split-repair` 3/3/2, `faulty-component` 3/3/3, `quaternary-probe` 2/2/1, `indistinguishable-twins`
none/none/1, `priced-probes` 1/2/n-a, and the binary column 3/3/3/4/none/2. It is independent of the
dynamic program though not of the outcome table (finding 2.7).

**Decision-sufficiency really is not transitive, and the demonstration is concrete rather than
asserted.** In `split-repair` the candidates are indexed by the seven nonempty supports over three
actuators and each one's optimal-action set is exactly its support, so `{V0}`, `{V0, V1}` and `{V1}`
are all present. `sharing_an_action_is_downward_closed_and_not_transitive` names those three
candidates, asserts the two inner pairs share an action and the outer pair does not, and then checks
downward closure exhaustively over every subset of the family. The argument in § 3 of the report — that
any equivalence-based stopping rule is determined by pairs, and this one is not — is sound as written,
and `dominant-repair` is a genuine counterexample to the equality quotient: all eight optimal-action
sets are distinct, so the quotient is the identity, and yet all eight share `do(V0 := 1)`.

**The certificate verifier is a real re-derivation and the corruption tests really mutate.** Detail in
finding 2.8.

**Baseline fairness in the arm comparison holds where it matters.** `UniformSplitting` samples
uniformly among columns that actually split the current mask, and `SeparatorOfSampledPair` samples a
confused pair from the mask and then a column separating it — which necessarily splits the mask, since
both members are survivors. So both arms make progress on every step and neither is charged for a
wasted question; the `stalled` column is `0` for both on every family, which confirms it empirically.
The report was right to demote `UniformExperiment` to a straw arm and to say so. The greedy-versus-exact
comparison is exhaustive rather than sampled — both are deterministic and `run_arm` walks every
candidate as the truth — so "greedy matched the optimum" needs no statistics in worst case (the mean
comparison is the separate wording problem in finding 2.13).

**Blame.** `blame` sums `weight_h · degree_h / (total weight)` as exact reduced rationals over the
family, calling probe 3's `responsibility` per candidate. On the uncertain forest fire the degrees are
`1/1` and `1/2`, giving `3/4` under a uniform prior and `5/8` under `1:3`; both are asserted by the
fixture and re-asserted by the binary, and both reproduce. The § 6 point that neither candidate
produces the blame value is correct and is the substance of the section.

**Determinism and module hygiene.** All randomness is `SplitMix` seeded from the `--seed` argument;
the search is iterative in the dynamic program and recursive only in the bounded nonadaptive
enumeration; there are no `HashMap` iteration-order dependencies. `HypothesisFamily::new` checks that
every candidate shares domains, observed set, intervention vocabulary and arity bound before anything
is compared, which is the right guard against silently comparing different questions.
`ActionSets::build` returns `Unreachable` rather than producing an empty action set. No off-by-one was
found in `block_of`, `binarized_tests`, `extract_certificate` or the metrics walk.

Two dead branches, recorded and not worth a patch: `HypothesisFamily::root_mask` and
`verify_certificate` both special-case `len() == 64`, unreachable because `MAXIMUM_HYPOTHESES` is 16
and `new` rejects anything larger.

One latent trap, not triggered here: the experiment vocabulary starts with the passive observation at
column zero, whose edit cost is zero, so it is also a zero-cost *action*. On all five decision families
the natural run gives outcome `0` against targets `1` and `3`, so it never enters an optimal-action set
— I checked the passive column of every exported table. On a family where the natural run already
reaches the target, every such candidate's optimal action would be "do nothing" at cost zero,
`share_an_action` would fire at the root, and the probe would report a decision at zero experiments for
a trivial reason. This is the design-layer echo of the null-intervention finding in probe 5's review
and deserves a docstring at minimum.

## 6. Which of probe 6's claims stand

|---|---|

**Verdict.** The structural half of probe 6 is solid and is the part worth keeping: the dynamic program
is correct and its ordering argument is enforced rather than assumed, the certificate verifier is a
real re-derivation with real corruption tests, and the non-transitivity result kills the plan's
decision-equivalence quotient outright and in a stronger form than the plan raised it. The measured
half needs two corrections. The separating experiment does carry a teaching signal — small, consistent
across forty seeds, and worth a sixth to a half of the gap between random and optimal — so the sentence
that it carries none, and the claim that this reproduces probe 5, must both be withdrawn; what survives
is that the signal is under the predeclared ten percent bar. And the gap between identification and
decision is reported without naming the stopping criterion that produces it, so the predeclared loss at
`1.00x` should be read as "under simultaneous optimality" rather than as a fact about the family. The
probe's own conclusion in § 8 — that probe 9 now has a stopping rule that is a covering condition
rather than a quotient, an exact plan with a replayable certificate, and blame over a family — is
unaffected by any of this.
