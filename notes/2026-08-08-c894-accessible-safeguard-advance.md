 C=\begin{pmatrix}0&k^{\mathsf T}\\k&S\end{pmatrix},

\]
where \(S^2=5I-kk^{\mathsf T}\). After switching, \(k=\mathbf1\).

six has a strongly regular core with parameters \((5,2,0,1)\), necessarily
the pentagon.

## Exact comparison with the C894 formula

C894 proves
\[
 A_B^2=5I-E,\qquad A_B\mathbf1=0.
\]
Taking \(S=A_B\) and \(k=\mathbf1\) in Haemers–Parsaei Majd Theorem 2 gives
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}0&\mathbf1^{\mathsf T}\\
                 \mathbf1&A_B\end{pmatrix}^2=5I.
\]
So their theorem directly attributes the last bordering operation once
\(A_B\) is known. It does not explain why the skew tournament matrix \(B\)
canonically determines \(A_B\), nor does it discuss the even lattice or its
golden quotient.

The safe endpoint citation posture is therefore:

- cite Haemers–Parsaei Majd for classical normalized conference bordering
  and the pentagon core;
- prove the short tournament-square formula inline;
- state the \(A_4\)-to-\(D_6\) sequence with proof and no novelty sentence;
- retain the human index query only for a possible prior occurrence of that
  more specific tournament/lattice package.
