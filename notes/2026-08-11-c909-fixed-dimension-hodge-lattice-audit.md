# C909 — hostile integral-Hodge audit of the fixed-dimension etale tower

Date: 2026-08-11  
Status: exact local lattice audit; no manuscript, PDF, mirror, Lean, or
commit change

## Verdict

The tower's proved assertion

\[
 \operatorname{PD}\langle\operatorname{NS}(A_a)\rangle^k
 =\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}^k\operatorname{NS}(A_a)
       \to H^{2k}(A_a,\mathbf Z)\bigr)
\tag{1}
\]

must not be upgraded to

\[
 \operatorname{Hdg}^{2k}(A_a,\mathbf Z)
 =\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}^k\operatorname{NS}(A_a)
       \to H^{2k}(A_a,\mathbf Z)\bigr).
\tag{2}
\]

For every odd \(p\), every \(a\ge1\), and every fixed dimension \(g\ge4\),
(2) already fails in codimension two at the bad prime.  The failure is an
explicit four-slot integral invariant after unramified splitting.  For the
same one-depth irreducible slope construction, the local calculation gives
(2) in all degrees for \(g\le3\).  Thus the correct crown is full PD
saturation, not integral Hodge equality.

The argument is a lattice falsification: a class after faithfully flat
unramified coefficient extension is enough to disprove equality over
\(\mathbf Z_p\), because both the Hodge lattice and divisor-product image
commute with that finite free base change.

## 1. Split local model

Use the tower notation \(R=\mathbf Z/p^a\), and extend to a finite unramified
splitting ring \(O\) for the degree-\(g\) slope algebra.  In the split
coefficient basis write

\[
 T=\operatorname{diag}(t_1,\ldots,t_g),
 \qquad t_i-t_j\in O^\times\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{3}
\]

Let \(x_i,y_i\) be the pullbacks of the two degree-one classes on the
elliptic-power cover.  The pullback lattice of \(H^1(A_a,O)\) has basis

\[
 X_i=p^a x_i,\qquad Y_i=y_i-t_i x_i,
\tag{4}
\]

up to the harmless sign convention for the graph.  Hence

\[
 x_i=p^{-a}X_i,
 \qquad y_i=Y_i+t_i p^{-a}X_i.
\tag{5}
\]

For the standard \(\mathrm{SL}_2\)-invariant contraction classes put

\[
 \Omega_{ii}=x_i\wedge y_i,
 \qquad
 \Omega_{ij}=x_i\wedge y_j+x_j\wedge y_i\quad(i\ne j),
\tag{6}
\]

and \(B_{ij}=X_i\wedge Y_j+X_j\wedge Y_i\).  Substitution gives the exact
formula

\[
 \Omega_{ii}=p^{-a}X_i\wedge Y_i,
 \qquad
 \Omega_{ij}=p^{-a}B_{ij}
  +p^{-2a}(t_j-t_i)X_i\wedge X_j.
\tag{7}
\]

This is the same matrix-of-ideals calculation as the graph NS theorem:
\(p^a\Omega_{ii}\) is an integral divisor class, while
\(p^{2a}\Omega_{ij}\) is the primitive off-diagonal divisor class.  The
unit difference in (3) is decisive.

For non-CM \(E\), the rational Hodge tensors on an isogenous power are the
diagonal \(\mathrm{SL}_2\)-invariants.  The \(\Omega_{ij}\) generate this
rational invariant algebra, subject to the usual matching/Pluecker
relations.  This is used only after extending coefficients to \(O\); no
Chow assertion is involved.

## 2. Exact codimension-two obstruction for \(g\ge4\)

Select four distinct split slots, say \(1,2,3,4\), and abbreviate
\(\delta_{ij}=t_j-t_i\).  Consider the invariant

\[
 Q=\delta_{13}\delta_{24}\,\Omega_{12}\Omega_{34}
   +\delta_{12}\delta_{34}\,\Omega_{13}\Omega_{24},
 \qquad H=p^{3a}Q.
\tag{8}
\]

The \(p^{-4a}\) terms in (7) cancel exactly: the first matching has
coefficient \(+\delta_{12}\delta_{34}\) on
\(X_1X_2X_3X_4\), whereas the second has coefficient
\(-\delta_{13}\delta_{24}\) because
\(X_1X_3X_2X_4=-X_1X_2X_3X_4\).  Every remaining term of \(Q\) has
denominator at most \(p^{3a}\), so (5)--(8) show

\[
 H\in \bigwedge^4 H^1(A_a,O).
\tag{9}
\]

It is a Hodge class because it is an \(\mathrm{SL}_2\)-invariant rational
tensor.  It is nonzero: the coefficient of
\(X_1Y_2X_3X_4\) in \(H\) receives a contribution from both summands (the
second matching uses the \(X_1X_3\) term in \(\Omega_{13}\) and the
\(X_4Y_2\) term in \(\Omega_{24}\)).  With the displayed exterior-order
convention it is

\[
\delta_{13}\delta_{34}(\delta_{24}+\delta_{12})
 =\delta_{13}\delta_{34}\delta_{14}\in O^\times,
\]

so it cannot vanish.  The earlier shorthand saying that the second
matching has no such monomial was inaccurate; only the leading
\(X_1X_2X_3X_4\) terms cancel.

Now grade the exterior algebra by the number of occurrences of each split
slot.  The multidegree \((1,1,1,1)\) part of the ordinary divisor-product
image is generated by products of two off-diagonal divisor classes, hence by

\[
 p^{4a}\Omega_{ij}\Omega_{kl}
 \quad\text{with}\quad \{i,j,k,l\}=\{1,2,3,4\}.
\tag{11}
\]

Every such product has coefficient divisible by \(p^a\) on the
\(B_{12}X_3X_4\) coefficient after its
\(X_1X_2X_3X_4\) coefficient is cancelled in a linear combination.  In
contrast, as computed just above, the collected coefficient of
\(X_1Y_2X_3X_4\) in \(H\) is

\[
                    \delta_{13}\delta_{34}\delta_{14}\in O^\times.
\tag{12}
\]

Therefore

\[
 H\notin\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}^2\operatorname{NS}(A_a)
              \otimes O\to H^4(A_a,O)\bigr).
\tag{13}
\]

The possible matching relation does not rescue (13): it only relates the
three pairings, and every divisor-product generator in (11) still carries
the displayed \(p^{4a}\) factor.  Diagonal divisor classes have different
slot multidegrees and cannot contribute to (11).

If equality (2) held over \(\mathbf Z_p\), its finite free unramified
extension to \(O\) would contain \(H\), contradicting (13).  Thus (2) is
false for all \(g\ge4\).  For \(g>4\), the same class is supported on the
first four slots; unused slots do not affect the codimension-two
calculation.

## 3. Positive low-dimensional boundary

For \(g\le3\), the split invariant tensors in degree at most the dimension
of the abelian variety have only the following slot patterns:

* degree two: one contraction, and \(\operatorname{Hdg}^2=\operatorname{NS}\);
* degree four: \((2,2)\) and \((2,1,1)\) patterns, represented respectively
  by \(\Omega_{ii}\Omega_{jj}\) and \(\Omega_{ii}\Omega_{jk}\), with the
  other matching written using the standard contraction relation;
* degree six for \(g=3\): the \((2,2,2)\) top pattern.

Formula (7) shows that integrality requires exactly the coefficients supplied
by the corresponding divisor products:

\[
 p^{2a}\Omega_{ii}\Omega_{jj},\qquad
 p^{3a}\Omega_{ii}\Omega_{jk},\qquad
 p^{3a}\Omega_{11}\Omega_{22}\Omega_{33}.
\tag{14}
\]

There is no four-distinct-slot cancellation in these dimensions.  The split
calculation therefore gives Hodge-lattice equality in every degree for
\(g\le3\); faithful flat descent gives the statement over \(\mathbf Z_p\),
and at primes away from \(p\) the isogeny is integral-locally invertible.
This low-dimensional assertion is restricted to the one-depth irreducible
finite-etale graph model and the non-CM Hodge group.

## 4. Consequence for the fixed-dimension tower

The tower draft's global existence, local order

\[
 \operatorname{End}(A_a)\otimes\mathbf Z_p=O+p^aM_g(\mathbf Z_p),
\]

and reduced-trace discriminant separation are compatible with this audit.
They do not imply Hodge-lattice equality.  The correct statement is:

* for every fixed \(g\ge2\), the tower has full cohomological PD(NS)
  saturation as in (1);
* for \(g\le3\), its non-CM fibers also satisfy integral Hodge equality in
  all degrees, subject to the standard integral invariant-tensor input;
* for \(g\ge4\), codimension-two integral Hodge equality fails by (8)--(13).

The failure is not a Chow or non-etale defect.  It is a purely finite-etale
integral invariant-tensor cancellation, so it belongs squarely to C909.

## 5. Final hostile grade

**MAJOR correction** to any claim of full integral Hodge equality in the
fixed-dimension tower for \(g\ge4\).  **GO** for the narrower full PD(NS)
theorem, and **GO with a dimension bound** for integral Hodge equality when
\(g\le3\).  The explicit class (8) is the earliest four-slot obstruction
and should be retained as a sharp boundary rather than hidden in a novelty
or moduli claim.
