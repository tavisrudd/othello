## 2. Findings

### 2.1 The `220x` is the ratio of two per-query array clears, not the compression a query cashes in — defect

This is the finding that matters, because `220x` is the number the exploration log's probe index row
carries and the number the report uses to say "quotienting the search graph is emphatically not the
thing that failed".

`ConcreteSearch::best_from_context` begins every query with

```rust
self.distances.fill(u64::MAX);
self.settled.fill(false);
```

over `lowering.presentation().state_count()` entries — 33,024 on the timing family. That is
`33,024 × 8` bytes of `u64` plus `33,024 × 1` byte of `bool`, or **297,216 bytes cleared per query**.
The compiled arm's inner loop, `WeightedGeneratorPlan::shortest_word_to_output_in` in the core, clears
`distances`, `parent_classes`, `parent_generators` and a settled bitmap over `class_count` = 74
entries, about 1.2 KB.

The work each arm then does is minute by comparison, and it is minute for a reason the plan already
established. Probe 1a's signature collapse says the reachable set from `(u, {})` is exactly
`{(u, I)}`: hard interventions are idempotent and commute on distinct variables, so a generator word
reduces to its final partial assignment. With 129 admissible interventions the concrete search's
reachable component is **129 states of the 33,024 it clears** — the carrier is 256 disjoint fibres of
129 states and a query searches one of them. So the concrete arm clears the entire carrier in order to
walk 0.4% of it.

The measured marginal costs say the clear is the whole story. From the four-workload run
(`--rounds 3 --workloads 1024,65536,131072,262144`), the concrete arm's per-query marginal cost is
`(7,377.780 − 36.978) ms / 261,120 = 28.11 µs` and `(3,647.069 − 36.978) / 130,048 = 27.76 µs` —
linear across a 256x range of workload lengths. Dividing the 297,216-byte clear by 28.11 µs gives
**10.6 GB/s**, which is single-threaded memset bandwidth. There is essentially no room left in that
number for search. The compiled arm without a memo comes out at
`(52.408 − 9.815) / 261,120 = 163 ns` per query against a 1.2 KB clear, which at the same bandwidth
accounts for about 112 ns and leaves roughly 50 ns of actual Dijkstra — so the compiled arm does
*more* real search work per query than the concrete one, and still wins by two orders of magnitude on
the clock.

The arithmetic that gives the game away: `33,024 / 129 = 256`, the number of exogenous contexts, and
the reported ratio is `213x` on my replay and `220x` in the report. **The published figure is, to
within a small constant, the number of fibres the concrete arm clears and does not search.** It is a
re-measurement of `states / classes` through memset bandwidth, not a measurement of what quotienting
buys a query.

Neither counter that would have caught this is printed. `ConcreteSearch::relaxations` and
`InterventionPlanner::queries` are both public fields, both maintained, and neither appears in the
report binary's output; there is no work count anywhere in probe 2's tables. The report's own
"Carried forward" note identifies the `std::collections::BinaryHeap`-versus-indexed-heap difference as
the caveat and dismisses it — "the 220x is far outside that" — while the much larger instrumentation
difference sits in the two lines above the heap.

The genuine search-work ratio, if it were measured, is bounded by the reachable-component sizes: at
most 129 concrete states against at most 74 classes, so under `2x` in nodes, with whatever the heap
and edge-representation differences add on top. Patch in § 3.1; predicted effect there.

**Effect on the reported verdict**: the `220x` claim and the sentence "quotienting the search graph is
worth 220x against the same search on concrete states" must be withdrawn, along with the exploration
log's probe index row "quotient worth 220x over concrete-state search". Nothing else in probe 2
depends on it — the timing failure, the compression table and the correctness gate are all
independent of this arm.

### 2.2 Even taking the published table at face value, the `220x` compares a memoized arm against an unmemoized one — defect

`run_compiled` takes a `memoize` flag and the `compiled+memo` column keys a `HashMap` on the root
class, so 74 entries cover 256 contexts and the arm goes flat. `run_concrete` has no memo of any
kind and no flag to give it one. The report's point 3 quotes `12.420 ms` (the memoized compiled
column) against `2,733.393 ms` (the unmemoized concrete column).

The like-for-like row is in the same table: `compiled` without a memo against `state search` without
one, `26.858` against `2,733.393`, which is `102x`. On my replay it is `18.133` against `1,754.505`,
`97x`. So the report's own data supports a figure less than half the one it publishes, before § 2.1's
correction is applied at all.

There is no reason the concrete arm could not have a memo. A per-context memo over 256 contexts is
exactly the structure `run_enumerated` already uses, and it would flatten the concrete arm the same
way the class memo flattens the compiled one — after 256 searches every further query is a hash
lookup. The report's point 4, "The class memo matters only for the compiled arm", is true of the
compiled arm's own drift and is not a reason to withhold a memo from the arm it is being compared
against.

### 2.3 The structural bound is an arithmetic identity, and the memoized arm it describes does half the solves it is charged with — defect in the argument, verdict direction unchanged

The report's verdict says the timing failure "is structural rather than a constant: on the flat
`(u, I)` carrier the compile solves the model once per materialized state, and that state count is
*exactly* the total work a memoized re-solve does to fill its table over every context. Measured
ratio: 1.00x."

Two problems, in increasing order of importance.

**The `1.00x` is printed by an identity.** The report binary computes it as
`lowering.cost().states as f64 / (contexts * interventions) as f64`, and the carrier is defined as one
state per `(context, admissible intervention)` pair, so `states = contexts × interventions` holds by
construction: `33,024 = 256 × 129`. Nothing is measured. As a structural argument this is fine and it
is what the report says it is; as a number printed under the heading "Measured ratio" next to a
timing threshold, it reads as an empirical tie and is not one.

**The baseline it describes is not the baseline that was measured.** The identity charges a memoized
re-solve with one solve per `(context, intervention)` pair. `best_intervention_enumerated` does not do
that: it prunes by cost before solving —

```rust
if best.is_some_and(|(incumbent, _)| cost >= incumbent) {
    continue;
}
```

— and supports are enumerated shortest-first, so the moment a cost-`c` answer is found every
candidate of cost `≥ c` is skipped unsolved. On the timing family the effect is a clean factor of two,
which I computed independently. Transcribing `deep_pipeline(8, 24, 8)` into Python from its mechanism
tables and running the oracle's own pruned order:

```text
admissible interventions: 129
states = 256 * 129 = 33024
contexts whose natural run already hits target 0: 128 / 256
memo fill, short-circuited solves: 16640
memo fill, exhaustive solves:      33024
ratio: 1.985
best-cost histogram: {0: 128, None: 128}
```

The arm's own timing corroborates the count: the memoized column reaches `1.653 ms` at 65,536
queries, which is 16,640 solves of a depth-25 chain at about 90 ns each, and the unmemoized
enumeration column reaches `270.371 ms`, which is 65,536 queries at an average of 65 solves each at
about 63 ns. Both fit the 16,640 count and neither fits 33,024.

So the compile does `33,024` solves where the memo does `16,640`, and the report's reading — "the
entire remaining gap is the lowering constant … a factor of about six for building transition tables
on top of **the same solves**" — attributes to table-building a gap of which about half is simply
twice as many solves. The per-solve costs then differ by only two to three times, not six.

Every part of this runs against the compile, so probe 2's verdict is untouched and if anything
understated: the compile is charged more solves than the strongest baseline actually performs, so
"a compile cannot come out ahead of exhaustive memoization on this carrier" holds a fortiori. What
needs restating is the mechanism, and the word "exactly".

### 2.4 The timing family's query has no nontrivial answer, so the timing section never times a search — defect

The same Python transcription gives the best-cost histogram above: on `deep-pipeline` with the target
output the arms actually ask for, **128 contexts answer at cost zero and the other 128 are
unreachable**. There is not one context in the workload whose answer is a nonempty intervention.

The consequence is that no arm ever runs the query the probe is about. For the 128 answerable
contexts the compiled arm returns at the `class_outputs[start] == target` check before entering its
loop and the concrete arm returns on its first pop; for the other 128 both arms exhaust their
reachable component and return nothing. The mechanism is the one the plan already named: with hard
interventions the reachable set from `(u, {})` is the 129-state fibre, and on this family the sink is
independent of the intervenable prefix for exactly half the contexts.

This also makes the timing section's own correctness cross-check — "arms return identical costs on the
4096-query workload: true" — nearly vacuous, since the answer vector is `Some(0)` and `None` and
nothing else. The real correctness gate is the six-family table in § "Correctness", which does
exercise nonzero costs across every attainable observation; the timing section's check adds almost
nothing to it.

I want to be careful about what this does and does not damage. It does not damage the timing verdict:
a workload of trivial queries is the *most* favourable one for the compiled arm, since a harder query
would cost the compiled arm a real Dijkstra per query while the memo would still be one hash lookup.
It does mean the timing table measures fixed costs and per-query overheads, not query cost, and the
report's framing of `deep-pipeline` as "the family most favourable to the compiled arm among the
hard-intervention shapes" — because "a re-solve costs the whole chain while a compiled query costs one
graph step" — describes a contest that this target output never sets up.

### 2.5 The published credit ratio is not the one that was predeclared, and the swap decides which families pass — defect

The plan says: "Report classes against `|U|`, against `|U|` after pruning non-ancestors of the
outcome, and against the orbits of the declared automorphism group; **count only the residue as
intervention-driven compression**." The predeclaration committed at `a6bd72e6a`, before any code, spells
the residue out: "Residual compression above the orbit baseline, **`orbits / classes`**, must exceed
1.5x on at least one family whose declared automorphism group is trivial."

What `CompressionReport` computes is `residual: orbits as f64 / joint.max(1) as f64` — `orbits / joint`,
where `joint` is the number of connected components of the union of the orbit relation and the class
relation. The report discloses the change and argues for it in § "The correction the measurement
forced", and **the argument is correct**: `verify_automorphism` requires
`O(σu, σI) = O(u, I)` for every context and every admissible pinned assignment, which makes a verified
symmetry permute the classes rather than merge them, so `orbits / classes` can and does sit below one.
I confirmed the equivariance claim by reading the verifier, and it is exhaustive over
contexts × supports × pin values, not asserted.

What the report does not do is re-score the per-family predictions under the new metric. Both numbers
are computable from the published table, and they disagree about which families pass:

| family | contexts | pruned | orbits | classes | joint | `orbits/classes` (as predeclared) | `orbits/joint` (as published) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `reliability-3of8`      | 256 | 256 |   9 | 164 |  6 | `0.055` | `1.500` |
| `weighted-threshold-8`  | 256 | 256 | 144 |  44 | 29 | `3.273` | `4.966` |
| `distractor`            |  32 |   8 |   4 |   8 |  4 | `0.500` | `1.000` |
| `identity`              |  18 |  18 |  18 |  18 | 18 | `1.000` | `1.000` |
| `wide-conjunction`      |  32 |  32 |   6 |  17 |  4 | `0.353` | `1.500` |
| `restricted-vocabulary` | 256 | 256 |  24 |  24 | 12 | `1.000` | `2.000` |

Under the predeclared metric exactly **one** family exceeds `1.5x`, not two:
`restricted-vocabulary` — a predicted **win**, and one of the two the report counts as clearing the
bar — sits at exactly `1.000x`, meaning the compiled quotient produces exactly as many classes as
symmetry produces orbits. Under the published metric it is `2.000x` because the two partitions have
the same block count while being different partitions, and their join halves it. That is a genuinely
interesting fact and the report is right to report it; it is not the quantity the prediction was made
against.

The threshold itself survives either way, on `weighted-threshold-8` at `3.27x` predeclared or `4.97x`
published, so the compression half still passes. What does not survive is "**two** families clear 1.5x
residual" and the implicit claim that the metric swap was bookkeeping.

There is a further reading problem with `orbits / joint` that the report does not surface. On
`reliability-3of8` the compiled quotient produces 164 classes on 256 contexts, a `1.56x` fold, while
symmetry alone produces 9 orbits, a `28x` fold: the quotient is eighteen times *worse* than the free
baseline on that family. `orbits / joint = 1.500` says something true and much narrower — that the
classes bridge four of the nine failed-count orbits, because with arity two a context with five or
more failures can never be repaired and all of them look alike. Both facts belong in the report. The
plan's warning was "in the reliability family the classes are just the failed-count orbits — reporting
that as a win would be self-deception"; the classes turn out not to be the orbits, and the metric that
replaced the check reports `1.500` where the check would have reported `0.055`.

### 2.6 The predeclaration text was rewritten in the commit that carried the results — process defect

Probe 2's predeclaration *order* is genuinely good, and better than probe 6's: `a6bd72e6a` at 16:06
carries the thresholds and five per-family verdicts, `b2bd15820` at 16:18 adds the two families built
during the work, and the library and report-tool commits are at 16:15 and 16:32, with the results
commit `6b958b2a6` at 16:34. Diffing `a6bd72e6a` against `b2bd15820` shows the second commit only
renames `weighted-threshold-10` to `weighted-threshold-8`, adds two rows, and adds the
"verified exhaustively" sentence. That is the shape the process rules ask for and the probe deserves
credit for it.

The problem is in `6b958b2a6`, the results commit, which edits the predeclaration section in place:

- threshold 2's metric clause "`orbits / classes`" is deleted (§ 2.5);
- "whose **declared** automorphism group is trivial" becomes "whose **verified** automorphism group is
  trivial **or near-trivial**".

The second edit is the one that needs flagging. `weighted-threshold-8`, the family the compression
threshold passes on, was predicted to have no symmetry at all — "distinct Fibonacci weights admit no
symmetry" — and the run found two of its 28 offered transpositions verify. Under the sentence as
committed before the run ("verified … trivial") the family no longer qualifies as the family the
threshold is about; under the sentence as committed with the results ("trivial or near-trivial") it
does. The report then presents the two-transposition surprise as a strength — "Had those two been
assumed away rather than verified, the family's credit would have been overstated" — which it is,
while the qualifying clause that the surprise falsified was quietly widened in the same commit.

None of this makes the threshold verdict wrong: read as "declared trivial", which is what the
pre-run text said, `weighted-threshold-8` qualifies and clears the bar on either metric. It does mean
the report's own sentence "Committed before the measuring code was written (`a6bd72e6a`, extended in
the next commit …, still before any run)" describes a predeclaration section that was edited a third
time, after the run, without a marker.

### 2.7 `reliability-3of8` is scored as a landed prediction on a number that contradicts it, and `1.500` is scored two different ways in one table — defect in the scoring

The predeclared verdict for `reliability-3of8` is: "**loss**: classes are the failed-count strata, so
orbits equal classes and residual compression is exactly 1.0." Measured: orbits 9, classes 164 —
not equal, off by a factor of eighteen — and residual `1.500`, not `1.0`. The report scores this as
"lost as predicted but by a different mechanism than predicted" and asserts "Every predeclared verdict
landed."

Nothing about that prediction landed except the one-word label. The mechanism was wrong, the equality
was wrong, and the number was wrong by half the distance to the pass threshold. The plan's own rule
covers this case explicitly — "a predicted loss that wins is a finding about the shape classifier, not
a success for this probe" — and `reliability-3of8` at `1.500` is scored, three paragraphs later in
the same section, as a *non*-loss: "`wide-conjunction` came in at exactly `1.500x`, matching its
'partial' call." The identical value is read as "partial" on one row and as "loss" on another.

The shape-classifier reading is where the finding should have gone. C5 (full symmetric group) was
read correctly for `reliability-3of8` — all 28 transpositions verify — and the inference drawn from it
("so orbits equal classes") is what failed, because a labelled edit vocabulary makes the quotient
equivariant rather than invariant. That is a correction to how the classifier's C5 row should be read
when the vocabulary is labelled, and it is a more valuable output than a scored prediction.

### 2.8 The orbit baseline is verified exhaustively, but only transpositions are offered, and every symmetry missed inflates the residual — qualification, not a defect

The report's claim that the orbit baseline is "verified rather than declared" is true and the
verification is strong: `verify_automorphism` checks domain compatibility, that the permutation maps
observed variables to observed and intervenable to intervenable, and then sweeps every context, every
admissible support and every pin assignment, requiring the packed observation to be *equal* rather
than merely corresponding. A candidate that fails anywhere is dropped. `weighted-threshold-8`'s
`2/28` and `restricted-vocabulary`'s `13/28` are real measurements.

The qualification is on the candidate set, not the verifier. `transpositions_of` offers only component
transpositions, and the group used is the one generated by those that individually verify. A symmetry
that is not generated by verifying transpositions — a 3-cycle or a longer cycle with no verifying
transposition inside it — is never offered and never found. Every such miss leaves the orbit count
higher than the family's true automorphism group would give, and since the published residual is
`orbits / joint`, a higher orbit count raises the residual. So the direction of the untested risk is
the one that flatters the probe, and the report's sentence "the orbit baseline is the largest symmetry
the family really has rather than the largest one that was declared" is true only within the
transposition-generated subgroup. On `reliability-3of8` this cannot bite — all 28 transpositions
verify, giving the full symmetric group — but on `weighted-threshold-8`, where only two do, it is
unchecked.

### 2.9 The certificate-policy miscompile does not touch a single reported number, for a stronger reason than the one that cleared probe 1 — no issue

The brief asked which policy produced probe 2's headline numbers and whether the repair note's claim
that they are unaffected was verified or asserted. Traced through the report binary, every measured
table is produced under one of the two policies the repair note certifies as correct in every row:

- `run()` sets `let policy = CertificatePolicy::ExhaustivePairAudit;` and passes it to
  `compression_report`, so the compression table, the states/classes/edges table, the correctness
  sweep, the vocabulary quotient and the minimax-regret section all compile under the audit;
- `timing_section` sets `let policy = CertificatePolicy::SplitTranscript;` for every timed arm and
  uses the audit only for the one-off comparison line.

`QuotientOnly`, `MultiwayTranscript` and `AdaptiveTranscript` appear in exactly one place, the
certificate-policy diagnostic table, whose entire purpose is to print what each policy does; their
outputs are never consumed by any other table. So the answer is a clean negative, and the searched
domain is the whole of `tasks/tools/src/best_intervention_report.rs` and
`src/best_intervention.rs` — every `compile(` call site in either file, of which the diagnostic loop
is the only one reaching a broken policy.

The reason that clears probe 1 does **not** apply here and the report should say so if it ever
repeats the claim. Probe 1's review cleared probe 1 twice over: the audit policy, and every fixture
below the 4,096-state multiway admission gate. Probe 2's families are 33,024 states, eight times the
gate, and `restricted-vocabulary` at 4,864 is above it too; four of its six families are admitted to
the broken refiner and three of them produce a wrong partition under deferred verification, per the
repair note's § 1. Probe 2 is safe only because of its policy choice, not because of its scale.

One thing worth recording as a near miss: the broken policies fail closed under
`compile_observational_with_policy`, which is what `CausalLowering::compile` calls, so the diagnostic
table shows errors rather than silently wrong class counts. Had probe 2's timing arm been configured
with `QuotientOnly` — the obvious "cheapest correct configuration" choice before the split transcript
was known to be needed — it would have errored rather than mismeasured.

### 2.10 "Agreeing with the audited compile class for class" is checked as class-count equality — wording problem

The report's § "Timing" justifies giving the compiled arm the split transcript with: "The policy table
below shows the split transcript compiling this shape and agreeing with the audited compile class for
class, so charging the arm for the exhaustive pair certificate would be an unfair boundary." The
§ "Certificate policies" bullet repeats it: "`SplitTranscript` succeeded everywhere and agreed with
the exhaustive pair audit **class for class** in every row."

What the binary evaluates is

```rust
policies agree: {}", audited.class_outputs().len() == compiled.class_outputs().len()
```

— the number of classes, and the policy table likewise prints `compiled.class_outputs().len()` per
policy. Equal counts are not class-for-class agreement; two different partitions with the same block
count are exactly what § 2.5's `restricted-vocabulary` row is about, so the distinction is live in
this very report.

The claim is nevertheless true. The repair note's reproduction harness compared the failing policies'
partitions against the split-transcript partition with block relabelling allowed, and the core gate
`every_certificate_policy_computes_the_same_quotient` added in `ergodis` `6cc9668` requires exact
equality of `state_classes`, `class_ranges`, `class_outputs` and class transitions. So the evidence
exists — it just does not live in probe 2's tool, and one half of it lives in an uncommitted scratch
crate. Patch in § 3.3 makes the tool check what the sentence says.

### 2.11 The enumeration oracle does not re-solve at every admissible partial assignment — wording problem, and it is what § 2.3 turns on

The report's § "Why the compiled answer is the true answer" says: "None of the three steps is taken on
trust: the enumeration oracle **re-solves the model at every admissible partial assignment**, and every
compiled witness is replayed through the model."

It does not. `best_intervention_enumerated` skips any candidate whose cost is not strictly below the
incumbent, before solving. In the correctness sweep this is harmless — the pruned candidates cannot
change the minimum, and the sweep's agreement is on cost — but the sentence is what the structural
bound in § 2.3 rests on, and there the factor of two it hides matters.

`EnumerationWorkspace` also carries a `solves` counter that nothing prints, which is the second missing
work count in this probe (§ 2.1 has the first two).

### 2.12 Oracle independence carries the same qualification as probe 1 and probe 5, unstated here — wording problem

`best_intervention_enumerated` and `replay` both live in `src/best_intervention.rs` and both call
`CausalModel::solve_into` and `CausalModel::observation_of`; the oracle additionally iterates
`enumerate_supports`, the same routine the lowering uses to build its sorts. So the correctness table
is agreement between the compiled query and a re-solve that shares the model layer with it, and the
witness replay checks the compiler's index bookkeeping against the same solver rather than against an
independent semantics.

This is exactly the qualification probe 5's review recorded as its § 2.5 and probe 1's review as its
§ 2.4, and it is the right qualification rather than a defect: the oracle *is* independent of the
compiler, which is the half that matters for "is the compiled answer the true answer". The report
should carry the sentence rather than leave a reader to infer it from "re-solves the model", and it
should say that the independent check on the model layer is probe 1's response-function gate, not
anything in probe 2.

### 2.13 Probe 2 does not reach the `class_of` bounds defect, and adds a sentinel of its own — no issue, one latent trap

Probe 1's review flagged `CausalLowering::class_of` for not bounding its `context` argument, so an
out-of-range context silently indexes a later sort, and recorded it as "consumed by probe 2". Traced:
`class_of` appears exactly once in probe 2, at `src/best_intervention.rs:939` inside `minimax_regret`,
and it is called with the fixed declared context of the candidate set (`0b111000 = 56` and
`0b101010 = 42`, both inside the 64 contexts of a six-source model). The timing and compression paths
use `InterventionPlanner::root_class`, which reads `compiled.state_classes().get(context as usize)`
and returns `None` out of range. So the defect is not reached.

Probe 2 introduces a smaller trap in the same line: `class_of(...).unwrap_or(u32::MAX)`. A `None`
becomes a sentinel class that every failing intervention would share, so any interventions whose class
could not be resolved would be merged into one quotient key and all but the cheapest discarded. Nothing
triggers it today — the candidates are enumerated from the same `enumerate_supports(intervenable, arity)`
the lowering uses, so every one resolves — but it converts a lookup failure into a silent merge rather
than an error.

While tracing this I found a scope error in the sibling probe 1 review that bears on probe 2's
baselines and should be corrected there: its § 2.3 says of `prune_irrelevant_exogenous` that "Its only
call site in the whole repository is … its own unit test", and its § 1 records the searched domain as
`src/causal*.rs` and `tasks/tools/src/causal*.rs`. `compression_report` calls it at
`src/best_intervention.rs:728`, which is outside that domain. The narrow claim — that
`CausalModel::lower` does not prune — stands; the repository-wide claim does not. This matters here in
probe 2's favour: the plan's second baseline, "`|U|` after pruning non-ancestors of the outcome", is
computed by a real pruning call, and the `pruned` column is the only place in the task where that
routine runs outside a test.

### 2.15 The paired `t = −347.299` is a timer-stability statistic, not an effect statistic — wording problem

the ratio has been tested. Contrast probe 5's review, where the sign test on paired seeds was doing
real work because the effect was small and the arms were randomized; here neither is true.

## 4. What was checked and found sound

- **Every non-timing table reproduces exactly** at `256959b`: compression, states/classes/edges,
  correctness, vocabulary quotient and minimax regret are cell-for-cell identical to the report. The
  certificate-policy table reproduces as the repair note predicts it now would, with all five policies
  agreeing.
- **The exactness gate is real and it is the strongest thing in the probe.** The compiled arm is run
  against the enumeration oracle on every context crossed with every attainable observation in all six
  families — 512, 512, 64, 324, 64, 512 queries — agreement is on cost including agreement on
  unreachability, and every witness is replayed by re-solving the model under the normalized pins. The
  witness count sitting below the query count is explained and correct: a query with no reachable
  target yields no witness. The independence qualification of § 2.12 does not touch this; the oracle is
  independent of the compiler, which is what the gate is for.
- **The argument that the compiled answer is the true answer is correct as stated.** A generator word
  from `(u, {})` lands at `(u, I)` for `I` the last-write-wins fold of its steps, so the word set and
  the intervention set have the same images; a word that overwrites pays for both writes while the
  normalized intervention pays for the last, so the minimum over words equals the minimum over
  interventions and never undercuts it; and `InterventionPlanner::best_from_class` performs exactly
  that fold when it converts the returned generator sequence into pins. The code comment at that fold
  states the inequality in the right direction.
- **`prune_irrelevant_exogenous` really is applied**, so the plan's second baseline is a measurement
  rather than a column of copies. It bites on exactly one family (`distractor`, 32 contexts to 8) and
  the report reads that correctly as "relevance pruning explains all of it".
- **`verify_automorphism` is exhaustive and demands invariance, not correspondence** (§ 2.7). The two
  verifying transpositions on distinct Fibonacci weights are a real and slightly surprising measurement,
  and the mystery-ledger entry explaining them — a threshold can be blind to a weight exchange when no
  attainable subtotal separates the two orderings — is the right explanation.
- **The equivariance correction is right.** A verified symmetry permutes the classes of a labelled edit
  vocabulary rather than merging them, so `orbits / classes` genuinely can and does sit below one. My
  disagreement in § 2.5 is with what was done about it, not with the diagnosis.
- **The vocabulary quotient is computed correctly and the report does not overread it.** `declared`
  counts distinct `(variable, value)` edits and comes out at twice the intervenable count on every
  family, which is right; the action vector is taken over the source sort's class range for each
  generator with a sentinel elsewhere, so two edits count as equal only when they agree wherever both
  are defined. The conclusion — that a labelled hard-edit vocabulary is pairwise distinguishable on
  `Q` almost everywhere, and that the measurement would have content only for soft, shifted or
  policy edits — is exactly what the numbers support and is correctly demoted to a one-line result, as
  the plan asked.
- **The minimax-regret half is built, not skipped, and is reported straight.** `minimax_regret`
  enumerates every admissible intervention with its per-model observation, scores
  `utility[observation] − cost`, computes regret against the best that model would have allowed, and
  minimizes the maximum; the quotient key is the tuple of classes an intervention reaches across the
  candidate models, only the cheapest representative of each key survives, and `agrees` compares the
  quotiented minimax value against the raw one. Both sets agree, the collapse is `1.00x` on one set and
  `1.22x` on the other, and the report says plainly that the saving is small and in one set absent.
  The inference it draws — that probe 6's decision-equivalence key cannot be the class key — is
  correct and, as probe 6's own review confirms, is what probe 6 went on to find.
- **The timing arms share an equal boundary.** Every compiled and concrete arm builds its lowering
  inside the timed region; the enumeration arms build nothing because they need nothing; the arm order
  is rotated by round; medians are taken over rounds; and the arms' answer vectors are compared for
  equality rather than assumed. The `deep-pipeline` family is also the fairest available choice in one
  respect the report does not claim credit for: its quotient is the observation partition itself, two
  classes in each of 37 sorts, so the compiled arm searches the smallest graph any family could give
  it and still loses.
- **The `1.19x` to `1.46x` plan-edge collapse and the `2.71x` to `84.24x` state-to-class ratios** are
  straightforward counts off the compiled artifact and reproduce; nothing in § 2.1 touches them. They
  are the defensible statement of how much smaller the compiled graph is, and they are what the `220x`
  should have been replaced by.
- **Determinism.** The workload generator is a counter-based xorshift seeded from `--seed`, so every
  arm and every round answers the identical query sequence; the compression and correctness paths have
  no randomness at all. Every table except the timings reproduced exactly across two runs at different
  round counts.
