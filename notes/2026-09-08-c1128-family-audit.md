## 1. Family and generic quartic del Pezzo model

Let (k) have characteristic zero and (B(x,y)) be a binary cubic. Write

\[
X_B:\ (z-x)u^2+6yuv+3(z+x)v^2-z^3
 +\frac34(x^2+3y^2)z+B(x,y)=0.
\]

Project to ([x:y]), and put (K=k(a)), (a=x/y), (A=a^2+3),
(\beta=B(a,1)). The supplied birational identities give the intersection

\[
Q_1=-au^2+6uv+3av^2+\beta w^2-zh=0,\qquad
Q_2=u^2+3v^2+\frac34Aw^2-z^2+wh=0.
\]

The point (P=[0:0:0:0:1]) lies on this surface (S/K). On (w\ne0),
(Q_2) solves for (h), and (wQ_1+zQ_2) is the generic cubic-surface
equation. In particular (k(X_B)=K(S)) when (X_B) is smooth.

For (c(t)=t^3-\frac34At-\beta), the pencil determinant is

\[
\det(Q_1+tQ_2)=\frac34(t^2-A)c(t),\qquad
\operatorname{disc}(c)=\frac{27}{16}A^3-27\beta^2.
\]

The rational function (A) is not a square in (k(a)), even if (k)
already contains \(\sqrt{-3}\): its geometric roots have valuation one.
Thus (A^3\ne16\beta^2). Also, for (s^2=A),

\[
c(s)c(-s)=\beta^2-A^3/16\ne0.
\]

The degree-five determinant is therefore squarefree. The regular pencil
defines a smooth quartic del Pezzo surface. This uses no assumption that
(c) is irreducible or has the full generic splitting group.

### The geometric bridge that should be stated

The conic bundle with five singular fibers belongs to the cubic-surface
model obtained by blowing up (P\in S) and projecting from the resulting
line. It is not a five-singular-fiber conic bundle on a degree-four del Pezzo
surface itself. The line contracts back to (P). Tschinkel–Zhang, v2,
Section 4 immediately before Proposition 4.1, identify the ten component
lines with the ten rulings of (S). This gives precisely the standard
(W(D_5)) action on the degree-four Picard lattice. Their Proposition 5.3
uses the same bridge for the explicit cubic example.

Their construction uses a point off the exceptional lines. The displayed
(P) satisfies this condition: its tangent plane is (w=z=0), and the
remaining two equations there are (-au^2+6uv+3av^2=0) and (u^2+3v^2=0).
Their binary resultant is (36(a^2+3)\ne0). Thus their only common
projective point in ([u:v:h]) is (P); no line on (S) passes through
(P). This verifies the geometric hypothesis rather than presuming the
displayed rational point is general.

## 2. Component labels, Galois containment, and uniformity

The affine conic equation is

\[
(t-a)u^2+6uv+3(t+a)v^2=c(t).
\]

Let (\rho_1,\rho_2,\rho_3) be the roots of (c), and take cyclic
differences (d_i=\rho_j-\rho_k). Then

\[
d_i^2=3(A-\rho_i^2).
\]

The supplied single equation for a component can become identically zero
when (\rho_i=a). Use instead the rank-one system

\[
\begin{pmatrix}\rho_i-a&3\pm d_i\\3\mp d_i&3(\rho_i+a)\end{pmatrix}
\binom uv=0.
\]

Its determinant vanishes by the displayed identity, and it never has rank
zero because the two off-diagonal entries sum to 6. This gives both lines
without an exceptional division and preserves the same Galois labeling.
For the three-parameter subfamily the problematic identity (c(a)=0)
cannot hold identically, but this repair makes the full binary-cubic statement
uniform as well.

For the other two fibers choose

\[
e_+^2=(s-a)c(s),\qquad e_-^2=(-s-a)c(-s),\qquad
e_+e_-=d_1d_2d_3/3.
\]

The last choice is possible because the product of the first two squares
is (\operatorname{disc}(c)/9). These quantities are nonzero.
An element inducing permutation (p\in S_3) on the roots sends all three
(d_i) with common sign (\operatorname{sgn}(p)). Its two signs on
(e_+,e_-) have product (\operatorname{sgn}(p)); it may also exchange
(s,-s). Thus its signed action belongs to

\[
H=\langle(45),c_4c_5,(123),c_1c_2c_3c_5(23)\rangle.
\]

The independent checker enumerates all 24 allowed sign patterns and proves
they equal this generated group. It also verifies an explicit signed
conjugator to the two-generator standard (I_3) presentation in TZ
Proposition 4.1. With rightmost permutation applied first and sign changes
acting on images, a conjugator is the signed tuple

\[
(-1,-2,-5,-3,4).
\]

This is stronger evidence than merely matching group orders. The same
four-generator presentation is printed in TZ Proposition 5.3. Their Lemma
4.2 supplies the stably permutation Picard lattice; restriction to a subgroup
preserves that property. Neither a minimality assumption on (S) nor equality
of the actual Galois group with (H) is required for this application.

Apply the manuscript surface theorem to (S(K)\ne\varnothing). It gives
(S\times\mathbf P^2\sim_K\mathbf P^4), and hence
(X_B\times\mathbf P^2\sim_k\mathbf P^5). For smooth (X_B), the other
manuscript supplies the obstruction after one stabilization. Over a general
characteristic-zero field, a hypothetical rationalization and inverse descend
with the coefficients to a finitely generated subfield, which embeds in
\(\mathbf C\). The same argument applies after any field extension. Thus
the exact-level-two conclusion is uniform in the asserted sense.

## 3. Three moduli directions and specialization

Set (B=\lambda x^3+\mu x^2y+\nu y^3). The supplied seed at
((\lambda,\mu,\nu)=(0,0,1)) is smooth. Its exact Gröbner check replays.
Independently, the degree-six Macaulay matrix of the five quadratic
derivatives of (4F_{\rm seed}) has rank 210 modulo 101, equal to its number
of rows. Its nonzero maximal minor therefore also proves full rank over
\(\mathbf Q\). Every degree-six monomial lies in the Jacobian ideal over
\(\mathbf Q\), which proves projective smoothness over \(\overline{\mathbf Q}\).

The orbit and augmented ranks are independently verified as

\[
(25,28)\quad\text{for cubics},\qquad (4,7)\quad\text{for binary sextics}.
\]

At a seed with automorphisms, do not automatically identify these with the
rank of the differential to a coarse moduli space. Instead apply the action
map: the (GL_5)-orbit together with the three family directions has
dimension at least 28, and cubic orbits have dimension 25. The image in
moduli has dimension three. Its parameter space also has dimension three,
so the map is generically finite onto its image. The same action-map
argument for (GL_2\times\mathbf A^3) has full rank seven in binary
sextics, proving dominance of the genus-two moduli map. This avoids the
coarse-tangent-space ambiguity entirely.

Let (M_B) be the closure of the cubic image inside smooth complex cubic
moduli. Its dimension is three and it is unirational. To justify the
closure assertion, pull back to the smooth cubic parameter space with its
universal family and take the closure of the (PGL_5)-saturated family
image. Its **geometric generic point** is rational after two variables:
the generic family rationalization spreads after the generically finite
parameter extension. Apply Nicaise–Ottem, v3, Theorem 4.1.1, to the
universal cubic times \(\mathbf P^2\) and \(\mathbf P^5\) over this
Noetherian base. Birational equivalence of geometric fibers is preserved
under specialization. The cubic lower bound then gives level two at every
smooth point of (M_B).

This reasoning does not infer generic rationality from a dense collection
of rational closed fibers. Nicaise–Ottem only give a countable union of
closed loci in general, and their discussion explicitly warns that density
alone is insufficient. The generic rationalization here is essential.

## 4. Genus-two torsion and what it actually encodes

On the open where (f=16B^2-q^3) is squarefree, with (q=x^2+3y^2), put

\[
C_B:\ \eta^2=f.
\]

The roots of (q) are distinct, and smoothness forces (B) to be nonzero
there. Let (D\subset C_B) be the two points on the sheet (eta=4B),
and (H) the fiber over (y=0). Then

\[
\operatorname{div}\left(\frac{\eta-4B}{y^3}\right)=3D-3H.
\]

The degree-zero class (\tau=[D-H]) is defined over (k) and has order
three: it is nonzero because its effective divisor (D) has points above
two different base points and hence is not a member of the hyperelliptic
pencil. This remains correct if the fiber (H) is ramified, with its divisor
multiplicity. On the common smooth open the family gives a map to genus-two
moduli with a chosen nonzero 3-torsion class, finite over ordinary genus-two
moduli. The rank calculation therefore gives the claimed generically finite
correspondence to the cubic locus. No degree is computed. In particular,
one must account for the hyperelliptic involution identifying (\tau) with
(-\tau) when counting coarse moduli fibers.

Bruin–Flynn–Testa, arXiv:1401.0580v1, Introduction and Section 2, proof of
Lemma 3, explicitly give this divisor construction for
(F=G^2+\lambda H^3). Here (G=4B), (H=q), (lambda=-1).
Thus the torsion construction is **already covered**, not a new standalone
theorem. Their larger level structures require two independent isotropic
3-torsion classes; the single class above does not by itself supply their
((3,3))-isogeny construction.

### A concrete marked interpretation

Let (E_B\subset\mathbf P^2_{[x:z:y]}) be the fixed plane cubic

\[
g=-z^3+\frac34qz+B=0,
\]

and let (o=[0:1:0]\notin E_B). Projection from (o) to ([x:y]) is a
degree-three map. Directly,

\[
\operatorname{disc}_z(z^3-\tfrac34qz-B)
=-\frac{27}{16}(16B^2-q^3).
\]

Thus, over \(\mathbf C\), (C_B) is the discriminant double cover of
this marked elliptic projection. Over a nonclosed field the literal
discriminant double cover has the constant quadratic twist (-3); the
equation defining (C_B) should not lose that distinction.

On the affine (y=1) chart, Cardano's identities read

\[
W^3=4B+\eta,\qquad z=\frac{W+q/W}{2}.
\]

They are checked after clearing denominators. On the common smooth open
over \(\mathbf C\), the cubic cover of (C_B) defined by (W) is
connected and étale: the valuations are multiples of three, and the
nonzero torsion class rules out a cube in the function field. It has genus
four. The involution ((\eta,W)\mapsto(-\eta,q/W)) fixes (z); together
with (W\mapsto\zeta_3W) it gives the (S_3) Galois closure of the
elliptic triple cover. The torsion class, up to sign, records that cyclic
triple cover. This describes actual marked geometry behind the equations.
It does **not** recover the marked point (o) from an unmarked cubic
threefold, nor prove a degree-one moduli map.

## 5. The intermediate-Jacobian curve is a separate comparison target

For the visible non-Eckardt involution, projection from the pointwise fixed
line gives the determinant

\[
\det\begin{pmatrix}z-x&3y&0\\3y&3(z+x)&0\\0&0&g\end{pmatrix}
=3(z^2-q)g.
\]

Its discriminant consists of the fixed plane cubic (E_B) and the smooth
conic (Q:z^2=q). Casalaina-Martin–Marquand–Zhang, v2, Lemma 2.4 and
Theorem 2.9 identify the anti-invariant part of the intermediate Jacobian
over \(\mathbf C\) with the Jacobian of the double cover (\widetilde Q)
of this conic branched at (Q\cap E_B), equipped with twice its principal
polarization. The plane cubic and conic meet transversely when (X_B) is
smooth, as required by that theorem.

Using the rational parametrization

\[
[x:y:z]=[1-3t^2:2t:1+3t^2],
\]

an affine equation for the discriminant double cover is

\[
\widetilde Q_B:\ h^2=-\frac32
\left(4B(1-3t^2,2t)-(1+3t^2)^3\right).
\]

Indeed, on the conic the splitting function is (-(z-x)g); removing the
square (t^2) gives this equation. The constant twist is immaterial over
\(\mathbf C\), but is retained for ground-field use. Its branch polynomial
at (B=y^3) is

\[
-27t^6-27t^4+32t^3-9t^2-1,
\]

which the checker verifies is squarefree. In contrast, (C_B) uses
(16B(x,y)^2-q(x,y)^3). Both constructions are concrete, but no isomorphism
or isogeny between their Jacobians is proved here.

There is an exact connection at the level of branch equations:

\[
\operatorname{Res}_z(z^2-q,4g)=16B^2-q^3.
\]

This expresses projection of the six conic/cubic intersection points to
([x:y]). It does not identify the two double covers, because the map from
the conic to ([x:y]) has degree two. The comparison between (C_B),
\(\widetilde Q_B\), and the marked projection is now a precise follow-on
target rather than an unspecified search for a Jacobian factor.

## 6. Cremona consequences and attribution boundary

Apply `2026-09-08-c1128-torus-audit.md` to each cubic in the accepted locus.
The rational rank-two torus actions on \(\mathbf A^5\) have exact trivial
linearization level two; their rank-one restrictions have level one and
their finite-subgroup quotient fields are rational over \(\mathbf C\).

Conjugacy of two such rank-two subgroups identifies their invariant fields,
hence makes the cubics birational. Kuznetsov, arXiv:math/0303037v1, Remark
2.19, explicitly states that smooth complex cubic threefolds are birational
if and only if isomorphic, invoking the classical Torelli theory. We checked
that exact source statement, not a new proof of the original Torelli inputs.
Thus distinct cubic moduli points yield nonconjugate subgroups. There is a
three-dimensional collection of conjugacy classes; a parameter chart may
still contain finitely many points representing the same class. After two
fixed variables, all these subgroups become conjugate to the standard
rank-two diagonal subgroup of \(\operatorname{Cr}_7\).

No regular polynomial actions on all of affine space are asserted. A
uniform rational action can be spread from a generic rationalization after
shrinking a parameter chart; no global fine moduli space of actions or
canonical choice of rationalization is claimed.
