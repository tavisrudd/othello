## The exact result to position

Here is the formulation that the audit supports as the working theorem.  It
is deliberately a theorem about a *marked elliptic-power presentation*, not a
bare ppav.

> **Finite-etale graph cofactor-saturation theorem (working form).**  Let
> `R=Z/p^a`, let `B` be a unimodular symmetric coefficient form on a free
> `R`-module `M`, and let a maximal-isotropic graph in the `p^a B` block be
> given by `T`.  In the matrix convention used here, graph isotropy is
> 
> \[
>                 BT^t\equiv TB\pmod {p^a}.
> \tag{1}
> \]
>
> (Equivalently, say explicitly which `B`-adjoint convention is being used;
> (1) is adjointness for `B^{-1}`, not automatically for `B`.)  If `R[T]` is
> finite etale over `R`, then, after the associated polarized elliptic-power
> graph quotient, the divided polarization cofactor
> \(
> \Theta^{g-1}/(g-1)!
> \)
> lies in the ordinary integral lattice generated by products of `g-1`
> divisor classes.

The proof mechanism is the nonstandard portion: split `R[T]` after a finite
unramified faithfully flat extension, use self-adjoint primitive idempotents
and exact mixed-adjugate identities to obtain the full cofactor there, then
reflect membership in the quotient lattice along the faithfully flat map.  A
trace or a normalized corestriction is neither used nor allowed: it could
multiply the class by a residue degree divisible by `p`.

For `a=1`, finite etaleness is squarefreeness of the *minimal* polynomial.
For `a>1`, it is strictly stronger than squarefreeness after reduction.  The
condition is invariant under changes of transverse **elliptic** ruling, where
the slopes are related by a defined fractional-linear transformation, but not
under arbitrary symplectic coordinates.  This chart restriction is part of
the theorem, not a cosmetic presentation issue.

### Best second application

The best current application is the prime-power root-weight family
`G_N=N I_{N-1}-J`, with `N=p^a`.  At the bad prime, its sum-zero block is
`p^aB` with `B` unimodular.  For odd `p` and `a>=2`, a rank-one trace transfer
from the unramified degree-`N-2` algebra supplies a field-valued slope.  For
`p=2`, `a>=3`, the intended construction uses an odd-degree unramified algebra
on a hyperbolic rank-two module.  In both cases `R[T]` is an unramified field,
not a split product, and `p | (N-2)!`.  Consequently this is a genuine test of
the theorem precisely where the factorial bound alone does not force
primitivity.

The application is worth retaining because it exhibits the descent argument
without an eigenvalue-by-eigenvalue split over the base ring, and because its
centralizer/orthogonal-idempotent argument can make the quotient polarized-
indecomposable when `End(E)=Z`.  It is *not* evidence for an all-Lagrangian
or presentation-free assertion.

## Red-team corrections before any paper claim

1. **Adjoint convention.**  Do not write simply ```T` is `B`-self-adjoint''
   and then use (1) unless the adjoint is declared.  The wrong convention
   silently changes the Neron--Severi integrality condition.

2. **Finite etale is higher-order data.**  At exponent `a>1`, squarefree
   reduction does not imply that `R[T]` is flat or etale.  The theorem must
   assume literal finite etaleness of `R[T]`.

3. **Faithfully flat descent must be lattice descent.**  The proof needs the
   product lattice to commute with unramified base change and its quotient to
   be detected faithfully flatly.  A trace average is not an alternative.

4. **Dyadic root transfer has an outstanding citation/proof gap.**  The
   assertion that the residual form `I+J` in rank `2q` is integrally hyperbolic
   over `Z/2^a` needs either an explicit Gram isometry or a precisely checked
   dyadic-unimodular-lattice reference.  Determinant/norm alone do not
   classify dyadic integral symmetric forms; parity and Arf-type information
   matter.  Jacobowitz was not read at a depth that can discharge this.

5. **Indecomposable is not simple.**  The root application may conclude
   polarized indecomposability under `End(E)=Z`; it must not advertise
   absolute simplicity, or claim a classification of all slopes.

6. **The scope is marked graphs.**  Finite etaleness survives only the
   allowed fractional-linear changes of transverse elliptic ruling.  It is
   not an invariant of an arbitrary Lagrangian chart or of the target ppav
   alone.

## Venue and priority ceiling

The current material is not an Annals/Inventiones package: it has neither an
all-Lagrangian invariant nor a non-etale defect classification, and the
root family is an application built from classical local-form machinery.

If the graph theorem is written with a complete integral Neron--Severi/product
calculation, a genuinely denominator-free finite-flat descent lemma, and the
nonsplit root construction is repaired at `p=2`, a focused paper has a
credible **Algebra & Number Theory / strong specialized algebraic-geometry
venue** ceiling.  It could reach the Compositio/Math. Ann. range only if the
theorem is made intrinsic across a substantial moduli class or is combined
with a new geometric consequence beyond an explicit ppav family.

The high-ceiling successor remains the C904 `p`-typical non-etale defect
theory: an intrinsic stratification of the integral Lefschetz saturation by
radical/Jordan thickness, with realizations.  The finite-etale theorem is the
zero-radical base case of that programme, and the nonsplit root examples are
the right stress test, not its replacement.
