# C909 — exact codimension-two Hodge/product quotient for the etale tower

Date: 2026-08-11  
Status: local theorem and faithful-flat descent audit; no manuscript, PDF,
mirror, Lean, or commit change

## Theorem and verdict

Consider the one-depth irreducible finite-etale tower at an odd prime \(p\):
the source coefficient block is \(p^a I_g\), the slope algebra is the
unramified degree-\(g\) algebra, and \(E\) is non-CM.  Write

\[
 I_A^2=\operatorname{Hdg}^{4}(A,\mathbf Z),\qquad
 P_A^2=\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}\operatorname{NS}(A)
          \to H^{4}(A,\mathbf Z)\bigr).
\]

Then

\[
 I_A^2/P_A^2\ \simeq\ (\mathbf Z/p^a)^{\binom g4}.
\tag{1}
\]

The statement is global for the \(p\)-primary graph quotient.  After a
finite unramified splitting extension \(S/\mathbf Z_p\), the corresponding
local quotient is

\[
 (I_A^2\otimes S)/(P_A^2\otimes S)
 \ \simeq\ (S/p^aS)^{\binom g4}.
\tag{2}
\]

Thus every \((2,2)\) and \((2,1,1)\) slot multidegree is product-saturated,
while each four-distinct-slot \((1,1,1,1)\) component contributes exactly
one copy of \(S/p^aS\).  The descended module is free of rank
\(\binom g4\) over \(\mathbf Z_p/p^a\); the Galois permutation of split
slots is descent data, not an extra multiplicity.

This sharpens the earlier four-slot counterexample: the entire codimension
two defect is now computed, not merely shown nonzero.

## 1. Split coordinates and the invariant lattice

Put \(R=\mathbf Z_p\), \(\pi=p^a\), and let \(S/R\) be a finite unramified
extension splitting the slope algebra.  In split coefficient coordinates,

\[
 T=\operatorname{diag}(t_1,\ldots,t_g),\qquad
 \delta_{ij}=t_j-t_i\in S^\times\quad(i\ne j).
\tag{3}
\]

If \(x_i,y_i\) are the degree-one classes pulled back from \(E^g\), the
pullback lattice \(W=f_a^*H^1(A,S)\) has basis

\[
 X_i=\pi x_i,\qquad Y_i=y_i-t_i x_i.
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently

\[
 x_i=\pi^{-1}X_i,\qquad
 y_i=Y_i+t_i\pi^{-1}X_i.
\tag{5}
\]

For the diagonal \(\mathrm{SL}_2\)-invariant contractions set

\[
 \Omega_{ii}=x_i\wedge y_i,\qquad
 \Omega_{ij}=x_i\wedge y_j+x_j\wedge y_i\quad(i\ne j),
\tag{6}
\]

and \(B_{ij}=X_i\wedge Y_j+X_j\wedge Y_i\).  Direct substitution gives

\[
 \Omega_{ii}=\pi^{-1}X_i\wedge Y_i,\qquad
 \Omega_{ij}=\pi^{-1}B_{ij}+\pi^{-2}\delta_{ij}X_i\wedge X_j.
\tag{7}
\]

The graph NS lattice in these coordinates has

\[
 D_i=\pi\Omega_{ii},\qquad
 D_{ij}=\pi^2\Omega_{ij}\quad(i<j)
\tag{8}
\]

as its primitive diagonal and off-diagonal divisor generators.  This is the
split form of the exact matrix-of-ideals calculation.

Because \(E\) is non-CM and \(A\) is isogenous to \(E^g\), the rational
degree-four Hodge space is the diagonal \(\mathrm{SL}_2\)-invariant space.
It decomposes directly by the number of occurrences of each split slot.
Each slot contributes at most two degree-one factors.

## 2. The \((2,2)\) and \((2,1,1)\) pieces

For distinct \(i,j\), the \((2,2)\) component is one-dimensional with basis
\(\Omega_{ii}\Omega_{jj}\).  From (7), its coefficient is integral in
\(\bigwedge^4W\) exactly when

\[
 c\,\Omega_{ii}\Omega_{jj}\in\bigwedge^4W
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad c\in\pi^2S.
\tag{9}
\]

The right side is generated by \(D_iD_j\), so this component contributes no
quotient.

For distinct \(i,j,k\), the \((2,1,1)\) component is one-dimensional.  Use
\(M_{i;jk}=\Omega_{ii}\Omega_{jk}\).  Its expansion contains the unique
monomial

\[
 \pi^{-3}\delta_{jk}
 (X_i\wedge Y_i)\wedge X_j\wedge X_k.
\tag{10}
\]

Since \(\delta_{jk}\) is a unit, integrality is equivalent to
\(c\in\pi^3S\); then all remaining terms, which have denominator at most
\(\pi^2\), are integral.  The lattice is generated by

\[
 D_iD_{jk}=\pi^3\Omega_{ii}\Omega_{jk}.
\tag{11}
\]

The alternative matching \(\Omega_{ij}\Omega_{ik}\) is the same invariant
up to the standard contraction sign, so it supplies no second direction.
Thus every \((2,1,1)\) component is also product-saturated.

## 3. One four-slot defect

Fix \(J=\{1,2,3,4\}\).  The \((1,1,1,1)\) invariant space has rank two.
Use

\[
 M_1=\Omega_{12}\Omega_{34},\qquad
 M_2=\Omega_{13}\Omega_{24}
\tag{12}
\]

as a basis; the third matching is eliminated by the Pluecker relation.
For \(c_1,c_2\in\operatorname{Frac}(S)\), expand
\(c_1M_1+c_2M_2\) with (7).

The two independent \(\pi^{-3}\)-coefficient rows in the expansions of
\(M_1,M_2\) have unit determinant after the top
\(X_1X_2X_3X_4\) row is removed.  Equivalently, the relevant minor is a
unit multiple of
\(\delta_{13}\delta_{34}(\delta_{12}+\delta_{24})
=\delta_{13}\delta_{34}\delta_{14}\).  Therefore integrality first forces

\[
 c_1,c_2\in\pi^3S.
\tag{13}
\]

After writing \(c_i=\pi^3u_i\), the only remaining condition is cancellation
of the \(\pi^{-4}\) all-\(X\) term:

\[
 \delta_{12}\delta_{34}u_1
 -\delta_{13}\delta_{24}u_2\ \in\ \pi S.
\tag{14}
\]

The minus sign is from
\(X_1\wedge X_3\wedge X_2\wedge X_4
 =-X_1\wedge X_2\wedge X_3\wedge X_4\).
No further condition remains: the \(\pi^{-2}\)-terms become integral once
(13) holds.

The product lattice in this component is exactly

\[
 P_J=\pi^4S M_1+\pi^4S M_2,
\tag{15}
\]

because the primitive off-diagonal divisors are \(D_{ij}=\pi^2\Omega_{ij}\).
The Hodge lattice is

\[
 I_J=\left\{\pi^3(u_1M_1+u_2M_2):
 \delta_{12}\delta_{34}u_1-\delta_{13}\delta_{24}u_2
 \in\pi S\right\}.
\tag{16}
\]

The map

\[
 \varphi:S^2\longrightarrow S/\pi S,\qquad
 (u_1,u_2)\longmapsto
 \delta_{12}\delta_{34}u_1-\delta_{13}\delta_{24}u_2
 \pmod{\pi}
\tag{17}
\]

is surjective because both displayed coefficients are units.  Equations
(15)--(17) give

\[
 I_J/P_J
 \simeq \ker(\varphi)/\pi S^2
 \simeq S/\pi S.
\tag{18}
\]

This is exactly one \(p^a\)-defect direction per four-subset, not two:
the Hodge component has rank two, but only one independent highest-term
cancellation lowers the product valuation from \(\pi^4\) to \(\pi^3\).

## 4. Complete split quotient

The slot multidegree decomposition is direct, and the only possible
codimension-two patterns are \((2,2)\), \((2,1,1)\), and \((1,1,1,1)\).
Combining (9), (11), and (18) gives

\[
 (I_A^2\otimes_R S)/(P_A^2\otimes_R S)
 \simeq (S/\pi S)^{\binom g4}
 = (S/p^aS)^{\binom g4}.
\tag{19}
\]

For \(g\le3\) the last summand is absent, recovering codimension-two
Hodge/product equality.  For \(g=4\) there is one defect copy, and the
class from the earlier four-slot note is a generator after choosing a
kernel basis in (17).

## 5. Faithfully flat descent and global integral quotient

Let

\[
 Q_R=I_A^2(R)/P_A^2(R)
\]

denote the \(p\)-adic quotient.  The Hodge lattice is defined by a rational
Hodge subspace intersected with the torsion-free cohomology lattice, so
finite free coefficient extension gives

\[
 I_A^2(R)\otimes_R S=I_A^2(S).
\tag{20}
\]

The divisor-product image also commutes with base change: the NS lattice is
finite free, and flatness gives

\[
 P_A^2(R)\otimes_R S=P_A^2(S).
\tag{21}
\]

Tensoring \(0\to P_A^2(R)\to I_A^2(R)\to Q_R\to0\) with the faithfully flat
ring \(S\) is exact.  Thus (19) implies

\[
 Q_R\otimes_R S\simeq(S/p^aS)^{\binom g4}.
\tag{22}
\]

In particular \(p^aQ_R=0\), by faithful flatness.  As a finite
\(R/p^a\)-module, \(Q_R\) becomes free after the faithfully flat extension
\(S/p^aS\); faithfully flat descent makes it finite projective of rank
\(\binom g4\).  Since \(R/p^a\) is local,

\[
 Q_R\simeq(\mathbf Z_p/p^a)^{\binom g4}.
\tag{23}
\]

At every prime \(\ell\ne p\), the graph isogeny is integral-locally
invertible.  The integral invariant tensors on the literal elliptic power
are generated by contraction products in degree four, so the quotient
localizes to zero.  Therefore the global quotient for the tower is

\[
 I_A^2/P_A^2\simeq(\mathbf Z/p^a)^{\binom g4}.
\tag{24}
\]

No Chow statement or non-etale input is used.  The defect is a finite-etale
integral Hodge-lattice cancellation.

## Final grade

**GO:** the codimension-two quotient is exactly (24), with split form (19).
The earlier non-equality was not an isolated counterexample: all and only
the four-distinct-slot components contribute, each with length \(a\).
