# C201 Gate 2 — conic-stabilizer order-13 orbits at `q=64`

**Lane:** `relconic`

## Candidate family

This is the first `q=64` family whose symmetry is aligned directly with the
prescribed conic.  In the symmetric-square action of `PGL(2,64)` stabilizing
the standard conic `XZ=Y^2`, choose an order-13 subgroup of a nonsplit torus.
Its action on `PG(2,64)` has one fixed point and 320 orbits of length thirteen.
Five length-thirteen orbits partition the conic; the fixed point is its
nucleus.

There are 315 length-thirteen orbits disjoint from the conic.  Exactly 310 are
arcs.  The exhaustive probe tests all of them, rather than sampling orbit
parameters.

## Exact obstruction

All 310 arcs have the same ordinary secant-index spectrum:

```text
(n0,n1,n2,n3,n4,n5,n6) = (1041,1560,1326,208,0,0,13).
```

Thus every orbit has

```text
|U(A)| = 1041,       6 Delta_U(A) = 6552,
quadratic rank = 6.
```

None is complete outside the 65-point conic.  More sharply, the unique fixed
point—the conic nucleus—is uncovered in every case, so this family fails the
relative-completeness condition before any quadratic-rank argument.  The
full-rank result is again explained by the elementary quadratic point bound
`|U(A)|>2q+1=129`.

This closes the most natural exact-size transitive family: a single
conic-stabilizer orbit of order thirteen cannot continue the `q=16`
low-defect mechanism.  It does not rule out asymmetric thirteen-arcs or unions
of smaller stabilizer orbits.

For scale, Bartoli–Davydov–Faina–Marcugini–Pambianco list 22 as the smallest
known complete-arc size at `q=64`; this is an upper-bound record, not an exact
value or a nonexistence theorem for smaller complete arcs:
<https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.3403>, Table 1.

## Validation and replay

The probe checks all orbit lengths, conic disjointness, the arc condition,
ordinary-uncovered locus, complete index spectrum, defect, quadratic rank,
and forced-hit count.  A fixed nontrivial projectivity plus point reversal
preserves the full profile of all 310 arcs.  Its `GF(64)`, incidence, and rank
implementation is the same one independently cross-checked against the raw
C++ enumerator in the preceding Baer-family gate.
