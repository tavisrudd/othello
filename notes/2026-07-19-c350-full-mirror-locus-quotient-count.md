# C350: full mirror-locus quotient and exact count

## Result

Let `q>3` be odd, fix a nonsquare `delta in F_q`, put `tau(t)=delta/t`, and burn the
`tau`-orbit `{infinity,0}`.  Let `M_q` be the set of four-centre sets made from two distinct
two-element `tau`-orbits such that

1. the four centres together with the burned orbit form a six-arc, and
2. neither centre orbit has a live point adjacent to its `tau`-mate.

Equivalence preserves the conic, the burned orbit, and the marked mirror `tau`, but forgets the
ordering of the two centre orbits and the choice of representative in each orbit.  Then

\[
 |M_q/\!\sim|=
 \frac{(q^2-1)(q-3)^2}{64}
 +\frac{(q-3)(q-\chi(-1))}{16}.
\]

This is an exact count for every odd prime power `q>3`, not only an asymptotic.  Its leading term
is `q^4/64`, with a power-saving `O(q^3)` boundary.  The geometric quotient is an irreducible
four-dimensional finite quotient of the C333 open locus.

The generic stabilizer of the marked configuration is `<tau>`.  Exactly

\[
 F_q=\frac{(q-3)(q-\chi(-1))}{8}
\]

classes have enhanced residual stabilizer `C_2 x C_2`; these are precisely the configurations in
which the residual sign projectivity swaps the two centre orbits.  There are no further stabilizer
types for the marked `tau` problem.  Recovering or forgetting `tau` can enlarge stabilizers and is
deliberately left to C351.

## Quotient coordinates and count

For a centre `(r,c,1)`, write

\[
 u=r+\delta c,\qquad v=r-\delta c,\qquad z=v^2.
\]

Conjugation by `tau` fixes `u` and negates `v`, so a two-element centre orbit is represented
faithfully by `(u,z)`.  Mirror nonadjacency is

\[
 \chi(u^2-4\delta)=-1,
\]

and the two-element condition is `v != 0`.  Because `delta` is nonsquare, nonadjacency already
forces the centre off the conic.  There are `(q+1)/2` possible values of `u`, and `(q-1)/2`
nonzero square values `z`; hence there are exactly

\[
 R_q=\frac{q^2-1}{4}
\]

individual centre orbits.

For two distinct centre orbits, the row collisions, column collisions, and four affine
three-centre determinants form a `tau`-stable incompatibility relation.  Direct elimination in
`(u,v)` gives

\[
 I_q=\frac{(q^2-1)(3q-7)}{16}
\]

incompatible unordered pairs.  Thus the number of legal marked centre sets before the residual
projective quotient is

\[
 L_q=\binom{R_q}{2}-I_q
     =\frac{(q^2-1)(q-3)^2}{32}.
\]

The stabilizer of `(tau,{0,infinity})` in `PGL_2(q)` is a Klein four group.  The element `tau`
already acts trivially on centre sets, leaving one effective involution, induced by `t -> -t`:

\[
 (u_1,z_1;u_2,z_2)\longmapsto(-u_1,z_1;-u_2,z_2).
\]

Its legal fixed locus has size `F_q` above.  Burnside's lemma gives `(L_q+F_q)/2`, proving the
displayed class count.

## Determinant sheets

For one centre orbit, its `PSL_2/PGL_2` determinant character is

\[
 \eta(u,v)=-\chi(u^2-v^2-4\delta).
\]

Writing `epsilon=chi(-1)`, the exact numbers of individual inner (`+`) and outer (`-`) centre
orbits are

\[
 R_q^+=\frac{(q+1)(q-2+\epsilon)}8,
 \qquad
 R_q^-=\frac{(q+1)(q-\epsilon)}8.
\]

Thus the three four-centre sheets are `++`, `+-`, and `--`.  Before the six-arc deletions their
counts are respectively `binom(R_q^+,2)`, `R_q^+R_q^-`, and `binom(R_q^-,2)`; the deletions are
explicit degree-at-most-two character conditions and contribute `O(q^3)` in each sheet.  After
the residual quotient,

\[
 N_{++}=q^4/256+O(q^3),\quad
 N_{+-}=q^4/128+O(q^3),\quad
 N_{--}=q^4/256+O(q^3).
\]

The enhanced fixed locus contains no `+-` point.  Its exact sheet split is

\[
 (F_q^{++},F_q^{--})=
 \begin{cases}
 ((q-1)(q-5)/16,(q-1)^2/16),&\epsilon=1,\\
 ((q-3)(q+1)/16,(q-3)(q+1)/16),&\epsilon=-1.
 \end{cases}
\]

The checker records exact legal and quotient sheet counts field by field.  The exact general
sheet refinements are equivalently the two bounded character sums obtained by weighting every
legal pair by `eta_1 eta_2` and by `eta_1+eta_2`; no uniform polynomial replaces those arithmetic
terms.

## Full-group, exceptional, and subfield strata

Let `H` be generated by the four projection involutions.  Applying the non-self-polar-triangle
subgroup theorem to any three centres gives the exhaustive structural alternatives

\[
 H\cong PSL_2(q_0),\ PGL_2(q_0),\ A_5,\text{ or }S_4
\]

for a subfield `F_(q_0) subseteq F_q`.  The determinant sheet decides the full-field endpoint:
`++` can reach only `PSL_2(q)`, while `+-` and `--` can reach `PGL_2(q)`.  Consequently

\[
 \#\{H=PGL_2(q)\}=3q^4/256+O(q^3),\qquad
 \#\{H=PSL_2(q)\}=q^4/256+O(q^3),
\]

and all proper-group strata together are `O(q^3)`.  This is the promised power-saving full-group
count; it does not pretend that every legal configuration is full-group.

The normalized subfield stratum has an exact intrinsic test.  For every maximal proper subfield
`F_(q_0)`, apply `q_0`-Frobenius to the unordered pair `(u_i,z_i)`.  The class descends precisely
when Frobenius returns the pair either literally or after the residual simultaneous sign.  The
smallest such `q_0`, together with the determinant sheet and generated order, separates the
`PSL_2(q_0)` and `PGL_2(q_0)` rows; the remaining orders `60` and `24` are the `A_5` and `S_4`
rows.  This criterion also exposes the semilinear descent cocycle rather than silently counting
only coordinate-wise subfield points.

## The exact square-`-1` elliptic correction

For the C333 one-dimensional slice, when `chi(-1)=1` the exact raw count is controlled by

\[
 E_\delta:\quad y^2=(b-1)((1+\delta b)^2-4\delta).
\]

Its discriminant and `j`-invariant are

\[
 \operatorname{disc}=16\delta^3(\delta-1)^4,
 \qquad
 j(E_\delta)=
 \frac{16(\delta^2+14\delta+1)^3}{\delta(\delta-1)^4}.
\]

If `S_delta` is the cubic character sum in C333, then

\[
 S_\delta=\#E_\delta(F_q)-q-1,
 \qquad
 N_\delta=\frac{q+1+S_\delta}{4}=\frac{\#E_\delta(F_q)}4.
\]

This identification is exact, but it is not a new elliptic family.  Over the quadratic extension,
putting `a=(delta+1)/sqrt(delta)` identifies its `j`-map with the classical Jacobi-quartic map

\[
 j=16(a^2+12)^3/(a^2-4)^2.
\]

Thus the new content is the four-centre mirror quotient and its use of this arithmetic correction,
not the Jacobi family or its isomorphism theory.
