# Priority-judo survey: where a conceded predecessor can become a corollary

**Date:** 2026-08-02

**Status:** read-only survey. This file is the only write. No manuscript, queue,
handoff, or ledger was touched. No new literature searches were run; every
verdict below rests on audits already in the repository plus one cached
full-text source that `notes/2026-08-02-cross-paper-novelty-ceilings-and-level-ups.md`
records as read at full text.

## The move, as sharpened

The C794 and C803 reports define the manoeuvre; the sharpened form (per the
coordinator's correction) is: **prove — or recognize that we already prove — a
broader statement of which the occupying result is a specialization at their
parameters.** The predecessor stops being a competitor and becomes a corollary
or an input. Secondary forms: compose their theorem with ours so theirs is the
engine of a new statement (C780's dictionary), and answer the first question
their object was never asked. Wording discipline throughout: "we prove", "to
our knowledge", never "first", no novelty claims on elementary parts.

This survey deliberately does not duplicate
`notes/2026-08-02-cross-paper-novelty-ceilings-and-level-ups.md` (the
"ceilings doc"). That document asks what *new* higher theorem to seek; this one
asks which conceded predecessors are *already* special cases of what the papers
prove today. Where the answers coincide I defer to its level-ups.

## 1. AME–LU paper: the local-dimension-two concession is written backwards at the engine level — and the fix needs no new mathematics

**Lane:** `ame-lu`. **Paper:** `papers/ame_lu`. **Strength gained: highest of
this survey, and essentially free.**

### What is conceded today

`papers/ame_lu/sections/01-introduction.tex` (lines 108–118) says: for q=2 the
full-Weyl marginal proposition supplies all three Pauli axes on each minimum
support, "so the AME profile satisfies their criterion. The LU-to-LC conclusion
of Theorem [lu-lc-rigidity] is therefore already covered by their result" —
their = Van den Nest–Dehaene–De Moor (VdN-DDM), quant-ph/0411115.

### The finding: the manuscript already contains the broader theorem, and their result specializes from it — but only one case of it

I checked the specialization in both directions against the cached full text of
VdN-DDM (cache key `arXiv:quant-ph/0411115`, SHA-256
`c0f8e192552369d5af9304ebf08995f59b6917e243a570f37ff1b29f3b4cb735`, the source
the ceilings doc records as read at full text), and against the paper's own
proof in `papers/ame_lu/sections/03-lu-rigidity.tex`.

**The paper's engine is not an AME theorem.** Corollary `full-weyl-cover`
(03-lu-rigidity.tex, lines 69–84) is stated for **arbitrary n-qudit states, any
local dimension, no stabilizer hypothesis**: if a product unitary carries one
state to the other and every party belongs to a set of at least three parties
on which both reduced operators are full-Weyl diagonal, then every local factor
is Clifford. The AME hypothesis enters only afterwards, in Proposition
`stabilizer-ame-support`, to verify the cover condition. The proof of the
engine (Proposition `full-weyl-marginal` + Lemma `diagonal-axes`) uses nothing
about AME, CSS form, or F_q-linearity.

**VdN-DDM's Corollary 1, case (iv), is exactly the q=2 stabilizer
specialization of that engine.** Their case (iv), verbatim from the cached
text: "There exists minimal supports ω1, ω2, … satisfying 3 = A_{ω1}(ψ) =
A_{ω2}(ψ) = …, such that every i ∈ {1,…,n} belongs to at least one ωj" ⟹
LU(ψ) = LC(ψ). Checking the reduction:

- A_ω = 3 at a minimal support ω means (their Lemma 1 proof) the elements
  supported inside ω form a group {I, M, M′, MM′} of order 4 = q² whose local
  components at every i ∈ ω run through {σx, σy, σz} — i.e. every coordinate
  projection of the label group is bijective. The reduced operator ρ_ω is the
  sum over exactly these four elements with nonzero (±) coefficients. That is
  the paper's full-Weyl diagonal condition (3.2) at q = 2, verbatim.
- The condition transfers to the second state automatically: minimal supports
  and A_ω are LU invariants (their §"Minimal supports"), so ρ^{ψ′}_ω is
  full-Weyl diagonal too, which is what Corollary `full-weyl-cover` needs on
  both sides.
- Boundary check: A_ω = 3 forces |ω| even, and full entanglement forces
  |ω| ≥ 4 (their Theorem 1 proof), clearing the corollary's |S| ≥ 3 floor. The
  |ω| = 2 exception is the Bell factor — the same m ≥ 2 boundary the paper
  already states as sharp.

So the composition runs in the C794 direction: **our Corollary
`full-weyl-cover` is the general theorem; VdN-DDM Corollary 1(iv) is its
q = 2 case.**

**What is genuinely NOT swallowed — and must stay conceded.** VdN-DDM's
Theorem 1 is strictly finer at q=2: its hypothesis is only that σx, σy, σz
each occur on every qubit **within the subgroup M(ψ) generated by all minimal
elements** — allowing A_ω = 1 supports and allowing the three Paulis at a qubit
to come from *different* minimal supports. Their proof handles the mixed case
by playing two minimal elements with different local Paulis against each other;
the paper's diagonal-marginal argument needs the whole local Weyl basis carried
by a *single* support. Our engine does not imply their Theorem 1, and no
rewording should claim it does. Cases (i)–(iii) of their Corollary 1 (S(ψ)
generated by minimal elements; GF(4)-linear stabilizers; the induction
condition) reduce to Theorem 1, not to case (iv), so they are also not
swallowed.

### The prescribed rewrite

The introduction paragraph should be inverted from concession to
specialization, precisely:

1. Present the full-Weyl marginal-cover corollary as a named, free-standing
   rigidity criterion (any local dimension, any states), not as a step inside
   the AME proof.
2. State: at q = 2, for stabilizer states, the criterion specializes exactly to
   case (iv) of VdN-DDM's Corollary 1; our theorem is the all-prime-power,
   arbitrary-state form of that case, and the stabilizer-AME support theorem is
   what verifies its hypothesis at every prime power.
3. Concede plainly, in the same breath: their Theorem 1 is a strictly finer
   qubit criterion (three Paulis per qubit within the minimal-support subgroup,
   mixed supports allowed) which we do not generalize.
4. Pose the residual as the first question their criterion was never asked
   beyond q = 2: **is there a prime-power minimal-support criterion whose q = 2
   case is their full Theorem 1** — i.e. can the single-support full-Weyl
   hypothesis be relaxed to "the local labels occurring at each party among
   minimal elements exhaust F_q²" with labels drawn from several supports? That
   is a real open question, in their vocabulary, at our generality, and it
   makes the paper the owner of the generalization frontier instead of a
   tenant of their qubit case.

No new theorem is required for steps 1–3; the manuscript already proves
everything cited. Step 4 is optional new work. **Audit flag:** none needed for
the specialization itself (the source is read at full text with a recorded
hash); ordinary care is needed only to cite Corollary 1(iv) rather than
Theorem 1 as the specialized statement, and to keep Rains's Theorem 13 credit
as is.

### The Tan concession, same test

Introduction lines 125–129 concede that Tan's four-qutrit computation
(arXiv:2601.19677, Theorem 5.3, local symmetry group of order 5832 with
Clifford local factors) "contains the q=3, m=2 automorphism subcase above."
The direction here is genuinely ours already: Theorem `lu-lc-rigidity` at
q = 3, m = 2 predicts that every local factor of every symmetry of AME(4,3) is
Clifford — Tan's Theorem 5.3 is an instance, and the fact that Tan's displayed
generators have qutrit-Clifford local factors is a *confirmation* of our
theorem, not a pre-emption of it. The rewrite: cite Tan's computation as an
independent instance and falsifier of the rigidity theorem and (once verified)
of the atlas exact sequence, exactly as C794 demoted its seven-vertex census.

**Gate before claiming the stronger form:** the atlas classification (the
centralizer-of-holonomies formula) should be shown to reproduce the order 5832
computationally. That is the same "cheapest falsifier" as the ceilings doc's
level-up 4 (correlation-tensor tomography); I defer the tomography ambition to
that document. But note the judo consequence the ceilings doc does not draw:
**if the atlas reproduces Tan's group, part of level-up 4's motivation is
already discharged for free** — the atlas is then demonstrably a computing
device, not only a proof device, and the intro can say so with Tan as the
worked example.

## 2. Two-uniform rigidity (Theorem A): discharge the finiteness hypothesis the AME literature carries conditionally

**Lane:** `ame-lu`. **Source audit:** `notes/2026-08-01-c775-two-uniform-rigidity-literature-audit.md`.
**Strength gained: high, one paragraph of writing plus one gated read.**

**What is conceded:** discreteness for binary stabilizer states is
Wirthmüller's (Theorem 7 + Corollary 11), and the four-qutrit instance is
Tan's. Both concessions are correct and stay.

**The composition available:** the C775 audit records Tan writing, verbatim:
"In general, for any r-uniform state |φ⟩, S(|φ⟩) ≤ U_D^{⊗n} **whenever
S(|φ⟩) is finite** [Gour–Wallach, Proposition 6]." The 2026 literature carries
finiteness as a standing side hypothesis. Theorem A proves that hypothesis for
every 2-uniform state, any local dimension. The composed statement nobody has:

> For every 2-uniform pure state, the local symmetry group is finite and
> (by Gour–Wallach Proposition 6, as used by Tan) embeds in diagonal form
> U_D^{⊗n}.

Their proposition becomes the second factor of a two-step theorem whose first
factor is ours; every conditional sentence of that shape in the literature
becomes unconditional for k ≥ 2. This is the C794 shape exactly: the published
result is the input, ours closes the hypothesis it was waiting on.

**Not a swallow:** Theorem A does not swallow Wirthmüller — his scope (all
binary stabilizer codes, with an exact identity-component computation and a
protected-qubit criterion) is transverse to ours (all 2-uniform states, all
dimensions, finiteness only). The audit's existing citation plan is right.

**Audit flag (blocking):** Gour–Wallach Proposition 6 was read at
abstract/metadata depth only (via Tan's citation). Before the composed
statement enters a manuscript, Proposition 6's exact hypotheses must be
verified from its full text — in particular whether "finite" there means
finite modulo phase and whether the diagonal form is up to local unitary
conjugation. One read, already located (arXiv:1103.5096).

## 3. Diagonal rigidity (C780): the audit already executed the judo; what is genuinely left is one composition and one owned question — the dictionary cannot be upgraded to a swallow

**Lane:** `ame-lu`. **Source audit:** `notes/2026-08-01-c780-diagonal-rigidity-novelty-audit.md`.

The brief asked whether more than the dictionary is available. Judgment:
**yes, but both extra items are already identified inside the audit, and
neither is a swallow.** Stated in the pattern's terms so they are not
underplayed when the manuscript is written:

1. **The composed existence theorem** (audit disposition item 1): claim 7
   ("finite diagonal symmetry group ⟺ projective code") plus claim 3 turn
   Honold–Kiermaier–Kurz–Wassermann's completed length classification into a
   complete, published-input answer to a quantum question no one asked: a CSS
   coset state with finite diagonal symmetry group containing a non-Clifford
   order-eight element exists exactly at lengths {15, 16, 30, 31, 32, 45–51}
   ∪ Z≥60. Their theorem is the engine; the quantum statement is new. This is
   composition at full C794 grade and should lead the section.
2. **The first question their object was never asked:** whether the d⊥ ≥ 5
   length set has a cofinite tail. The audit's structural observation — every
   length-lifting construction in the divisible-codes toolkit adjoins an
   (affine) subspace and destroys 4-independence, so the semigroup argument
   that makes the projective case cofinite has no analogue one rung up — is
   the paper-owned lemma, and C779 owns its resolution.
3. **What cannot be done:** the length classification itself cannot be
   swallowed. It is a hard finite-geometry result at parameters we do not
   reprove; it stays an input forever. Any framing that makes our plateau
   look like it generalizes their theorem would be a forced reframing and
   should be refused — the audit's demotion of the plateau to a computational
   remark on [49, 69] ∪ [75, 80] is the correct posture.

**One conditional swallow worth recording, gated:** claim 1 (the
Smith-normal-form classification, torus T^{n−r} × torsion) computes the
identity component of the *diagonal* symmetry group of a CSS coset state. Via
Gross–Van den Nest Theorem 1 (product symmetry = Clifford · diagonal ·
Clifford — proved, per the audit's correction), a complete reduction would make
Wirthmüller's identity-component theorem, restricted to binary CSS coset
states, a corollary of claim 1. The audit itself flags why this cannot be
claimed yet: the residue of the GVdN reduction is a two-state diagonal problem
(the diagonal factor relates two local-Clifford images, not the state to
itself), the same scope gap recorded for C784. Until that residue is closed,
this is a candidate, not a claim. **Audit flag:** blocked on the C784-scope
argument, not on literature.

## 4. Clebsch Paper II: the trade-only classification is the judo form of level-up 5 — it would convert the conceded classical endpoint from input to conclusion

**Lane:** `clebsch`. **Source audits:**
`notes/2026-08-02-c803-paper-ii-fixed-line-literature-audit.md`, ceilings doc
level-up 5.

C803 conceded the exceptional one-factorizations to Cameron–Korchmáros and Han
and relocated the claim to the fixed-line/Chow bridge — the "classical
endpoint, new bridge" form. The ceilings doc's level-up 5 (trade-only
classification: prove the one-dimensional two-valued quadratic trade alone
forces the sheet structure and hence the B3/F7 and H3/F11 configurations)
is, viewed through the sharpened pattern, the upgrade from *bridge* to
*swallow-adjacent*: the hidden one-factorization becomes a **conclusion** of
the trade classification rather than a carried hypothesis, so the classical
objects appear in the paper as outputs of our theorem rather than imported
inputs. Cameron–Korchmáros/Han's full doubly-transitive classification is
still not swallowed — only the occurrence of the two exceptional objects
inside our configurations changes status — and the citation stays.

I defer entirely to level-up 5 for route, falsifier (the q=121 nonretracting
L(6) module gate, C665), and effort. The point this survey adds: if level-up 5
lands, the C803 concession paragraph should be rewritten a second time, from
"the combinatorial endpoint is classical" to "the classical combinatorial
endpoint is recovered by the trade classification," which is a strictly
stronger position than the bridge framing. Worth stating now so the eventual
rewrite is planned rather than rediscovered.

## 5. Arcs complete outside a conic: the line-hole literature is already a specialization of the defect identity — make its results corollaries, not neighbours

**Lane:** `relconic` (entry handoff `notes/handoffs/2026-07-17-c210.md`).
**Source:** snapshot section "The prescribed hole answers a question already
asked."

**What is conceded:** the line-hole instance of the prescribed-hole framework
— completeness outside a line = complete affine arcs — sits inside the
hyperfocused-arc / secret-sharing line (Giulietti–Montanucci;
Korchmáros–Szőnyi), who "state explicitly that some such constructions are
complete in exactly this sense." The snapshot words this carefully as "the
line-hole instance was previously asked for."

**The available move:** the general defect identity, its equality pattern, and
the zero-defect rigidity theorem are proved for an *arbitrary* prescribed
hole; the line is one value of the parameter. The C794-shaped rewrite: state
the specialization as a theorem — "at the line hole, the defect identity
yields [explicit complete-affine-arc bound], with the zero-defect theorem
classifying equality" — and then check whether the completeness statements and
any size bounds in the hyperfocused literature are *implied by* that
specialization. If they are, their results become corollaries and worked
examples of the identity, and the paper owns the general parameter with their
question as its oldest instance. The snapshot's incidence count (every secant
meets a disjoint line exactly once, so the count is C(k,2)) suggests the
specialization is clean.

**Where it can fail, stated plainly:** if Korchmáros–Szőnyi's completeness
results use structure (even characteristic, group-theoretic hyperfocus
structure) that the defect identity does not see, the implication will not
hold and the current "previously asked for" wording is the correct final
position. **Audit flag (blocking):** no full-text audit of
Giulietti–Montanucci or Korchmáros–Szőnyi exists in the repository as far as
this survey's inputs record; the exact statements of their completeness
results must be read before any corollary claim. Rank: medium — real if it
lands, cheap to test, unaudited today.

## Where the move is NOT available — plain negatives

- **PRS beyond-redundancy-four, the higher-Lucas endpoint criterion
  (Wang–Wu–Hu Proposition 11).** Verified collision; the ceilings doc says
  import, and nothing the paper proves today has their proposition as a
  special case. The judo here would require the carrier-wide classification
  (level-up 2), which is new work, not a reframing. Until then: import and
  cite. Lane `reed-solomon`.
- **Two-uniform Fisher isotropy (C775 claim 3).** Braunstein–Caves and
  Hyllus/Tóth own the machinery; our content is a one-line specialization
  plus an interpretation. The audit's prescription (remark with citations, no
  theorem number) is correct; any broader-statement framing would be
  manufactured. Same for claim 2's Taylor/Łojasiewicz ingredients.
- **The golden operator programme's five pre-emptions** (snapshot "Priority"
  section): the Howard–Millson–Snowden–Vakil centered-square formula is theirs
  verbatim, the six-sisters/pentagon material is theirs and Seidel's, the
  Fano-component realization is largely Gripaios–Nguyen's, the order-ten
  shadow is classical, the anomaly inverse is prior art. I found no broader
  statement in the current results of which any of these is a special case;
  the surviving operator layer is already the correct relocation, and C794 is
  already this programme's executed judo. Attribution surgery, as the snapshot
  says — nothing more. Lane `golden`.
- **Paper IV passant code vs Droms–Mellinger–Meyer / Ma–Liu–Tian.** The exact
  d = 12 theorem supersedes the published bound 8 ≤ d ≤ 12 in the ordinary
  way a solution supersedes a bound; calling that judo would inflate it. The
  family-faithfulness question is level-up 3's, and it is new work. Lane
  `clebsch`.
- **Wirthmüller vs Theorem A directly.** Transverse scopes (all binary
  stabilizer codes with exact identity component, vs all 2-uniform states
  with finiteness only); neither swallows the other. The only path is the
  conditional CSS-restricted route via C780 claim 1 recorded in item 3 above.
- **Continuation, complete-ports, frob-eq, nofil, cap-game lanes:** no
  repository audit identifies a conceded predecessor to relocate against, and
  the brief forbids new searches. Not assessed rather than negative.
