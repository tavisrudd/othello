### 2.1 The per-state constant `4(nd + 1)` is the interior constant, and the arity-bounded envelope is about six times too pessimistic — defect

This is the finding that matters, because probe 1a's envelope is what promoted probe 7 from "gated"
to "expected necessary" and what the whole task's scaling story rests on.

Probe 1a § 3 derives `transitions ≈ 4 · n · d · |X_k|` bytes and therefore `4(nd + 1)` bytes per
state, with the parenthetical "(interior sorts)" attached to the derivation and then dropped from
the conclusion. In the arity-bounded regime the interior sorts are a vanishing minority. A sort
whose support has size `j < k` carries `nd` generators; a sort at the bound `j = k` carries only
`kd`, because the generators that would add a further variable are omitted — which is exactly the
mechanism probe 1a itself proposes in § 2 and the lowering implements in `sort_generator_count`.
And `C(n,k) d^k` dominates `Σ_{j<k} C(n,j) d^j`.

Recomputing the true average from the same model:

| `n` | `k` | `d` | nominal `4(nd+1)` | realized bytes/state | overstatement | `|X|` at 2 GB, nominal | `|X|` at 2 GB, realized |
|-----|-----|-----|-------------------|----------------------|---------------|------------------------|-------------------------|
| 20  | 2   | 2   | 164               | 27.4                 | `6.0x`        | `1.2e7`                | `7.3e7`                 |
| 20  | 3   | 2   | 164               | 39.0                 | `4.2x`        | `1.2e7`                | `5.1e7`                 |
| 30  | 2   | 2   | 244               | 27.6                 | `8.8x`        | `8.2e6`                | `7.2e7`                 |
| 10  | 3   | 2   | 84                | 37.7                 | `2.2x`        | `2.4e7`                | `5.3e7`                 |

The consequences run through every downstream sentence probe 1a wrote:

- The envelope table's `max |U|` column is the cap divided by `π_k`, so it is wrong by the same
  factor. At `n = 20, d = 2, k = 2` the correct figure is about `91,000`, not `~15,000`. At
  `n = 30, k = 2` it is about `40,000`, not `~6,900`.
- "With a declared exogenous alphabet of about `10^4` states, arity 2 to 3 on 20 to 30 endogenous
  variables is comfortable, and full arity is not" understates by an order of magnitude: `10^5` is
  the right headline.
- "Applied fixtures … fit, at arity 2 to 3, up to roughly 14 independent binary exogenous sources"
  should read roughly 16 (`2^16 = 65,536` fits under `91,000`; `2^17` does not).
- "A failure vector over 14 components gives `|U| = 16,384`, which sits at `1.3 × 10^7` states with
  `n = 20, k = 2`, right at the ceiling" — the state count is right, the "right at the ceiling"
  reading is not; it is comfortably inside a `7.3e7` ceiling.
- The one conclusion that does **not** move is the canonical-exogenous verdict, because that fails
  on `|U|` by six to twelve orders of magnitude and no constant of order ten rescues it. So probe
  7's promotion survives the correction on its real argument (`16^10 ≈ 1.1e12` for ten binary
  variables with two parents each, which I confirmed) even though the argument probe 1a wrote next
  to it — the flat envelope being narrow — is six times less binding than stated.

The same error is visible inside probe 1's own report table, which is why it is a defect rather than
a modelling choice. The "B/state" column prints `carrier_cost().bytes_per_state`, which
`src/causal.rs` computes as `4 * (sort_generator_count(&[]) + 1)` — the interior constant by
construction. On the three fixtures where `k < n` it disagrees with the neighbouring columns of the
same table: `response-function` prints `1,140` states, `21,600` transition bytes and `28` B/state,
but `21,600 + 4 × 1,140 = 26,160`, which is `22.9` bytes per state, not `28`. `collider-disjunctive`
prints 28 against a realized `22.9`; `diamond-conjunctive` prints 36 against a realized `24.4`. The
four fixtures whose arity bound equals their intervenable count (`chain`, `fork`, `wide-conjunction`,
`identity-predicted-loss`) print the constant exactly, which is why the discrepancy was invisible:
at `k = n` the boundary sort carries `nd` generators too, and the model is exact.

The report's sentence "the last column is the per-state constant `4(nd + 1)`, which is where the
memory actually goes" is the single sentence that asserts the wrong thing. Patch in § 3.1.

### 2.2 The plan's third correctness gate was replaced, not run — defect

The plan predeclares three correctness gates for probe 1, "in order of strength", and its third is:
"Separating interventions printed for every separated pair and replayed by that oracle." I confirmed
by diffing the exploration log between the probe-0 fold (`395263d82`, 15:12) and the probe-1 record
(`7bec7c55b`, 15:26) that this text was not edited when the results landed, so it stands as
predeclared.

What exists instead:

- `causal_lowering_report.rs` prints **one** separator per fixture (`break` after the first
  non-empty path), not one per separated pair.
- Nothing replays it. Probe 1 owns no replay routine; `replay_separator` lives in probe 5's
  `causal_abstraction.rs` and is not reachable from probe 1's code. The library test
  `separator_paths_decode_to_intervention_words` checks that each decoded step names an intervenable
  variable with an in-domain value and that the record's two outputs differ — it never re-solves the
  model under the decoded word.
- The report's "Gate 3" heading is the arity-boundary fixture, a different and genuinely valuable
  check that was added later "on the strength of the concern raised when probe 1a was reported".

So the report's verdict "the gates pass" and the probe index's "all three gates pass" are true of
three gates, two of which were predeclared. The substitution is not disclosed anywhere.

I ran the missing gate. Re-solving each printed word against a Python transcription of the model:

| fixture | printed word | replayed outputs | reported outputs |
|---|---|---|---|
| `collider-disjunctive` | `do(V0:=0)` | 1 vs 0 | 1 vs 0 |
| `diamond-conjunctive` | `do(V1:=1)` | 0 vs 1 | 0 vs 1 |
| `wide-conjunction` | `do(V0:=1) do(V1:=1) do(V2:=1)` | 0 vs 1 | 0 vs 1 |
| `response-function` | `do(V1:=1)` | 2 vs 3 | 2 vs 3 |

All four separate, and the `wide-conjunction` word is semantically right for the reason the report
gives: pinning three of the four inputs to one makes the conjunction read the fourth, so contexts
`0000` and `0001` split. So the gate would have passed had it been run on what the binary prints.
What is unverified is the "for every separated pair" half — the binary shows one pair per fixture,
so no claim about the other separators is backed by anything.

This is also the item the brief asked about under "does the replay path close a loop through the

the compiler and *not* independent of the model solver, exactly the qualification probe 5's review

### 2.3 Relevance pruning is not in the lowering — defect (claim), no arithmetic effect

Probe 1a's requirement 4 and the plan both say relevance pruning belongs in the lowering, not the
measurement. The report says: "Relevance pruning is in the lowering, not the reporting, as probe 1a
required: exogenous variables that are not ancestors of the observation set are dropped and their
mechanism table axes collapsed before the carrier is built."

`CausalModel::lower` does not call `prune_irrelevant_exogenous`, and neither does `carrier_cost`,
`arity_tower`, `observation_tower`, any fixture, or the report binary. Its only call site in the
whole repository is `pruning_drops_exogenous_variables_that_no_observation_can_read`, its own unit
test. The routine is an opt-in transform on `CausalModel` that no caller opts into. Concretely,
`lower` computes `carrier_cost()` on the unpruned model and rejects against the budget with the
unpruned size, so a model carrying an irrelevant exogenous variable is charged its full domain.

No reported number changes, because on all seven fixtures every exogenous variable is an ancestor of
the observation set (checked fixture by fixture in the Python transcription). But the report asserts
a property of the lowering that the lowering does not have, and probe 1a's cost-model argument —
that pruning "is not optional bookkeeping, it is part of the cost model" — is therefore not
implemented anywhere in the compile path.

The routine itself is correct where it is invoked, which is worth stating separately since the brief
asked whether an over-aggressive ancestor test could silently drop a variable that matters. It
cannot. `relevant_endogenous` is seeded from the observation set and closed backwards over
`endogenous_parents` in one reverse pass, which is a complete transitive closure because every
endogenous parent is strictly earlier in the order. Ancestry is taken over the **full** mechanism
graph rather than the post-intervention graph, and every intervention only deletes edges, so the
retained set is a superset of the ancestors under any admissible pinning — conservative in the safe
direction, and the code comment says so. The table collapse pins each dropped axis at digit zero,
which is exact precisely because no retained observation can read that axis.

### 2.4 The oracle enumerates partial assignments directly, but it is not independent of the model layer and is not the Python oracle the plan demanded — wording problem, with the independence now supplied

This was the brief's highest-value check, so it is worth separating the two halves.

**The half that mattered passes.** `signature_classes` in `src/causal.rs` builds each context's
signature by iterating `enumerate_supports` and, within each support, the mixed-radix pin index —
partial assignments enumerated directly. It never constructs a generator word, never consults
`CausalLowering`, and never calls the compiler. The failure mode the plan named, "words up to the
diameter, which would import the compiler's own view", does not occur.

**The half that does not.** The plan says "An independent **Python** oracle". What exists is Rust in
the same module as the lowering, sharing three routines with it: `solve_into` (the acyclic solve and
the mixed-radix decode of the context index), `observation_of` (the observation packing), and
`enumerate_supports` (the support enumeration and its order). A misreading in any of the three is
shared between the object under test and its oracle, so the agreement in the report's second table
is agreement about the compiler's refinement, not about the model's semantics. The report's phrase
"An independent oracle in the same module … so that a misreading shared between the model and the
compiler cannot manufacture agreement" is accurate about the *compiler* and overstated about the
*model*: a misreading shared between the model and the oracle is exactly what remains possible.

Note also that `arity_tower` and `observation_tower` compute every rung through `signature_classes`
and never compile anything, so the compiler-versus-oracle agreement is checked only at each
fixture's declared arity and declared observation set — one point per fixture — not at any tower
rung. The report is straight about the first fact (contract item 3, "computed by the direct oracle
per rung") and does not draw attention to the second.

I supplied the missing independence. The Python transcription shares no code and reproduces: the
carrier cost of all seven fixtures column for column; the root quotient class count of all seven;
every rung of both towers; and the Balke–Pearl partition class-for-class. Nothing disagrees. So the
shared-misreading risk is now closed by measurement rather than by argument, and the finding is a
wording problem rather than a defect in the result.

### 2.5 "60 contexts to exactly 32 classes" is the published object, and the product-versus-joint clause is handled correctly — no issue

The brief asked whether the number is real or an artifact both sides produce from a shared
assumption. It is real, and the check is stronger than the report claims for itself.

Rebuilding Balke and Pearl's construction from the definition in Python, with no reference to the
Rust: for each exogenous value, the response function of `V0` (no parents) is the constant it
returns, of `V1` is the pair `(f1(V0=0, u1), f1(V0=1, u1))`, and of `V2` is the 4-tuple over
`(V0, V1)`. Distinct response functions: 2 at `V0`, 4 at `V1`, 4 at `V2`. Product `= 32`. The joint
partition induced by the response *tuple* also has 32 classes, and the two coincide because each of
the three exogenous variables feeds exactly one endogenous variable — which I checked directly
(`{U0: 1, U1: 1, U2: 1}`) and which is the case probe 0's clause says calls for the product form.
The report states this explicitly, and so does the docstring on the fixture's test. That clause is
discharged properly.

The Python signature quotient equals the Python response-function partition class for class, not
just in count, so the `32` is not a coincidence of cardinalities. The compiled quotient, the Rust
oracle and the Rust expectation all equal the same vector, which the report's test asserts with
`assert_eq!` on the class vectors rather than on their lengths.

The arity bound is not hiding anything either. The tower runs to `k = 3`, which is full arity for
`n = 3`, and the class count is 32 at `k = 2` and 32 at `k = 3`, so the stabilization is measured
rather than imposed by the declared bound of 2.

One undisclosed deviation from the plan's wording, in the fixture's favour. The plan says "Compile a
**canonical**-exogenous model". The fixture is not canonical: it declares arbitrary exogenous
domains of sizes 2, 6 and 5 and checks the *induced* response-function partition. That is the
stronger test — on a canonical exogenous space every response tuple is distinct by construction, the
partition is the identity, and the gate would assert nothing. The report should say it chose the
non-vacuous variant rather than leaving the reader to notice the substitution.

### 2.7 "The signature collapse from probe 1a made visible" misattributes the empty certificates — wording problem

The report writes: "Three of seven fixtures have no non-empty separating word at all. On chain,
fork, and the identity fixture, every separated pair already differs before any intervention. The
compiler's certificate is empty there. This is the signature collapse from probe 1a made visible."

The first three sentences are right and I reproduced them. The fourth is the wrong mechanism. The
signature collapse (probe 1a § 4) says that every *word* reduces to its final partial assignment, so
word structure carries nothing beyond the pin it induces. It says nothing about whether a separating
word is empty. The actual reason those three certificates are empty is that on those three fixtures
the observation partition is already the identity — `chain` has 2 contexts in 2 classes at `k = 0`,
`fork` 3 in 3, `identity-predicted-loss` 6 in 6 — so there is no pair left for an intervention to
separate. That is a property of the fixture selection, not of the vocabulary.

The same fact deserves stating more plainly than the report does elsewhere: on three of the seven
fixtures the arity tower is flat because the `k = 0` rung is already the identity, so those three
rows carry no information about what interventions buy, in either direction. The report's closing line on those rows —
that they are negative results which belong in the table — reads as though a flat tower were an
informative negative; a tower that starts at the identity cannot be anything else.

### 2.8 "Stabilizes at exactly the maximum in-degree" is contradicted by the neighbouring row of its own table — wording problem

The report writes: "**The response-function tower stabilizes at exactly the maximum in-degree.** It
reaches 32 at `k = 2` and never moves again. That is the theoretically expected stabilization point
… so the tower is measuring the right thing."

The observation on the response-function fixture is correct — I confirmed the in-degrees are
`[0, 1, 2]` and the tower is `7, 28, 32, 32`. The general statement is an upper bound, not an
equality, and the `wide-conjunction` row three lines above it in the same table is a counterexample:
maximum in-degree 4, tower `2, 6, 12, 16, 16`, stabilized at `k = 3`.

The correct statement, which is provable rather than measured and worth having: when the observation
set is every endogenous variable and every variable is intervenable, arity equal to the maximum
in-degree suffices to read every response function off, so the quotient reaches the response-function
partition — which is the finest partition any observation of `O(u, I)` can induce, since `O(u, I)` is
a function of the response tuple. Hence the tower stabilizes **at or below** the maximum in-degree
under those hypotheses, and the response-function fixture attains the bound. On a fixture observing
one outcome (`wide-conjunction`, `chain`, `collider-disjunctive`, `diamond-conjunctive`) the bound
still holds and is generally not attained, which is what the table shows.

### 2.9 Reported state counts obey the `|U|`-is-not-states rule everywhere except the B/state column — mostly no issue

Probe 1a's requirement 2 and the plan both forbid reporting `|U|` as "raw states" and require the
materialized `(u, I)` count plus the transition-table bytes. Checked every sentence and every column:

- The carrier table's `contexts` column is `|U|` and is labelled `contexts`; the `states` column is
  `carrier_cost().states`, the materialized `(u, I)` count. Transition bytes are present. The report
  preamble states the rule explicitly.
- The report binary's module docstring restates it. The `CarrierCost` docstring restates it.
- The one compression-shaped sentence, "a `4.6x` refinement from the intervention vocabulary alone"
  for `7 → 32`, is a ratio of class counts at two tower rungs, not a state ratio, so it is not the
  forbidden form. The probe index's "60 contexts to 32 classes" is likewise contexts-to-classes,
  which is what a quotient statement should say.
- The only violation is the B/state column carrying a nominal constant that contradicts its own
  row's transition bytes (§ 2.1).

### 2.10 `class_of` does not bound the context, so an out-of-range context silently returns another sort's class — latent defect

`CausalLowering::class_of` validates the pin support, rejects duplicate pins, and range-checks each
pinned value against its domain. It does not check `context` against the sort's context count. It
computes `local = context * pin_states + pin_index` and then
`compiled.state_classes().get(sort_start + local)`, whose only bound is the global state array. For
any sort but the last, an out-of-range context therefore indexes into a *later sort* and returns a
well-formed but wrong class id instead of `None`.

Nothing currently exercises this: probes 2 and 3 both derive contexts from `0..exogenous_states`.
It is a latent trap in the one part of the four-item contract that probe 1 hands downstream, and the
fix is three lines. Patch in § 3.3.

### 2.11 Predeclaration order is clean; evidence retention is not — one defect

**Evidence retention fails.** `ergodis-private/evidence/` contains no probe 1 artifact; the four

reproduce exactly at `256959b`, so nothing is lost in fact, but the repository-wide reproducibility
rule — the report, the generator and a compact certificate as one git-visible bundle — is not met
for probe 1. The remedy is a retained evidence file, not a code change.

One smaller retention gap: `identity-predicted-loss` is described in the report as a predeclared
loss, and the fixture comment says so, but the prediction is a code comment rather than a row in the
plan's probe index, which carries only one predeclared threshold for probe 1 (class-for-class
agreement on the response-function fixture). The other six fixtures' shape verdicts are not
predeclared anywhere I could find, searching the exploration log's probe-1 section and the fixture

### 3.2 Replay every separator, and run the gate the plan predeclared

`ergodis-private/src/causal.rs`, a new function beside `signature_classes`, plus an assertion in the
report binary. This is the plan's gate 3, and it is cheap.

```diff
--- a/src/causal.rs
+++ b/src/causal.rs
@@
+/// Replay a decoded separating word against the model rather than against the
+/// compiler: re-solve both contexts under the pins the word induces and check
+/// that the observations differ.
+///
+/// This shares `solve_into` and `observation_of` with the lowering, so it is
+/// independent of the compiler and not of the model solver; the independent
+/// check on the solver is the response-function gate, whose expectation is
+/// built from the mechanism tables directly.
+pub fn replay_word(
+    model: &CausalModel,
+    word: &[InterventionStep],
+    left: u64,
+    right: u64,
+) -> bool {
+    let count = model.endogenous_domains.len();
+    let mut pins = vec![u32::MAX; count];
+    for step in word.iter() {
+        pins[step.variable as usize] = step.value;
+    }
+    let mut assignment = vec![0u32; count];
+    model.solve_into(left, &pins, &mut assignment);
+    let left_output = model.observation_of(&assignment);
+    model.solve_into(right, &pins, &mut assignment);
+    left_output != model.observation_of(&assignment)
+}
```

In `tasks/tools/src/causal_lowering_report.rs`, replace the `break` after the first printed
separator with a loop that replays **every** separator whose two states are roots of the same
lowering, counts them, asserts each one separates, and prints the count alongside the one example
already shown. The word's pins are the fold of the path with later overwrites winning, which is what
`decode_word` already returns in order.

**Predicted effect.** No number in any existing table changes. One new line per fixture reporting
how many separators replayed, and a hard failure if any does not. On the four fixtures with a
non-empty separator I have already replayed the printed word by hand and all four pass, so the
expected outcome is a clean gate.

## 4. What was checked and found sound

- **Every table in probe 1's report reproduces exactly** at `256959b` under the published replay
  command: the carrier cost table (all seven rows, all seven columns), the oracle-agreement table,
  the arity tower, the observation tower, and the separator line for each fixture including the
  three "every separated pair differs before any intervention" rows.
- **Every carrier-cost figure is independently correct.** Recomputing `Σ_S |U| d^{|S|}`, the

  `18/48/76/132/1296/72/1140` states, `16/24/36/64/128/20/36` generators and
  `288/1152/1440/2688/41472/1440/21600` transition bytes.
- **The Balke–Pearl gate is genuine** (§ 2.5), including the product-versus-joint clause and the
  class-for-class rather than count-only comparison.
- **The oracle enumerates partial assignments directly**, not words (§ 2.4). This was the brief's
  highest-value check and the code does the right thing.
- **Both towers refine monotonically as partitions**, not merely in class count. I checked every
  rung of every fixture pairwise: no pair separated at a coarser rung merges at a finer one. This is
  also structurally guaranteed and worth recording as such — `enumerate_supports` emits supports
  shortest-first, so the arity-`k` signature vector is a strict prefix of the arity-`k+1` one, and
  growing the observation set refines because the larger packing determines the smaller. The report
  asserts the tower ordering; the binary prints only counts, so the guarantee is by construction
  rather than by measurement, and it holds.
- **`enumerate_supports` returns variable ids, not positions into the intervenable list.** The
  docstring flags this as the trap and the code avoids it: `sort_states`, `split_local`,
  `join_local` and `sort_generator_count` all index `endogenous_domains` by variable id. Probe 5's
  review found this routine's empty-first ordering silently degrading a *different* module's rule;
  in probe 1 the empty support is sort 0 by design, it is what `root_state` and `root_classes`
  depend on, and nothing selects "the first" support as a witness, so the contamination the brief
  asked about does not reach probe 1.
- **The arity bound is enforced by omission, correctly.** The boundary test in `causal.rs` checks
  that every size-two support on an eight-variable chain carries exactly the four in-place overwrite
  generators for its own variables while interior sorts carry all sixteen, and that the compiled
  quotient still matches the oracle there. This gate is a real strengthening over the `n ≤ 4`
  response-function fixture, which barely reaches `k < n`, and the report is right that it was

## 5. What could not be checked, and why

- **Class-for-class agreement between the Rust and my Python across all seven fixtures.** I compared
  class *counts* on all seven root quotients and on every tower rung (all agree), and compared the
  full class vector only inside Python for the response-function gate. Comparing the Rust class
  vector against the Python one would need either an edit to the report binary to dump the vector or
  a scratch crate linking `ergodis-private`, both excluded by the read-only constraint of this
  review. The residual risk is a permutation of class labels that preserves every count on every
  rung of every fixture, which is not a failure mode any plausible bug produces; and the
  response-function fixture, the only one whose quotient is non-trivial in a way that could hide
  such a thing, is checked vector-for-vector inside Python and vector-for-vector inside Rust against
  an expectation built from the mechanism tables.
- **Whether the compiled quotient equals the oracle at each tower rung.** `arity_tower` and
  `observation_tower` never compile, so this is unmeasured by construction, not merely unchecked by
  me (§ 2.4). Establishing it needs the code change probe 1 already lists as outstanding — the
  incremental path through `plan_layered_greedy_schedule`.
- **Whether every separator replays, as against the four printed ones.** The binary prints one per
  fixture and stops; the others are not emitted, so there is nothing to replay without the § 3.2
  change.
- **The positioning claim against Kekić, Schölkopf and Besserve's Targeted Causal Reduction and
  against Balke–Pearl priority.** That is probe 0's deliverable and its audit was not in scope here.
  I checked only that the report's use of probe 0's product-versus-joint clause is correct, which it

- **Peak memory of the audited compile on probe 1's fixtures.** The core repair note reports 6.9 GB
