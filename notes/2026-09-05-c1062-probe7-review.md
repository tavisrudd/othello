## 2. Findings

### 2.1 The composed quotient does not always reach the product ceiling — the mystery ledger's open gate, closed with a counterexample

The report's mystery ledger states the gate precisely and leaves it open:

> **Why does the composed quotient reach the product ceiling on every coordinate of every fixture?**
> Settled as far as the evidence goes, not proved. … A fixture where the passes fall strictly short of
> the ceiling would need a *non-uniform* per-coordinate effect that still produces a coordinate-wise
> partition, and I could not construct one. **Open, with a named gate**: a proof that the composed
> local partition always equals the coarsest product refinement, or a counterexample.

There is a counterexample, it has four contexts, and its mechanism is the over-approximation the
report's own § 1 introduces. The forward pass records, per endogenous variable, a *set* of reachable
values, and every later quantification ranges over the **product** of those sets. Two endogenous
variables that can each take the value one but never simultaneously are still enumerated at `(1, 1)`.
A mechanism that reads an exogenous variable only at that impossible configuration is therefore split
by the local pass, while the flat quotient — and hence the ceiling, which is defined from the flat
quotient — merges it.

Concretely, with `V0 = u0`, `V1 = 1 − V0`, `V2 = V0 ∧ V1 ∧ u1`, `V3 = V0`, observing `{V2, V3}`,
intervenable `{V2}` at arity one (so no pin can create `(V0, V1) = (1, 1)`):

```text
mutually-exclusive-mask    contexts   4  composed   4  ceiling   2  flat   2  sound True
                           local class counts [2, 2], ceiling coordinate counts [2, 1]
                           contexts 0 and 1 share a flat class and not a composed one
dead-conjunction           contexts   4  composed   4  ceiling   1  flat   1  sound True
                           local class counts [2, 2], ceiling coordinate counts [1, 1]
```

`u1` gets two local classes because the local pass sees `V2 = u1` at `(V0, V1) = (1, 1)`; the flat
quotient sees `V2 = 0` always, so `u1` is invisible and its ceiling coordinate has one block. The
composed quotient is still **sound** — it refines the flat quotient, as the second column records —
so exactness is untouched. What fails is the equality between composed and ceiling.

Three consequences, in order of practical weight.

- **The report's claim is correctly scoped and stays true.** "On every coordinate of every fixture the
  composed local partition equals the coarsest one any coordinate-wise construction could produce" is
  a statement about thirteen families and I verified it as partition equality on all thirteen (§ 4).
  What must change is the inference the report draws next: "So the whole gap to the flat quotient is
  the product-partition price, and the three passes are not leaving anything on the table." That
  holds on these families and does not hold in general, and the report's § 2 framing — that the middle
  term "separates *the price of factoring* from *a defect in the three passes*" — should say that the
  second term is measured at zero here rather than known to be zero.
- **The gate as coded asserts a false-in-general property.** Both the report binary
  (`ensure!(class_total(reduction.local_classes(source)) == class_total(coordinate), "…misses the
  product ceiling")`) and the library test (`assert_eq!` on the same counts) will hard-fail the first
  time someone adds a fixture with a correlated pair of endogenous parents feeding a shared mechanism
  — which is an ordinary modelling shape, not a pathology. The invariant that does hold is
  refinement, and I checked that it holds: the composed quotient is a product partition refining the
  flat quotient, so coordinate-wise `composed_j` refines `ceiling_j` always. Patch in § 3.1.
- **The named mechanism is now available.** The ledger asked for "a proof … or a counterexample" and
  guessed the counterexample would need "a non-uniform per-coordinate effect". It does not; it needs
  correlated endogenous parents plus a mechanism that reads an exogenous variable only where the
  correlation forbids. That is a sharper statement of what the passes give up, and it points at the
  fix: a reachability pass that tracks joint configurations rather than a product of per-variable sets
  would close the gap on this family, at the cost of the correlation problem the report already names
  as the obstacle to an arity-aware pass. The two open items are the same problem.

The fixtures do not probe this axis at all, which is why the gate looked closed. Nine of the thirteen
models have exactly one endogenous parent per mechanism or none; `saturating-count` does have
correlated accumulators (`S_1 ≥ S_0` always), but no mechanism there reads an exogenous variable only
at a jointly-impossible configuration, so the over-approximation is present and harmless.

### 2.2 The four predicted losses are three phenomena and one implementation limit — defect in the framing

The report presents Gate 1 as thirteen predictions under one mechanical rule — "exact when the flat
partition is a product, strictly finer when it is not" — and counts four predicted losses. Three of
them are genuine non-product flat partitions with three distinct causes, each carrying its own witness
pair, and each is real:

- `diamond-conjunctive` — context-dependent masking, `V3 = AND(V1, OR(V0, u1))`, so `u0`'s effect
  disappears exactly when `u1 = 1`. This is the one that broke the author's earlier hypothesis and it
  is a pre-existing probe 1 fixture, not one built to lose. That deserves credit.
- `shared-mechanism-parity` — `V0 = u0 XOR u1`, two exogenous parents on one mechanism.
- `saturating-count-5-sealed` — the sources are not intervenable, so only the count survives.

The fourth, `wide-conjunction-arity-1`, is different in kind. Recomputing the flat quotient at full
arity on every fixture (my Python arm, § 4) gives a flat quotient identical to the declared-arity one
on twelve of thirteen fixtures; the exception is exactly this one, where flat at arity one has 6
classes and flat at full arity has 16 — and the composed quotient is 16. So at full arity there is no
gap at all: the composed quotient equals the flat quotient exactly, and the entire "loss" is that the
passes compute the full-arity rung while the fixture declares arity one.

The report says this plainly in § 6 ("it is exactly why `wide-conjunction-arity-1` is a predicted
loss: the flat quotient at arity one has 6 classes and the composed quotient has 16"), so nothing is
concealed. But Gate 1 lists it as a fourth instance of the product-partition rule, and the mystery
ledger's "the four predicted losses all land where the product-partition statement says they must"
folds it in with the other three. It is an artifact of a declared dial the passes do not implement,
not a property of the model's shape, and the two should not be counted together. Under the product
rule it does qualify — the arity-one flat partition of 6 classes is indeed not a product, its ceiling
being the discrete 16 — but that is a consequence of the arity restriction rather than an independent
observation.

### 2.3 The predeclaration is a single commit, six of thirteen verdicts are a default, and the prediction rule was replaced after a run — process defect

Commit order, checked in both repositories:

- `othello` `40129cc39` at 17:43 — the novelty argument.
- `ergodis-private` `d57cb07` at 19:05 — `src/causal_composition.rs`, the fixtures **including every
  `predicted` verdict**, and the report binary, in one commit.
- `othello` `43d082035` at 19:07 — the report with all its tables.

So nothing on disk separates "verdict entered" from "measurement taken", for any of the thirteen. This
is the probe 6 situation rather than the probe 2 one, where `a6bd72e6a` and `b2bd15820` genuinely
preceded the measuring code. The report's Gate 1 sentence "Every fixture's verdict was entered in
`compositional_fixtures()` before the run" is unverifiable from the record.

Two further things make the gate weaker than the count of thirteen suggests, and neither is disclosed.

**Six of the thirteen verdicts are a default, not an entry.** `compositional_fixtures()` builds seven
of its families by iterating probe 1's `flat_lowering_fixtures()` and assigning the verdict with

```rust
let (predicted, broken) = match fixture.name {
    "diamond-conjunctive" => (ComposedVerdict::StrictlyFiner, "context-dependent masking; …"),
    _ => (ComposedVerdict::Exact, ""),
};
```

so `chain`, `fork`, `collider-disjunctive`, `wide-conjunction`, `identity-predicted-loss` and
`response-function` receive `Exact` from the wildcard arm. A seventh probe 1 fixture added later would
silently inherit a prediction nobody made. The six that inherit it are also the six where the composed
quotient equals the number of contexts or a value probe 1 already published, so the prediction carries
little risk.

**The prediction rule is not predictive, and it was replaced after a contradicting run.** The report
§ 2 says so itself: "This replaces a weaker hypothesis I had entered before running: 'at most one
exogenous parent per mechanism, every endogenous variable intervenable, arity not binding'. The
diamond fixture satisfies all three and is still strictly finer." Disclosing the replacement is the
right thing and it is the most valuable paragraph in the report. What follows from it is that the rule
under which thirteen of thirteen hold was adopted after at least one run. And the replacement rule —
"exact when the flat partition is a product" — cannot be evaluated without first computing the flat
partition, which is the object the gate compares against. Applying it is a hand-computation of the
answer, not a prediction.

Re-scoring every family myself against what is in the fixture file, from my own Python arm, the
thirteen do all hold. The gate is a genuine check that the implementation agrees with a hand-computed
product-partition analysis on thirteen families, and three of the four losses were built to lose while
the fourth was a surprise. That is worth having. It is not the "at least one predicted loss entered
before the run" that the process rules ask for, because nothing here was at risk.

### 2.4 The scale row's `1.000e12x` is a fixture dial, and the arithmetic behind it is exact — one wording problem, no arithmetic error

Every figure recomputes exactly in integer arithmetic:

| quantity | published | recomputed |
|---|---|---|
| source contexts, `20^12` | `4,096,000,000,000,000` | `4096000000000000` |
| composed classes, `2^12` | `4,096` | `4096` |
| flat carrier over the source alphabet, `20^12 × 25` | `102,400,000,000,000,000` | `102400000000000000` |
| flat carrier over the reduced alphabet, `2^12 × 25` | `102,400` | `102400` |
| reduction ratio, `(20/2)^12` | `1.000e12x` | `10^12` |
| twenty sources, `20^20` | `1.048576 × 10^26` | `104857600000000000000000000` |
| twenty sources, composed, `2^19` | `524,288` | `524288` |

The `25` is the arity-one pin-configuration count, `1 + 12 × 2`, on twelve intervenable binary source
variables, and the 24 endogenous variables of `saturating_count(12, 20, …)` are twelve thresholded
sources plus twelve accumulators of which only the last is observed. The incidental finding about the
twentieth source is also right: `beyond_flat_envelope` sets thresholds `1..sources`, so at twenty
sources over a domain of twenty the last source has threshold 20 on values `0..19` and can never fire,
giving it one local class and `2^19` rather than `2^20`.

The wording problem is the ratio. `1.000e12x` is `(20 / 2)^12`: the fixture declares an exogenous
domain of twenty per source and each source's mechanism is a threshold, so each source's local
partition has two blocks by construction. The reduction ratio is a dial the fixture sets, not a
property discovered about a causal model. What the row genuinely demonstrates — and it is what the
probe exists for — is that the three passes run to completion on a model whose flat carrier is ten
orders of magnitude past probe 1a's envelope, and that the reduced model then compiles through the
ordinary flat lowering with its class vector matching the direct-enumeration oracle exactly. The
verdict sentence "a model with `4.096 × 10^15` exogenous contexts reduces to 4,096, exactly, in 25.6
microseconds" reads as a compression measurement; the compression is declared and the exactness and
the pipeline are what was measured.

### 2.5 The timing is real work on a real input, but the input is the mechanism tables, not the context space — wording problem

The 25.6 microseconds covers `CompositionalReduction::compile` plus `reduce`, both inside one
`Instant` bracket, and it is not a constant-time index computation: `compile` visits every row of
every mechanism table three times over (forward, backward, local), which on this model is twelve
threshold tables of twenty rows plus one accumulator chain, and `reduce` rewrites every table at the
class representatives, which for the accumulator mechanisms means rebuilding tables over the reduced
exogenous widths. The measured time divided by the row count lands at a couple of nanoseconds per row
visit, which is what that work should cost.

What the number is not is a rate at which `4.096 × 10^15` contexts were processed. Nothing enumerates
contexts anywhere in `compile` or `reduce` — that is the entire point of the module and the report
says so — but the verdict's phrasing puts the two numbers in one sentence ("`4.096 x 10^15` exogenous
contexts reduces to 4,096, exactly, in 25.6 microseconds") in a way that invites the rate reading. The
defensible sentence is that the reduction's cost is linear in the mechanism table size and independent
of the context count, which is a stronger and more interesting claim than any microsecond figure.

Two smaller points about the same number. It is a single un-repeated measurement quoted to five
significant figures; my replay of the identical binary gave `30.967 µs`, about twenty percent higher,
so the trailing digits are not meaningful. And the report's "no flat arm exists to compare it against"
is right, which means the figure has no baseline and its only content is "small".

### 2.6 The `u64` context index saturates silently rather than erroring, and two routines disagree about the limit — latent defect

The report is straight that the `u64` context index, not memory, now caps the scale claim, and it
records that at twenty sources over domain twenty "only the compile is available". Checked, and the
underlying interface is worse than "unavailable": it is silently wrong rather than absent.

`CausalModel::new` computes `exogenous_states` with `saturating_mul`, so a model whose context count
exceeds `u64` is constructed without complaint and `exogenous_states()` returns `u64::MAX` — a number
that is not the context count and is not flagged as a saturation. Any caller that iterates
`0..model.exogenous_states()` — `signature_classes` does, and so does the report binary's own Gate 2
loop — would run over a fabricated range. Probe 7's binary avoids this by never calling those routines
on the wide model and by reporting `source_context_count()`, which is computed in `u128` inside the
reduction. That is the right defensive choice and it is undocumented; nothing prevents the next caller
from taking `exogenous_states()` at face value.

Separately, three routines disagree about where the limit is. `CausalModel::solve_into` clamps with
`self.exogenous_domains.len().min(64)` and silently treats coordinates past the sixty-fourth as zero;
`CompositionalReduction::class_index` writes into a fixed `[0u32; 64]` and would panic on a wider
model; `class_of_context` writes into a caller-supplied buffer and has no limit at all. None of the
three says so. Patch in § 3.3.

The headline figure is not at or past that cap: `20^12 = 4.096e15` is well inside `u64`, and it is the
twenty-source row that exceeds it. So the cap does not undercut the published scale claim; it caps the
next one.

### 2.7 The reduction's real gate is not the one the report labels Gate 2 — understatement worth correcting

The report presents four gates and gives Gate 2, "the reduction preserves the flat partition,
exhaustively", a five-row table with a note that "the remaining eight fixtures reduce by 1.00x and
also keep the partition". Read literally that undersells the evidence and oversells those eight rows.

The eight `1.00x` rows are near-vacuous as a test of the reduction: a fixture that reduces by `1.00x`
has every exogenous class a singleton, so the representative of each value is itself, `reduce` rebuilds
an identical model, and "partition kept" cannot fail. Of the five rows that do reduce, three are the
same `saturating_count` shape at different symmetry and intervenability settings, so the exhaustive
reduction gate really covers three distinct shapes: the Balke–Pearl response function, a collapsing
chain, and a saturating count.

What is much stronger, and the report does not point at it, is the unconditional
`ensure!(refines(&composed, &flat), "…the composed quotient is not sound")` that runs on **all
thirteen** fixtures before anything else in Gate 1. That assertion is exactly the exactness condition:
every composed class lies inside a flat class, and each context's representative is in its own composed
class by construction, so `refines(composed, flat)` says precisely that `u` and `rep(u)` have identical
signatures under every admissible pin set — which is `O(M_I(u)) = O(M'_I(class(u)))`. Probe 7's
exhaustive exactness evidence is thirteen fixtures, not five.

One thing neither gate checks is observation *values* as opposed to the partition they induce.
`signature_classes` labels by first appearance, so Gate 2 compares partitions; the only place actual
observations are compared between the source and reduced models is the randomized check of § 2.8.
Since `reduce` reuses the source mechanism tables verbatim at the representatives, value equality
follows, but it is inferred rather than asserted anywhere on the small fixtures.

### 2.8 The randomized gate covers the plumbing, not the theorem — defect in what it is offered as

Gate 4 is 200,000 sampled `(context, pin set)` pairs on the twelve-source model, comparing the source
model solved at `u` against the reduced model solved at `class(u)`. The report calls it "randomized
exactness above the flat envelope" and adds, correctly, "This is a check, not a proof". The
qualification it needs is about *what* the check can reach.

The sampling itself is well built: the context is uniform over the full `4.096 × 10^15` range, and
the pin draw covers the empty pin set a third of the time and a uniformly chosen single pin otherwise,
which is the entire admissible vocabulary at arity one. There is no coverage hole in the pin
dimension.

The problem is the model. On `beyond_flat_envelope(12, 20)` each exogenous variable feeds exactly one
endogenous variable; that variable is a threshold, so its local partition is exactly `{below,
at-or-above}`; the observation is the saturating count of the fired thresholds; and therefore the
observation is a function of the composed class *by construction of the fixture*. There is no
configuration of this model in which the reduction could be wrong unless the digit packing, the
representative selection or the table rewrite is broken. So the 200,000 samples test the plumbing at
scale — which is worth testing, and it is the only thing testable there — and they cannot reach any
of the situations in which the theorem has content: a shared exogenous parent with several children,
a non-discrete `Π_V` on a variable with more than one child, masking, or the correlated-reachability
gap of § 2.1. Every one of those lives only in the small fixtures.

Stated the other way: the exactness claim above the flat envelope rests on the theorem of § 2 plus a
plumbing check, and the theorem is where all the weight sits. Since I read the theorem as sound
(§ 4), that is a defensible position — but the report's "the theorem, the exhaustive gate on small
models, and the 200,000-sample randomized check at scale agree" reads as three independent
confirmations of one claim, and the third is not one.

### 2.9 No fixture exercises a shared exogenous parent where it merges anything — gate coverage

Both documents single out the shared-exogenous-parent case as what the exogenous-side factorization
gets right for free: "Factoring on the *exogenous* side is what makes a shared exogenous parent correct
by construction rather than by a side condition — probe 0's clause about an exogenous variable feeding
several endogenous ones is satisfied without a special case."

The construction is right. `local_classes` builds one signature per exogenous value by concatenating,
over every child mechanism in index order, the `Π` block of the output at every live row, so the local
class is the **joint** tuple across children rather than a product of per-child partitions, which is
what probe 0's clause requires.

The coverage is thin. Exactly two of the thirteen fixtures have an exogenous variable with more than
one endogenous child: `fork` (`U0` drives `V0` and `V1`, three contexts) and `diamond-conjunctive`
(`u1` drives `V1` and `V2`, four contexts). In both, that variable's local partition comes out
discrete — three of three and two of two — so no fixture exists in which a shared exogenous parent
*merges* two values. `shared-mechanism-parity`, despite its name, is the opposite shape: two exogenous
parents on one mechanism.

This is mitigated but not closed. Dropping the observed child from the signature on `fork` would make
`U0`'s partition coarser, the composed quotient coarser than the flat one, and the unconditional
`refines(composed, flat)` assertion would fire — so the guard catches the error that matters, since
only a too-coarse composed quotient breaks exactness. What no gate would catch is a signature that
drops an *unobserved* child whose `Π` is trivial, which changes nothing today and would change
something on a model where that child has observed descendants. A fixture with a shared exogenous
parent whose local partition is non-trivial is a cheap addition and is the one shape the thirteen do
not cover.

### 2.10 The novelty argument's three differences check out against the paper, and I discharged half of its own deferred gate — no issue

I read arXiv:2601.10531v2 from the cache rather than the report's summary of it. All three differences
hold, and two of them hold more strongly than stated.

- **Carrier.** `Algorithm 1: Function RePaRe(G)` operates on a DAG `G = ([d], E)` and a coarsening
  `G = (Π, E)` where `Π` partitions the node set; Definition 5's `Refine`-oracle "takes in `Π` and
  returns a part `π* ∈ Π` that itself is partitioned into `π_a, π_b`". The paper's keyword list
  carries "cluster DAGs" and "partition refinement lattice". Confirmed: a partition of the variable
  set, and no exogenous or latent object anywhere in the coarsening definition.
- **Epistemic status.** Corollary 16: "Given Gaussian distributed `X_V` satisfying Assumption 9,

**The gate the note deferred is half discharged.** § 8 of the novelty argument says that before any

secretly computing an exogenous-side object under a variable-side presentation." Definition 8 settles
that without needing the proof: the interventional coarsening `G_I` is the coarsening whose partition

**The exactness theorem is sound as written, and the code implements the object it is about.** The
proof in § 2 of the report has one step that could fail and does not. To move `u` to its
representative one exogenous coordinate at a time, the endogenous values downstream shift within their
`Π` blocks, and the next coordinate's swap is then evaluated at a *mixed* configuration — some parents
already moved, some not. Those mixed configurations need to lie inside the set the local and backward
conditions quantify over, or the chaining breaks. They do, because the forward pass records a
reachable *set per variable* and every quantification ranges over the product of those sets, so any
combination of individually reachable values is included. That is what the report's "quantified over
configuration sets that contain every intermediate point" means, and it is why the report is right
that the over-approximation is what keeps the reduction exact rather than a wart on it. In the code,
`local_classes` and `observable_blocks` filter a mechanism row out only when some *other* endogenous
parent's digit is unreachable, coordinate by coordinate, which is exactly the product set. The same
over-approximation is what makes the ceiling reachable only sometimes (§ 2.1) — soundness and slack
come from one place.

**The rest of the chaining checks out.** `Π_V` is discrete at an observed variable, so block equality
at an observed variable is value equality and the packed observation agrees. Pinned variables take the
same value in both runs, so the arity bound and the intervenable set never weaken the argument, as the
report says. `observable_blocks` runs variables in reverse index order and `reachable_values` in
forward index order, which are reverse-topological and topological because `CausalModel` requires every
endogenous parent to be earlier; a DAG needs no fixpoint iteration and one pass is right.
`mechanism_axes` rejects a repeated parent explicitly, with the correct reason in its comment — the
passes vary one axis at a time and a repeated parent would silently under-split.

**The mechanism row encoding matches.** `mechanism_axes` orders endogenous parents first and then
exogenous, most significant first, and `CausalModel::solve_into` builds its row index the same way. A
mismatch here would corrupt every pass silently; it is right, and my independent transcription using
the same convention reproduces every fixture.

**`coarsest_product_refinement` really is the ceiling.** Two values of a coordinate are equivalent when
swapping one for the other never changes the flat class, for every setting of the others; that relation
is signature equality, hence an equivalence; its product refines the flat quotient by swapping one
coordinate at a time; and any product partition refining the flat quotient is contained in it, since a
one-coordinate swap inside a block of that partition stays inside a flat class. The mixed-radix
enumeration that builds the signatures is aligned correctly — for a fixed digit at one coordinate the
remaining coordinates advance in the same order in every row — and the same alignment argument holds
for the `live` filters in `local_classes` and `observable_blocks`, because those filters depend only on
the other axes' digits.

**The composed quotient really is a product partition, and it refines the ceiling coordinate-wise.**
`class_of_context` maps each coordinate independently, so the composed partition is the product of the
local ones; the soundness assertion makes it a product refining the flat quotient; and for product
partitions, `∏A` refining `∏B` implies `A_j` refines `B_j` for every `j` (fix the other coordinates and
swap). That is the invariant § 3.1 proposes asserting.

**The Balke–Pearl base case is what the novelty argument says it is.** When an exogenous variable feeds
exactly one endogenous variable and that variable is observed, `Π` is discrete there and the local
signature is the child's value as a function of its other parents — the per-variable response function.
The `response-function` fixture's `60 → 32` reproduces here and matches probe 1's flat result, so the
identification is not just formal. The one qualification worth stating: the local pass restricts to
reachable configurations of the other parents, so it agrees with Balke and Pearl up to that
restriction, and on this fixture the restriction is empty.

**The reduced model's oracle check is exact.** `beyond_the_flat_envelope` compares
`root_classes(&lowering, &compiled)` against `signature_classes(&reduced)` with `==` on the class
vectors, not on their counts, and it does so on the 4,096-context reduced model with its 300 classes.
That is the strongest single check in the probe and it is stated modestly.

**The scale arithmetic is exact**, every figure recomputed in integers (finding 2.4), including the
`2^19` incidental finding.

**The probe 1 review's judgement about probe 7's promotion is correct, and probe 7 makes it moot.**
That review corrected probe 1a's per-state constant from the interior `4(nd + 1)` to a realized figure
several times smaller, raising the flat envelope from `|U| ≲ 10^4` to `|U| ≲ 10^5`, and judged that
probe 7's promotion survives because the canonical exogenous space fails by twelve orders of magnitude
regardless. Checked against probe 7's delivered result rather than against the envelope: the twelve-source

times the uncorrected envelope and `1.4 × 10^9` times the corrected one — nine orders rather than ten,

  no instrumented row counter, and the report prints no work count, so this is an inference rather than

**Verdict.** Probe 7 delivers the scaling half of C1062 and it survives this pass. The reduction is
exact, the theorem's one fragile step is the one the code actually implements, and the thirteen-family
table reproduces cell for cell under an implementation that shares no code with it — including the
strongest claim in the probe, which I was able to verify as partition equality where the tool only
compares block counts. The report is also the most carefully hedged in the task: it scopes its ceiling
claim to its fixtures, names the arity dial as missing, names the `u64` cap, and discloses that its
prediction rule replaced an earlier one a run had contradicted. What I found is mostly in the space
those hedges leave open. The product-ceiling gate the mystery ledger names as open is closed in the
negative by a four-context model whose two endogenous parents can each be one but never together, and
the mechanism is the report's own forward-pass over-approximation, so the same correlation problem
blocks both open items; more practically, the equality the gate currently asserts is false in general
and will break the tool on the first fixture that exhibits it. Beyond that: one of the four predicted
losses is an arity-rung mismatch rather than a non-product shape, the `1.000e12x` ratio is a dial the
fixture sets, the randomized check at scale tests plumbing rather than the theorem, the predeclaration
is one commit with six verdicts coming from a wildcard, and the novelty argument's promise of two
compositional dials and a separating-intervention witness was delivered as one dial and no witness.
None of that touches the exactness result, which is what probe 7 exists to deliver and what probe 7
delivered.
