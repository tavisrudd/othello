## Result

Let `H` be generated by three distinct involutions `S={a,b,c}`, and give the left Cayley graph
`Cay(H,S)` its generator colours. Every colour-preserving graph automorphism is a right
translation. Consequently every fixed-point-free involutory colour-preserving automorphism has
the form

\[
\rho_z(h)=hz,\qquad z^2=1,
\]

and it is nonadjacent exactly when the conjugacy class of `z` is disjoint from `S`.

For odd `q`, a mixed-class generating triple in `PGL2(q)` meets both involution classes. It
therefore admits no colour-preserving nonadjacent pairing. In particular, the right-regular mirror
whose complete defect is the centralizer coset `C_H(a)` cannot be patched only on that coset while
preserving the three generator colours: agreement with the old mirror at one vertex propagates
through the connected coloured Cayley graph. A successful peel must change colours, change the
pairing globally, or carry a recursive boundary state.

## Proof of colour-preserving rigidity

Let `phi` preserve every generator colour and put `z=phi(1)`. If a word in `S` represents `h`,
repeated colour preservation gives

\[
\phi(h)=h\phi(1)=hz.
\]

Thus `phi=rho_z`; connectedness makes the value independent of the chosen word. The translation is
an involution precisely when `z^2=1`, and is fixed-point-free when `z` is nonidentity. Finally,
`h` and `hz` are joined by an `s`-edge precisely when

\[
s h=h z,
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
hzh^{-1}=s.
\]

Hence no pair is adjacent exactly when `z^H` misses `S`. Odd `PGL2(q)` has two involution classes,
so a mixed triple leaves no possible class for `z`.

The same propagation proves the local-patch statement. Two colour-preserving automorphisms that
agree at one vertex agree across every incident coloured edge and therefore across the connected
graph. A putative patch equal to `rho_a` off the centralizer coset cannot change its action only on
that coset.

## Revised silver frontier

The centralizer localization remains useful, but its transfer state must retain more than the
uncoloured dihedral coset. The next theorem should use the three-colour alternating-backbone
coordinates and admit a state transition after a move on the defect coset. The exact `(2,3,4)`
`PGL2(3)` graph is the first mandatory direct base case; the seven obstructed `PGL2(5)` types are
the first falsification gate for any proposed recursive rule. A rule that merely installs another
colour-preserving mirror or degrades immediately to paths and cycles is now closed.
