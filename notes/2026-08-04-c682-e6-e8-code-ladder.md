## Result

The Clebsch-to-(E_6) and Lie-(E_8) constructions do support the same
operator-to-code move used in the Paper-IV exploration: retain the canonical
support of an invariant operation and take the binary kernel of its incidence
matrix.  At the (E_6) level this lands on an unrestricted optimal parameter
set.

Use the standard (6+15+6) labels

\[
 x_i,\quad w_{ij},\quad y_i
\]

for the twenty-seven minuscule weights, equivalently the twenty-seven lines
on a cubic surface.  The Cartan cubic has forty-five square-free monomials:
thirty mixed terms (x_i y_j w_{ij}), (i\ne j), and fifteen Pfaffian
terms indexed by perfect matchings of six labels.  Let (H_{E_6}) be their
(45\)-by-\(27) binary exponent matrix.  Exact elimination and complete
enumeration give

\[
 \boxed{D=\ker H_{E_6}=[27,6,12]_2},
 \qquad
 W_D(z)=1+36z^{12}+27z^{16}.
\]

The current Grassl table gives matching lower and upper bounds (12) for
binary \([27,6]\) codes.  Thus this particular code attains the unrestricted
best possible minimum distance for its parameters.  This is a parameter
comparison, not a novelty claim for the code or for its geometric
description; the relevant literature has not been audited.  The table also
records a \([27,7,12]\) code, so the present six-dimensional code is not
dimension-maximal at distance twelve.

The minimum shell is the exact series connection.  Its thirty-six
weight-twelve supports are precisely the thirty-six Schläfli double-sixes,
and they span all of (D).  This statement is checked without accepting a
stored double-six list: the checker reconstructs the line-intersection graph
from the forty-five tritangents, enumerates its seventy-two sixers, pairs them
into thirty-six double-sixes, and compares the resulting supports
word-for-word with the minimum shell.

The shell also reconstructs the full marked (E_6) incidence geometry up to
coordinate permutation.  Among the \(\binom{27}{2}=351\) coordinate pairs,
135 pairs occur together in eight minimum words and 216 occur together in
six.  The count-eight pairs are exactly the intersecting-line relation.  Its
forty-five triangles are exactly the original forty-five tritangent planes.
Thus

\[
 \boxed{
 \text{minimum codewords}
 \longrightarrow
 \text{double-sixes}
 \longrightarrow
 \text{27-line graph and 45 Cartan triples}.}
\]

This is the same minimum-shell reconstruction architecture as Paper IV, now
on the classical (E_6) minuscule carrier and at an unrestricted optimal
parameter point.

The order-three decomposition

\[
 \mathfrak e_8=(78,1)\oplus(1,8)\oplus(27,3)
 \oplus(27^\vee,3^\vee)
\]

supplies the next carrier.  In a minuscule basis, the support of the bracket
on (27\otimes3) consists of one (E_6) Cartan triple together with a
bijection of its three entries to the three (A_2) colors.  Taking all six
bijections for each of the forty-five triples gives 270 weight-three checks
on 81 coordinates.  Their support-incidence kernel is

\[
 \boxed{K=[81,8,36]_2}
\]

with complete weight enumerator

\[
 W_K(z)=1+36z^{36}+81z^{38}+108z^{42}
          +27z^{48}+3z^{54}.
\]

The rank of the (270\)-by-\(81) incidence matrix is 73 over each of

\[
 \mathbf F_2,\mathbf F_3,\mathbf F_5,\mathbf F_7,
 \mathbf F_{11},\mathbf F_{13}.
\]

The binary minimum distance is two below the unrestricted optimum: the
Grassl table gives exact value (38) for \([81,8]_2\).  The exceptional
lift is therefore unusually strong and rigidly explained, but is not a new
best-known binary code.

## The reusable (A_2)-transversal lift theorem

The computation is an instance of a general normal-form theorem.

Let (mathcal H) be a connected three-uniform hypergraph on (n) vertices
whose two-section is connected, let (H) be its binary edge-incidence
matrix, and put

\[
 D=\ker H=[n,k,d]_2.
\]

On (V(\mathcal H)\times\{0,1,2\}), impose a weight-three check for every
hyperedge and every bijection from its vertices to the three colors.  Then
every lifted kernel word has the unique normalized form

\[
 x_{v,a}=u_v+s_a,
 \qquad
 u\in D,
 \qquad
 s\in\{000,011,101,110\}.
\]

Consequently

\[
 K(\mathcal H)\cong D\oplus\mathbf F_2^2,
\]

\[
 W_{K(\mathcal H)}(z)
 =W_D(z^3)+3\sum_{u\in D}z^{2n-\operatorname{wt}(u)},
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 d(K(\mathcal H))
 =\min\{3d(D),\,2n-\maxwt(D)\}.}
\]

To prove the normal form, compare two checks that differ by transposing two
colors.  Along every hyperedge this shows that each color difference
(x_{v,a}+x_{v,b}) is independent of the chosen vertex.  Connectedness of
the two-section propagates the equality through all vertices, so
(x_{v,a}=u_v+s_a).  There is one gauge change
((u,s)\mapsto(u+\mathbf1,s+111)); because three is odd, it selects a unique
representative with (s_0+s_1+s_2=0).  Substitution in one lifted check then
gives (Hu=0).  If (s=000), the lifted weight is (3\operatorname{wt}(u)).
For any of the three nonzero even color words, it is
(2n-\operatorname{wt}(u)).  This proves the enumerator and distance
formulas.

For the Cartan hypergraph, (n=27), (d(D)=12), and
(maxwt(D)=16), so

\[
 d(K)=\min\{36,54-16\}=36.
\]

This also proves that no relabeling or basis-free reinterpretation of the
same all-six-bijection support lift can reach the optimal distance 38.  A
stronger 81-coordinate code must alter the coupling, not merely repeat this
support construction.

## What is exact, and what is not

The 270 checks are the support incidence of the nonzero
(E_6\times A_2\) bracket tensor in the frozen minuscule labeling.  This is
a genuine invariant-operation connection: its triples are structure-constant
supports, not a numerical analogy.

It is not a scalar cubic invariant on (27\otimes3).  The (E_6) Cartan
tensor is symmetric while the (A_2) volume tensor is alternating, so their
full contraction on one commuting vector cancels.  Calling the code a
``Cartan cubic on 81 variables'' would therefore be false.  The safe name is
the (E_8) bracket-support code.

The checker reconstructs the forty-five Cartan monomials from C697's frozen
labels, independently derives both binary kernels by row reduction, enumerates
all 64 and 256 words, and compares the 81-coordinate kernel word-for-word
with the normal-form construction.  The normal-form equality is therefore
checked by two routes: elimination from 270 checks and direct lifting from
the 27-coordinate code.  It separately enumerates every sixer and double-six,
checks equality with the minimum shell and its full span, reconstructs the
pair relation from shell cooccurrences, and recovers the forty-five
tritangents as its triangles.  It also verifies two-section connectedness and
the six modular ranks.  It does not identify the codes up to equivalence with
a previously catalogued construction, establish novelty, or compute kernels
of signed bracket linearizations.

## Where to lift next

The two-point gap at length 81 is useful evidence: the exceptional carrier is
good, while the all-support operation is slightly too rigid.  The highest-EV
finite next computation is operator-faithful rather than another support
variant.

1. Choose a canonical Clebsch/Coble vector (z\in27\otimes3), form the
   signed linearization
   \[
   \operatorname{ad}_z:(27,3)\longrightarrow(27^\vee,3^\vee),
   \]
   and compute its integral Smith data.  At every rank-jump prime, take the
   kernel and Gram-return codes, enumerate their minimum shells, and test
   whether those shells reconstruct the minuscule carrier.  This is the
   closest (E_8) analog of Paper IV's operator-kernel-reconstruction loop.
2. On the affine-(E_8) side, reduce C682's exact (3\)-, (4\)-, and
   (4\)-dimensional boundary-quotient transfer systems at the smallest good
   primes.  Their coefficients are polynomial in the invariant exponents,
   so modulo (p) the true superperiod is enlarged by (p); bare
   period-60 tail-biting is not yet justified.  Build a systematic graph code
   from the input/output transfer, then compute free and column distances over
   one exact superperiod.
3. If either construction has a homogeneous minimum shell, repeat the
   Paper-IV step: transpose that shell as a new incidence layer, recover its
   coordinate carrier, and test the full invariant commutant rather than only
   one chosen operator.

The first gate is finite and small.  The second is the plausible infinite
ladder, but it still needs a positive-rate code definition and a Hamming
distance theorem: C682's maximal-rank and full-corner results alone do not
imply either.
