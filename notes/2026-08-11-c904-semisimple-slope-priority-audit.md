# C904 semisimple graph slopes and divided-power defects: priority audit

Date: 2026-08-11

Status: bounded concept and primary-literature audit; no manuscript or Lean
change

## Executive verdict

No exact predecessor was located for either of the following statements:

1. squarefree minimal polynomial of a graph slope implies primitive integral
   divisor-product minimal class; or
2. the nilpotent part of the slope controls the `p`-primary Smith defect of the
   integral Lefschetz product lattice.

The new dyadic census is therefore potentially a real organizing theorem, not
just a new label for a standard abelian-variety result.  Existing integral
Fourier/divided-power work treats algebraicity or integral transforms, usually
after denominators or in etale Chow.  It does not compute the ordinary
integral subring generated by divisors after a maximal-isotropic gluing.

There is, however, substantial adjacent modular-representation theory.  Exterior
and symmetric powers of indecomposable cyclic `p`-group modules, hence of
nilpotent Jordan blocks in characteristic `p`, have classical or recursive
descriptions.  This likely supplies a major proof engine.  It does **not**
identify the graph-gluing Neron--Severi lattice, its integral carry terms, or the
minimal-class Smith index.  The novelty boundary should therefore be stated as
the bridge from the slope's modular Jordan type to the integral Lefschetz
defect, not as a new calculation of exterior powers of Jordan blocks.

The strongest near-term theorem worth trying to prove is:

> **Etale-slope primitivity theorem.**  For a prime-`p` principal graph gluing
> of `p I_g`, if the slope `T in Sym_g(F_p)` has squarefree minimal polynomial
> -- equivalently, `F_p[T]` is etale -- then the minimal class
> `Theta^(g-1)/(g-1)!` lies in the ordinary integral divisor-product lattice.

The stronger Annals-shaped target suggested by all present exact data is a
`p`-typical Jordan-height formula.  If

\[
  m_T(x)=\prod_f f(x)^{e_f},\qquad h(T)=\max_f e_f,
\]

then the candidate is

\[
 v_p\bigl(\operatorname{ord}(\gamma_T\bmod L_T^{g-1})\bigr)
 \stackrel{?}{=}
 \min\left\{v_p((g-1)!),\ \lfloor\log_p h(T)\rfloor\right\}.
 \tag{0.1}
\]

Formula (0.1) is a conjectural target, not an inference licensed by the current
census.  It simultaneously predicts:

- zero defect for semisimple slopes (`h=1`);
- the dyadic squarefree/non-squarefree dichotomy at the first layer;
- no ternary first-layer defect from a Jordan block of length two;
- the observed ternary defect for minimal polynomial `(x-1)^3`; and
- order four for the regular-nilpotent dyadic slope of length five.

That last compatibility is especially suggestive: the first, second, ...
`p`-primary defect layers should appear when the nilpotent height reaches
`p,p^2,...`, and the universal factorial ceiling truncates them.

## 1. Exact evidence and what it says

The new certificate exhausts all symmetric dyadic graph slopes:

| rank | graph slopes | squarefree minimal polynomial | nonsquarefree minimal polynomial |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 64 | 34, all order 1 | 30, all order 2 |
| 4 | 1,024 | 498, all order 1 | 526, all order 2 |

The rank-five deterministic check samples eight slopes from each class and
again finds order one versus order two.  It is evidence only.  Separately, the
regular-nilpotent dyadic rank-five certificate has exact order four.

At `p=3,g=4`, the certified counterexample has minimal polynomial
`(x-1)^3` and exact order three.  Thus the first odd-primary defect occurs at
the exact Jordan length `p`, as (0.1) predicts.

The use of the **minimal** polynomial is essential.  A semisimple operator may
have repeated eigenvalue multiplicities and hence nonsquarefree characteristic
polynomial.  The squarefree minimal polynomial is equivalent to vanishing of
the nilpotent part in Jordan--Chevalley decomposition over the perfect field
`F_p`.

## 2. Why semisimplicity is conceptually natural

For a graph slope `T`, the complete integral divisor lattice is the preimage of
the symmetric centralizer

\[
 R_T=\{D\in\operatorname{Sym}_g(\mathbf F_p):DT=TD\}
\]

with the additional integral carry

\[
            (\widetilde D\widetilde T-
              \widetilde T\widetilde D)/p.
\]

If `m_T` is squarefree, the commutative algebra `F_p[T]` is finite etale.
Primary idempotents are separated by Bezout and lift by Hensel.  Because `T` is
self-adjoint for the coefficient form, distinct primary pieces are orthogonal.
The trace pairing on an unramified etale lift is perfect, so this decomposition
introduces no `p`-denominator.  This is precisely the setting in which one can
hope to descend the scalar/Jordan-scalar mixed-adjugate identities block by
block and then take trace/norm back to `Z_p`.

When `m_T` is not squarefree, `F_p[T]` has a nonzero nilpotent radical.  Its
Jordan filtration supplies nontrivial extensions rather than etale summands.
Ordinary products and divided powers cease to agree integrally at degrees
divisible by `p`; the internal Tor boundary already isolated in the prime
gluing note is the exact receptacle.  This gives a structural explanation for
why semisimple and nonsemisimple slopes separate in the dyadic first layer.

This paragraph is a proof program, not yet a proof.  In particular, a perfect
trace pairing for the etale slope algebra does not by itself show that the
fully lifted divisor products contain the minimal class.

## 3. Proposed theorem ladder

### A. Safe first target

Prove etale-slope primitivity for every prime and dimension.  A useful stronger
version would show that every polarization divided power needed for the
minimal curve class lies in the ordinary integral Lefschetz subring of the
graph quotient.  It should be formulated locally over `Z_p` and then combined
with the already proved prime-support theorem.

### B. Dyadic first-Bockstein theorem

For every dyadic graph slope, prove

\[
 \beta_{2,T}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m_T\text{ is squarefree},
\]

where `beta_2,T` is the first internal divided-power Bockstein.  In dimensions
three and four, `v_2((g-1)!)=1`, so this is exactly the full order-one/order-two
classification observed by the census.  In higher dimensions it classifies
only the first layer, not the exact order.

### C. `p`-typical Jordan-height theorem

Prove or correct (0.1).  A safer intermediate statement is a filtration
theorem:

> the `r`-th `p`-primary Tor boundary depends only on the quotient of the
> nilpotent slope algebra through radical height `p^r`, and vanishes whenever
> every primary Jordan exponent is less than `p^r`.

This formulation explains why `p=2` detects every failure of semisimplicity at
the first layer, while `p=3` need not detect a square Jordan block.  It also
allows bilinear-type or multiplicity corrections before asserting the exact
closed formula (0.1).

### D. Full crown

Extend the formula from graph charts to arbitrary maximal-isotropic gluings,
express it intrinsically on the finite Lagrangian Grassmannian, and realize all
allowed `p`-power defect orders geometrically.  This would turn the factorial
ceiling into an exact stratification theorem and produce unbounded integral
Lefschetz defects inside a single non-CM elliptic isogeny world.

## 4. Literature boundary

### Abelian varieties and integral Fourier transforms

Moonen--Polishchuk construct divided powers for the Pontryagin product and
integral Fourier transforms in controlled cases.  Beckmann--de Gaay Fortman
characterize algebraicity of the minimal class and integral Fourier duality.
Hasan--Hassan--Lin--Manivel--McBeath--Moonen retain explicit factorial and
polarization-degree denominators.  Kahn's 2026 theorem constructs divided
powers in etale Chow away from the characteristic and up to two-primary
ambiguity.  None of these sources computes an ordinary divisor-product Smith
index, uses the reduction of a gluing slope, or gives a semisimple/nilpotent
criterion.

Milne's Lefschetz-class functoriality and the standard Hodge-group literature
are rational.  De Gaay Fortman--Schreieder give the exact isogeny-lattice and
polarization-matrix dictionary used here, but do not study higher integral
products on that lattice.

Van Geemen--Marrani identify the characteristic-two principal-minor image of
the Lagrangian Grassmannian with a spinor variety.  This remains a plausible
coordinate-free language for the first dyadic Bockstein, but their theorem has
no integral lift, no divisor-product lattice, and no semisimple-slope
stratification.

### Modular representation theory

This is the important adjacent prior art.

- Almkvist--Fossum give exterior and symmetric power decompositions for
  indecomposable modules of a cyclic group of prime order in characteristic
  `p`.
- Himstedt--Symonds prove recursive formulas for all exterior and symmetric
  powers of modules for cyclic `2`-groups.  Their proof uses a Koszul complex
  built from squares and makes the powers-of-two filtration explicit.
- Korhonen computes the Jordan decomposition of nilpotent operators on
  exterior and symmetric squares in characteristic two, with closed formulas
  for regular nilpotents, building on Gow--Laffey and the cyclic-group module
  literature.

These results make the proposed `p`-typical height law substantially more
plausible and may reduce its nilpotent linear-algebra core to known recurrences.
But they do not contain the graph gluing, the commutator/carry description of
`NS`, the saturation quotient, or the distinguished minimal class.  No exact
preemption follows.

Berhuy proves that over a perfect characteristic-two field every monic
polynomial occurs as the minimal polynomial of a symmetric matrix of the same
size.  Thus the semisimple and nilpotent slope strata are genuinely broad;
they are not artifacts of a restrictive symmetric-matrix realization theorem.
This source does not address abelian varieties or integral products.

## 5. Red-team hazards

1. **Squarefree cannot mean characteristic polynomial.**  The correct
   invariant is the minimal polynomial, or equivalently the etaleness of
   `F_p[T]`.

2. **The dyadic converse should not be exported verbatim to odd primes.**  At
   `p=3`, a nonsquarefree exponent-two primary block may be invisible to the
   first divided-cube obstruction.  The certified example has exponent three,
   exactly the first `p`-typical threshold.

3. **Similarity is not enough.**  Replacing a symmetric slope by Jordan normal
   form uses an arbitrary change of basis, while the graph polarization retains
   the unit symmetric coefficient form.  The relevant classification is by
   self-adjoint modules or orthogonal congruence, particularly delicate in
   characteristic two.

4. **The reduced centralizer is not the invariant by itself.**  Previous
   censuses already contain identical reduced Neron--Severi signatures with
   different defects for general Lagrangians.  A proof must carry the integral
   commutator quotient, not only `R_T`.

5. **Etale descent still needs an integral norm identity.**  Orthogonal primary
   decomposition and a perfect trace pairing do not automatically express the
   minimal class as an integral sum of mixed divisor products.

6. **Irreducible semisimple factors need descent.**  Diagonalizing after an
   unramified extension proves membership only after base change.  One must
   show that trace/corestriction does not multiply the target class by the
   residue degree.

7. **Higher layers can mix.**  Formula (0.1) may need the full partition of
   Jordan block sizes, bilinear type on each primary module, or base-`p` digits
   of `g-1`, not merely the maximum exponent.  The modular-representation
   formulas are recursive for exactly this reason.

8. **Graph-chart scope is real.**  A general maximal isotropic subgroup is not
   a graph in a fixed chart.  Squarefreeness of a chosen slope is not yet an
   intrinsic invariant of all gluings, although it is stable under the obvious
   graph symmetries and under fractional-linear chart changes where defined.

9. **The current rank-five sample is not a census.**  It supports the first
   layer only.  The order-four regular-nilpotent example is separate exact
   evidence.

10. **Cohomology versus Chow.**  The entire claim concerns membership in the
    ordinary integral cohomological divisor-product lattice.  It must not be
    advertised as a Chow-ring identity without an additional cycle-level
    proof.

The conceptual punchline is sharper than “nilpotent slopes are bad”:

> The integral Lefschetz subring detects the non-etale infinitesimal thickness
> of the finite slope algebra, one `p`-power of Jordan height at a time.

That is both more invariant and more likely to generalize correctly.

## Bottom line

**GO** on semisimple/etale slope as the next theorem target.  It has a clean
local-algebra meaning, exactly fits the complete dyadic ranks three and four,
and is not preempted by the audited abelian-variety literature.

**DO NOT YET CLAIM** that nonsquarefree is equivalent to defect outside the
dyadic first layer.  The stronger and more credible generalization is
`p`-typical: Jordan lengths below `p` may be invisible, and successive defect
layers should occur at lengths `p,p^2,...`.  Formula (0.1) is the high-value
conjecture to test and then prove or correct.
