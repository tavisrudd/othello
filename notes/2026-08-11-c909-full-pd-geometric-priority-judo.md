# C909 geometric priority-judo: consequences of full cohomological PD saturation

Date: 2026-08-11  
Scope: bounded consequences of the finite-etale elliptic-power graph theorem;
cohomological statements only, no manuscript, PDF, mirror, Lean, or commit
change

## Executive verdict

Assume the repaired finite-etale graph theorem in its genuinely full form:
for the marked quotient (A), with

\[
 P_A^k=\operatorname{im}\bigl(\operatorname{Sym}^k\operatorname{NS}(A)
       \to H^{2k}(A,\mathbf Z)\bigr),
\]

one has (D^k/k!\in P_A^k) for every (D\in\operatorname{NS}(A)) and
every (k).  The useful consequences are integral Lefschetz statements in
all codimensions, a degree-one top-class certificate, and integral stability
of the polarization divided-power line under Fourier/Lefschetz operations.

The current equal-depth draft proves the distinguished-polarization line
more directly than this full-NS hypothesis; its own scope caveat excludes
some mixed divisor terms. Accordingly, the all-NS consequences below are
conditional until that mixed-term gap is closed.

The theorem does **not** by itself give an integral Fourier theorem on the
entire divisor-product algebra, the integral Hodge conjecture in all
codimensions, an effective curve or zero-cycle, a Chow statement, or a
Franchetta theorem.  The finite-etale condition is a marked level/lattice
condition, not automatically a new Hodge or Shimura condition.

## 1. Immediate formal consequences

### All polarization powers

For a principal polarization (Theta),

\[
 \Theta^{[k]}:=\frac{\Theta^k}{k!}\in P_A^k,
 \qquad 0\le k\le g.
\]

Thus the old minimal-class conclusion is only the (k=g-1) member of a
complete cohomological sequence.  The (k=g) member is the top point class

\[
 \Theta^{[g]}=[\mathrm{pt}]\in H^{2g}(A,\mathbf Z).
\]

Since (H^{2g}(A,\mathbf Z)\simeq\mathbf Z), this gives a signed integral
combination of (g)-fold products of divisor classes representing the
cohomological generator.  Equivalently, the gcd of the degrees of all
zero-dimensional divisor intersections from (operatorname{NS}(A)) is one.
This is a degree-one **cohomological** product certificate, not a degree-one
zero-cycle in (operatorname{CH}_0(A)).

If the theorem is genuinely all-(D), then the same holds for every integral
divisor class (D), not just (Theta).  In particular the Chern-character
terms (\operatorname{ch}_k(L)=c_1(L)^k/k!) of all line bundles (L) lie in
the ordinary divisor-product lattice.  This remains a statement about the
image in singular cohomology.

### Integral Lefschetz algebra

Writing

\[
 L_A^\bullet=\bigoplus_k P_A^k
 \subset \bigoplus_k H^{2k}(A,\mathbf Z),
\]

the theorem says that every canonical divided power of a divisor class is
already in the ordinary integral Lefschetz image.  This removes the
factorial denominator for the specific divided powers, in every codimension.
It does not say that (L_A^\bullet) is saturated in the full integral Hodge
lattice, nor that every Hodge class is a divisor product.

The distinction is essential for isogenous elliptic powers: rational Hodge
classes can be Lefschetz while the integral lattice can have a finite index
between (P_A^k) and the full integral Hodge lattice.  Full PD saturation
kills the factorial obstruction generated by the named divisor classes; it
does not automatically kill every isogeny-denominator obstruction.

## 2. Fourier and Lefschetz operations: strongest safe form

The principal polarization gives the usual cohomological Fourier transform

\[
 \mathcal F_\Theta(\alpha)=p_{2*}\bigl(p_1^*\alpha\,e^{c_1(\mathcal P)}\bigr),
\]

whose action on integral cohomology is integral (up to the standard sign and
the pullback by ([-1])).  On the polarization line one has, with convention-
dependent signs,

\[
 \mathcal F_\Theta(\Theta^{[k]})=\pm\Theta^{[g-k]}.
\]

Full PD saturation therefore makes the entire span

\[
 \bigoplus_{k=0}^g\mathbf Z\,\Theta^{[k]}
\]

Fourier-stable inside the ordinary divisor-product lattice.  Likewise the
inverse Lefschetz operator satisfies

\[
 \Lambda_\Theta(\Theta^{[k]})=(g-k+1)\Theta^{[k-1]}
\]

under the usual normalization, so the polarization divided-power line is
stable under the integral action of the Lefschetz (\mathfrak{sl}_2) on this
submodule.

This is the strongest unconditional Fourier consequence.  It is **not** a
proof that

\[
 \mathcal F_\Theta(P_A^k)\subset P_A^{g-k}.
\]

The transform of an arbitrary divisor product can involve the dual
polarization/isogeny and rational inverse matrices.  Full saturation of pure
powers does not control all mixed Fourier images.  An integral Fourier theorem
on the whole (P_A^\bullet) requires a separate mixed-product closure lemma.
The audited integral-Fourier literature distinguishes exactly this issue
from algebraicity of divided powers.

The same boundary applies to the Lefschetz standard correspondence.  The
principal polarization and Poincare bundle give the usual algebraic
cohomological correspondence, and its restriction to the displayed
polarization line is integral.  Integrality on the entire divisor-product
lattice or on all Hodge classes is not a formal consequence.

## 3. Integral Hodge conjecture: no automatic upgrade

Always

\[
 P_A^k\subset \operatorname{Hdg}^{2k}(A,\mathbf Z).
\]

Full PD saturation proves that the distinguished classes
(D^k/k!) lie in this smaller subgroup.  It does not prove

\[
 \operatorname{Hdg}^{2k}(A,\mathbf Z)=P_A^k,
\]

nor the integral Hodge conjecture for one-cycles or higher codimension.
To obtain such a consequence one needs an independent theorem identifying
the full integral Hodge lattice with the saturation of the divisor-product
lattice and then proving that saturation is trivial.  Rational invariant
theory for a non-CM elliptic power is insufficient: an isogeny quotient can
retain integral lattice indices even when its rational Hodge algebra is
Lefschetz.

Accordingly, the safe language is “all polarization divided powers are
integral divisor products,” not “all Hodge classes are algebraic” or “the
integral Hodge conjecture holds.”

## 4. Deformation and Franchetta boundary

Suppose the marked graph construction spreads over a smooth connected base
after a finite etale level cover, with the coefficient graph and its local
NS lattice forming a finite local system.  The graph congruences and the
rank-one straightening identities then spread in relative integral
cohomology.  The classes (\Theta^{[k]}) remain in the fiberwise product image,
and at special fibers with extra Neron--Severi classes the target product
lattice only enlarges.

This is a relative **cohomological** persistence statement.  Faithfully flat
descent of the local coefficient lattice does not produce global relative
divisors unless the rank-one classes carry compatible descent data.  Nor does
it produce a relative Chow cycle or a decomposition of the diagonal.  A
Franchetta conclusion would require a separate Chow-theoretic universal-cycle
argument, including algebraic square-zero identities and monodromy descent.

Thus the finite-etale graph theorem supports a marked cohomological family
statement, but no new Franchetta property of the unmarked moduli space.

## 5. Moduli and geometric priority

The finite-etale slope condition is discrete (p)-adic level data.  Varying
the elliptic curve with fixed graph data gives at most the corresponding
one-dimensional modular family after finite level, and the PD identities are
constant integral-local-system identities along it.  This does not by itself
define a positive-codimension special subvariety in \(\mathcal A_g\): a PEL
or Shimura conclusion needs a global Rosati-stable field/order action, which
the local trace algebra does not supply automatically.

The genuinely useful geometric message is therefore narrower but clean:
finite-etale elliptic-power graph quotients have no factorial divided-power
defect for the entire marked cohomological divisor lattice in the degrees
covered by the theorem.  This is an integral lattice statement, not an
effectivity, Jacobian, stable-rationality, or moduli-rigidity statement.

## EJ/TT closeout

- **Formal/known:** all (\Theta^{[k]}), including the degree-one
  cohomological point class, are ordinary integral divisor products under full
  PD saturation; the polarization line is stable under Fourier and Lefschetz
  operations.
- **Useful but limited:** this packages the finite-etale result as an
  all-codimension integral Lefschetz theorem for marked graph quotients.
- **Not implied:** Fourier stability of the entire product lattice, the
  integral Hodge conjecture, Chow/effectivity, or a Franchetta theorem.
- **Open:** prove mixed-product Fourier closure or a global PEL realization;
  either would create a genuinely stronger geometric consequence.
