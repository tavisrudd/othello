## The supported-two-subspace lemma

Let \(M\) be a finite-dimensional Hermitian space and let \(L,U\subset M\)
be nonzero subspaces. Denote their orthogonal projections by \(P_L,P_U\)
and their full supported algebras by

\[
\mathcal E_L=P_L\operatorname{End}(M)P_L,\qquad
\mathcal E_U=P_U\operatorname{End}(M)P_U.
\]

### Lemma

If

\[
L+U=M,\qquad L\not\perp U,
\]

then the unital algebra generated by \(\mathcal E_L\) and
\(\mathcal E_U\) is \(\operatorname{End}(M)\).

### Proof

An operator commuting with \(\mathcal E_L\) is block diagonal for
\(M=L\oplus L^\perp\) and acts by a scalar \(\lambda\) on \(L\). An operator
commuting with \(\mathcal E_U\) similarly acts by a scalar \(\mu\) on \(U\).
Since \(L\not\perp U\), choose \(l\in L,u\in U\) with
\(\langle l,u\rangle\ne0\). Applying the same statement to the adjoint
shows \(\lambda=\mu\). Because \(L+U=M\), the common commutant consists only
of scalars. The generated algebra is a finite-dimensional star algebra, so
the double-commutant theorem gives \(\operatorname{End}(M)\).
\(\square\)

For one McKay multiplicity space,

\[
L=\operatorname{im}D_{\rho,n-6},\qquad
U=\operatorname{im}D_{\rho,n}^\dagger.
\]

The all-weight two-sided-defect theorem gives \(L+U=M_{\rho,n}\) above
degree \(52\). If neighboring full path corners are available, pushing
their matrix units through the edge maps supplies
\(\mathcal E_L,\mathcal E_U\). At a codimension-two peak with multiplicity
\(m\),

\[
\dim L=m-2,\qquad\dim U=m-1.
\]

For \(m\ge4\), their dimension sum exceeds \(m\), so they cannot be
orthogonal. The lemma therefore closes such a peak once both neighboring
corners are already known.

This proves the local algebra statement anticipated in the plan. It does
not solve the plateau anchoring problem.

A deterministic sweep computes \(\ker\Delta_n\) at

## Why the induction fails

Assume maximal rank in all weights. On the displayed trivial plateau:

- the six maps between degrees \(124,130,\ldots,154\) are square
  isomorphisms;
- the map \(118\to124\) has one-dimensional image in a
  two-dimensional space;
- the map \(154\to160\) is injective into a three-dimensional space.

Transport across the square edges says that if any plateau corner is full,
then all are full. It does not make the first one full. Induction on
multiplicity knows the degree-\(118\) corner but not the degree-\(160\)
corner. Induction on degree knows the left boundary but supplies only its
supported line. The two procedures therefore refer back to the same
unknown multiplicity-two corner.

This plateau repeats after a \(60\)-degree shift with every multiplicity
raised by one. It is not a finite low-degree anomaly.

The same issue explains the logical distinction in the proof plan:
neighboring full *path* corners would provide full supported algebras, but
the three local return operators must themselves furnish the missing
plateau-entry mixing before those neighboring corners are available.
