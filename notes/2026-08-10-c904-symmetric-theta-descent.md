# C904 symmetric-theta descent lattice

Date: 2026-08-10
Status: quarantined Paper V research; no manuscript or Lean edits
Scope: integral descent through `Theta x Theta -> Sym^2 Theta` for the
ambient/tautological codimension-three carrier

## Verdict

The symmetric quotient does **not** turn the licensed ordered theta cut into
an odd carrier.  For every degree-six integral class pulled back from
`Sym^2 J`, restriction to an ordered addition fibre lies in

\[
                 2\bigwedge^6 H^1(J,\mathbf Z).
\]

This is the actual Nakaoka pullback lattice, not merely the swap-invariant
lattice.  Since the principal `Theta^2` functional on
`Lambda^6 H^1(J,Z)` has image `2Z`, the upstairs degree ideal after descent
is exactly

\[
                              4\mathbf Z.
\]

The quotient map has degree two, so the corresponding degree ideal on
`Sym^2 Theta` is exactly `2Z`, not `Z`.  An explicit algebraic witness is the
single divisor triple `D0 D1 D2`: its ordered degree is `-2`, its descended
pullback upstairs has degree `-4`, and the quotient cycle has degree `-2`.

Thus the symmetric quotient does not rescue the proposed `D3,3` construction
when its codimension-three cut is made from the ambient/horizontal Chow
lattice already licensed in C904.  The odd projective-plane transfers remain
odd and therefore preserve this final factor two.

There is one exact boundary.  This calculation does not rule out a genuinely
intrinsic codimension-three cycle on `Sym^2 Theta` whose class does not come
from `Sym^2 J`.  Such a class would have to use primitive integral theta
cohomology and would amount to a new integral-Chow lifting theorem for the
theta divisor.  No such algebraic lift is presently certified.

## 1. Nakaoka's integral lattice in degree six

Put `A=J` and let

\[
                    q:A\times A\longrightarrow\operatorname {Sym}^2A.
\]

Gugnin's Theorem 1, based on Nakaoka's integrality lemma, gives an additive
basis for the torsion-free integral cohomology of a symmetric product.  For
`n=2`, the degree-six pullback lattice is generated by:

1. `chi(a)=a x 1 + 1 x a` for an integral degree-six class `a`;
2. signed transfers
   \[
       a\mathbin\otimes b+(-1)^{|a||b|}b\mathbin\otimes a,
       \qquad |a|+|b|=6;
   \]
3. `2 b x b` for a repeated primitive **even-degree** class `b`.

The third type cannot occur in total degree six: it would require `|b|=3`,
which is odd.  This is the integral correction that a swap-invariant audit
alone would miss.

Over the generic addition point `j`, identify the ordered fibre by

\[
             r_j:A\longrightarrow A\times A,qquad
             x\longmapsto(x,j-x).
\]

Translations act trivially on cohomology, while the second coordinate acts
by `(-1)^k` in degree `k`.  Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
 r_j^*\chi(a)&=2a,\\
 r_j^*\left(a\otimes b+(-1)^{|a||b|}b\otimes a\right)
   &=2(-1)^{|b|}a\smile b.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every generator therefore restricts into `2 Lambda^6 H^1(A,Z)`.  Conversely
the classes `chi(e_I)`, for the 210 standard six-fold exterior basis
elements, restrict to `2e_I`.  Thus the image is exactly

\[
        r_j^*q^*H^6(\operatorname {Sym}^2A,\mathbf Z)/\mathrm {tors}
        =2\bigwedge^6H^1(A,\mathbf Z).
\]

The replay enumerates all 38,760 signed Kunneth transfers; 13,440 have
nonzero restriction.

## 2. Branch and Chow descent

The fixed diagonal has codimension five in `A x A` and codimension four in
`Theta x Theta`.  It cannot support a codimension-three cycle.  Localization
therefore shows that removing the branch locus introduces no new
codimension-three supported class.  At the integral-cohomology level, the
possible repeated-factor correction is exactly Nakaoka's `2 b x b`; as
above, it is absent in degree six.

The generators used for the algebraic witness have actual Chow descent.  If
`Z` is a codimension-three cycle on `A`, then

\[
      q_*[Z\times A]
\]

is a cycle on `Sym^2 A`, and away from the irrelevant branch codimension its
pullback is

\[
                  [Z\times A]+[A\times Z]=\chi([Z]).
\]

Intersecting with `Sym^2 Theta` gives the desired descended theta carrier.
Similarly, signed transfers are represented by orbit sums of product
cycles.  This is why the exact factor four is a Chow statement for the
ambient/tautological lattice, not only a cohomological parity observation.

For line bundles the restriction is even already in codimension one.  A
swap-descended divisor on the ordered fibre has class `2D`; three descended
first-Chern-class cuts consequently carry an even larger factor.  Allowing
general ambient codimension-three Chow classes reduces the exact upstairs
ideal to four, but never to two.

## 3. Exact degree ideal and witness

Write the principal polarization as

\[
              \theta=\sum_{i=1}^5 a_i\wedge b_i.
\]

Then

\[
       \theta^2=2\sum_{i<j}
       a_i\wedge b_i\wedge a_j\wedge b_j,
\]

so its functional on `Lambda^6 H^1(A,Z)` has image exactly `2Z`.  Combining
this with the Nakaoka restriction lattice gives

\[
 \left\{\int_A\theta^2 r_j^*q^*\gamma:
 \gamma\in H^6(\operatorname {Sym}^2A,\mathbf Z)/\mathrm {tors}\right\}
 =4\mathbf Z.
\]

The bound is algebraically sharp on the exotic marked Jacobian.  In the
certified Neron--Severi basis,

\[
                    z=D_0D_1D_2
\]

satisfies

\[
                    \int_A\theta^2z=-2.
\]

The descended cycle `q_*(Z x A)` therefore has ordered pullback degree `-4`
and quotient degree `-2`.  This proves that the two degree ideals are exactly
`4Z` upstairs and `2Z` downstairs.

## 4. What this proves for D3,3

The `D3,3` architecture factors through `Sym^2 Theta`: its two twisted-cubic
coordinates have odd transfer degree, while the proposed horizontal cut is
codimension three on the symmetric theta base.  Every such cut coming from
the ambient/horizontal Chow lattice has even degree by the theorem above.
Multiplying by odd transfer degrees cannot change that parity.

Therefore:

> No ambient divisor, tautological-Chern, or ambient codimension-three Chow
> cut on the symmetric theta base produces an odd `D3,3` carrier.

An unrestricted statement about all of `CH^3(Sym^2 Theta)` would require
control of primitive theta classes.  Topologically, `H^6(Theta,Z)` contains
classes not restricted from `J`; Nakaoka's theorem includes their `chi`
classes as well.  To turn one into an odd algebraic carrier, one would need
an algebraic primitive class whose addition degree is odd.  The current
divisor/Hodge saturation on `J` neither constructs nor excludes that class.

## 7. Mystery ledger

- **Settled:** pullback from the symmetric square is smaller than the naive
  invariant lattice in exactly the way prescribed by Nakaoka.
- **Settled:** the ambient descended degree ideal is `4Z` upstairs and `2Z`
  downstairs; the quotient does not produce an odd carrier.
- **Settled:** diagonal/branch corrections cannot enter codimension three,
  and the only relevant Nakaoka repeated-factor correction is absent in
  total degree six.
- **Open:** whether primitive integral theta cohomology contains an algebraic
  codimension-three class of odd addition degree.  This is the sole possible
  symmetric-theta escape beyond the licensed ambient Chow lattice.

## 8. EJ + TT closeout

### Uniform upgrade

The Nakaoka argument is not special to dimension five.  Let `A` be any
abelian variety with torsion-free exterior cohomology and let `k>0`.  On the
addition fibre `y=j-x`, every degree-`2k` Nakaoka generator restricts into
`2 H^{2k}(A,Z)`, while the classes `chi(a)` show that every element of
`2 H^{2k}(A,Z)` occurs.  Repeated even factors cause no exception: their
generator is `2b x b`, whose restriction is already in twice the integral
lattice.  Hence

\[
 r_j^*q^*H^{2k}(\operatorname {Sym}^2A,\mathbf Z)/\mathrm {tors}
 =2H^{2k}(A,\mathbf Z).
\]

For a principally polarized `g`-fold, an ambient codimension-`g-2` cut of
`Sym^2 Theta` therefore has upstairs degree ideal `4Z` and downstairs degree
ideal `2Z`, because `theta^2` has exact divisibility two.  The symmetric
quotient never removes the last factor two in this entire ambient
architecture, in any dimension.

### The real remaining degree of freedom

The quotient arithmetic is now closed.  The only possible escape is an
integral Chow--Lefschetz defect on the theta divisor itself.  In dimension
five, if `i:Theta -> A`, an intrinsic class `a in CH^3(Theta)` would give
downstairs degree equal to the Pontryagin/addition degree of `i_*a` with
`Theta`.  An odd carrier is therefore equivalent, within this route, to an
algebraic primitive theta class whose pushforward has odd addition degree.
The already constructed primitive minimal curve on `A` makes this a sharp
question rather than vague slack, but no algebraic preimage under
`i_*:CH_1(Theta)->CH_1(A)` is known.
