## 0. Verdict

**No.** The labelled costs of the dual tower are not a function of the labelled costs of the primal
tower, and the counterexample is as small as it can be: two binary two-level towers with a
two-dimensional message space and four leaves, differing only in how the inner repetition lengths
are split, `3 + 1` against `2 + 2`. They have identical labelled cost profiles; their duals have
different ones, different even as multisets, so no relabelling of the dual message space reconciles
them.

The reason is exact and it names the missing invariant: **the labelled cost profile sees which
functional classes the leaves realise, and not how many leaves realise each class.** The dual code
sees the multiplicities. So the profile is blind to precisely the data that duality depends on.

What survives is a clean sufficient condition, proved in §3 and corroborated in every sweep: for a
**projective** tower — leaves nonzero and pairwise non-proportional — the labelled cost profile
determines the compiled tower up to monomial equivalence, hence determines its dual's labelled
costs. And the amount profile still dualises: Wei duality is untouched, which is exactly what makes
the failure informative rather than merely negative.

## 1. The setting, and the two profiles

A compiled tower over `F_q` with message-functional space `M^* = F_q^k` is a generator matrix `G`
of size `k × n` whose column `j` is the leaf functional `g_j ∈ M^*`; the observation model is one
unit per leaf at cost 1. Write `C = rowspace(G) ≤ F_q^n` for the code it generates.

* The **labelled cost** of a secret functional `f ∈ M^*` is `Λ(f) = min{|H| : f ∈ span{g_j : j ∈ H}}`,
  probe 5's per-class minimum coalition cost. It is constant on projective classes, so the
  **labelled cost profile** is a function on the `(q^k − 1)/(q − 1)` classes.
* The **amount profile** is probe 2's `Γ_t = min{|H| : dim(S ∩ V_H) ≥ t}` with `S = M^*`.

**Lemma 1 (labelled costs are coset-leader weights).**
`Λ(f) = min{wt(x) : x ∈ F_q^n, G x^T = f^T}`, the least weight in the coset of `C^⊥` with syndrome
`f`.

*Proof.* `f ∈ span{g_j : j ∈ H}` says `f = Σ_{j ∈ H} x_j g_j` for some `x` supported on `H`, that is
`G x^T = f^T` with `supp(x) ⊆ H`; the least such `|H|` is the least weight of such an `x`. The
solutions of `G x^T = f^T` form a coset of `ker(x ↦ G x^T) = C^⊥`. ∎

So the primal labelled profile is the coset-leader weight function of `C^⊥` indexed by syndrome,
and the **dual tower's** labelled profile — the tower whose generator matrix is a basis of `C^⊥` —
is the coset-leader weight function of `C`. The question of the probe is therefore whether the
coset-leader function of `C^⊥` determines that of `C`. There is no MacWilliams identity at that
level, which is the one-sentence reason to expect the negative; the coset-leader weight enumerator
and its refinements are studied precisely because they are not dual-determined.

The contrast that makes this a question at all: Wei duality *does* determine the dual's amount
profile from the primal's, and the relative version for a nested pair is the single-level statement
the probe brief quotes. So the probe asks whether the labelled refinement inherits that.

## 2. The counterexample

Both towers are over `F_2` with message dimension `k = 2` and `n = 4` leaves; both are honest
two-level towers with the `[2, 2]` identity outer code over `F_2` and repetition inner codes, and
they differ only in the inner lengths.

| tower | inner lengths | leaves (columns of `G`) | code `C` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `T(3,1)` | 3 and 1 | `e1, e1, e1, e2` | `{(a, a, a, b)}` |
| `T(2,2)` | 2 and 2 | `e1, e1, e2, e2` | `{(a, a, b, b)}` |

**Primal labelled costs, on the classes `e1, e2, e1 + e2`:** `(1, 1, 2)` for both. Each class of a
leaf costs 1; `e1 + e2` needs one leaf of each kind, so 2. The profiles are equal as labelled
functions under the identity labelling of the message space, so no weaker notion of agreement is
being used.

**Dual towers.** `T(3,1)^⊥` is generated by `(1,1,0,0)` and `(1,0,1,0)`, whose columns are
`(1,1), (1,0), (0,1), (0,0)`; every nonzero class appears as a single column, so its labelled costs
are `(1, 1, 1)`. `T(2,2)^⊥` is generated by `(1,1,0,0)` and `(0,0,1,1)`, whose columns are
`(1,0), (1,0), (0,1), (0,1)`; the class `(1,1)` is not a column, so its labelled costs are
`(1, 1, 2)`. As multisets, `{1,1,1} ≠ {1,1,2}`.

**Proposition 2.** The labelled cost profile of a tower does not determine the labelled cost
profile of its dual, while the amount profile of the dual is determined. `T(3,1)` and `T(2,2)` have
the same labelled profile and the same amount profile `(Γ_1, Γ_2) = (1, 2)`; both duals also have
amount profile `(1, 2)`, as Wei duality requires; and the duals' labelled profiles differ. ∎

Two remarks on the reading of "dual tower".

1. Above, the dual tower is the dual code `(A ∘ B)^⊥` compiled as a tower in its own right. Under
   the other reading — concatenate the level-wise duals, `A^⊥ ∘ B^⊥` — the negative is even more
   immediate, because the inner duals of `T(3,1)` are the `[3, 2]` and `[1, 0]` codes while those of
   `T(2,2)` are `[2, 1]` twice, so the two dual towers do not even share a shape. The trace-dual
   inner code is the classical condition making the two readings agree; it is irrelevant here,
   because the counterexample is a pair of *primal* towers that the primal labelled data cannot
   distinguish, and every reading of dualisation separates them.
2. Nothing in the counterexample needs the repetition presentation. It is the statement that two
   codes with the same coset-leader function on the dual side can have different ones on the primal
   side; the repetition presentation is only the shortest way to exhibit it as a tower, and it is
   the shape a real inner code has.

## 3. The sufficient condition, and the exact missing invariant

**Theorem 3 (projective towers).** Let a compiled tower have leaf functionals that are nonzero and
pairwise non-proportional. Then its labelled cost profile determines the tower up to permutation
and scaling of leaves and a change of basis of the message space, and therefore determines the
labelled cost profile of its dual.

*Proof.* `Λ(f) = 1` exactly when `f` is a nonzero multiple of some leaf functional, so the profile
names the set of leaf classes. Projectivity makes that set the whole leaf multiset, so `G` is
determined up to column order, column scaling, and a change of basis of `M`. Those operations act
on `C` as a monomial transformation composed with a generator change of basis, and they carry `C^⊥`
to the monomial image of `C^⊥` under the inverse-scaled monomial map. Monomial maps preserve
supports, hence preserve the labelled costs of Lemma 1. ∎

**Corollary 4 (the missing invariant).** In general the labelled cost profile *together with the
multiplicity of each cost-1 class* determines the dual's labelled cost profile, by the same
argument. So exactly one integer per leaf class is missing from the labelled data, and that is the
whole gap.

**Corollary 5 (why the negative is the practical case).** A two-level tower whose inner codes have
dimension 1 — repetition, spreading, and every masking layout that writes one message symbol into
several leaves — has proportional leaves whenever any inner length exceeds 1, so it is not
projective. The towers for which labelled duality would be usable are therefore not the towers a
tower is normally built to be.

## 4. The sweep

**What is enumerated.** For a field, a message dimension `k` and a leaf count `n`, every tower is
enumerated as a multiset of `n` projective classes of `M^*` — that is exactly a two-level tower
whose outer part is the set of distinct classes and whose inner code on each outer coordinate is a
repetition code of length its multiplicity. Towers whose leaves do not span `M^*` are dropped, since
their dual has the wrong dimension and the comparison is vacuous. The remainder are grouped by
labelled cost profile, and a group is **ambiguous** when two of its members have different dual cost
multisets. Comparing multisets rather than labelled functions is deliberate: it makes each
counterexample immune to any relabelling of the dual message space.

**Trusted boundary.** The core's `SmallField` arithmetic and probe 2's `FlatInstance::contains`, both
already load-bearing. The parity-check construction is new and is checked directly in a unit test:
every dual leaf column pairs to zero with every generator row.

Readings:

* **Ambiguity is the norm, not the exception.** At `q = 2`, `k = 3`, `n = 6` and `n = 7`, 84 of the
  91 and 92 profile classes contain two towers whose duals differ. The labelled profile is very far
  from a complete invariant.
* **The smallest counterexample has four leaves**, the `T(3,1)` against `T(2,2)` pair of §2, found
  as the first collision of the `q = 2`, `k = 2`, `n = 4` sweep.
* **No projective ambiguity anywhere**, as Theorem 3 requires. The projective column also shows why
  that is not a practical escape: projective towers are a vanishing fraction as `n` grows past the
  number of classes, and there is exactly one at `q = 2`, `k = 3`, `n = 7`.

## 5. What this means for the product

1. **The engine must compile and analyse the dual tower directly; it cannot transport a labelled
   privacy statement across duality.** The cost is one extra compile and one extra sweep, with no
   new machinery — the dual tower is an ordinary instance of the same type.
2. **Amount-level claims do transport.** A statement about `Γ_t` on one side gives one on the other
   through Wei duality, and probe 2's Theorem A computes `Γ_t` on either side in one pass. So the
   engine can answer "how many symbols leak at cost `c`" dually and "which functional leaks at cost
   `c`" only natively. That is a scope line worth stating in the interface, because a user will
   assume the labelled answer transports as well.
3. **A cheap fast path exists and should be implemented as a check, not an assumption**: test
   whether the compiled leaves are nonzero and pairwise non-proportional; if so, Theorem 3 licenses
   deriving the dual's labelled costs from the primal's. Expect the check to fail on any tower with
   a repetition or spreading layer, which is most of them.
4. **The finding sharpens what "labelled" buys.** The lane's product claim is that labelled
   composition is exact where the surveyed tools are amount-only. This probe shows the other face of
   that claim: the labelled data is strictly finer than the amount data in what it says, and
   strictly coarser than the code in what it determines, so it does not inherit the amount level's
   duality. The engine's exactness comes from computing, not from an invariant that closes under
   duality.
