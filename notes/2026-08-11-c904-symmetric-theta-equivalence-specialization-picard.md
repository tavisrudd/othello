# C904: symmetric-theta equivalences, cusp specialization, and the generic Picard/Brauer flank

Date: 2026-08-11  
Status: quarantined theorem/reduction note; no manuscript or Lean edits  
Scope: the relative cubic lifting gate, semistable specialization, and the
universal generic unordered-theta fibre

## Verdict

There is an exact equivalence, but it is narrower than the tempting slogan.
For the generic unordered-theta fibre

\[
 Y_a=\bigl(\Theta\cap(a-\Theta)\bigr)/(x\mapsto a-x)
\]

the following are equivalent:

1. `ind(Y_a)=1`;
2. `Y_a` has an odd-degree zero-cycle;
3. the degree image of
   `CH^3(Sym^2 Theta) -> CH^0(J)=Z[J]` contains an odd integer.

The known degree-two cut is essential in `2 => 1`.  After the verified odd
`D_{3,3}` relays, this is also equivalent to index one for the charge-three
Abel--Jacobi fibre **along that channel**.  It therefore gives an integral
relative identity correspondence by Bezout with the known multiplier two.

Two stronger-looking statements imply this core but are not known to be
equivalent to it:

- a primitive or odd-theta-degree one-cycle supported directly on `Theta`;
- a horizontal Shen half on `D_+=F+F` for each support component of the
  relative minimal cycle.

The converse fails formally because an odd class on `Sym^2 Theta` may have a
middle-Kunneth component and need not be a product with `Theta` or arise from
`D_+`.  Likewise an arbitrary integral cubic identity correspondence need
not factor through `M_9` or `D_{3,3}`.  Thus the equivalence with the identity
correspondence is exact only for the Voisin/charge-three construction unless
one first proves a factorization theorem for all identities.

Semistable cusp specialization does not presently close the positive gate.
Specialization sends generic zero-cycles to special zero-cycles, so special
index divides generic index.  A special index-two model would obstruct the
generic odd cycle, but a special odd cycle can be vertical and need not lift.
Gabber--Liu--Lorenzini compute the local index from a regular proper model,
but local index one over a cusp henselization still does not imply index one
over the global surface function field.

The universal generic Picard/Brauer flank has a clean negative answer.  Let
`A` be a fixed principally polarized abelian fivefold over an algebraically
closed field of characteristic zero, let `K=k(A)` and let `a` be its generic
point.  On the smooth free locus of the above quotient,

\[
              \operatorname{Br}(K)\longrightarrow
              \operatorname{Br}(K(Y_a))
\]

is injective.  Hence neither the charge-two gerbe nor any other central
simple algebra over `K` can prove `ind(Y_a)=2`.  The missing two-primary bit,
if nonzero, is a genuinely higher-Chow/index obstruction rather than a
Brauer obstruction.

This Brauer-kernel theorem is for the universal field `k(A)`.  After pullback
to a special support surface the Kummer torsor can shrink and the Picard
invariant calculation must be repeated; no supportwise index conclusion is
claimed from it.

## 1. The exact implication diagram

Let `m:Sym^2 Theta -> J` be addition and put `K=k(J)`.  Its generic fibre is
`Y`.  The ordered fibre is

\[
 W=\Theta\cap(a-\Theta),
\]

and the involution is `iota(x)=a-x`.  For generic `a`, `W` is smooth and
`iota` is free: a fixed point would put `a` in the proper divisor
`[2](Theta)`.

An existing degree-two algebraic cut gives

\[
                         \operatorname{ind}(Y)\mid2.
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{ind}(Y)=1
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \deg CH_0(Y)\text{ contains an odd integer}
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \deg_m CH^3(\operatorname{Sym}^2\Theta)\not\subset2\mathbf Z.}
\]

The last equivalence uses closure/spread: a zero-cycle on the generic fibre
spreads to a codimension-three cycle over a dense open of `J`, and an
addition multisection restricts to a generic zero-cycle.

### 1.1 Theta support is sufficient, not equivalent

If `i:Theta -> J` and `gamma in CH^3(Theta)` has odd theta degree, then

\[
 \delta=q_*(\gamma\times[\Theta])
       \in CH^3(\operatorname{Sym}^2\Theta)
\]

has odd addition degree.  In particular, if `i_*gamma=c=Theta^4/4!`, then

\[
                         \deg_m(\delta)=\int_J c\Theta=5.
\]

Thus

\[
 \text{primitive theta support}
 \Longrightarrow \operatorname{ind}(Y)=1.
\]

There is no proved reverse implication.  `CH^3(Sym^2 Theta)` contains
middle-degree correspondence classes not generated by products
`gamma x Theta`.  An odd generic zero-cycle may use one of those classes.
Failure to support `c` on `Theta` would therefore not prove index two.

### 1.2 The horizontal `D_+` half is supportwise sufficient

Common-line normalization gives

\[
 F\subset\Theta,
 \qquad \operatorname{Sym}^2F\dashrightarrow D_+=F+F
\]

birationally.  Once a horizontal Shen minimal cycle `eta` on `D_+` is
available, its strict transform gives degree one on the unordered theta
fibre over the generic point of every nonexceptional component.  On the
exceptional locus, the degree-two centered lift and the degree-five
plane-quintic lift combine by `3*2-5=1`.  Hence a complete horizontal Shen
half implies index one on every support field occurring in `eta`.

The converse is not formal.  A degree-one signed zero-cycle on `Y` need not
push to the prescribed minimal Chow class on `D_+`, and choices of halves
form a torsor under an actual Chow kernel, not under a known finite
`J[2]`-module.

### 1.3 Exact equivalence with the charge-three channel

Let `S_i` be a support surface of the existing relative minimal cycle and
put `K_i=k(S_i)`.  Let

- `Y_i` be the corresponding unordered-theta fibre;
- `V_i` be the generic fibre of Voisin's charge-three Abel--Jacobi map;
- `D_i` be the normalized `D_{3,3}` incidence.

The degree-fifteen packet and the plane-cubic symmetric-square relay give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
  \operatorname{ind}(V_i)&\mid\operatorname{ind}(D_i)
                    \mid15\operatorname{ind}(V_i),\\
  \operatorname{ind}(Y_i)&\mid\operatorname{ind}(D_i)
                    \mid3\operatorname{ind}(Y_i).
 \end{aligned}
\]

Consequently

\[
                 v_2(\operatorname{ind}V_i)
                  =v_2(\operatorname{ind}Y_i).
\]

Both endpoints already have index dividing two in the C904 construction.
Conditional only on the all-good degree-fifteen packet meeting the generic
marked `A_5` fibre, this yields the exact channel equivalence

\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{ind}(Y_i)=1
 \Longleftrightarrow\operatorname{ind}(V_i)=1.}
\]

If this holds for every support component, choose a common odd lifting
degree `n`.  The Voisin construction gives an integral correspondence
inducing `[n]` on the relative intermediate Jacobian.  Together with the
existing integral correspondence inducing `[2]`, Bezout gives an integral
identity correspondence.

Conversely, an identity produced through this same `M_9/D_{3,3}` channel
forces odd index.  No converse is claimed for an arbitrary identity
correspondence: a different carrier might avoid `M_9` entirely.

## 2. Specialization: the direction that is actually valid

Let `R` be a DVR with fraction field `L` and residue field `kappa`, and let
`mathcal Y/R` be proper and flat with smooth proper generic fibre.  Every
generic zero-cycle specializes with its degree unchanged.  Hence

\[
                       \operatorname{ind}(Y_0)mid
                       \operatorname{ind}(Y_L).
\]

This has asymmetric force:

- special index two proves that the generic index is even;
- special index one says only that `1` divides the generic index.

For a regular irreducible model with special divisor

\[
                         \sum_i r_i\Gamma_i,
\]

Gabber--Liu--Lorenzini, Theorem 8.2, prove

\[
 \gcd_i\{r_i\operatorname{ind}(\Gamma_i^{\rm reg}/\kappa)\}
       \mid\operatorname{ind}(Y_L),
\]

with equality when `R` is henselian.  Thus a cusp can close the gate
**negatively** if every displayed term is even.  A multiplicity-one split
rational component merely proves local index one over the henselization.

There is a second local/global trap.  The fields relevant to the relative
cycle are `C(S_i)`, where `S_i` is a surface.  Index one over one or all cusp
henselizations does not imply index one over `C(S_i)` without a local-global
theorem for an identified obstruction.  No such theorem applies to the
present Chow-half torsor, which has not been represented by a finite
cohomology class.

### 2.1 What would make a positive cusp argument valid

One of the following additional inputs would suffice:

1. an actual horizontal odd multisection over the original DVR, not merely
   a point on the special fibre or its henselization;
2. a relative Hilbert/Chow component through the special cycle which is
   smooth at that cycle and has a section over the original base;
3. a finite two-primary obstruction whose global class is determined by
   its divisorial residues, together with vanishing of the unramified
   remainder; or
4. a global relative cycle over the whole compactified support surface,
   rather than separate formal cycles at its boundary curves.

The current obstruction is a Chow-kernel torsor, so Tsen, Steinberg, and
Graber--Harris--Starr do not supply any of these statements over a complex
surface function field.

### 2.2 The chordal degeneration is a useful warning

Casalaina-Martin--Grushevsky--Hulek--Laza show that the chordal boundary is
mapped to the hyperelliptic genus-five Jacobian locus.  On such a special
Jacobian, theta contains the Abel curve of minimal class.  That effective
minimal curve cannot deform horizontally to a smooth cubic intermediate
Jacobian: by Matsusaka--Ran, an indecomposable ppav containing an effective
minimal-class curve is a Jacobian.

Thus even a very natural odd theta-supported curve on a special fibre can
be purely vertical.  Totally degenerate semiabelic cusps, whose special
theta strata contain many toric/rational cycles, require at least as much
care.

## 3. Minimal compactification theorem still needed

The existing C904 cusp calculation determines the marked widths `2,2,6,6`,
the monodromy lattices, log one-motives, and component groups.  It does not
determine the stable theta strata or the closure of addition.  A stable
semiabelic variety is not a group, so addition does not automatically
extend.

For a theorem-grade **local index calculation**, one needs:

1. a regular proper flat model of the generic unordered-theta fibre,
   constructed from the Alexeev stable semiabelic pair;
2. the schematic closure of the addition graph in the triple product;
3. flat theta-by-theta incidence and compatibility with the transposition
   quotient;
4. all special components, multiplicities, and indices of their regular
   loci over the residue field.

These data feed directly into Gabber--Liu--Lorenzini's gcd formula.  For a
**positive global theorem**, add a horizontal-lifting or local-global input
from Section 2.1.  The log one-motive extension by itself supplies neither.

The highest-value explicit test remains the width-six cusp: export its full
monodromy quadratic form and theta Fourier expansion, build the periodic
Delaunay model, and compute the saturated initial ideal of
`theta(x)=theta(a-x)=0`.  Component-group counts alone are insufficient.

## 4. Universal generic Picard/Brauer theorem

This section treats a fixed abelian variety `A/k`, with `k` algebraically
closed of characteristic zero, and the universal target point
`a in A(K)`, `K=k(A)`.  Let `L` be a symmetric principal polarization,
let `Theta` be a symmetric divisor, and assume the generic intersection

\[
                W=\Theta\cap(a-\Theta)
\]

is smooth.  The fixed locus of `iota(x)=a-x` lies over `[2](Theta)`, so the
generic quotient `Y=W/<iota>` is smooth and the cover `W->Y` is etale.

> **Generic unordered-theta Brauer-kernel theorem.**  If the restriction
> `Pic(A_bar K)->Pic(W_bar K)` is an isomorphism (as it is here by
> Grothendieck--Lefschetz), then
> \[
>                  \operatorname{Br}(K)\hookrightarrow
>                  \operatorname{Br}(K(Y)).
> \]

### 4.1 Centering and geometric Picard group

Adjoin a half `b` of the generic point, `2b=a`.  The extension

\[
            K'=k(A_b)/k(A_a)=K'/K
\]

is the generic `A[2]`-torsor of degree `2^(2g)`.  In centered coordinates
`z=x-b`, the involution is `z -> -z` and

\[
             W_b=(\Theta-b)\cap(\Theta+b).
\]

Grothendieck--Lefschetz identifies `Pic(W_bar K)` with `Pic(A_bar K)`.
The `[-1]`-invariant part fits into

\[
 0\longrightarrow\widehat A[2]
  \longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(W_{\bar K})^{[-1]}
  \longrightarrow\operatorname{NS}(A)\longrightarrow0.
\]

Every Neron--Severi class has a symmetric representative, and the kernel is
exactly the two-torsion in `Pic^0`.

For the free double quotient, equivariant descent over the algebraically
closed field gives

\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathbf Z/2\langle\varepsilon\rangle
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(Y_{\bar K})
 \longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(W_{\bar K})^{[-1]}
 \longrightarrow0.
\]

Here `epsilon` is the character line bundle of the etale cover.  Surjectivity
on the right follows from
`H^2(C_2,\bar K^*)=\bar K^*/(\bar K^*)^2=0`.

### 4.2 The full `A[2]` monodromy calculation

The deck group `A[2]` changes the chosen half by `b -> b+t`.  In centered
coordinates this is translation by `t`.  If `N in NS(A)` and `H_N` is a
symmetric lift on the quotient, then modulo the cover character

\[
                t(H_N)=H_N+D_{\phi_N(t)},
\]

where `phi_N:A->Ahat` is the homomorphism associated to `N` and `D_s` is the
class descending the two-torsion line bundle `P_s`.

For `s in Ahat[2]`, translation changes its `[-1]`-linearization by the
perfect evaluation pairing:

\[
                 t(D_s)=D_s+e_2(s,t)\varepsilon.
\]

These two formulas determine the invariants.  If a class with
Neron--Severi component `N` is invariant under every `t`, then
`phi_N(t)=0` for all `t in A[2]`.  Hence `phi_N` factors through `[2]`, so

\[
                         \phi_N=2\psi.
\]

The identification of `NS(A)` with symmetric homomorphisms
`A->Ahat` makes `psi=phi_M` for some `M in NS(A)`, and therefore `N=2M`.
After removing this doubled class, perfectness of `e_2` forces `s=0`.
Consequently

\[
 \operatorname{Pic}(Y_{\bar K})^{G_K}
       =2\operatorname{NS}(A)\oplus
         \mathbf Z/2\langle\varepsilon\rangle
\]

in the above presentation.

Both types of invariant class already descend over `K`:

- `epsilon` is defined by `W->Y`;
- a doubled class is the descent, with the swap linearization, of
  `M|_W tensor iota^*M|_W`.

Thus

\[
             \operatorname{Pic}(Y_K)\twoheadrightarrow
             \operatorname{Pic}(Y_{\bar K})^{G_K}.
\]

### 4.3 Hochschild--Serre consequence

The low-degree Hochschild--Serre sequence contains

\[
 \operatorname{Pic}(Y_{\bar K})^{G_K}
   \xrightarrow{d_2}\operatorname{Br}(K)
   \longrightarrow\operatorname{Br}(Y).
\]

The preceding surjectivity makes `d_2=0`, hence `Br(K)->Br(Y)` is
injective.  Since `Y` is regular integral, `Br(Y)->Br(K(Y))` is injective as
well.  This proves the theorem.

In particular the nonzero charge-two class `alpha` cannot be the Picard
descent obstruction of `Y`.  More strongly, no class in `Br(K)` is killed by
`K(Y)`.  This kills the proposed Brauer route but does not prove index one:
higher-dimensional index-two varieties can have trivial Brauer kernel.

## 5. Boundaries and next valid attacks

- **Exact:** index one is equivalent to odd degree on the generic unordered
  theta fibre.
- **Exact, channel-relative:** after the odd `15/3` relays, this is
  equivalent to odd index for the charge-three fibre and yields the cubic
  identity by Bezout with `[2]`.
- **Only sufficient:** primitive theta support and a horizontal `D_+` Chow
  half.
- **Not equivalent without a new theorem:** an arbitrary cubic identity
  correspondence.
- **Cusp specialization can obstruct:** a regular model with even special
  gcd proves generic evenness.
- **Cusp specialization cannot presently construct:** special odd cycles
  and local index one do not produce a global surface-field cycle.
- **Brauer route dead over `k(J)`:** the generic unordered-theta fibre has
  trivial base-field Brauer kernel.
- **Still live:** an explicit intrinsic middle-Kunneth odd cycle; a global
  horizontal `D_+` half; or a regular cusp model whose special gcd is even.

## 7. Red-team caveats

1. The Picard/Brauer theorem uses the full generic Kummer torsor over
   `k(A)`.  It must not be copied unchanged to `k(S_i)`.
2. It assumes the generic complete intersection is smooth and that Picard
   Lefschetz applies; both hold on the stated generic open, not at the cusp.
3. The equality of endpoint indices in Section 1.3 remains conditional on
   the all-good degree-fifteen packet meeting the generic special `A_5`
   family, exactly as in the packet audit.
4. The charge-three equivalence is an equivalence of index parity, not an
   isomorphism of Chow motives.
5. No special-fibre theta component or multiplicity has been inferred from
   Neron component groups or cusp widths.
6. A zero Brauer kernel is not a proof of index one.
