# Paper II framing, unity, and ceiling review

**Date:** 2026-08-03
**Lane:** `clebsch` (Paper II stream; advisory, no manuscript edit)
**Author:** session review (Fable main agent) after a same-day brief read of the
Paper II source: abstract, introduction, the headline theorem, the
matching-product and classification machinery, and the statements of the
uniform balanced-orbit classification, fixed-line/Chow, and balanced-sheets
theorems, with the section skeleton of the back half. Companion context: the

## Assessment baseline

Brief-read grade for the released version with the C749 human-proof freeze and
C856 standards closure behind it: mid-nineties, the best-shaped paper of the
series. Reasons: it asks one question ("what does restriction to a conic
forget, and what does the quotient remember"), answers it with one headline
theorem whose clauses form an arc — classification, sharpness, orientation,
Gorenstein identification — and its classification clause is genuinely
uniform over all odd prime powers, which neither Paper I nor Paper III can
say of their centerpieces. The priority judo is already executed and
exemplary: the Rodríguez-Pajares–Ruano–Salizzoni pre-emption is absorbed as
background and the theorem runs in the reverse direction, with the credit
line stated plainly in the abstract.

The fixed-line/Chow theorem is the modern highlight: the failed stronger
reconstruction (trade-only faithfulness) is not a remark but a sharp theorem
— an affine line of structurally produced counterexamples with the unique
Chow point as the exact repair. Sharpness as theorem rather than caveat is
the pattern Papers I and III should imitate (and Paper I now does, via the
order-specificity witness).

## Where the ceiling sits

The configurations are exceptional objects, so the significance ceiling is
the same niche one the series shares. But the uniform classification pushes
against it harder than the siblings: "over every odd finite field, exactly
two matching orbits satisfy this intrinsic condition" is a complete answer
to a natural question, and referees reward completeness.

## Unity of mathematical level

The spine (matching products, classification with the modular obstruction,
balanced sheets and cubic orientation) is level-consistent theorem-grade
work; the socle/outer-parity interior is dense but load-bearing and uniform.
The unity problem is the back half. After the balanced-sheets theorem the
paper continues through six-profile reconstruction, modular depth, the
arithmetic splitting of the marker algebra, the cubic structure of the
profile plane, and the relative-cubic Tate plane — a refinement suite whose
register drops from "theorem answering the question" to "structural
exploration of the surviving objects." Placing the blocks:

| register                   | blocks there |
|----------------------------|--------------|
| uniform theorem            | classification; fixed line and Chow point; balanced sheets and cubic |
| recognition                | six-profile reconstruction |
| exploratory refinement     | modular depth; marker-algebra splitting; profile-plane cubic; Tate plane |

Levelers, ranked:

**L1 — promote six-profile reconstruction to the paper's second theorem.**
It is recognition-genre (the incidence profiles reconstruct the matching
rows), which matches the recognition re-centering recommended for Paper III
and gives the back half its own quotable result instead of a trailing
notebook. Cheap: mostly a framing and placement change.

**L2 — decide the refinement suite's fate explicitly.** The modular-depth,
marker-splitting, profile-plane, and Tate-plane sections are good
mathematics at the wrong register for the body. Options: compress each to
its one structural statement with the development moved to appendices (the
paper already has an appendix culture), or extract the suite toward a
future forward version once one of them produces a theorem at spine level.
Keeping them in the body at current length is the main drag on the read.

**L3 — a beyond-the-carrier open problem.** The fixed-line theorem shows
the two-valued trade condition lives on non-matching orbits too. The
natural completion — classify one-dimensional two-valued trades over all
orbit types, not only perfect-matching orbits — is exactly the kind of
displayed open problem that raises a classification paper's ceiling; the
fixed-line analysis already provides the first infinite family of examples.

## Series cross-anchors

The sheet-sign cubic of the icosahedral case is the same golden object that
Paper I reconstructs from syndrome data and Paper III realizes
operatorially; a short remark with cross-citations (through Paper I's now
self-contained Dye package rather than through Dye directly) would let the
series' three cubics be visibly one. Low cost, do in the C577 repackaging
window rather than as a separate pass.

## Venue instinct

The strongest JCTA case of the three papers: one-factorization
classification, incidence reconstruction, and a uniform completeness
theorem are that journal's diet, and the executed priority judo reads well
under hostile review. Journal of Pure and Applied Algebra or Journal of
Algebra are defensible alternates if the Gorenstein/inverse-system and
modular-representation content should pick the referee pool instead.
Designs, Codes and Cryptography remains the safe home. Since versions are
already publicly released, this bears on any journal submission decision,
not on the release identity.
