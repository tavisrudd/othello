## Mathematics and scope

The AME recognition theorem supplies homogeneous equations `A Q_G = Q_H A` in four entries. Their solution space `W≤M_2(F_q)` is computed in O(m²) field operations once systematic maps are available. Restrict `Q(A)=det(A)` to W. For odd prime q, symmetric elimination uses constant field work and gives a diagonal form of rank r. Rank zero never represents one; rank one represents one precisely when its nonzero coefficient is a square; rank at least two always represents one. This yields deterministic decision, without searching the field or classifying the endomorphism algebra.

For nonzero a,b, the exact count of solutions to `a x²+b y²=1` is `q−chi(−ab)`. Uniform x produces a square-or-zero right side with probability at least `(q−1)/(2q)≥1/3`. Cipolla extraction is also Las Vegas: for a nonzero square c, `t²−c` is a nonsquare for exactly `(q−1)/2` choices of t; exponentiation in a quadratic extension extracts the root in O(log q) base-field operations. Every returned determinant and propagated frame is verified. There is no Monte Carlo error or bounded-trial negative answer.

Result: deterministic decision and expected witness construction both cost O(m³+log q) field operations from promised check matrices, O(m²+log q) from systematic maps. With b=ceil(log_2 q), schoolbook bit cost is O(m³ b²+b³); no primality/AME recognition cost or party-permutation search is included. At the prime two there are at most sixteen candidates. The implementation is prime-field only, not an extension-field symplectic solver.

When an invertible intertwiner A0 exists, W=A0 E for the common centralizer E. The scalar/dual-number types correspond to rank-one square-class tests, the split/field types to rank-two forms, and full matrices to rank four. Directly restricting determinant avoids the preliminary task of finding A0. No theorem is based on the tests.
