# C536 — coherent polar-flag Fano boundary

**Lane:** `reed-solomon` · **Date:** 2026-07-24 · **Status:** complete

## Result

There is a sharp, scheme-theoretic contained-component theorem behind C512.
Let \(E\) be a rank-two locally free module, use divided-power coordinates
\(\Gamma^nE\), and let
\[
 \Phi_f:\mathbf P(E^\vee)\longrightarrow\mathbf P(\Gamma^{n-1}E),
 \qquad [\lambda]\longmapsto[\iota_\lambda f]
\]
be the first-polar family.  Write \(\Sigma_{2,n}\) for the rank-at-most-two
Hankel/catalecticant scheme in \(\mathbf P(\Gamma^nE)\).

For every \(n\ge5\), over \(\mathbf Z\) and hence after arbitrary base change,
\[
 \{f:\Phi_f(\mathbf P^1)\subset\Sigma_{2,n-1}\}
   =\Sigma_{2,n}                                             \tag{1}
\]
as closed schemes.  Degree five is sharp: for \(n=4\),
\(\Sigma_{2,3}=\mathbf P(\Gamma^3E)\), so every quartic polar line is
contained while \(\Sigma_{2,4}\) is still a proper cubic hypersurface.

For a positive-characteristic NRC nucleus with coordinate support \(S\), its
consecutive pullback is the reduced linear scheme
\[
 \mathbf P\langle e_j:j\in S,\ j-1\in S\rangle,              \tag{2}
\]
with the evident endpoint conventions.  After \(\ell\) consecutive lifts,
the support consists exactly of indices \(j\) for which
\[
 j,\ j-1,\ldots,j-\ell\in S.                                \tag{3}
\]
Thus a modular coherent flag exists exactly when the Lucas support contains
a run of \(\ell+1\) consecutive indices.  Equations (2)--(3) are kernels of
maps of locally free modules, so they have no hidden nonlinear equations or
embedded components and commute with base change.

Consequently, for a lower carrier presented componentwise as the rank-two
secant scheme plus specified NRC nuclei, the reduced geometric coherent-Fano
boundary has exactly two kinds of components:

1. the classical upper rank-two secant/tangent scheme \(\Sigma_{2,n}\); and
2. the Lucas linear schemes (2), iterated by (3).

There is no third component in this componentwise problem.  Cyclic, wild,
and residual splitting carriers are not silently absorbed into this
statement; their possible coherent pullbacks remain the level-specific
lower-package work already required by C512.  In particular, C531's
degree-nine positive-moduli arithmetic is not displaced.

This is the task card's success exit.  It recovers the contained-component
mechanism of C498, C509, C513, and C516 without a syndrome census and without
making a Chebotarev claim.

## 1. The coherent Fano functor

For a closed subscheme
\(X\subset\mathbf P(\Gamma^{n-1}E)\), define
\(\operatorname{CFano}_n(X)\) on a base scheme \(T\) by
\[
 \operatorname{CFano}_n(X)(T)=
 \{[f]\in\mathbf P(\Gamma^nE)(T):
   \iota_\lambda f\in X
   \text{ for every }[\lambda]\in\mathbf P(E^\vee)_T\}.      \tag{4}
\]
Definition (4) is taken coefficientwise: pull every homogeneous equation of
\(X\) back along the universal contraction, expand in the two coordinates of
\(\lambda\), and set all coefficients to zero.  It therefore remains valid
when the contraction map drops rank and does not require a rational map to a
Grassmannian.  It is a closed functor and commutes with arbitrary base
change.

For an actual rank-two polar line, (4) is the ordinary Fano condition
together with the extra integrability constraint that its two generators are
consecutive Hankel rows.  Hence \(\operatorname{CFano}\) is not the full Fano
scheme of lines on \(X\).  That distinction is the source of the rigidity in
(1).

For iterated contractions, use C512's ordered marker space
\[
 U_\ell=(\mathbf P(E^\vee))^\ell
       \setminus\bigcup_{i<j}\{\lambda_i=\lambda_j\}.
\]
The contractions commute, but \(U_\ell\) retains the ordered factors and the
forbidden divisors \(\lambda_i\mid g\).  The contained scheme is pulled back
from (4); the marker data then remove the same collision incidences as in
C512.  Thus the Fano classification does not erase the retained forbidden
factor or turn it into an after-the-fact deletion.

## 2. Integral rank-two secant theorem

Choose divided-power coordinates
\[
 f=(a_0,\ldots,a_n).
\]
The \(3\times(n-1)\) upper Hankel matrix has columns
\[
 v_i=(a_i,a_{i+1},a_{i+2})^{\mathsf T},
 \qquad 0\le i\le n-2.
\]
Write
\(\Delta_{ijk}=\det(v_i,v_j,v_k)\).  The ideal
\[
 I_n=(\Delta_{ijk}:0\le i<j<k\le n-2)                       \tag{5}
\]
defines \(\Sigma_{2,n}\).

For the universal contraction \(s\iota_xf+t\iota_yf\), the lower Hankel
columns are
\[
 w_i=sv_i+tv_{i+1},\qquad0\le i\le n-3.                     \tag{6}
\]
Let \(J_n\) be the ideal generated by every coefficient in \(s,t\) of every
\(\det(w_i,w_j,w_k)\).  By definition,
\(\operatorname{CFano}_n(\Sigma_{2,n-1})=V(J_n)\).

Every coefficient of a determinant in (6) is an integral sum of the minors
in (5), so \(J_n\subset I_n\).  Conversely:

- the \(s^3\) coefficients give every
  \(\Delta_{ijk}\) with \(k\le n-3\);
- the \(t^3\) coefficients give every
  \(\Delta_{ijk}\) with \(i\ge1\);
- the only missing minors are
  \(\Delta_{0,j,n-2}\);
- for \(j=1\), the \(s^2t\) coefficient of
  \(\det(w_0,w_1,w_{n-3})\) is
  \(\Delta_{0,1,n-2}\) modulo minors already obtained; and
- for \(2\le j\le n-3\), the \(st^2\) coefficient of
  \(\det(w_0,w_{j-1},w_{n-3})\) is
  \(\Delta_{0,j,n-2}\) modulo minors already obtained.

All coefficients used here are \(0,\pm1\).  Therefore
\[
 J_n=I_n\qquad(n\ge5)                                      \tag{7}
\]
over \(\mathbf Z\), not merely after passing to an algebraic closure or
discarding nilpotents.  This proves (1) under arbitrary base change.

For \(n=4\), the lower Hankel matrix has only two columns and has no
\(3\times3\) minor.  Thus \(J_4=0\), while \(I_4\) is the single cubic
Hankel determinant.  This proves the sharp boundary.

Once \(f\in\Sigma_{2,n}\), contraction cannot increase catalecticant rank, so
every iterated polar remains in the corresponding lower secant scheme.
Conversely the first contained step already forces \(f\in\Sigma_{2,n}\) by
(7).  Hence no new classical component appears at a longer flag length.

Over an algebraic closure the closed points of \(\Sigma_{2,n}\) are the
secant/tangent closure of the degree-\(n\) NRC.  Over the ground field, C512's
existing arithmetic separation remains unchanged: rational secants and rank
one are shallow, while tangent and conjugate-quadratic secants give the
persistent families.  The orbit law \(T/T^n\) modulo inversion/Frobenius and
the tangent cocycle \(z\mapsto z+nu\) are arithmetic consequences after the
geometric component is supplied; they are not part of the Fano calculation.

## 3. Linear Lucas pullback theorem

Let a lower linear carrier be
\[
 L_S=\langle e_i:i\in S\rangle\subset\Gamma^dE.
\]
The two contractions of an upper coordinate vector \(e_j\) land, up to the
chosen divided-power convention, in \(e_j\) and \(e_{j-1}\).  Therefore
\[
 \operatorname{CFano}_{d+1}(\mathbf P(L_S))
 =
 \mathbf P\ker\!\left(
   \Gamma^{d+1}E\longrightarrow
   \operatorname{Hom}(E^\vee,\Gamma^dE/L_S)
 \right)
 =
 \mathbf P\langle e_j:j,j-1\in S\rangle.                   \tag{8}
\]
This proves (2) scheme-theoretically.  Applying (8) repeatedly proves (3).

For the order-\(k\) nucleus of the degree-\(d\) NRC in characteristic \(p\),
Gmainer--Havlicek's criterion gives
\[
 S_{d,k,p}=
 \left\{i:
   \binom ri\equiv0\pmod p
   \text{ for every }k<r\le d
 \right\}.                                                  \tag{9}
\]
Lucas' theorem makes (9), followed by the adjacent-run test (3), a complete
finite digit calculation.  This is the sharp modular boundary: positive
characteristic contributes a coherent linear component if and only if the
relevant support (9) contains the required consecutive run.

In particular, if \(d=p^s\), the top nucleus has
\[
 S=\{1,\ldots,d-1\},
\qquad
 \operatorname{CFano}_{d+1}(L_S)
   =\mathbf P\langle e_2,\ldots,e_{d-1}\rangle.              \tag{10}
\]
For \(s=1\), (10) is the prime-diagonal series
\[
 \mathbf P(\det^2\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^{p-3}E).
\]
For \(p=3,5,7\) it is respectively a point, a projective plane, and a
projective four-space.  For \(p=2,s=2\), it is C498's projective line; for
\(p=2,s=3\), it is C529/C531's six-dimensional vector carrier.  Formula
(10) identifies the linear scheme only.  Root-cover monodromy and deepness
on it remain arithmetic, exactly as C529 warns.

## 5. Fixed-level corollaries

The theorem separates geometry from the already proved splitting arithmetic.

\(\mathbf P\langle e_2,e_3\rangle\) by (8).  This is precisely C498's
recurring \(3\)-nucleus line.  There is no third component in the

### C509

At upper degree six, the classical part is again exactly the upper rank-two
scheme.  Pulling back C498's characteristic-two line gives the unique point
\(\mathbf P\langle e_3\rangle\), explaining the central sextic.  The
characteristic-five projective plane in the table is a geometric nucleus
candidate, not a C498 lower bad component; the established split witnesses
remove it from C509's deep carrier.  Thus C509's persistent-plus-central
contained list is recovered without promoting every nucleus to a deep locus.

### C513

At upper degree seven, (7) gives the persistent component and (9) gives
exactly the two candidate lines already computed in C513:
\[
 p=3:\mathbf P\langle e_2,e_5\rangle,\qquad
 p=5:\mathbf P\langle e_3,e_4\rangle.
\]
C513's conic and \(t^5-t\) witnesses prove both are shallow in its theorem
range.  The characteristic-two central point has no adjacent-support lift,
so its coherent flag stops exactly there.

### C516

At upper degree eight, (7) gives the persistent component.  The lower
degree-seven Lucas table gives a characteristic-five point
\(\mathbf P\langle e_4\rangle\) and the characteristic-seven prime-diagonal
carrier
\[
 \mathbf P\langle e_2,e_3,e_4,e_5,e_6\rangle
 \cong\mathbf P(\det^2\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^4E).
\]
These are exactly C516's shallow characteristic-five lift and its
binary-quartic characteristic-seven carrier.  C516's residual-quadratic
analysis supplies the arithmetic on the latter; C536 supplies only its
canonical Fano selection.

Thus four fixed-level calculations are instances of one integral secant
identity plus one Lucas adjacent-run rule.
