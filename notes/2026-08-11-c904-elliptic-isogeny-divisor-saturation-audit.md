# C904 elliptic-isogeny divisor-saturation audit

Date: 2026-08-11

Status: bounded primary-source priority/red-team audit; no manuscript or Lean
changes

## Executive verdict

There are two sharply different statements hiding in the proposed successor.

1. **Underlying abelian variety isomorphic to `E^g`: GO, and the Jordan-scalar
   hypothesis is unnecessary.**  If `End(E)=Z`, every principal polarization on
   `E^g` is represented by a positive definite unimodular symmetric matrix `G`.
   Its minimal class has curve-coordinate matrix `adj(G)=G^{-1}`.  Primitive
   elliptic graph classes are complete intersections of `g-1` divisor classes
   and span all integral symmetric curve-coordinate matrices.  Hence

   ```text
   theta_G^(g-1)/(g-1)! lies in the integral divisor-product subgroup
   ```

   for **every** principal polarization on `E^g`, including the indecomposable
   `E8` polarization.  This is an elementary theorem once the classical
   polarization-matrix dictionary is installed.

2. **Underlying abelian variety merely isogenous to `E^g`: genuinely open in
   this audit.**  Pullback to an elliptic power solves the rational problem, but
   descent across the isogeny is exactly where integral denominators occur.
   Milne and Kahn prove preservation of Lefschetz classes with rational
   coefficients.  Hasan--Hassan--Lin--Manivel--McBeath--Moonen obtain integral
   Fourier duality only after inverting an explicit factorial and the
   polarization degree.  Kahn's 2026 divided-power theorem works in etale Chow
   groups, away from the characteristic and modulo the two-primary ambiguity;
   it does not put the minimal class in the ordinary integral subring generated
   by divisors.  No primary source located here removes these denominators for
   all non-CM elliptic isogeny lattices.

Thus the strongest safe successor is presently:

> Every principal polarization on the literal power `E^g`, with `End(E)=Z`,
> has primitive minimal class in the integral divisor-product subgroup.

The all-isogenous statement is a high-value classification problem, not a free
corollary.  No counterexample inside the non-CM elliptic isogeny locus was
located, but this bounded search does not license an absence claim.

Nine individually discussed primary sources were consulted: one at full-text
depth and eight at partial depth.  The source ledger records the version,
sections, cache key, and SHA-256 for each.

## 1. Exact property

For a complex abelian variety `A` of dimension `g`, write

```text
L_Z^(g-1)(A)
  = image(Sym^(g-1) NS(A) -> H^(2g-2)(A,Z))
```

for the additive subgroup generated by ordinary products of `g-1` integral
divisor classes.  The property under audit is

```text
gamma_theta := theta^(g-1)/(g-1)! in L_Z^(g-1)(A).
```

This is stronger than all of the following:

- `gamma_theta` is an integral cohomology class;
- `gamma_theta` is algebraic;
- the integral Hodge conjecture for one-cycles holds on `A`;
- `gamma_theta` belongs to the rational Lefschetz algebra;
- `gamma_theta` is supplied by a divided-power operation in a Chow group.

The literature frequently uses "Lefschetz class" only with rational
coefficients.  That convention must not be imported into this integral claim.

## 2. The literal elliptic-power theorem

### Theorem candidate

Let `E/C` be an elliptic curve with `End(E)=Z`, let `A=E^g`, and let `theta_G`
be any principal polarization on `A`.  Then

```text
theta_G^(g-1)/(g-1)! in L_Z^(g-1)(A).
```

No Jordan-scalar, Weyl, or gluing hypothesis is needed.

### Proof

Fix the product principal polarization.  The standard Rosati dictionary gives

```text
NS(E^g) = Sym_g(Z).
```

Write `D_M` for the divisor class corresponding to a symmetric matrix `M`.
De Gaay Fortman--Schreieder, Lemma 5.2, identifies principal polarizations with
positive definite unimodular symmetric matrices `G`.

For a primitive vector `v in Z^g`, let

```text
C_v = { (v_1 x,...,v_g x) : x in E } subset E^g.
```

Extend `v` to a basis of `Z^g`.  The resulting automorphism of `E^g` carries a
coordinate elliptic axis to `C_v`.  A coordinate axis is the transverse
intersection of `g-1` coordinate-kernel divisors.  Therefore

```text
[C_v] in L_Z^(g-1)(E^g).
```

Its intersection with a divisor is

```text
C_v . D_M = v^t M v.
```

On the other hand, Riemann--Roch and differentiation of the determinant give

```text
(theta_G^(g-1)/(g-1)!) . D_M
  = d/dt det(G+tM)|_(t=0)
  = tr(adj(G) M).
```

Since `G` is unimodular, `adj(G)=G^(-1)` is an integral symmetric matrix.  The
rank-one matrices `vv^t` with primitive `v` span `Sym_g(Z)` integrally:

```text
e_i e_i^t,
(e_i+e_j)(e_i+e_j)^t-e_i e_i^t-e_j e_j^t
```

give the diagonal and off-diagonal generators.  Explicitly, if
`A_G=adj(G)=(a_ij)`, then the integral divisor-product cycle

```text
Gamma_G = sum_i a_ii [C_ei]
        + sum_(i<j) a_ij
            ([C_(ei+ej)]-[C_ei]-[C_ej])
```

has intersection `tr(A_G M)` with every `D_M`.  Poincare duality on Hodge
classes identifies the rational curve-class space with the dual of the divisor
space, so `Gamma_G=gamma_theta_G` in integral cohomology.

### Red-team checks

- The off-diagonal normalization is correct: the graph difference pairs with
  `D_M` as `2M_ij`, matching the two off-diagonal occurrences in
  `tr(adj(G)M)`.
- No claim is made in the Chow ring modulo rational equivalence; the theorem is
  a cohomological divisor-product identity.
- The argument is independent of the classification of unimodular forms.  In
  particular it includes the `E8` form, which de Gaay Fortman--Schreieder use as
  the first indecomposable principal-polarization form on a literal power.
- The proof uses `End(E)=Z` for the clean identification with symmetric integral
  matrices.  Extra CM divisors should only enlarge the available subgroup, but
  that extension is not part of this theorem as stated.

### Priority boundary

The polarization-matrix classification is classical and fully preempted.  No
source located in this bounded search states the resulting primitive
divisor-product formula for every unimodular `G`, but the proof is short enough
that the claim should be sold as a useful elementary strengthening, not as a
standalone major crown.  The novelty value lies in using it as the split endpoint
of an exact arbitrary-isogeny obstruction theory.

## 3. Why isogeny is the real gate

Let `f:E^g -> A` be an isogeny and let `theta` be a principal polarization on
`A`.  Pullback gives an integral positive symmetric matrix `G` on the source,
usually non-unimodular.  Upstairs,

```text
f^* gamma_theta = (f^*theta)^(g-1)/(g-1)!
```

again has the integral adjugate matrix `adj(G)` and is generated by elliptic
graph complete intersections.  This does **not** descend integrally for free.

The exact classical lattice input is de Gaay Fortman--Schreieder, Lemma 5.5.  If
`A_0^k -> B` is an isogeny and the pulled-back principal polarization is the
matrix `beta`, then, writing `M=H_1(B,Z)` inside `H_1(A_0,Z)^k`, the principal
symplectic form is

```text
E_M(x,y)=E_H(x,beta^(-1)y)=E_H(beta^(-1)x,y).
```

Their paper does not compute the integral image of higher divisor products on
this intermediate lattice `M`.

The available functoriality results stop at the relevant boundary:

- Milne, Corollary 5.5, proves that pushforward by a map of abelian varieties
  preserves Lefschetz classes, but his Lefschetz algebra is defined over `Q`.
- Kahn, Theorem 1, proves the analogous statement in Chow groups with rational
  coefficients.
- Hasan et al., Theorems 4.5 and 5.5, obtain integral transforms only after
  multiplying/inverting explicit factorials; their `sl_2` theorem also inverts
  the polarization degree.
- Kahn 2026 constructs divided powers in an etale Chow quotient away from the
  characteristic and up to two-primary torsion.  This is not an expression by
  ordinary integral divisor products in Betti cohomology or in the ordinary
  Chow ring.

Consequently, "pull back, solve on `E^g`, and push forward" proves only a
rational statement unless one separately computes the local cokernel of the
ordinary divisor-product map.  The Jordan-scalar theorem does precisely this
for one controlled class of local lattices.

## 4. What Beckmann--de Gaay Fortman actually implies

Their Theorem 1.1 says, for a principally polarized complex abelian variety,
that algebraicity of the minimal class is equivalent to the integral
Fourier/IHC conditions.  Their Theorem 1.2 proves IHC for products of
Jacobians, hence for a literal product of elliptic curves.  Condition 7 says
that divided powers `alpha^i/i!` of algebraic cohomology classes are algebraic.

None of these assertions says

```text
alpha^i/i! lies in the Z-subring generated by ordinary divisor products.
```

Remark 4.2(3) makes the distinction visible: arbitrary integral Hodge curve
classes are written using the integral Fourier correspondence and divided
powers of theta-type classes, not as denominator-free ordinary monomials in
`NS(A)`.

Proposition 4.6 is also only a prime-by-prime criterion for algebraicity/IHC
under suitable prime-to-`p` isogenies to products of Jacobians.  It does not
identify the integral divisor-product lattice.

Scavia--Suzuki sharpen the significance of the isogenous extension.  Over
`C`, algebraicity of the minimal class is equivalent to the underlying abelian
variety being an actual direct summand of a product of Jacobians.  Therefore an
all-isogenous elliptic-power divisor theorem would imply, in particular, that
every principally polarized member of the non-CM `E^g` isogeny locus has this
integral direct-summand property.  Mere isogeny is not what their theorem
assumes or proves.

## 5. Known counterexamples to the implication "minimal algebraic => divisor-product"

There is a clean and classical family.

Let `C` be a very general complex curve of genus `g>=3`.  Then

```text
End(J(C))=Z,
NS(J(C))=Z theta,
gamma_theta=[C]
```

where the last equality is Poincare's formula.  Hence the codimension-`g-1`
piece of the integral divisor-product subgroup is

```text
Z theta^(g-1) = Z (g-1)! gamma_theta.
```

Thus `gamma_theta` is algebraic but is not in the integral subring generated by
divisors.  This remains true even though the rational Hodge ring of a very
general Jacobian is generated by the polarization.

This example is outside the elliptic-power isogeny locus: its role is to refute
any attempt to derive divisor-product membership from BdGF algebraicity or IHC
alone.

Two nearby negatives are **not** counterexamples to the proposed elliptic
statement:

- Engel--de Gaay Fortman--Schreieder prove that on a very general ppav of
  dimension at least four the minimal curve class is not algebraic at all.
- Esnault's and Kahn's divisibility phenomena concern ordinary versus etale
  Chow groups and torsion/divided powers, not the present Betti-cohomological
  membership for a non-CM elliptic isogeny power.

No primary source or explicit example was located in which a ppav isogenous to
`E^g` with `End(E)=Z` has algebraic minimal class but that class fails to lie in
the integral divisor-product subgroup.  This is an open coverage boundary, not
evidence that no such example exists.

## 6. Exact next theorem and proof gates

The mathematically meaningful upgrade is an obstruction/classification theorem.
At each prime `p`, attach to a principally polarized integral Hodge lattice in
the rational isotypic space

```text
H_1(E,Q) tensor Q^g
```

the cokernel of

```text
Sym^(g-1) NS(A) -> Hdg^(2g-2)(A,Z).
```

Then determine exactly when the class `gamma_theta` vanishes in that cokernel.
The Jordan-scalar theorem is one positive local case and the literal product is
the split case.

An all-isogenous proof must close at least these gates:

1. classify the relevant self-dual `p`-adic symplectic lattices, not just scalar
   graph gluings;
2. compute the integral `NS` lattice for nonscalar and non-Jordan blocks;
3. prove a mixed-adjugate generation theorem in each local type, with special
   care at `p=2`;
4. show the obstruction and its vanishing are independent of the chosen source
   elliptic curve and isogeny;
5. compare the resulting positive theorem with the BdGF--Voisin direct-summand
   criterion;
6. produce an explicit negative local lattice if the statement fails, and then
   verify that it is realized by a complex ppav in the non-CM elliptic isogeny
   locus.

Until these are done, the safe split theorem should not be phrased with
"isogenous" in place of "isomorphic."

## 8. Search protocol and negative boundary

The following ranked web/arXiv query families were screened over titles,
abstracts, and snippets; promoted sources were read individually:

- `"integral Lefschetz" subring abelian varieties divisors Hodge classes`
- `"powers of an elliptic curve" "integral Hodge"`
- `"isogenous to a product of elliptic curves" "integral Hodge conjecture"`
- `"integral" pushforward "Lefschetz classes" abelian varieties`
- `minimal class product of divisors principally polarized abelian variety`
- `adjugate principal polarization elliptic curve power minimal class`
- `direct summand product elliptic curves abelian variety isogenous`

The provider returned ranked results without a stable total, so this was not
treated as an enumerated database set.  Exact-phrase searches found no source
coupling the unimodular-form classification to the primitive integral
divisor-product formula, and no all-isogenous theorem or explicit non-CM
elliptic-isogeny counterexample.

Coverage limits:

- MathSciNet: NOT COVERED; institutional authentication unavailable.
- zbMATH Open: NOT systematically covered.
- Google Scholar: NOT COVERED; automated access not used.
- OpenAlex, Crossref, and Semantic Scholar forward-citation graphs: NOT RUN.
- Integral quadratic/symplectic lattice monographs: NOT exhaustively searched.

Therefore the strongest supported negative is "no predecessor or counterexample
was located in this bounded primary-source and exact-phrase audit."

## 10. EJ + TT closeout and mystery ledger

### Cheap upgrades exposed

1. Promote the literal-power statement immediately: it covers every unimodular
   form, including `E8`, with a short human proof.
2. Rename the broader target as an **elliptic-isogeny integral Lefschetz
   obstruction theorem**.  This makes the new content and the local gates
   visible.
3. Use the very general Jacobian as the standard one-line warning that BdGF
   algebraicity is not ordinary divisor-product membership.
4. Treat every rational Lefschetz-motive citation as denominator-bearing unless
   an integral lattice statement is printed explicitly.

### Mysteries

- **Arbitrary non-CM elliptic isogeny lattice:** unresolved.  Does a genuine
  obstruction occur, or does principal self-duality force the minimal adjugate
  into the divisor-product lattice in every local type?
- **Two-primary exceptional blocks:** unresolved and highest risk.  Kahn's
  residual two-primary ambiguity and the exotic `F4` gluing both point to prime
  two as the first place to seek a counterexample or the decisive local lemma.
- **Direct-summand consequence:** unresolved.  If the all-isogenous theorem is
  true, BdGF--Voisin implies a stable integral splitting into Jacobians throughout
  the locus.  No source located here states that consequence independently.
- **Hidden lattice antecedent:** unresolved.  The literal-power proof may be a
  standard unrecorded mixed-adjugate observation; MathSciNet and lattice
  monographs remain uncovered.

## Bottom line

**GO** for the theorem on every principal polarization of the literal power
`E^g`; it is stronger than the Jordan-scalar formulation and safely proved by
elliptic graph complete intersections plus `adj(G)`.

**MAJOR / open** for replacing "isomorphic to" by "isogenous to."  Existing
literature proves rational Lefschetz functoriality, algebraicity, or divided-power
statements, not denominator-free membership in the ordinary integral divisor
subring.  The high-EV next move is a complete `p`-local lattice census aimed first
at nonscalar two-primary blocks.
