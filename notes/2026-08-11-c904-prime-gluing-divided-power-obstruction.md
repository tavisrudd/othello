# C904: prime gluings and the divided-power obstruction

Date: 2026-08-11
Status: theorem plus explicit counterexample; broader orbit formula conjectural
Scope: integral divisor-product saturation for arbitrary maximal-isotropic
gluings of the source polarization `p I_g`; no manuscript or Lean edits

## Verdict

The proposed odd-prime theorem is false.  There is an explicit graph
Lagrangian for `p=3`, `g=4` for which

\[
            \frac{\Theta^3}{3!}
\]

has exact order three modulo the integral lattice generated by triple
products of divisor classes.

The `p=3,g=3` exhaustive positivity has a simpler explanation: here
`p>(g-1)`, so `p` does not divide `(g-1)!` and a `p`-primary obstruction is
impossible.  It is not evidence that odd primes are uniformly harmless.

What is true is a clean local theorem.

> **Prime-support and factorial-threshold theorem.**  Let `A_K` be the
> principally polarized quotient obtained from the source polarization
> `p I_g` on `E^g` by any maximal isotropic subgroup
> \(K\subset E[p]^g\), with `E` non-CM.  Put `n=g-1`, and let `P_K` be the
> integral subgroup of \(H^{2n}(A_K,\mathbf Z)\) generated by products of `n` divisor
> classes.  The class
> \[
>                    c_K=\Theta_K^n/n!
> \]
> has no prime-to-`p` obstruction modulo `P_K`, and its `p`-primary order
> divides
> \[
>                         p^{v_p(n!)}.
> \]
> In particular every prime-`p` gluing is primitive when `p>g-1`, equivalently
> when `p>=g`.

At the first wall `p<=g-1<2p`, the exact obstruction is not an ordinary Arf
invariant or an ambient Steenrod square.  It is the Tor connecting class,
called here the internal divided-power Bockstein,

\[
 \beta_{p,K}=p\,c_K\pmod {pP_{K,p}}
      \in\ker\left(P_{K,p}/pP_{K,p}\longrightarrow
                         S_{K,p}/pS_{K,p}\right),
\]

where `S_K` is the saturation of `P_K` in the integral Hodge lattice and
the subscript denotes `p`-adic localization.  Then

\[
                         c_K\in P_{K,p}
              \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                         \beta_{p,K}=0.
\]

For `p=2,g=3,4`, this is precisely the one-bit obstruction governing the
reported `72/63` and `963/1332` splits.  For `p=3,g=4`, the same construction
is a divided-**cube** Tor boundary and is nonzero in the example below.  The
phenomenon is therefore factorial-primary, not dyadic.

## 1. Set-up

Let

\[
 L_0=H_1(E^g,\mathbf Z)=X\oplus Y,
 \qquad
 J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_g\\-I_g&0\end{pmatrix},
\]

and give `L_0` the alternating form `pJ`.  A Lagrangian

\[
                       K\subset L_0/pL_0
\]

defines the self-dual overlattice

\[
                  L_K=L_0+\frac1p\widetilde K.
\]

The resulting rational Hodge structure is still that of `E^g`.  Its
rational divisor space is the symmetric coefficient space

\[
 T\longmapsto
 \Omega_T=\begin{pmatrix}0&T\\-T&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad T=T^t,
\]

while the integral divisor lattice consists of those forms integral on
`L_K`.

Let

\[
 P_K^{(n)}=\operatorname{im}\left(
  \operatorname{Sym}^n\operatorname{NS}(A_K)\longrightarrow
  H^{2n}(A_K,\mathbf Z)\right),
 \qquad n=g-1,
\]

and let `S_K^(n)` be its saturation in the rational span.  Since

\[
                         \Theta_K^n=n!c_K,
\]

the class of `c_K` in `S_K/P_K` always has order dividing `n!`.  The issue is
exactly whether that formal factorial can be divided **inside** the integral
divisor-product lattice.

## 2. Proof of the prime-support theorem

Let `q` be a prime different from `p`.  The quotient isogeny

\[
                         E^g\longrightarrow A_K
\]

has `p`-power degree, hence induces isomorphisms on the `q`-adic homology and
Néron--Severi lattices.  Its inverse is obtained by dividing by a power of
`p`, which is a unit in `Z_q`.  Therefore the divisor-product question for
`A_K` over `Z_q` is the source question for `E^g`.

More explicitly, the isogeny preserves the rational degree-two Hodge
subspace, and Lefschetz \((1,1)\) identifies its integral points with the
Néron--Severi lattice.  Thus the homology isomorphism away from `p` identifies
the localized divisor lattices and, after cup product, the localized product
lattices.  Membership in the saturation is local at every prime.

On the source, write

\[
             d_i=x_i\wedge y_i,
       \qquad \Theta_{\rm src}=p\sum_{i=1}^g d_i.
\]

Then

\[
 \frac{\Theta_{\rm src}^{g-1}}{(g-1)!}
   =p^{g-1}\sum_{i=1}^g\prod_{j\ne i}d_j,
\]

an integral sum of divisor products.  Thus the obstruction has no
`q`-primary part for `q != p`.

At `p`, the identity `Theta^n=n!c_K` shows that the order divides
`p^{v_p(n!)}`.  If `p>n`, the factorial is a `p`-adic unit, so

\[
                       c_K=(n!)^{-1}\Theta^n
\]

already lies in (P_K\otimes\mathbf Z_p).  Together with the prime-to-(p) argument,
this proves the theorem.

This proof uses no enumeration and no orbit classification.

Within the same non-CM elliptic-power setting, the localization argument gives
a composite-degree corollary.  If
\(f:E^g\to A\) is any isogeny of degree \(D\) onto a principally polarized
abelian variety, then the defect of \(c_A\) is supported only at primes
dividing both \(D\) and \((g-1)!\), and its order divides

\[
             \prod_{p\mid D}p^{v_p((g-1)!)}.
\]

Indeed, the pullback of \(c_A\) to the literal power is represented by the
integral adjugate of the pulled-back polarization matrix and hence by an
integral signed sum of elliptic graph complete intersections.  Therefore an
isogeny of degree coprime to \((g-1)!\) always preserves primitive
divisor-product saturation.

## 3. The coordinate-free Tor boundary

Put

\[
 r=v_p(n!),\qquad u=n!/p^r\in\mathbf Z_p^\times.
\]

Because

\[
             p^r c_K=u^{-1}\Theta_K^n\in P_{K,p},
\]

there is a canonical class

\[
 \boxed{
 \beta^{(r)}_{p,K}:=p^r c_K\pmod {p^rP_{K,p}}
 \in\ker\left(P_{K,p}/p^rP_{K,p}\longrightarrow
                    S_{K,p}/p^rS_{K,p}\right).}
\]

It is coordinate-free: it uses only the polarized integral lattice and the
integral Lefschetz subring.  Since `S_K` is torsion-free,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \beta^{(r)}_{p,K}=0
 &\Longleftrightarrow p^r c_K=p^r z\text{ for some }z\in P_{K,p}\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow c_K=z\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow c_K\in P_{K,p}.
 \end{aligned}
\]

At the first wall `r=1`, the exact sequence obtained by tensoring
`0->P_K->S_K->S_K/P_K->0` with `F_p` identifies

\[
 \ker(P_K/pP_K\to S_K/pS_K)
       \cong (S_K/P_K)[p].
\]

This is the `Tor_1` connecting injection.  Under it, the class of `c_K` maps
exactly to `beta_{p,K}`.  The term “internal divided-power Bockstein” below
always means this Tor boundary; it is not an ordinary cohomological
Bockstein.  This is the conceptual local obstruction sought by the census.

For `p=2,g=3`, it is represented by

\[
                  \Theta^2\pmod {2P_K},
\]

which vanishes in ambient mod-two exterior cohomology because an integral
alternating square is twice its divided square.  Its possible nonvanishing
is therefore a **secondary/internal** operation: the relation vanishes after
mapping to ambient cohomology but need not be divisible by two within the
product lattice.  Ordinary `Sq^2`, computed after entering ambient mod-two
cohomology, has already forgotten this information.

For `p=2,g=4`, the representative is

\[
             \Theta^3/3\equiv\Theta^3\pmod {2P_{K,2}}.
\]

For `p=3,g=4`, it is

\[
             \Theta^3/2\pmod {3P_{K,3}},
\]

and the next section gives a nonzero example.

## 4. An explicit odd-prime counterexample

Take `p=3`, `g=4` and the graph Lagrangian

\[
 K=\{(x,Ax):x\in\mathbf F_3^4\},
 \qquad
 A=\begin{pmatrix}
 1&1&0&0\\
 1&1&2&2\\
 0&2&1&0\\
 0&2&0&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

The matrix `A` is symmetric, so `K` is Lagrangian.  In the principal basis

\[
 B=\begin{pmatrix}p^{-1}I&p^{-1}A\\0&I\end{pmatrix},
\]

the source form `pJ` becomes `J`.  A rational Hodge divisor comes from a
symmetric source matrix `S`.  Integrality of its cross block forces
`S=pT`, and its upper-left block is integral exactly when

\[
                         TA-AT\equiv0\pmod p.
\]

Thus the full integral divisor lattice is

\[
 \operatorname{NS}(A_K)=\left\{
 \Omega_T=
 \begin{pmatrix}(TA-AT)/3&T\\-T&0\end{pmatrix}:
 T=T^t,\ TA-AT\equiv0\pmod3\right\}.
\]

The commutator map from `Sym_4(F_3)` has rank five.  The certificate supplies
a rank-ten integral basis of its preimage with determinant `3^5`, so the
displayed divisor lattice is complete, not a searched sublattice.

Use the exterior basis

\[
                e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3,f_0,f_1,f_2,f_3.
\]

Define a coefficient functional

\[
 \lambda:\bigwedge^6\mathbf Z^8\longrightarrow\mathbf F_3
\]

by summing the
coefficients of

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &e_0e_1e_2f_0f_1f_2,\\
 &e_1e_2e_3f_0f_1f_2,\\
 &e_1e_2e_3f_1f_2f_3.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Direct multilinear expansion on the complete ten-element divisor basis
gives

\[
              \lambda(D_1D_2D_3)=0
       \quad\text{for all integral divisors }D_i.
\]

There are only \(\binom{10+3-1}{3}=220\) basis products; the replay checks all
of them exactly.  On the other hand, for

\[
                  c_K=\Theta^3/6
\]

the same coefficient functional gives

\[
                           \lambda(c_K)=1\in\mathbf F_3.
\]

Hence `c_K` is not in the integral divisor-product lattice.  Its class has
order dividing six, and the prime-support theorem removes the two-primary
part because the gluing degree is a power of three.  Its order is therefore
exactly three.

This disproves every formulation asserting primitive divisor-product
saturation for all odd-prime gluings.

## 5. What the bounded census now means

The existing exhaustive Sage data are consistent with, but are not the
proof of, the theorem above.

- `p=2,g=2`: `1!` has no prime factor, so primitivity is automatic.
- `p=3,g=3`: `2!` is a 3-adic unit, so all 1120 gluings are primitive by the
  theorem, without enumeration.
- `p=2,g=3`: the first Bockstein can vanish or not; the split is `72/63`.
- `p=2,g=4`: the same first Bockstein can vanish or not; the split is
  `963/1332`.
- `p=3,g=4`: the new exact example has nonzero first Bockstein and order
  three.

The elementary quadratic refinement

\[
                         q(x,y)=x\cdot y\pmod2
\]

does not classify the dyadic answer.  In the `g=3` census, the same recorded
relative-position and `q|K` invariants occur with both orders; for example
the invariant tuple `(0,0,1,0,(0,0,0))` contains six primitive and six
order-two Lagrangians.  Even the reduced NS signature has a mixed-order
case.  The obstruction remembers the integral embedding

\[
                         P_K\subset S_K,
\]

including its carry/Bockstein data, not only the mod-two Lagrangian or an
obvious Arf bit.

## 6. Theorem/conjecture boundary

### Proved here

1. Prime-to-`p` divisor-product primitivity for a prime-`p` gluing.
2. The bound `p^v_p((g-1)!)` on the remaining obstruction.
3. Universal primitivity for `p>=g`.
4. The exact coordinate-free divided-power Bockstein criterion.
5. A `p=3,g=4` graph-Lagrangian counterexample of exact order three.

### Bounded evidence only

1. The exhaustive counts at `(2,3)`, `(2,4)`, and `(3,3)`.
2. The 57 source-symmetry orbits at `(2,4)`.
3. Any pattern inferred from the numerical proportions of primitive and
   nonprimitive Lagrangians.

### Conjectural / open

1. A closed formula for `beta_{p,K}` directly in Plücker, pure-spinor, or
   Maslov coordinates on the finite Lagrangian Grassmannian.
2. Whether the dyadic first Bockstein is a named universal spinor/Maslov
   characteristic class after retaining the necessary integral lift.
3. The distribution of its zero locus for arbitrary `p,g`.
4. Higher successive divided-power obstructions when
   `v_p((g-1)!)>1`.

Van Geemen--Marrani prove that in characteristic two the principal-minor map
from the Lagrangian Grassmannian has spinor-variety image, via the exceptional
`Sp(2g)-Spin(2g+1)` isogeny.  That makes a spinorial reformulation plausible.
It does not identify the present integral Bockstein, and the graph-chart
labels are not a linear function of the principal-minor coordinates in the
bounded test.  No spinor or Arf formula is claimed here.

## 8. Primary literature boundary

1. B. van Geemen and A. Marrani, *Lagrangian Grassmannians and Spinor
   Varieties in Characteristic Two*, `arXiv:1903.01228`, especially
   Proposition 4.2 and Section 5.3.  They identify the characteristic-two
   principal-minor image with a spinor variety and explain the exceptional
   isogeny; they do not study integral divisor-product saturation.
2. T. Thomas, *The Maslov index as a quadratic space*,
   `arXiv:math/0505561`, constructs canonical quadratic spaces for tuples of
   Lagrangians.  This is adjacent language, not an identification of the
   divided-power Bockstein above.
3. Beckmann--de Gaay Fortman prove algebraicity/IHC results for products of
   Jacobians and divided powers.  As recorded in the existing priority
   audit, those theorems do not identify the primitive minimal class with
   the integral subgroup generated by ordinary divisor products.
