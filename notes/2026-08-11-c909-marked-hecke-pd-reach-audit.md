# C909 — marked Hecke-scale reach of finite-etale graph PD saturation

Date: 2026-08-11
Status: bounded structural audit; durable note only; no manuscript, PDF,
mirror, Lean, or commit change

## Verdict

There is one honest moduli-scale consequence beyond the explicit root
families.  The arbitrary-depth graph theorem applies simultaneously to the
union of all **marked finite-level Hecke presentations** of ppavs obtained
from a polarized elliptic power, provided that every positive-depth local
slope algebra is finite etale and block-respecting.  Every geometric fiber
in this marked union has

\[
 \operatorname{PD}\langle\operatorname{NS}(A)\rangle^k
 =\operatorname{im}\!\left(\operatorname{Sym}^k\operatorname{NS}(A)
       \longrightarrow H^{2k}(A,\mathbf Z)\right)
 \quad(0\leq k\leq g),
\tag{1}
\]

and hence every \(D^{[k]}=D^k/k!\) is an ordinary integral divisor product.
This is genuinely broader than the root examples: it allows arbitrary
depth profiles, unrelated finite-etale algebras at different depths, all
unramified residue data, and no distinguished root-weight form.

The result should be advertised as a theorem about a **marked Hecke
groupoid**, or about its forgetful image with that marking retained.  It is
not a new special subvariety of \(\mathcal A_g\), not a theorem for every
ppav isogenous to \(E^g\), and not an integral-Hodge, Chow, or stabilization
statement.  The top-tier geometric reach requested in the task is therefore
not present without an additional invariant or a non-etale theorem.

## 1. Precise marked locus

Fix \(g\).  Let \(\mathscr H^{\mathrm{et}}_g\) be the groupoid of tuples

\[
 (E,A,\lambda_E,\lambda_A,\phi,\mathcal M),
\]

where \(E\) is an elliptic curve, \(\phi\) is a polarized graph quotient
from a coefficient-polarized copy of \(E^g\) to the ppav \((A,\lambda_A)\),
and \(\mathcal M\) records at each bad prime the depth decomposition, the
block-respecting graph slope, and its finite-etale truncated slope algebras.
At primes not recorded in \(\mathcal M\), use the literal elliptic-power
presentation.  The local data are exactly those in
`2026-08-11-c909-arbitrary-depth-etale-graph-pd.md`; in particular, if
blocks \(i=(a,\lambda)\) and \(j=(b,\mu)\) have scalar lifts \(t_i,t_j\),

\[
 e_{ij}=\max\{a,b,a+b-v_p(t_j-t_i)\},
\tag{2}
\]

with \(v_p(0)=\infty\).  Thus

\[
 e_{ij}\geq\max\{a,b\}\geq\left\lceil\frac{a+b}{2}\right\rceil,
\tag{3}
\]

which is the only inequality needed for rank-one saturation.

This is a moduli-scale object in the modest, precise sense needed here:
for fixed discrete graph/depth data it is dominated by a finite-level Hecke
correspondence over the modular curve for \(E\), and allowing all finite
levels gives a countable union of such marked correspondences.  The
forgetful map

\[
 q:\mathscr H^{\mathrm{et}}_g\longrightarrow \mathcal A_g
\]

has image in the Hecke saturation of the product-elliptic locus.  No claim
is made that the image is closed, irreducible, dense, or special.

## 2. Human proof of the fiber theorem

For a fixed tuple, work prime by prime.  At a bad prime, after finite
unramified faithfully flat splitting at each positive depth, the graph
coefficient lattice is the symmetric matrix-of-ideals lattice

\[
 \bigoplus_i p^{a_i}\operatorname{Sym}(L_i^*)
 \ \oplus\!\!\bigoplus_{i<j}p^{e_{ij}}
       (L_i^*\otimes L_j^*)_{\mathrm{sym}}.
\tag{4}
\]

Formula (2) follows by multiplying the graph basis and intersecting the
middle integrality condition \(p^{-a_i}A_{ij}\in M(O)\) with the commutator
condition \(p^{-a_i-a_j}(t_j-t_i)A_{ij}\in M(O)\).  The \(S_i\)-error terms
are integral because of the first condition.  Inequality (3) gives the
DVR rank-one criterion, including \(p=2\).  The polarization coefficient
lattice is therefore generated by rank-one forms.

Each rank-one form pulls back to a decomposable alternating class on
\(E^g\), hence has square zero.  For an integral divisor
\(D=\sum_rR_r\),

\[
 D^{[k]}=\sum_{|I|=k}\prod_{r\in I}R_r,
\tag{5}
\]

so the divided power is an ordinary product over the unramified splitting
ring.  Product-image formation commutes with finite free unramified base
change, and faithful flatness detects membership, so (5) descends to
\(\mathbf Z_p\).  At good primes the literal elliptic-power calculation
applies.  Intersecting the local statements gives (1) over \(\mathbf Z\).

The proof uses no root system, no common etale algebra across depths, and no
Chow cycle.  It is a cohomological lattice proof on the marked graph
presentation.

## 3. What is stable under Hecke moves and deformation

There is a useful but deliberately conditional functoriality statement.

* Finite etale base change of the marking preserves (1).  A transverse
  ruling change with
  \(T'=(aT+b)(cT+d)^{-1}\), where the denominator is invertible, preserves
  the generated local algebra and finite-etaleness; if it also preserves
  the block chart, the same rank-one proof applies.

* A Hecke isogeny of degree prime to the bad-prime set identifies the
  relevant integral \(p\)-adic cohomology and divisor lattices for every bad
  \(p\): on cohomology this is because \(f^*\) has inverse
  \(d^{-1}\widehat f^{\,*}\) when \(d=\deg(f)\in\mathbf Z_p^\times\), and
  the same identity holds on the Neron--Severi lattice.  Thus it transports
  (1) at those primes.  At a prime dividing the new Hecke degree, one must
  supply a new marked graph presentation with the same finite-etale
  hypothesis.  Arbitrary same-prime Hecke composition is not claimed to
  preserve the graph chart or etaleness.

* In a family over a connected base, once the graph, finite-level marking,
  and divisor classes spread after a finite etale level cover, (2)--(5) are
  identities in the integral cohomology local system.  The fiberwise
  divided-power assertion therefore persists.  At a Noether--Lefschetz
  fiber, extra divisor classes only enlarge the target product image.  This
  is a relative cohomological persistence statement, not a relative Chow
  cycle or a Franchetta theorem.

These are closure properties of the marked theorem, not evidence that the
condition descends to the unmarked ppav.  The finite-etale slope algebra is
local graph data and need not define a global Rosati-stable order or a PEL
tensor.  Consequently there is no justified Shimura/special-subvariety
claim for \(q(\mathscr H^{\mathrm{et}}_g)\).

## 4. Second consequences that really follow

The strongest intrinsic cohomological corollaries on every fiber are

\[
 \Theta^{[k]}\in \operatorname{im}(\operatorname{Sym}^k\operatorname{NS}(A))
 \quad(0\leq k\leq g),
 \qquad \Theta^{[g]}=[\mathrm{pt}],
\tag{6}
\]

and, for every line bundle \(L\),

\[
 \operatorname{ch}_k(L)=c_1(L)^k/k!
\]

lies in the same ordinary divisor-product lattice.  Hence the gcd of
degrees of zero-dimensional divisor intersections is one **in cohomology**.
The polarization span \(\bigoplus_k\mathbf Z\Theta^{[k]}\) is stable under
the standard cohomological Fourier and Lefschetz operators, with the usual
convention-dependent signs.  No claim for the whole divisor-product
algebra follows: mixed Fourier images can involve the dual polarization
and inverse isogeny denominators.

In particular, (1) does not imply the integral Hodge conjecture for
one-cycles, equality with the full integral Hodge lattice, effectivity of a
degree-one zero-cycle, a Jacobian obstruction, or a decomposition of the
diagonal.

## 5. Source-backed scope and novelty discipline

The primary-literature audit in
`2026-08-11-c909-etale-saturation-priority-audit.md` supports the following
boundaries:

* Dolgachev--Lehavi support maximal-isotropic principal quotient descent;
  Lange and de Gaay Fortman--Schreieder support the Rosati/symmetric-form
  and polarized-decomposition dictionaries.
* Bayer--Fluckiger--Parimala--Serre support the standard trace-transfer
  input; the present finite-etale matrix-of-ideals calculation remains the
  load-bearing argument.
* Moonen--Polishchuk and Beckmann--de Gaay Fortman distinguish divided-power
  and integral-Fourier issues from the smaller ordinary divisor-product
  lattice; Milne's rational Lefschetz theorem cannot supply the integral
  saturation.

No exact predecessor for the finite-etale graph-to-ordinary-product theorem
was found in that bounded source set.  This is a bounded search finding, not
a novelty theorem.  The marked Hecke-scale formulation is consequently a
safe synthesis of the audited theorem, not a claim that the image in
\(\mathcal A_g\) is new or geometrically special.

## Final grade

**GO** for recording the marked Hecke-scale theorem and the all-codimension
cohomological consequences.  **MAJOR LIMITATION** for the requested
top-tier unmarked geometric reach: no second moduli geometry, integral-Hodge
one-cycle theorem, or full Fourier closure is currently proved.  A stronger
result would require either an intrinsic presentation-free criterion, a
global PEL/Rosati action, or a non-etale defect theorem; those are outside
this C909 pass and are not silently inferred here.
