# C904: integral Fourier duality does not saturate the p15 numerical lattice

Date: 2026-08-11

Status: theorem-grade reduction to one finite integral saturation quotient;
Paper V research only; no manuscript or Lean change

## Verdict

An algebraic minimal class and the resulting integral Fourier/Poincare
duality do **not formally imply** that every integral numerical
codimension-three class on

\[
                         B=J\times J
\]

belongs to the divided-power envelope of \(\operatorname {NS}(B)\).
They prove closure under Fourier transform and divided-power operations;
they do not prove generation or integral saturation.

For the generic non-CM \(A_5\) fivefold the boundary is especially sharp.
Let

\[
 \mathcal D^3_{\mathrm {PD}}(B)
 \subset N^3(B)
\]

be the numerical lattice generated by all degree-three divided-power
monomials in the full rank-55 Neron--Severi lattice.  Then

\[
 \mathcal D^3_{\mathrm {PD}}(B)\otimes\mathbf Q
       =N^3(B)\otimes\mathbf Q,
\]

but the integral quotient

\[
                 \mathcal Q^3_{\mathrm {alg}}(B)
       :=N^3(B)/\mathcal D^3_{\mathrm {PD}}(B)             \tag{0.1}
\]

is only known to be finite.  Neither Beckmann--de Gaay Fortman nor
integral Fourier duality proves that (0.1) vanishes.

The existing full-NS certificate computes the p15 image of the denominator:

\[
 \rho_{15}(\mathcal D^3_{\mathrm {PD}}(B))
                 =2\operatorname {End}(J).                \tag{0.2}
\]

Therefore the sole missing algebraization datum is the induced map

\[
 \bar\rho_{15}:\mathcal Q^3_{\mathrm {alg}}(B)
       \longrightarrow
       \operatorname {End}(J)/2\operatorname {End}(J).    \tag{0.3}
\]

The full p15 even no-go follows if \(\bar\rho_{15}=0\).  An odd p15
algebraization follows if its image contains the normalized identity.  The
current theorems decide neither statement.

There is a concrete cohomological counterboundary: the integral Hodge
lattice contains the normalized p15 identity, while (0.2) proves that it is
not in the divided-power divisor lattice.  What remains open is exactly
whether that Hodge coset is algebraic.  Thus the proposed implication is
false for integral Hodge classes and unproved for actual integral numerical
classes of algebraic cycles.

## 1. Three lattices that must not be conflated

Write

\[
\begin{split}
 \mathcal D^3_{\mathrm {PD}}(B)
   &=\text{divided-power divisor classes},\\
 N^3(B)
   &=Z^3(B)/\!\sim_{\mathrm {num}},\\
 \operatorname {Hdg}^6(B,\mathbf Z)
   &=H^6(B,\mathbf Z)\cap H^{3,3}(B).
\end{split}                                                \tag{1.1}
\]

Numerical cycle groups are torsion-free.  On a complex abelian variety,
numerical and homological equivalence agree with rational coefficients.
Since integral Betti cohomology is torsion-free, the cycle class realizes
\(N^3(B)\) as a lattice in the integral Hodge group.  Hence

\[
 \mathcal D^3_{\mathrm {PD}}(B)
   \subset N^3(B)
   \subset \operatorname {Hdg}^6(B,\mathbf Z).             \tag{1.2}
\]

The first inclusion may have finite index even when all three groups have
the same rational span.  That possible finite index is the entire problem.

Chow torsion and homologically trivial cycles do not alter (1.2): they are
numerically zero and act trivially on integral cohomology.  They may matter
for later Chow descent, but cannot repair or obstruct the numerical p15
coefficient.

## 2. Why rational generation is complete

The generic non-CM coefficient description is

\[
 H^1(B,\mathbf Q)=U\otimes V,
 \qquad \dim U=10,quad \dim V=2,
\]

where the Hodge group is \(\operatorname {SL}(V)\).  The first fundamental
theorem for \(\operatorname {SL}_2\) says that invariant tensors are
generated by contractions with \(\bigwedge^2V\).  Those contractions are
exactly divisor classes on powers of the abelian variety.  In degree six,

\[
 \left(\bigwedge^6(U\otimes V)\right)^{\operatorname {SL}(V)}
       \cong \mathbf S_{(2,2,2)}U,                         \tag{2.1}
\]

of dimension

\[
                         2475.
\]

Equivalently, the cup-product map

\[
 \operatorname {Sym}^3
   \left(\operatorname {Sym}^2U\right)
 \longrightarrow \mathbf S_{(2,2,2)}U                    \tag{2.2}
\]

is surjective over \(\mathbf Q\).  Here
\(\operatorname {Sym}^2U\) is the rank-55 rational divisor space.
Therefore every rational Hodge class in codimension three is a rational
divisor polynomial and is algebraic.  This proves

\[
 \mathcal D^3_{\mathrm {PD}}(B)_{\mathbf Q}
 =N^3(B)_{\mathbf Q}
 =\operatorname {Hdg}^6(B,\mathbf Q).                    \tag{2.3}
\]

Milne's Lefschetz-class theorem is deliberately a theorem over a
characteristic-zero coefficient **field**.  Equation (2.3) is exactly its
scope here.  It says nothing about the index of one integral lattice in
another.

## 3. What the minimal class and integral Fourier transform really give

The algebraic class \(\Theta^4/4!\) on \(J\) implies the corresponding
minimal class on \(B=J^2\) is algebraic, since for the product
polarization \(x+y\),

\[
 \frac{(x+y)^9}{9!}
 =\frac{x^4}{4!}\frac{y^5}{5!}
  +\frac{x^5}{5!}\frac{y^4}{4!}.                         \tag{3.1}
\]

Beckmann--de Gaay Fortman Theorem 3.8 consequently gives:

1. a weak integral Fourier transform on \(CH(B)/\mathrm {tors}\);
2. integral algebraic lifts of the components of
   \(\operatorname {ch}(\mathcal P_B)\); and
3. an intersection divided-power structure on the full positive Chow ideal
   modulo torsion.

In particular every divided-power monomial in Neron--Severi classes has an
integral numerical realization.  This constructs
\(\mathcal D^3_{\mathrm {PD}}(B)\).

None of the three conclusions says that an arbitrary codimension-three
cycle is a polynomial in divisors.  A PD structure assigns divided powers
to every element of an ideal; it is not a presentation of that ideal by its
degree-one part.  Likewise, an integral Fourier transform is an integral
automorphism of cycle groups, not a theorem that every cycle lies in the
subring generated by divisor kernels.

The distinction survives after passing to numerical equivalence.  One may
have a primitive numerical class \(z\) in degree three and its Fourier mate
in degree seven without violating any Fourier or PD identity.  Proving that
no such primitive class exists is an additional integral invariant-theory
or motive-generation theorem.

## 4. Why Fourier/Poincare duality does not remove the factor two

The cohomological Fourier transform exchanges cup product with the
Pontryagin product and conjugates the Lefschetz raising and lowering
operators.  It does not give the **normalized** inverse to hard Lefschetz
integrally.  On a symplectic lattice, contraction is integral, but

\[
             \Lambda L|_{H^k}=(g-k)\operatorname {Id}
\]

on primitive degree \(k\).  The scalar needed to turn contraction into the
inverse Lefschetz operator can be even.  These are precisely the elementary
divisors detected in the p15 lattice.

Thus composing an integral Fourier kernel with theta multiplication yields
an integral multiple of the desired projector, not automatically its
primitive normalization.  The existing exact calculation identifies that
multiple on the complete divisor-PD subring as two:

\[
                 \rho_{15}(\mathcal D^3_{\mathrm {PD}})
                   =2\operatorname {End}(J).               \tag{4.1}
\]

The equality is stronger than a failed search: all 55 divisor directions,
all 25 cross-endomorphism directions, and every degree-three PD
polarization are included.  Fourier functoriality preserves the rational
Lefschetz subspace and the full numerical lattice.  It does not by itself
prove that the integral intersection-PD divisor sublattice is Fourier
stable; failure of such stability could itself produce a class in (0.1).
In either case it supplies no saturation theorem.

## 5. Beauville grading does not close the integral gap

Over \(\mathbf Q\), the Beauville decomposition separates the eigenspaces
of multiplication maps.  Any positive Beauville-grade class has trivial
Betti class and is numerically zero.  Hence the numerical ring is rationally
concentrated in grade zero, and the divisor-PD envelope is also grade zero.

This does not prove integral equality.  The Beauville and
Deninger--Murre projectors use rational denominators; an ordinary integral
grade-zero projector is exactly part of the unresolved two-primary problem.
An integral class can lie in the saturation of the rational grade-zero
divisor space without belonging to the integral divisor lattice.

Kahn's integral projector results make the boundary explicit: the mixed
theta--Poincare formulas exist canonically in etale Chow modulo two-torsion,
while his Question B.8 asks whether the corresponding projectors already
exist integrally in ordinary Chow even for Jacobians.  Therefore Beauville
grading or integral etale projectors cannot be cited as an ordinary-Chow
saturation theorem here.

## 6. The exact p15 gate

The normalized p15 coefficient defines, on integral algebraic numerical
classes, a homomorphism

\[
              \rho_{15}:N^3(B)\longrightarrow
                         \operatorname {End}(J).           \tag{6.1}
\]

By (4.1) it induces (0.3).  There are now two exact alternatives.

### Negative closure

Prove

\[
                         \bar\rho_{15}=0.                  \tag{6.2}
\]

Then every integral algebraic p15 coefficient is even.  Together with the
existing full-NS certificate, this is the desired complete p15 no-go.
The stronger equality
\(N^3(B)=\mathcal D^3_{\mathrm {PD}}(B)\) would imply (6.2), but is more
than necessary.

### Positive closure

Construct

\[
 \Gamma\in CH^3(B)
 \quad\text{with}\quad
 \rho_{15}([\Gamma])\equiv\operatorname {Id}\pmod2.       \tag{6.3}
\]

Modulo \(\mathcal D^3_{\mathrm {PD}}\), this is the missing nonzero coset in
(0.1).  Cohomologically it is an integral algebraic half of the rational
inverse-Lefschetz correspondence.  The full integral Hodge lattice already
contains such a class; (6.3) is exactly its algebraization.

After (6.3), swap transfer and descent to
\(\operatorname {Sym}^2M\) still require the existing quotient
normalization audit, but no further p15 numerical mystery remains.

Thus the direct crown is not a general integral Fourier theorem.  It is the
specific two-local saturation problem (0.3).

## 7. Counterboundaries

- **Integral Hodge version: false.**  The normalized p15 identity is an
  integral Hodge class outside the divisor-PD lattice by (0.2).
- **Integral numerical/algebraic version: open.**  Algebraicity of that
  coset is precisely the live p15 gate.
- **Rational numerical version: true.**  It follows from
  \(\operatorname {SL}_2\) invariant theory and divisor algebraicity.
- **Chow version: much stronger and unsupported.**  Homologically trivial
  and positive Beauville-grade cycles prevent any reduction of the full
  Chow group to numerical divisor generation.

These four statements should not be compressed into “integral Fourier
duality generates all cycles.”

## Mystery ledger

- **Settled:** minimal class plus integral Fourier/PD constructs and
  stabilizes the full divided-power divisor lattice; it does not prove that
  lattice saturated.
- **Settled:** rational codimension-three Hodge/numerical classes are all
  divisor-generated; the problem is purely integral and finite.
- **Settled:** Chow torsion, homologically trivial cycles and positive
  Beauville grades are invisible to the numerical p15 coefficient.
- **Settled:** the normalized integral Hodge identity is a concrete class
  outside the divisor-PD lattice, so integral Hodge generation is false.
- **Open, exact:** compute (0.3).  Vanishing proves the full p15 even no-go;
  the identity in its image gives the missing algebraic inverse-Lefschetz
  half.
- **Open, stronger than needed:** prove or disprove
  \(N^3(B)=\mathcal D^3_{\mathrm {PD}}(B)\).
