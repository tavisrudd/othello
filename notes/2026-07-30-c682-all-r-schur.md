# C682 all-\(r\) exceptional signed Schur transversality

Date: 2026-07-30

## Theorem

For every integer \(r\ge6\), all four exceptional modulo-\(20\) monotone
entrances
\[
  4_6,\qquad 4_{s,3},\qquad 5_4,\qquad 6_5
\]
are endpoint-transverse.  Equivalently, if
\[
 A=D_{\rho,n-6},\qquad B=D_{\rho,n},\qquad
 T=B^\dagger B,\qquad n=a+20r,
\]
then the incoming hyperplane \(L=\operatorname{im}A\) is not
\(T\)-invariant.  Hence there is \(x\) at the lower endpoint with
\[
  T A x\notin L.
\]
This closes all twelve exceptional modulo-\(60\) entrance phases.

The result is actually valid on the full real parameter ray \(r\ge6\) for
the rational boundary obstruction below.

## Scalar-chain reduction

The growing block Schur complements are not needed for the proof.  On a
fixed \(C_5\)-coefficient chain, the third transvectant is scalar
tridiagonal.  A source coefficient at index \(j\) meets target indices
\[
 j-2,\qquad j+3,\qquad j+8
\]
with the exact coefficients
\[
\begin{aligned}
d_1(n,j)&=330(j)_2(n-4j+6),\\
d_6(n,j)&=660\bigl(
2(n-j)_3-9(n-j)_2j+9(n-j)(j)_2-2(j)_3
\bigr),\\
d_{11}(n,j)&=-330(n-j)_2(3n-4j-6).
\end{aligned}
\]

Use source-chain residues \(4,2,2,0\) for
\(4,4_s,5,6\), respectively.  Their current \(C_5\)-weights are
\(2,-2,-1,-1\).  The exact three-phase Kostant audit shows that each
selected chain has exactly one new weight line and that it belongs to the
named module.  Translation by degree \(60\) preserves this audit for all
\(r\ge6\).  In particular, the full coefficient-chain incoming image is a
hyperplane, and its cokernel line is the desired module line.

This also resolves the apparent \(6\)-module complication.  Its weight-zero
space has multiplicity two, but the residue-zero chain above is a simple
weight-\(-1\) chain.

## Boundary obstruction

Let \(\lambda\) span the left kernel of the scalar incoming matrix.  For
\(4,4_s,5\), its last two coordinates are fixed by the two-term right
boundary column; for \(6\), its first two coordinates are fixed by the
two-term left boundary column.  The scalar chain recurrence determines the
next boundary coordinates.

The incoming hyperplane is \(T\)-invariant exactly when
\(\lambda^{\mathsf T}T\) is proportional to \(\lambda^{\mathsf T}\).
Taking the alternating \(2\times2\) boundary minor gives an obstruction
\(\Omega_\rho(r)\).  Exact elimination gives
\[
 \Omega_\rho(r)=\frac{N_\rho(r)}{D_\rho(r)},
\]
where
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\rho&\deg N_\rho& D_\rho(r)\\ \hline
4&17&r-\frac56\\
4_s&18&(r-\frac{11}{12})(r-\frac7{12})\\
5&17&r-\frac{14}{15}\\
6&18&(r-\frac{49}{60})(r-\frac{29}{60}).
\end{array}
\]
After writing \(r=6+s\), every coefficient of every \(N_\rho(6+s)\)
is strictly negative.  Every displayed denominator is positive for
\(s\ge0\).  Therefore
\[
 \boxed{\ \Omega_\rho(r)<0\quad\text{for every real }r\ge6.\ }
\]
In particular, no incoming hyperplane is \(T\)-invariant.

This is an identity proof, not interpolation from an unbounded pattern.
The local boundary expression has formal numerator and denominator degree
bounds \(36\) and \(18\).  The fitted formulas have degrees at most
\(18\) and \(2\), so their cleared differences have degree at most \(38\).
The certificate verifies each identity at the \(105\) exact points
\(6\le r\le110\), more than the degree bound requires.

## Canonical positive endpoint

The transversality can be given a basis-free positive witness.  Choose a
Fischer-unit vector \(y\) spanning \(L^\perp\) and put
\[
 x=A^\dagger T y.
\]
The boundary obstruction says \(x\ne0\).  Then
\[
 \langle y,T A x\rangle
 =\langle A^\dagger T y,x\rangle
 =\langle x,x\rangle>0.
\]
Thus the endpoint can be chosen canonically from the boundary line.  This
proves the invariant transversality needed for propagation; it does not
assert a new closed formula for each arbitrary coordinate endpoint used in
the earlier finite audit.
