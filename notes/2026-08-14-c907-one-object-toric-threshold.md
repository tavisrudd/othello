# C907: a one-object marked comparison at a toric discrepant wall

**Date:** 2026-08-14

## Result

Iritani's weak-Fano toric blowup theorem gives an unconditional geometric
model of the marked one-object mechanism required by the ordinary-threshold
part of `hyp:marked-threshold`.

Let
\[
  \phi:X_+\longrightarrow X_-
\]
be an ordinary toric blowup between smooth projective weak-Fano toric
varieties satisfying Iritani's Section 7.1 lattice hypothesis.  Let
\(Z\subset X_-\) be the center, choose a closed point
\(p\in X_-\setminus Z\), and let \(\widetilde p\in X_+\setminus E\) be its
lift.  On the common sector and along the paths in Iritani's
Theorems 7.25 and 7.33, the Gamma-framed point row has the exact form
\[
  \ell_{X_+,\widetilde p}
  = (0,\ldots,0,\ell_{X_-,p})
\]
under the analytic residual-plus-ambient decomposition of the quantum
connection.  Consequently the finite dual cyclic Rees module generated by
the point row is the pullback of the base cyclic module.  The comparison is
induced by one Gauss--Manin object and its specified continuation paths; it
is not an isomorphism chosen after the two boundary modules are known.

The smallest example is the discrepancy-one blowup
\[
  \operatorname{Bl}_{p}\mathbf P^2\longrightarrow\mathbf P^2.
\]
Both varieties are smooth projective weak-Fano toric surfaces, and the
center can be a torus-fixed point.  Thus the one-object mechanism has a
genuine discrepant geometric model.

This result verifies the intrinsic Gamma/QDM form of the
sign/stability-threshold mechanism.  A literal instance of the manuscript's
tail hypothesis also requires identifying its Woodward clutching-tail modules
with these toric mirror boundary modules.  That comparison is not proved
here.  The result also does not prove the zero-mode nearby-cycle clause or
show that an arbitrary Wlodarczyk completion is gauged-admissible.

There is a further logical boundary: the discrepancy-one blowup direction is
nonneutral.  The manuscript handles nonneutral homogeneous coefficients by
Laurent finiteness, so this example calibrates the one-object and marked-row
mechanism but does not test the load-bearing neutral threshold hypothesis.
Ordinary flops provide the proved path-local neutral model.  The next direct
test of the remaining hypothesis must be a neutral threshold not already
covered by that theorem, or a zero-mode threshold.

Coates--Iritani--Jiang supply exactly such a neutral calibration in the
linear toric category.  Their Theorems 6.1 and 6.3 construct the two chamber
quantum connections and a continuation gauge between them that intertwines the
quantum and grading connections, preserves the Poincare pairing, and carries
Gamma-framed flat sections by Fourier--Mukai.  Gluing along the gauge gives a
local one-object calibration.  A
skyscraper at a common-open point is unchanged by the graph Fourier--Mukai
transform after non-equivariant specialization.  Pairing preservation then
carries its Gamma point row exactly.
Together with Woodward's identification of the linear toric localized graph
potential with the toric I-function, this is a genuine neutral calibration of
the ordinary intrinsic marked-continuation mechanism at the QDM/I-function
level in the common toric Fourier coordinates.  It does not test
the zero-mode reduced-nearby-cycle clause or identify the thresholds of an
arbitrary projective Wlodarczyk master.

There is a closer linear-gauged comparison.  Woodward's Example 9.15,
equations (63)--(65), identifies the localized gauged graph potential of a
torus representation with Givental's toric \(I\)-function.  Iritani's global
mirror starts from the same type of quotient \([\mathbf C^S/\!/\mathbb L]\):
one Brieskorn module has the chamber \(I\)-functions as its large-radius
expansions, and Theorems 7.25 and 7.33 identify its ambient Gamma lattice and
sectorial continuation.  Thus the analytic one-object and marked-row clauses
have a genuine realization in the **linear toric gauged model**.  This is not
yet the whole ordinary-threshold hypothesis.  Definition 8.1 of the
manuscript uses a smooth projective \(\mathbf G_m\)-completion, and no cited
quantum reduction-in-stages theorem identifies its Woodward clutching tails
with the linear-presentation tails in the same equivariant input-and-derivative
frame.  This is now the exact gap between the positive model and a literal
example of the manuscript hypothesis.

The smallest blowup presentation makes the marked input completely explicit.
Woodward's Example 5.23 presents \(\mathbf P^2\) and
\(\operatorname{Bl}_p\mathbf P^2\) as the two quotients of \(\mathbf C^4\)
by \((\mathbf C^\times)^2\) with weights
\[
 (1,0),(1,0),(1,1),(0,1).
\]
Write \(x,y\in H^2_{(\mathbf C^\times)^2}(\mathbf C^4)\) for the two
character classes.  In the \(\mathbf P^2\) chamber the Kirwan map sends
\(x\mapsto H\) and \(y\mapsto0\).  In the blowup chamber it sends
\(x\mapsto H-E\), \(y\mapsto E\), and \(x+y\mapsto H\).  Therefore the
single ordinary equivariant class
\[
 a_p=x(x+y)
\]
maps to the point class on both sides.  At the intervening wall the fixed
quotient is a point, so this positive-degree class restricts to zero.  Thus
the affine model passes the marked-input test in ordinary equivariant
cohomology; compact support is not needed for this example.

This calculation does **not** make the affine model a literal instance of
the manuscript's admissible completion.  The affine presentation has a
rank-two gauge torus, whereas the cobordism in Definition 8.1 has one
residual \(\mathbf C^\times\).  Quotienting one torus factor first gives a
smooth quasiprojective rank-one master, but that master contains projective
curve classes.  Woodward's vector-space computation no longer identifies
its clutching tails without a quantum reduction-in-stages theorem.  Hence
dropping the word ``projective'' from Definition 8.1 would not close the
remaining comparison; the obstruction is the rank-one common-frame descent,
not properness of the two endpoint quotients or existence of the point lift.

## Proof

Iritani's Theorem 1.3, assembled from Theorems 7.25, 7.31, and 7.33, gives
an angular sector \(I\) of opening greater than \(\pi\), an open set \(W\),
a holomorphic submersion \(f\), and an analytic decomposition
\[
  \operatorname{QDM}^{\mathrm{an}}(X_+)|_{W\times I}
  \cong
  \mathcal R_{-h}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal R_{-1}
  \oplus f^*\operatorname{QDM}^{\mathrm{an}}(X_-)|_{W\times I}
  \oplus\mathcal R_0\oplus\cdots.
\]
The decomposition is preserved by the Gauss--Manin connection.  Via the
Gamma integral structure, its K-theoretic decomposition is
\[
  K(X_+)=
  K(Z)_{-h}\oplus\cdots\oplus K(Z)_{-1}
  \oplus\phi^*K(X_-)
  \oplus K(Z)_0\oplus\cdots,
\]
and Theorem 7.25 identifies the ambient analytic inclusion with
\(\phi^*\) in this fixed Gamma frame.

Use Iritani's flat pairing convention
\(\bigl[s(A),s(B)\bigr)=\chi(A,B)\).  Since \(p\) lies outside the center,
\[
  \phi^*[\mathcal O_p]=[\mathcal O_{\widetilde p}].
\]
For every \(V\in K(X_-)\),
\[
  \chi(\phi^*V,\mathcal O_{\widetilde p})
  =\chi(V,\mathcal O_p)
  =\operatorname{rk}(V).
\]
Every class in a residual group \(K(Z)_k\) is supported on the exceptional
divisor, which is disjoint from \(\widetilde p\).  Hence
\[
  \chi(R,\mathcal O_{\widetilde p})=0
  \qquad (R\in K(Z)_k).
\]
These two identities give the displayed point-row formula.

The ambient summand is a flat subconnection.  Its dual is therefore stable
under formal monodromy, the Stokes filtration on the chosen sector, and the
relevant continuation action.  Since the point row annihilates every
residual summand, its complete monodromy/deck orbit and its strict cyclic
Rees saturation stay in the ambient dual summand.  Theorem 7.25 identifies
that summand and its marked row with the pullback of the corresponding
object for \(X_-\).

Finally, this comparison comes from the single global Landau--Ginzburg
Gauss--Manin system used to construct Iritani's analytic lift.  Restricting
the analytic connection to finite Artin jets commutes with continuation,
input differentiation, and the Rees \(z\)-connection.  A contractible tube
around the specified continuation path supplies one simply connected domain
for the intrinsic comparison.  This proves the claim.

## What this changes

The one-object and marked-row clauses are geometrically realizable: they hold
for a nontrivial discrepant birational transformation, and in fact in the
whole weak-Fano toric blowup class above.  The example also
shows why the common-open skyscraper is the right marking.  Its Gamma row is
intrinsic, survives on the ambient summand, and annihilates the wall-supported
summands before any primary projector is applied.

The remaining universal problem is narrower but still real:

1. extend this one-object Gamma/window construction from the toric blowup
   class to the locally finite thresholds of a general gauged-admissible
   Wlodarczyk cobordism;
2. treat zero-mode thresholds by a canonical nearby-cycle object with no
   loss of the full row-generated cyclic module; and
3. prove that every required birational map admits a gauged-admissible
   completion.

The minimal type-\((1,2)\) toric flip of Gu--Yu--Yu is the next sharper
test.  Their rank-two representation has weights
\[
  (1,0)^2,\qquad (0,-1)^3,\qquad (1,-1),
\]
whose wall-normal sum is nonzero.  Therefore Aleshkin--Liu's Calabi--Yau
wall theorem does not apply to the bare discrepant flip.  Adding the
anticanonical spectator weight \((-3,4)\) makes the total charge zero:
\[
  2(1,0)+3(0,-1)+(1,-1)=(3,-4).
\]
For the wall normal \(h=(1,1)\), the original restricted weights are
\(1,1,-1,-1,-1,0\), and the spectator contributes the missing \(+1\).
The naive completion nevertheless fails before the window check.  Write
\(C=(1,-1)\) for the weight of the old \(O\)-coordinate and
\(P=(-3,4)\) for the spectator.  Then
\[
  \chi_+=(1,0)=P+4C.
\]
Thus the locus on which only the old \(O\)-coordinate and the spectator are
nonzero is semistable for the completed positive phase, although the
corresponding old locus was unstable.  More generally, if several added
coordinate weights have total \((-3,4)\), turning all of them on gives the
same monomial relation.  No zero-superpotential coordinate completion with
the required total charge preserves the original positive chamber.

Consequently Aleshkin--Liu cannot be imported by merely adjoining an
anticanonical coordinate.  A viable completion would need a superpotential
or critical-locus condition that removes the new semistable stratum, followed
by a descent to the compact quotient; the alternative is a direct
non-Calabi--Yau Barnes/window theorem.  The balanced completion must not be
presented as a proof for the original flip.

The point-row theorem above is a direct consequence of Iritani's analytic
Gamma/K-theoretic decomposition plus the support calculation.  Identifying
the resulting toric mirror module with the manuscript's Woodward clutching
tail on a projective master is not part of Iritani's or Woodward's theorem
and is not asserted here.  The final paragraph's Calabi--Yau completion is a
proposed next route, not a proved descent theorem.

For the type-\((1,2)\) flip, the next proof attempt is therefore finite:

1. construct a superpotential or derived critical-locus completion whose
   geometric phases recover the two compact quotients and exclude the new
   spectator-stabilized stratum;
2. construct one grade-restricted window representative of the
   common-open skyscraper and check Aleshkin--Liu's strict window inequality;
3. show that the resulting hemisphere object generates the required marked
   cyclic connection, rather than only one scalar central charge; and
4. descend it through equivariant quantum Serre/Riemann--Roch while proving
   that the point row and its Stokes filtration are preserved.

The calculation above kills the naive version of item 1.  Failure of item 2
would kill any repaired completion immediately.  Failure of item 3
would show that the scalar one-object theorem is still too weak for
`hyp:marked-threshold`.  Item 4 is the only step that returns from the
crepant completion to the original discrepant flip.
