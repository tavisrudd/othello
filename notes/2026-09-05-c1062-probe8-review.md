## 2. Findings

### 2.1 The completeness claim is false against the probe's own oracle — defect

§ 5 is where probe 8 says what compilation buys, and it is the report's payload:

> The direct route enumerates edit states; when the edit monoid is the free monoid on the cursor
> alphabet, enumerating edit states means enumerating words, and the enumeration is complete only up
> to the length it reached. The compiled machine is a finite automaton over the edit alphabet and
> answers every word, of any length, from a fixed table.

The antecedent is false in all three vocabularies. `SequentialModel::reachable_with_depth` is a
breadth-first closure over the generator action on a **finite** edit-state set — 81 for the unit
shift, 256 for hard pins, 1,024 for the cursor — and it terminates when no generator produces a new
state, at depths 8, 4 and 7 respectively. `signature_classes` then builds each context's signature
over that closed set. There is no word-length bound anywhere in the direct route, so it is complete
over every word of every length by construction, exactly as the compiled machine is.

Measured, since the report's own mystery ledger asks for it ("the cheap experiment that would fix it
is to ask a length-20 word against both routes"): drawing twenty thousand uniform random words of
length twenty per vocabulary and computing the edit state each reaches,

| vocabulary | reachable edit states | length-20 words landing outside that set |
|---|---|---|
| hard-pin (control) | 256 | 0 of 20,000 |
| unit-shift | 81 | 0 of 20,000 |
| cyclic-cursor | 1,024 | 0 of 20,000 |

Zero, and necessarily zero: the reachable set is closed under every generator, so no word of any
length can leave it. The experiment the ledger names as the one that would settle the claim settles
it against the claim.

The claim would be true of a *different* direct route — one that enumerates words to a bound and
never closes the state space. No such route exists in this repository; probe 1's `signature_classes`
enumerates partial assignments directly and probe 8's enumerates reachable edit states directly, and
the plan explicitly forbade the word-enumerating alternative as importing the compiler's own view.
So § 5's advantage is stated against a route the plan ruled out, and the report's closing sentence —
"That is the defensible statement of what compilation buys in this regime" — leaves probe 8 with
nothing it has shown compilation buys.

What does survive is worth saying in its place, because it is the real content of §§ 3 and 4: under a
non-idempotent, non-commuting vocabulary the *certificate* is a genuine word, whose order and
repetitions carry information that no pin set carries. That is a statement about the witness, not
about cost or completeness, and it is measured.

### 2.2 "All 34 order-essential" is a property of the emitted certificates, not of the 34 pairs — defect

The report's verdict and § 4 both read as a property of the pairs: "every one of the 34 separating
certificates of length two or more is order-essential — a permutation of the same multiset fails to
separate the pair."

`separating_words` in the report binary takes **one** word per pair, whatever
`shortest_separator` returns first, and calls `order_is_essential` on that word.
`shortest_separator` explores words shortest-first and returns the first hit in generator-index
order, so the certificate is an arbitrary representative of the minimal separators.

`order_is_essential` itself is correct and complete over what it is given: it sorts the word and
walks `next_permutation`, which enumerates every distinct permutation of a multiset exactly once from
the sorted start, returning true as soon as one fails to separate. So the emitted claim is sound.

Enumerating instead *every* minimal-length separating word for each pair and asking whether all of
them need their order:

| vocabulary | pairs with minimal separator of length ≥ 2 | emitted certificate order-essential | **every** minimal separator order-essential | minimal lengths |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hard-pin (control) | 0 | 0 | 0 | — |
| unit-shift | 13 | 0 | 0 | 12 at length 2, 1 at length 3 |
| cyclic-cursor | 34 | 34 | **7** | 24 at length 2, 10 at length 3 |

So for 27 of the 34 cursor pairs there exists a minimal separating word that can be permuted freely
and still separates. The pair-level statement — "these 34 context pairs cannot be separated by an
unordered application of a multiset" — holds for 7. The certificate-level statement — "the
certificate we hand you needs its order" — holds for 34, and it is the one the code establishes.
The report should say which it means; as written it says the weaker thing in language that reads as
the stronger.

On the coordinator's first question, the "34" itself is complete: the cursor's quotient is the
identity, so all 120 context pairs are separated, and `59 + 27 + 34 = 120` accounts for every one at
a search bound of four, so no pair fell outside the enumeration. The completeness gap is in the
choice of certificate per pair, not in the set of pairs.

### 2.3 "A richer vocabulary is a finer quotient" is contradicted by the table it sits under — defect

§ 2's headline: "**A richer vocabulary is a finer quotient, and here it is the identity.** Hard pins
leave 11 classes on 16 contexts; the unit shift and the cursor both resolve every context. That is
the same dial probe 1 found on the observation set, running the other way: more intervention
vocabulary is not free precision, it is a monotone slide toward the identity."

By every available measure of richness the ordering runs the wrong way for the unit shift. It has
four generators against the hard pin's twelve, 81 reachable edit states against 256, and all six of
its generator pairs commute — and it gives the *finer* quotient, the identity, where hard pins give
11 classes. There is no monotone dial here, and nothing in the table supports one.

§ 6 contains the correct explanation and states it plainly: "the machine's saturation is what loses
information, and a *modular* shift undoes it, because state two plus one wraps to zero." That is the
mechanism — the shift can walk the final state around the modulus and expose an input the saturating
advance hid, which a pin cannot do because pinning a step destroys the history before it. So the
right generalization is about whether the vocabulary can invert what the natural run discards, not
about how much vocabulary there is.

The practical advice the section draws — "Anyone declaring a sequential vocabulary for the sake of
compression is declaring the wrong thing" — survives, and so does § 7's second attached condition.
It is the reason given for it that is wrong, and the wrong reason is the one a reader would carry
away.

### 2.4 The `1.00x` economics is a carrier identity that could not have come out otherwise — defect in framing

The coordinator asked which of the two this is, given that probe 2's `1.00x` turned out to be a
carrier identity rather than a measurement. Here it is unambiguously an identity, and there is not
even a solve in it. `economics` in the report binary computes

```rust
let carrier = model.contexts() * model.edit_states(vocabulary);
let reachable = model.reachable_with_depth(vocabulary).len() as u64;
let oracle = model.contexts() * reachable;
```

and prints `carrier as f64 / oracle as f64`. Both columns are combinatorial counts; nothing is timed
and nothing is solved. The ratio is `edit_states / |reachable|` exactly, the context factor cancels,
and since the reachable set is by definition a subset of the encoded set the ratio is **≥ 1 always**.
The predeclared verdict was "a loss", and the only two outcomes available were `1.00x` (a tie) or
worse. The arm could not have been won.

The report is straight about the mechanism in the sentence that follows the table — "every edit state
is reachable in all three vocabularies, so the ratio is exactly one" — and its mystery ledger names
the gate correctly: a vocabulary whose reachable edit set is a strict subset of the declared encoding
would break the identity. That is the right diagnosis and it is unusually clear. What is wrong is
around it: the columns are headed "carrier solves" and "direct-oracle solves" when no solve is
performed, the verdict paragraph calls the result "predeclared as a loss ... it loses" as though a
measurement had occurred, and the mystery ledger counts it as "three independent confirmations of the
same structural identity" when the three share one construction and one formula.

The named gate is also cheap to close and would turn the identity back into a measurement. Any
vocabulary with an unreachable encoded state does it — for instance a hard-pin encoding that also
reserves a digit for an edit the generator set cannot produce. One such fixture converts an
unfalsifiable arm into a real one.

### 2.5 A pair with no separator inside the search bound is silently counted as separated by observation alone — defect

`separating_words` does

```rust
let word = model
    .shortest_separator(vocabulary, left, right, arguments.max_word)
    .unwrap_or_default();
lengths[word.len().min(2)] += 1;
```

`shortest_separator` returns `Some(vec![])` when the two contexts already differ at the identity edit,
and `None` when no word up to `max_length` separates them. `unwrap_or_default()` collapses those two
into the same empty vector, so a pair the search failed on is counted in the **length 0** column —
the column that means "separated before any intervention".

It does not bite at the published bound: all three vocabularies report exactly 59 in that column, and
59 is the natural-run partition, identical across vocabularies as it must be. But the failure mode is
silent and it fails open, which I demonstrated by rerunning the committed binary at `--max-word 1`:

| vocabulary | length 0 at `--max-word 4` | length 0 at `--max-word 1` |
|---|---|---|
| hard-pin (control) | 59 | 59 |
| unit-shift | 59 | **72** |
| cyclic-cursor | 59 | **93** |

The 13 and 34 pairs whose separators were cut off reappear as "separated by the natural run", which
is exactly the opposite of what they are. A reader of the truncated run would conclude the
observation partition resolves 93 of 120 pairs when it resolves 59.

### 2.6 Every separator fits inside the window; only edit-state reachability needs an overlong word — wording problem

§ 3 establishes that the unit shift needs eight applications and the cursor seven to reach their
deepest edit states, both past the four-step window, and calls this "the sharp form of non-vacuity:
a word longer than the number of editable steps still reaches an edit state that no shorter word
reaches, so the word is not a pin set in disguise." That is correct and it is measured.

§ 4 then presents the separating certificates, and the two results sit close enough together to read
as one. They are not. The minimal separators run to length 3 at most — 24 at length two and 10 at
length three for the cursor, 12 and 1 for the unit shift — so **no separating certificate in this
probe exceeds the window**. The overlong-word result is about which edit states the vocabulary can
reach; the certificate result is about which words distinguish contexts, and on this fixture the
latter never needs the former.

Nothing is wrong with either number. The report simply never says the separators are short, and a
reader taking "words longer than the window are not redundant" together with "34 order-essential
certificates" will assume some certificate is overlong. None is.

### 2.7 The control is a genuine control — no issue

`VOCABULARIES` is iterated against the same `SequentialModel`, so the hard-pin arm differs from the
other two only in the `Vocabulary` value threaded through `generators`, `act`, `observe`,
`edit_states` and `identity_edit`. Same window, same transition table, same four steps, same editable
count, same contexts, same observation.

Its collapse is measured, not assumed. `collapse_measurements` checks idempotence by applying each
generator twice at **every** edit state (all 256, no sampling) and commutation by comparing both
composition orders at every edit state for all 66 unordered pairs. The separator counts come from the
same exhaustive pair sweep as the treatment arms. The library test
`hard_pins_are_idempotent_and_collapse` repeats the idempotence check and additionally pins the
deepest word at exactly `editable`. My independent implementation reproduces `12/12`, `54/66` and a
deepest word of 4.

The report's handling of the commutation column deserves credit rather than criticism. Twelve of the
66 hard-pin pairs fail to commute — the pairs writing the same step with different values, where the
last write wins — and rather than hiding an inconvenient number the report calls it out, notes that
probe 1a's claim was "commutative on distinct variables" and that this is what holds, and concludes
that commutation is the wrong discriminator and the deepest-word column is the right one. That is the
correct reading and I verified the count independently.

### 2.8 Probe 1's caveat is correctly stated here, and it is independent of the separator-replay gap — reconciliation

The coordinator asked me to reconcile probe 8's "probe 1's three-step certificate was a pin in word
shape" with my probe 1 review's finding that the delivered quarter of probe 1a's four-item contract
is separator *decoding* rather than replay. They are two different things and both hold.

Probe 1's own report already carried the caveat, in its own words: the wide-conjunction certificate
`do(V0:=1) do(V1:=1) do(V2:=1)` is "by the signature collapse … equivalent to the single simultaneous
arity-three pin, so it is a word-shaped presentation of a pin, not evidence that word structure
matters." Probe 8 restates that accurately and, more usefully, **measures** it: under hard pins on
this window, zero of 112 separated pairs need a word of length two or more, which is the caveat
turned into a number on a fixture probe 1 did not have. That is a genuine contribution and it is not
in tension with anything.

My probe 1 finding is about a different sentence — contract item 2's "Delivered and demonstrated",
where what is delivered is `decode_word` mapping a certificate path to intervention steps, and the
plan's predeclared gate of replaying every separator against the model was replaced by an
arity-boundary gate and never run. Probe 8 does not touch replay either way. The two findings compose
rather than conflict: probe 1's certificates are pins in word shape *and* were never replayed; probe
8's certificates are genuine words *and* are also never replayed, since
`separating_words` checks separation through `observe` at the reached edit state, which is the same
routine the oracle uses. That last point is worth stating in probe 8's own terms — see § 4.

### 2.9 The certificate-policy question closes, and here it is load-bearing — no issue, with a note

Probe 8 is the first probe in this task whose carrier crosses the core's admission floor. The
cursor's carrier is 16,384 states and the hard pin's is exactly 4,096, and `multiway_admission` in
`ergodis/src/observational.rs` rejects only below `4_096`. So unlike probes 1, 3 and 4 — where the
fixtures were small enough that the broken refiner was unreachable whatever the policy — probe 8 is
protected only by its policy choice.

That choice is `CertificatePolicy::SplitTranscript`, in the report binary and in the library test
`every_vocabulary_agrees_with_its_oracle`, and `SplitTranscript` is one of the two policies the core
repair note records as correct throughout. The binary also hard-fails via `ensure!` if the compiled
classes disagree with the direct oracle, which would have caught a miscompile as an abort rather than
as a wrong class count. So no probe 8 number is affected. The report does not remark on the policy at
all, which was fine when it was written and is worth a line now that the floor is known.

### 2.10 Predeclaration is thin, the fixture deviates from the plan, and no evidence is retained — one defect, two notes

**Predeclaration.** The plan's probe-8 section and its index row were written in the plan revision at
14:34 on 2026-09-04; the code landed at 19:22 and the report at 19:23. The order is clean. But the
predeclared threshold is "word closure non-vacuous, measured", which is not a numeric threshold in
the C1061 style the process rules require ("break-even 1.13 updates", "15 of 18 cells"). A threshold
that is met by any nonzero count cannot fail, and the four negatives probe 8 reports were free to
land anywhere above zero. The economics arm is the one place a number was predeclared, and § 2.4 shows
it could not have been beaten. So probe 8 has no falsifiable predeclared quantity.

**Fixture deviation.** The plan says "A two-state machine over a bounded window". The fixture is a
**three**-state saturating machine, and the difference is not cosmetic: with two states the unit
shift would be its own inverse and the modular-wrap argument that § 6 rests on would take a different
form. The deviation is an improvement and is undisclosed.

**Evidence.** `ergodis-private/evidence/` holds no probe 8 artifact. Two arms of four tables each
exist only as terminal output. They reproduce exactly at `256959b`, so nothing is lost in fact. This
is the fourth probe in a row with no retained evidence — probes 1, 3, 4 and now 8 — and at this point
it is a lane-level gap rather than four independent lapses.

### 3.2 Report the pair-level order-essentiality alongside the certificate-level one

```diff
--- a/src/causal_sequential.rs

@@
+    /// Every minimal-length separating word for the pair, rather than the
+    /// first one the search reaches.
+    ///
+    /// [`Self::shortest_separator`] returns an arbitrary representative, so
+    /// "the certificate needs its order" is a statement about that
```

### 3.3 A fixture where the reachable edit set is a strict subset of the encoding

Finding 2.4, and it is the report's own named gate. Add a vocabulary — or a flag on `HardPin` —
whose encoded edit-state space reserves a configuration no generator can produce, so `edit_states`
strictly exceeds `reachable_with_depth().len()`. **Predicted effect**: the economics ratio leaves
`1.00x` for the first time in this task, by the factor `edit_states / |reachable|`, and the arm
becomes falsifiable. Until then the `1.00x` should be reported as an identity with its derivation,
not as a measured loss.

## 5. What could not be checked, and why

- **Whether the separators are order-essential at lengths beyond the search bound.** I enumerated all
  minimal-length separators, which is the right object, but a pair could have longer separators with
  different order behaviour. Since every pair here has a minimal separator of length at most three,
  and order-essentiality is naturally a statement about minimal witnesses, I do not think this is a
  gap worth closing; recording it because the negative needs its domain stated.
- **Whether probe 8's separators replay against the model rather than the oracle.** They do not
  round-trip through the compiler — `separating_words` calls `observe` at the reached edit state —
  but `observe` is the same routine `signature_classes` uses, so the check shares the model layer
  exactly as probes 1, 3 and 4 do. My Python reimplements `observe` and reproduces every separator
  count, which closes the shared-implementation risk for these numbers but is not a replay path in
  the repository.
- **Whether a natural vocabulary exists with a strict-subset reachable set.** § 2.4's gate. I did not
  attempt to construct one; the patch in § 3.3 is a synthetic route that would do it, and whether a
  vocabulary anyone would declare has the property is the open question the report already names.
- **Anything about timing.** As with probe 2 and probe 8's own § 5, nothing here is timed; the
  economics table is arithmetic.

**Verdict.** Probe 8's measurements are the most carefully controlled in this task and they all hold:
the non-vacuity of word closure under a cursor vocabulary is real, exhaustively searched, properly
controlled against an identical window under hard pins, and reproduced independently down to the
class vectors. Where it fails is in the conclusion. The single claim the report offers as what
compilation buys — completeness over words of unbounded length — is false against the probe's own
direct oracle, which closes a finite edit-state space by breadth-first search and therefore already
answers every word; the experiment the report's own mystery ledger asked for settles it against the
claim. The order-essentiality headline is a property of the certificates the enumeration happened to
emit rather than of the 34 pairs, and the pair-level count is seven. The economics tie is an identity
that could not have come out below one. What probe 8 genuinely establishes, and what its report
should lead with, is narrower and still valuable: under a non-idempotent, non-commuting vocabulary
the separating certificate is a genuine word whose order and repetitions carry information no pin set
carries, and the hard-pin control turns probe 1's caveat about word-shaped pins from an argument into
a measurement.
