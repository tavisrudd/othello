## Summary and contribution

The paper claims that every smooth complex cubic threefold remains irrational after product with one projective line, and more generally that rationality is unchanged by this operation for every smooth complex Picard-rank-one Fano threefold. A second theorem says that, for the nine families detected by the numerical argument, birational first stabilizations have isomorphic rational third Hodge structures. The latter assertion yields a very-general cancellation statement for cubic and quartic hypersurfaces, subject to the cited rational Torelli theorem.

The proposed mechanism is concrete. The paper selects whole primary factors of the generic quantum connection. For an even rank-two factor with a nonzero square-zero centered Euler operator, an elementary modification produces a regular-singular residue. Its two eigenvalues distinguish two numerical counts: separation modulo integers, and separation in the canonical modified lattice. A third selector records odd dimension on even rank-three factors. The essential geometric claim is that every possible point, curve and surface center has zero contribution under the quantum blowup comparison. Weak factorization then gives fourfold birational invariants. Small quantum calculations, combined with persistence in all even bulk directions, supply the endpoints and the doubling under a projective-line product.

The Hodge part changes the base of quantum parameters while retaining the entire cohomology fiber, carries out an equivariant comparison, and then uses representation cancellation and rational descent. The appendices give broader bundle and additive formulas, a second cubic calculation, the finite Fano input and its geometric scope, and an arithmetic finiteness application.

## Significance and scope

If the proof and external identifications withstand specialist checking, the main results have substantial interest for birational geometry. The statement concerns every smooth member, and the dimension of stabilization is part of the conclusion. The introduction explains this particularly well by contrasting the asserted obstruction with both very-general stable irrationality and the cited stably rational examples. This tells readers why a one-step theorem is worth having even in the presence of stable-rationality results. The zero-cycle consequences further explain what information the quantum construction detects beyond universal CH0-triviality.

The Hodge theorem has independent value: it provides restrictions on birationality between irrational fourfolds rather than merely distinguishing them from projective space. The draft correctly stops at rational Hodge structures. It does not quietly promote them to integral or principally polarized identifications, and the Torelli and arithmetic applications visibly import additional results.

The scope is ambitious for 32 pages. Nevertheless, the numerical and Hodge parts are separated honestly, and the appendices are mostly placed at the right rhetorical level. I do not regard the breadth itself as a reason to split the paper. I would preserve the cubic as its title-page identity and leading model.

## Correctness at the actual reading boundary

I found no demonstrated internal contradiction or proof defect in this reading. This is materially weaker than verifying the theorems. The main deductions are coherent provided the quantum comparisons possess exactly the stated coefficient, connection and lattice properties and the imported quantum matrices have the asserted geometric meaning.

Several arguments are unusually helpful to a referee. The rank-two modification displays the contribution of the first positive-order coefficient and the derivative-induced shift; it does not infer the residue from the leading matrix alone. The cyclic persistence proofs address the passage from the small connection to all even bulk directions, including mixed directions on the product. The surface proof distinguishes nef-canonical and ruled cases and obtains ruled-surface vanishing using point centers; it does not circularly invoke fourfold invariance. The Hodge proof explains both constancy of representation multiplicities and why complex representation isomorphism descends to a rational Hodge isomorphism. The determinant argument in the last step is a good inclusion.

The principal unresolved verification boundary is the implementation of Iritani's comparison in §3 and its fixed-base form in §7.2. The paper needs the comparison and inverse to preserve the original z-lattices, not just meromorphic connections. It also needs faithful recovery of each intrinsic center coefficient field and generic separation of different occurrences. These requirements are identified, and arguments are supplied, but I have not checked that the cited completed coefficient rings, shifts and inverse coordinate maps support every operation asserted. In particular, I have not independently verified the hypotheses used in the least-Novikov-degree argument or the coefficientwise realization as regular z-series. This is an unverified external interface, not an observed counterexample to it.

A separate source boundary occurs in Appendix D. The finite matrix manipulations do not establish that the matrices are the quantum multiplication operators of every family. The manuscript says this itself. The attribution of that identification needs the clarification in Major Comment 1 below. I have not rerun either finite checker. Similarly, the external stable-rationality examples, rational controls, Torelli statements and bounded-isogeny theorem are imported claims at this read depth. The printed formalization discussion does not certify those statements, and I have not treated it as doing so.

## Exposition, organization and literature positioning

The title works: it is precise, memorable and immediately mathematical. The broader Fano theorem is adequately advertised by the abstract and the second theorem, without requiring a longer title. The abstract answers the object, result, mechanism and refinement questions. A whitespace count of the extracted abstract, including separately extracted mathematical tokens, is 132 words, safely below 200. Its last sentence makes the numerical/Hodge dependency boundary explicit. I would not add more technical terminology to it.

Readers know why they should care by the end of page 2. The early explanation of the obstruction from surface centers is more valuable than another paragraph of general history would be. The initial numerical picture—two on the stabilized cubic and zero on projective four-space—is also effective.

The main weakness is the first transition into quantum connections. Pages 2–3 introduce the coefficient field, primary factors, the elementary modification, Lax rigidity, exponent classes and the stronger lattice count before the adjacent reader sees the worked rank-two example on page 5 or the cubic endpoint on pages 12–13. Then §3 asks the reader to absorb substantial completion and comparison detail. This is justified mathematical work, but the first-pass reading path is less explicit than the overall architecture.

The literature positioning is more than a list. The introduction identifies a common atom framework, credits the earlier cubic exponent values, states what Cai's application does, distinguishes a general product theorem from the specialized argument used here, and identifies equivariant and Hodge precedents. This is a fair and useful shape for attribution on the face of the draft. I cannot certify the accuracy or priority of these characterizations without reading the cited versions. The modest fourfold-application citation cluster is the least informative portion, but it is short enough not to be a serious defect. The paper should not respond to this review by adding a larger survey.

The numerical/Hodge split and the final introduction paragraph identify major safe stops. The local instruction to omit the triple-primary lemma for the cubic is particularly good. The remaining improvement is to specify safe proof omissions while retaining their exact outputs. An adjacent reader can postpone detailed gauge recursion, the faithful-center proof and the universal matrix reduction; that reader cannot omit the conclusions that these arguments preserve the original lattice, recover intrinsic centers, separate occurrences, and continue the small endpoint. Those are premises, not optional refinements.

## Major comments, in priority order

1. **Identify the reconstruction input precisely.** Appendix D.2, “Sources and all smooth members” (`sec:fano-data`), says: “The finite coefficient comparison checks normalization; the GW identification is the imported reconstruction result.” The preceding text refers to reconstruction data in *Naive atoms of blowups*, period formulas in Sections 3–17 of CCGK, and a specific theorem for genera six and eight. As printed, I cannot identify a single precise theorem or clearly delimited argument supplying the claimed reconstruction for all the other displayed families. Matching a finite number of period coefficients is not, by itself, a reader-visible uniqueness proof for the full quantum-product matrix. Please give a theorem/proposition-level reference, with its assumptions and normalization, for the period-to-matrix determination actually used; identify which displayed families it covers. If several inputs are needed, a short family-to-source mapping suffices. If a uniqueness argument is supplied by the present paper instead, state it and explain why the finite coefficient range determines the allowed matrix. This is a citation-precision and exposed-premise gap. I have found no evidence that the import is invalid or that a displayed matrix is wrong.

2. **Make the indispensable comparison output available before its technical proof.** At §3, “The comparison and its lattice,” `lem:faithful-center-base-change` and `prop:numerical-blowup`, first collect the three outputs in one short statement: faithful center fields, regular original-lattice transport, and separation of occurrences. State exactly which are supplied by the cited theorem and which are established locally. Then explicitly allow a first-pass reader to retain these outputs and proceed to surface vanishing. The current text does discuss all three, but their reader-facing hierarchy is spread across several paragraphs and a large conventions table. This change would also help a specialist identify the source verification to perform. It must not weaken the lattice requirement to preservation of exponent classes: the resonant cases use the stronger requirement.

3. **Provide one earlier model and a short local reading route; do not reorganize the whole paper.** At the end of the conceptual introduction or the opening of §2 (`sec:atomic-one-step`), give a compact preview of the actual comparison: the cubic rank-two residue has eigenvalues −1/6 and −5/6, whereas the eligible positive-genus curve residue has two equal eigenvalues. Say that §3 extends center vanishing from curves to all surfaces. The existing 2-by-2 modification on page 5 can remain where it is; a preview should replace some dense orientation prose, not duplicate its calculation. A route through the statements of Proposition 2.5, Proposition 3.2, Proposition 3.4, the cubic endpoint and Lemma 4.2 would then let an adjacent reader understand the argument before returning to proofs. Keep the nilpotent/cyclic and full-even-bulk hypotheses visible. A major structural relocation is unnecessary.

4. **Give the reader an explicit trust boundary for the headline results.** Appendix D.3 (`sec:fano-data`, “Reproduction and formal boundary”) states that new geometric theorems are absent from formalization and algebraic fragments retain their boundaries. That is useful, but the printed source annotations themselves are invisible. Add one compact, accessible statement identifying the role of the finite computation and the external geometric comparisons, and provide a stable public location/version for the verification bundle when preparing submission. Avoid implying that the AI disclosure or the presence of two finite implementations checks the geometric theorem. No long formal-verification section is needed; the paper's main proofs should remain mathematical.

## Minor comments

1. **§1, surface-center paragraph.** The example of the degree-five blowup contribution communicates the problem well. “The direct intermediate-Jacobian argument therefore gives no obstruction” is broader than the displayed degree comparison alone establishes. “Does not directly give the required obstruction” would state the precise motivational point without suggesting that every possible refinement of that method has been excluded.

2. **§2.1, primary-block conventions (`prop:generic-spectral-connection-splitting`).** The distinction between a whole generalized eigenspace and an individual Jordan block is essential and correctly present. Consider putting that sentence immediately before the first proposition rather than after its proof; it controls what the reader should imagine throughout the splitting argument.

3. **§4.1 and §4.2 (`prop:universal-rank-two-residue`, `subsec:shared-cubic-packet`).** The universal calculation is economical for the three degree families but precedes the promised cubic model. A one-line instruction that the first-pass cubic reader may accept the universal formula and go to §4.2 would be enough. There is no need to duplicate or move the full calculation.

4. **§5.2, endpoint table (`prop:fano-endpoints`).** The table is clear and legitimately central. If any column is added, the most useful is the detecting invariant or a brief note distinguishing the nonintegral and resonant rank-two rows, not more raw numerical data. The four odd dimensions matter chiefly through positivity, as the proof already says.

5. **§6.1 (`thm:index-two-classification`).** This is a direct specialization of Theorem 1.2 and reads more naturally as a corollary than as another theorem. Its arithmetic qualifications and the distinction between the two counts justify retaining the subsection.

6. **§7.2 (`lem:hodge-fixed-comparison`).** Repetition of the fixed-base proof is defensible because restriction could otherwise destroy faithful center coordinates. Add a sentence identifying the exact new check—algebraic divisor coordinates remain fixed—before repeating the Vandermonde argument. A first-pass Hodge reader can then read the statement and this change, and postpone the parallel proof.

7. **Appendix B, “Limits of the construction” (`sec:motivic`).** The dimension-five counterexample and the failure of higher-rank kernel-flag preservation are worthwhile mathematical boundaries. The introduction's roadmap could point to this discussion by name. It helps explain why the main theorem stops after one stabilization and is more informative than a generic concluding paragraph would be.

8. **Rendered layout.** No clipping or illegible collision was apparent in the full 32-page image pass. The §2 opening paragraph breaks across pages 3–4; Appendix D.1's “The nine inputs are:” is separated from its table across pages 28–29. Keep those units together if pagination permits. Pages 14 and 20 have conspicuous lower-page whitespace caused by the following kept blocks; this is preferable to damaging the endpoint table or final Hodge proof, but can be tightened during final typesetting. The tables themselves are readable. The appendix and bibliography sizes are proportionate to the present scope.

- **Specialist comprehension: 4/5.** A birational geometer familiar with quantum-connection arguments can identify the constructions, important hypotheses and dependency structure, and can locate the delicate comparisons. The score is limited by the precision of the reconstruction attribution and by the work required to align the completion conventions with the external theorems. - **Adjacent-reader accessibility: 3/5.** An algebraic or Hodge geometer without quantum-connection expertise can understand the question, theorem, importance of surface centers and the eventual Hodge conclusion. ## Editorial recommendation

I recommend revision before acceptance, with targeted specialist verification of the quantum comparison and reconstruction imports. I would support continued consideration rather than rejection: the claimed contribution is substantial, the mathematical route is coherent at this reading boundary, and the exposition already has a workable two-part structure. The revisions I am asking for are focused: expose the reconstruction premise precisely, clarify the indispensable comparison outputs, and add a usable first-pass route for adjacent geometers. I do not recommend a wholesale rewrite or a longer literature survey.

An unconditional acceptance recommendation would exceed this review's actual verification. Conversely, the fact that I have not checked the cited sources is not evidence against them. The journal should assign that source-level verification explicitly, especially for regular-lattice transport and the all-family quantum-matrix identification.
