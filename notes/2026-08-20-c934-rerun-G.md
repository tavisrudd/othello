### 1. All-degree clean restriction and the Fano lift pass

**Locations:** Lemma 4.1 and (12), printed p. 6; equation (24), printed p. 10.

The proof of Lemma 4.1 is independent of the degree of `T`. The equality
`q^{-1}(X)=P` and the fibre-degree comparison give `q^*X=P` with multiplicity

Albanese-embedded Fano surface, so `psi_*T=[F]=theta^[3]`. Equation (24) is
therefore exact; the degree-six generic degree of `psi` never enters this
restricted point-input calculation.

### 2. Infinite order and the rational rank step pass

**Location:** the paragraphs between (24) and (25), printed pp. 10--11.

The restriction of `u_4|_U` to the link is the order-three class `tau`. The
global class `u_4|_U` has infinite order: if it vanished over `Q`, the pair
sequence would make `u_4` rationally supported on `X`; its pushforward to `J`
would then factor through the constant map from `X` and vanish, contradicting
`b_*u_4=[F] != 0`.

In the pair sequence

`H^2(X,Q) -> H^4(M,Q) -> H^4(U,Q) -> H^3(X,Q) -> H^5(M,Q)`,

the first end map is injective because self-intersection is multiplication by
`-h`, and the second is injective by Theorem 1.2. Krämer's rational
decomposition (18) contributes exactly one degree-four point summand, so
`dim H^4(U,Q)=dim IH^4(Theta,Q)`. The injective truncation map between these
spaces is thus an isomorphism, forcing `H^4(U,Q)->H^4(K,Q)` to vanish.

### 3. The index-three attachment (25) passes

### 4. The constant-to-IC comparison (26) passes

**Location:** the final paragraph of Section 7 and equation (26), printed p. 11.

Normality, `H^1(K)=H^2(K)=0`, and the degree-three Deligne truncation give

`Z_Theta[4] -> IC_Theta(Z) -> H^3(K,Z)_0[1] -> +1`.

Section 6 identifies `IH^3(Theta,Z)=H^3(U,Z)`, and the Mayer--Vietoris argument
in Proposition 2.2 identifies this group with `H^3(M,Z)`. Proposition 4.3 makes
the map to `H^3(K,Z)` surjective through the ten integral exceptional
restrictions. Hence the connecting map to `H^4(Theta,Z)` vanishes. Since the
cone has no further cohomology sheaf, `H^k(Theta,Z)->IH^k(Theta,Z)` is an
isomorphism for every `k>=4`, including the endpoint `k=4`.

### 5. Corollary 1.5 has the correct shifts and exact multiplier `+3`

**Locations:** statement on printed p. 3; proof on printed p. 10; stalk and
costalk identifications (19)--(20), printed p. 9.

For `K=R sigma_* Z_M[4]`, the integral splitting is

`K = Z_0[2] direct-sum P direct-sum Z_0[-2]`.

Consequently

`pH^{-2}(K)=Z_0` and `pH^2(K)=Z_0`.

On the central stalk these outer summands occur in
`H^{-2}(i^*K)=H^2(X,Z)=Zh` and
`H^2(i^*K)=H^6(X,Z)=Z[pt]`. The unit blocks `[−1]` on the two sides of (20)
normalize the splittings so these are the displayed generators.

The line bundle `O_M(-X)` is relatively ample for the blow-up, and

`e^*O_M(-X)=N_{X/M}^{-1}=O_X(1)`.

Thus `eta=c1(O_M(-X))` restricts to `h`. The relative Lefschetz map for `k=2`
is therefore

`eta^2:pH^{-2}(K)->pH^2(K),   h |-> h^3=3[pt]`.

Relative to the unit-normalized source and target generators, the matrix is
exactly `[+3]`, not merely `±3`. The two outer unit blocks remain split after
derived reduction modulo three, so the corresponding map
`F_{3,0}->F_{3,0}` is zero and cannot be an isomorphism. Corollary 1.5's
modular relative-hard-Lefschetz failure follows. Over `Q`, multiplication by
three is invertible, in agreement with ordinary relative hard Lefschetz.
