# C130 — do the icosahedral levers feed the C84 parent program? [REPORTED 2026-07-13]

Skeptical transfer check of the two levers from
[icosahedral deep-holes](handoffs/2026-07-13-icosahedral-mds-deep-holes.md) ("LOOP-BACK" section)
against the C84 abundance-first lane
([residual graphs](2026-07-12-conic-involution-residual-graphs.md),
[Schreier graphs](2026-07-12-conic-involution-schreier-graphs.md)).

## Lever 1 — secant-capacity counting (15(q−1) ≥ q²−6 ⟹ q ≤ 14)

**Verdict: SHARED-MACHINERY-BUT-NO-NEW-CONTENT for abundance; standard-tool overlap with the
sealing lane only.**

- Direction mismatch: the icosahedral use is an *upper* bound on q forcing a sporadic extremal
  (complete coverage possible only at small q, zero slack at q=11). C84 needs a *lower* bound
  `#{y : 𝒢=0} ≥ c·q²` holding for **all** q. A finiteness filter cannot anchor a density floor.
- Dimension mismatch: k centres give ≤ C(k,2) secants covering Θ(k²·q) points — dimension-1
  counting. The parent frontier already states its wall precisely: every known certificate is a
  homography fixed locus, dimension 1, Θ(q); density needs a dimension-2 family. Lever 1's
  arithmetic lives entirely on the wrong side of that wall.
- Regime mismatch: the icosahedral inequality bites only because the extremal demands the secants
  cover *everything* off the conic. Generic size-3/size-4 residuals cover a Θ(q) sliver of P¹ and
  no filling condition arises.
- The one real landing spot is ranking item #3 (sealing = complete-arc/saturating-set): there the
  same capacity-vs-coverage first moment gives `C(k,2)(q−1) ≥ q² − O(kq)` ⟹ `k ≳ √(2q)` — but that
  is the classical saturating-set lower bound already contained in the literature the sealing
  entry cites. Nothing new transfers.

## Lever 2 — D(S)=∅ ⟺ all pairwise products elliptic

**Verdict: SHARED-MACHINERY-BUT-NO-NEW-CONTENT; Clebsch hexagon at most a code-validation data
point, not an extremal test case for abundance.**

- The equivalence is a two-line corollary of the parent's own Theorem 2.1 apparatus: `t ∈ D(S)`
  from pair {x,y} ⟺ σ_xσ_y has a fixed point on P¹ ⟺ the product is split/unipotent ⟺
  tr²−4det square — the §6.3 trace-classifier `tr(A_xA_y) = −B(x,y)` verbatim. The icosahedral
  lane *instantiated* this at n=6, q=11; it did not extend it.
- No new lemma for the escape kernel: the escape analysis concerns generic size-4 children with
  full PSL/PGL `H_S` and *nonempty*, varied D(S); the all-elliptic stratum is just one stratum
  the catalogue already evaluates (no fixed points ⟹ pure 2r-cycle components, Cor 2.2).
- The Clebsch hexagon is a single PGL-orbit at the single prime q=11 — dimension 0 in the
  uniform-in-q sense. The frontier requires a dimension-2 constructible family carrying a
  uniform-in-q value-0 proof; a sporadic q=11 configuration cannot feed that, as a bound, a
  worst case, or a best case. As a *sanity input* it is mildly useful: a 6-centre state with
  maximal live conic (D(S)=∅, |live|=12) and H_S = full PGL₂(11) exercises the boundary evaluator
  at its emptiest-saturation corner — worth one line in a test suite, not a lemma.

## Bottom line

**No — the spin-off does not feed the C84 odd-plane abundance program.** The machinery
(D(S), trace classifier, subgroup catalogue) is shared *by construction*, and everything the
icosahedral lane returned through it is either a specialization of existing parent theorems or
a sporadic single-q extremal pointing in the opposite quantifier direction (finiteness upper
bound vs all-q density lower bound; dimension 1 vs dimension 2). The spin-off's value is
internal to its own coding-theory/rigidity story. No C84 next step is created; do not route
C84 effort through the icosahedral results.
