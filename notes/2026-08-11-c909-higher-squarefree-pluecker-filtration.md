# C909 — higher squarefree Plücker filtration in the finite-etale graph lattice

Date: 2026-08-11  
Status: bounded higher-codimension audit; no manuscript, PDF, mirror, Lean,
or commit change

## Scope and verdict

This note isolates the squarefree `2k`-slot summand of codimension `k`.  It
does not claim that repeated-slot summands have been eliminated; those are a
separate divided-power/rank-one problem.  The squarefree summands already
give a uniform higher-codimension obstruction.

Let `O` be the unramified splitting DVR, let `v(p)=1`, and let
`δ_ij=t_j-t_i` be units for distinct spectral parameters.  On a fixed set
`S` of `2k` distinct slots, write

\[
 p^{2a}\Omega_{ij}=\delta_{ij}X_iX_j+p^aB_{ij},
 \qquad B_{ij}=X_iY_j+X_jY_i.
\tag{1}
\]

The rational squarefree invariant space has rank

\[
 C_k=\frac1{k+1}\binom{2k}{k},
\tag{2}
\]

the Catalan number: this is the multidegree `(1,…,1)` piece of the
`SL_2` contraction/Plücker module, equivalently the `(k,k)` Specht weight
space in the skew-Cauchy decomposition.  Choose the `C_k` noncrossing
matchings as an integral standard-monomial basis.  The product lattice on
this block is generated by the corresponding products of (1).

For the generic distinct-parameter calculation, the Smith valuations of the
product lattice inside its saturation are:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
k&C_k&\text{valuations, with multiplicity}\\ \hline
1&1&0\\
2&2&0,1\\
3&5&0,1^3,2\\
4&14&0,1^5,2^7,3\\
5&42&0,1^7,2^{18},3^{15},4\\
6&132&0,1^9,2^{33},3^{57},4^{31},5.
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]

Each valuation `r` in (3) means a quotient factor `O/p^{ar}O`.  Thus the
squarefree block contributes, for `k=2,…,6`, respectively,

\[
 O/p^a,quad
 (O/p^a)^3\oplus O/p^{2a},\quad
 (O/p^a)^5\oplus(O/p^{2a})^7\oplus O/p^{3a},
\tag{4}
\]

and the analogous rows for `k=5,6`.  For `g` slots, the direct sum runs over
the `\binom{g}{2k}` choices of `S`, provided `2k\le g`.

The `k=2` row recovers the exact four-slot defect
`(Z/p^a)^{\binom g4}`.  The first genuinely new prediction is codimension
three: every six-slot set contributes

\[
 (\mathbf Z/p^a)^3\oplus \mathbf Z/p^{2a}.
\tag{5}
\]

This higher squarefree pattern is not a theorem located in the literature;
it is the explicit weighted Plücker/Specht Smith calculation recorded here.

## Filtration formulation

For a noncrossing matching `P` of `S`, expand its product as

\[
 v_P=\prod_{(i,j)\in P}(\delta_{ij}X_iX_j+p^aB_{ij})
     =\sum_{r=0}^k p^{ar}v_{P,r}.
\tag{6}
\]

The coefficient `v_{P,r}` lies in the exterior basis block having exactly
`2r` letters `Y`.  Let `F_{k,r}` be the matrix whose columns are the
`v_{P,r}` in that block, and define

\[
 \rho_{k,r}=\operatorname{rank}_{\kappa}
 \begin{bmatrix}F_{k,0}\\F_{k,1}\\\cdots\\F_{k,r}\end{bmatrix},
 \qquad \rho_{k,-1}=0.
\tag{7}
\]

The block-valued matrix in (6) is
`[F_{k,0};p^aF_{k,1};…;p^{ak}F_{k,k}]`.  DVR elimination along the increasing
`Y`-degree filtration gives Smith valuation multiplicity

\[
 e_{k,r}=\rho_{k,r}-\rho_{k,r-1}.
\tag{8}
\]

The computed cumulative ranks are

\[
\begin{array}{c|l}
k& (\rho_{k,0},\rho_{k,1},\ldots,\rho_{k,k})\\ \hline
1&(1,1)\\
2&(1,2,2)\\
3&(1,4,5,5)\\
4&(1,6,13,14,14)\\
5&(1,8,26,41,42,42)\\
6&(1,10,43,100,131,132,132).
\end{array}
\tag{9}
\]

Subtracting consecutive entries gives (3).  Formula (8), rather than a
closed formula for the numbers `e_{k,r}`, is the clean general structural
statement currently justified by the calculation.  The first and last
pieces are uniform: `e_{k,0}=1`, `e_{k,1}=2k-3`, and `e_{k,k-1}=1` for
`k\ge2`.  No simple Catalan/Narayana identification was assumed: the middle
rows in (3) do not equal the usual Narayana or area distributions.

## Why the quotient is exact on this block

The ordinary matching relation has coefficients `±1`, so DCEP straightening
allows all pairings to be replaced integrally by the noncrossing basis.  The
columns in (6) therefore generate exactly the divisor-product image on the
squarefree block, not merely a finite-index sublattice of it.

The saturation of their rational span inside the ambient exterior lattice is
the integral squarefree Hodge lattice: if `M` is the coefficient matrix, the
nonunit Smith factors of `M` are precisely the torsion factors of
`(\operatorname{Sat}(\operatorname{im}M))/\operatorname{im}M`.  Thus (8) gives
the quotient between the squarefree Hodge lattice and products.  No
analytic Hodge realization is used in this local statement beyond the
standard non-CM identification of rational Hodge tensors with diagonal
`SL_2` invariants.

The equal-depth graph assumption is essential for the simple exponent `ar`:
each off-diagonal divisor has the common `p^{2a}` scale, and each `B` term
then carries one extra `p^a`.  Unequal depths replace `p^a` in (1) by
pair-dependent weights and require a multigraded Smith calculation.

## Conditional all-degree consequence

Let `Q^k_sqfree(A_p)` be the squarefree part of the codimension-`k` Hodge /
divisor-product quotient.  The local calculation gives the exact conditional
decomposition

\[
 Q^k_{\mathrm{sqfree}}(A_p)
 \cong
 \bigoplus_{S\in\binom{[g]}{2k}}
 \bigoplus_{r=1}^{k-1}(\mathbf Z/p^{ar})^{e_{k,r}},
\tag{10}
\]

after descent from the split chart, with `e_{k,r}` from (8).  A full graded
theorem would follow if the repeated-slot invariant sectors are shown to be
product-saturated by the existing finite-etale divided-power/rank-one
argument.  Under precisely that additional hypothesis, (10) is the whole
codimension-`k` quotient.  Without it, (10) is a rigorous squarefree direct
summand/lower bound and should not be promoted to a whole-degree equality.

## Prior-art classification

The classical input is the unweighted Plücker/standard-monomial skeleton.
De Concini--Eisenbud--Procesi, *Hodge algebras* (Astérisque 91, 1982), §11,
Theorem 11.1, explicitly treats the Grassmannian minor algebra over `R=Z`
and supplies a free standard-monomial basis with integral straightening.
This controls the Catalan rank, the noncrossing basis, and the `±1` matching
relations.

It does **not** state the graph-lattice rescaling (1), the filtered ranks
(7)--(9), or the Smith factors `p^{ar}`.  Rational FFT likewise identifies
the invariant vector space only after tensoring with `Q`; it cannot see the
finite quotient.  The higher filtration is therefore best described as a
weighted Plücker-descent/Specht-lattice application of classical integral
straightening, not as a classical theorem already proving (10).

## Bounded computation and audit record

For `k≤6`, columns were generated from noncrossing matchings and expanded in
the exterior basis, with `t_i=i` and a test prime `p=101>2k`; row reduction
over `F_p` of the cumulative blocks gives (9), and DVR elimination gives (3).
All differences `t_j-t_i` are units in this test.  The calculation is an
audit/prediction, not a substitute for a proof that every distinct residue
configuration has the same rank profile.  A clean proof gate is to show that
the relevant maximal minors factor into products of Vandermonde differences;
the four-slot minor already has this form.

## Bottom line

The four-slot `p^a` defect is the first member of a nontrivial higher
filtered family.  The precise scalable statement is the squarefree
Specht/Plücker filtration (6)--(10).  It gives a uniform finite-etale target
for all codimensions while leaving one explicit theorem gate: prove the
rank-profile/Vandermonde lemma for arbitrary distinct etale parameters and
prove repeated-slot saturation before claiming the full graded quotient.
