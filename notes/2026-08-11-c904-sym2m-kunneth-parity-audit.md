# C904 full-Künneth parity audit for Sym²(Bl₀ Θ)

Date: 2026-08-11

Status: exact reduction; Paper V research only; no manuscript or Lean edit

## Verdict

Let \(A\) be the exotic principally polarized intermediate Jacobian, let

\[
 b:M=\operatorname {Bl}_0\Theta\longrightarrow\Theta\hookrightarrow A,
 \qquad
 \mu:\operatorname {Sym}^2M\longrightarrow A
\]

be the resolved-theta addition map, and let
\(q:M^2\to\operatorname {Sym}^2M\) be the quotient. For an integral
codimension-three algebraic class, the degree-six Künneth channels are

\[
 (0,6),\quad(1,5),\quad(2,4),\quad(3,3)
\]

and their swaps.

One proposed hidden odd channel closes exactly, while the other survives:

1. every \(H^3\otimes H^3\) transfer has **even** degree, because the
   relevant pairing contains \(\theta^2=2\theta^{[2]}\);
2. the \(H^1\otimes H^5\) channel has a genuine ten-dimensional dyadic
   cohomological/Hodge escape. Its ordered trace is even, but division by
   the degree-two symmetric quotient removes that factor. Algebraicity of
   an odd coset is open.

The product/Tate part of \(H^2\otimes H^4\) is also even (the familiar
Pascal coefficient is \({5\choose2}=10\)). An odd decomposable class in
this channel would already be an intrinsic algebraic surface on \(M\)
intersected with an integral divisor, hence an odd-theta-degree curve on
\(\Theta\).

There is exactly one caveat. For a general algebraic cycle on \(M^2\), its
mixed \((2,4)\) Künneth component need not split algebraically into a
divisor and a surface. Extracting those factors integrally requires a
two-local algebraic Künneth projector. The universal-sheaf diagonal
construction currently supplies only twice the needed codimension-three
projector. Thus the full result is:

\[
 \boxed{\begin{array}{l}
 \text{No odd }(3,3)\text{ escape exists.}\\
 \text{The live mixed channels are the dyadic }(1,5)\text{ coset}\\
 \text{and the two-local }(2,4)\text{ projector gate.}
 \end{array}}
\]

Thus the middle-Künneth route has two sharply isolated two-primary
possibilities. A \((2,4)\) odd part either algebraically extracts an odd
theta-supported curve or is precisely the unresolved integral-at-two
failure of that extraction. The \((1,5)\) quotient is different: it already
contains odd integral Hodge cosets, but no algebraic representative is
known. No odd mixed algebraic class is produced here.

## 1. Transfer-degree formula

Take torsion-free integral cohomology. Nakaoka's degree-six pullback lattice
for the symmetric square is generated by

\[
 \chi(\gamma)=\gamma\otimes1+1\otimes\gamma
\]

and by transfers

\[
 \tau(\alpha,\beta)
 =\alpha\otimes\beta+(-1)^{pq}\beta\otimes\alpha,
 \qquad p+q=6.
\]

There is no repeated-even-class generator in bidegree \((3,3)\), since
degree three is odd. The diagonal of \(M^2\) has codimension four and
cannot support a codimension-three correction.

For a transfer, the two ordered contributions agree up to the displayed
Koszul sign. Since \(q\) has degree two, its quotient addition degree is

\[
 \deg_\mu\tau(\alpha,\beta)
 =\pm\int_A b_*\alpha\smile b_*\beta.                 \tag{1}
\]

This can be read either by pulling back the class of a general point of
\(A\), or by intersecting the two pushed-forward cycles after inversion on
one factor. Formula (1) is integral and already includes the quotient
factor two.

For the end channels it says

\[
 \deg_\mu\chi(\gamma)
 =\int_A[\Theta]\smile b_*\gamma
 =\int_M b^*\theta\smile\gamma.                      \tag{2}
\]

Thus an algebraic \(H^0\otimes H^6\) component is exactly a curve on \(M\),
and its addition degree is its theta degree. A minimal curve has degree
five, which is why this channel can be odd.

## 2. The (3,3) channel is even before Hodge theory

Use the established integral identification

\[
 b^*:H^3(A,\mathbf Z)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^3(M,\mathbf Z).
\]

Write \(\alpha=b^*a\) and \(\beta=b^*c\). Projection formula gives

\[
 b_*\alpha=\theta a,\qquad b_*\beta=\theta c.
\]

Therefore (1) becomes

\[
 \pm\int_A\theta^2ac.
\]

In the integral exterior algebra of \(H^1(A,\mathbf Z)\),

\[
 \theta^2=2\theta^{[2]}.
\]

Every \((3,3)\) transfer consequently has even degree. This conclusion
does not assume that the Künneth factors are Hodge or algebraic separately.

## 3. The (1,5) channel: the quotient removes trace parity

The tempting proof that \(H^1\xrightarrow{\theta}H^3\) controls all of
\(H^5\) is false integrally at two: the corresponding exterior-lattice map
has a ten-dimensional mod-two primitive quotient. A first trace argument
also appeared to kill this quotient, but that argument missed the
degree-two symmetric quotient.

Poincaré duality identifies an integral tensor

\[
 z_{15}\in H^1(M,\mathbf Z)\otimes H^5(M,\mathbf Z)
\]

with an integral map

\[
 \varphi_z:H^3(M,\mathbf Z)\longrightarrow
             H^1(M,\mathbf Z)(-1).
\]

If \(z_{15}\) is a Hodge class, then \(\varphi_z\) is a Hodge morphism.
Let

\[
 L_\theta:H^1(M,\mathbf Z)\longrightarrow H^3(M,\mathbf Z)(1)
\]

be cup product with \(b^*\theta\). Put
\(T=\varphi_zL_\theta\). The ordered transfer pairing is

\[
                 \deg_{\mathrm {ord}}(z_{15})
          =\pm\operatorname {tr}(T).                  \tag{3}
\]

It is an integral Hodge endomorphism of \(H^1(M)\). On the generic non-CM
exotic family,

\[
 H^1(M,\mathbf Q)=W_5\otimes V,
\]

where \(V\) is the two-dimensional standard representation of the generic
Hodge group \(\mathrm {SL}_2\). Hence

\[
 T_{\mathbf Q}=A\otimes\operatorname {id}_V
\]

for some \(A\in\operatorname {End}_{\mathbf Q}(W_5)\), and

\[
 \operatorname {char}_T(x)=\operatorname {char}_A(x)^2.
\]

Because \(T\) preserves the integral lattice, its characteristic polynomial
is monic in \(\mathbf Z[x]\). A monic rational polynomial whose square lies
in \(\mathbf Z[x]\) lies itself in \(\mathbf Z[x]\), by Gauss's lemma.
Consequently the **ordered** trace is even:

\[
 \operatorname {tr}T=2\operatorname {tr}A\in2\mathbf Z.
\]

But \(q:M^2\to\operatorname {Sym}^2M\) has degree two. Therefore the
quotient degree is

\[
 \deg_\mu(z_{15})
 =\frac12\deg_{\mathrm {ord}}(z_{15})
 =\pm\operatorname {tr}A.                            \tag{4}
\]

It can be odd: the identity coefficient has
\(\operatorname {tr}A=\dim W_5=5\). Equivalently, the ten-dimensional
mod-two primitive quotient is a genuine cohomological/Hodge escape, not a
defect of the wedge-rank calculation.

What remains unknown is algebraicity. An algebraic cycle has a Hodge class,
but the converse for this dyadic coset is not supplied by the Hodge
decomposition or by the universal-sheaf diagonal. Thus (4) identifies an
exact new target:

> Determine whether the odd \((1,5)\) integral Hodge coset contains the
> Künneth component of an algebraic codimension-three cycle on
> \(\operatorname {Sym}^2M\).

## 4. What remains in (2,4)

For algebraic product factors \(D\in CH^1(M)\) and \(S\in CH^2(M)\),
formula (1) is the theta intersection

\[
                     \int_M b^*\theta\,D\,S.          \tag{5}
\]

The ambient/Tate lattice has even image; in the principal divided-power
normalization the coefficient is \({5\choose2}=10\). Exceptional factors
contracted by \(b\) have zero theta degree. If (5) were odd for an
intrinsic surface \(S\), the algebraic curve \(D\cdot S\) would already
have odd theta degree. Thus a decomposable odd \((2,4)\) class is not a
bypass of the primitive-theta problem; it solves it.

For a general algebraic class

\[
 Z\in CH^3(M\times M),
\]

however, algebraicity of \(Z\) does not imply algebraicity of each integral
Künneth component. A rational Chow--Künneth projector separates \((2,4)\),
but denominators cannot be discarded in this parity problem. The exact
universal-sheaf diagonal formula on \(M\times M\) yields the required
projector only after the codimension-three Chern-character factor \(2!\).
It therefore extracts twice the candidate curve and cannot decide its odd
degree.

This is the sharp residual statement:

> An odd non-product \((2,4)\) contribution is possible only through failure
> of integral-at-two algebraic Künneth extraction. Proving that extraction,
> or constructing such a mixed cycle and showing that it is not extractable,
> is the same surviving two-local gate already isolated in the resolved-theta
> diagonal audit.

## 5. Consequence for the Paper V crown

The search for an odd generic addition multisection may discard the full
\(H^3\otimes H^3\) space by divided-power parity of \(\theta^2\). It may
not discard primitive \(H^5\): the symmetric quotient turns its even
ordered trace into the possibly odd coefficient trace.

The only live paths are therefore:

1. an actual odd-theta-degree curve on \(M\) (the \((0,6)\) channel);
2. an algebraic representative of the odd dyadic \((1,5)\) Hodge coset;
3. an intrinsic \((2,4)\) algebraic correspondence whose oddness detects
   the failure of the integral two-local Künneth projector; or
4. a non-cohomological Chow descent mechanism invisible to this audit.

This note does not claim that an odd cohomological mixed class is algebraic,
and it does not promote the rational Künneth splitting to an integral Chow
splitting.

## Mystery ledger

- **Settled:** the full \((3,3)\) channel is even integrally.
- **Settled:** product/Tate \((2,4)\) oddness is already an odd theta curve,
  not a separate bypass.
- **Open:** the ten-dimensional dyadic \((1,5)\) quotient has odd integral
  Hodge cosets; whether any has an algebraic representative is unknown.
- **Open:** integral-at-two algebraic extraction of a general \((2,4)\)
  Künneth component. This is exactly the prior universal-diagonal
  factorial-two gate, not a new mystery.

Vibe check: the audit kills the \((3,3)\) escape but uncovers a real
\((1,5)\) Hodge-level route. The crown now has two precise algebraicity
gates rather than one falsely closed parity argument.
