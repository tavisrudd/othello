# C864 — generated-certificate portfolio audit across working roots

**Lane:** `build-sys` · **Date:** 2026-08-05

**Verdict: no stale certificate shadow survives in any paper mirror or backup working tree.  The
findings are all on the package side: the order-25 candidate differs from the monorepo authority in
every one of its generated files by a deliberate banner rewrite that has never been declared, the
two projective-cap candidates hold no payload at all, and three generated modules have already
propagated out of the monorepo into finitegeom and into a published paper export.**

## Finding: the order-25 candidate rewrites every banner, undeclared

Its `MANIFEST.json` seals 9,531 sources.  Audited against the current monorepo, 9,500 differ and 31
match.  The difference is not drift and not staleness in the usual sense — it is uniform and
deliberate:

```text
- # Generated C151 Q25 finite-certificate data
- DO NOT EDIT: generated by `notes/2026-07-15-c151-line-mask-generator.py`.
+ # Generated q=25 certificate Q25 line-mask data
+ DO NOT EDIT: generated by `scripts/line-mask-generator.py`.
```

Every generated file has had its task identifier removed from the heading and its generator repathed
from the internal notes directory to a package-local `scripts/` path.  That is the right
transformation: it is exactly what the referee-facing artifact rules require, and it is the repair
that the internal-reference problem in these banners needs.  Doing it at extraction time is also the
cheapest moment to do it.

What is missing is the declaration.  The acceptance criteria require every intentional difference
between an official source and the pre-deletion authority to be listed and justified, and a
transformation touching 9,500 files is the largest such difference in the portfolio.  Until it is
written down in the package's provenance, an audit cannot distinguish it from corruption — which is
precisely the position this audit was in before reading a diff.  The order-eleven package sets the
precedent to follow: its `PROVENANCE.md` names its one transformation, the corrected Dye pinpoint
citation, in a sentence.

Two smaller notes on the same package.  The rewritten headings read "Generated q=25 certificate Q25
line-mask data", where the substitution left the family name twice; worth a pass when the
transformation is declared.  And the candidate lacks 122 files that the monorepo's residual-cover
family has gained since the copy was taken, so it is behind as well as transformed.

## Finding: the two projective-cap candidates are empty shells

Neither `finitegeom-projective-cap-q11-certificates` nor `finitegeom-projective-cap-q13-certificates`
holds a single generated Lean source.  They exist as directories with package scaffolding, so a
reader of the package list would reasonably assume those extractions are underway; they are not
started.  The task card already treats them as candidates rather than authorities, which is correct,
and this audit confirms there is nothing in them to reconcile.

## Finding: unclassified generated modules have already left the monorepo

Three generated modules — the Clebsch arithmetic-gluing data, the split-quadratic pinching module,
and the four-copy contraction module under `RelativeConicArcs/AMELU/` — are byte-identical in the
monorepo, in the finitegeom repo, and in the golden quantum statistics export.  The golden copy is
not a shadow: that repository is a materialized base export and carries a base subset by design.
That is the point.  These are three of the four sources recorded as `resident-unclassified` in the
pending-family table, and because finitegeom carries them, every paper export built on finitegeom carries
them too.

The public shared-Lean contract says generated leaves belong only to their owning package, so
whatever these three turn out to be, finitegeom is currently the wrong place for them, and the cost of
leaving them unclassified is not three files but three files times every export.  This raises their
priority above what their count suggests; it does not decide their disposition, which needs a read of
what they actually contain.

## Tool limitation found while using it

`lean-package-source-audit.py` iterates the package's sealed sources, so it reports package files
that differ from the authority but cannot report authority files the package lacks — the 122 missing
order-25 files were found by the portfolio sweep's counts, not by the audit.  Before a package is
adopted as an authority, the audit needs the reverse direction as well.  Recorded here rather than
fixed, because the fix should land with the first cut that depends on it.
