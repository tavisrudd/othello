# 1. Target 4: a general quadratic-forgetting/cubic-recovery theorem

## 1.1 General algebraic theorem

Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic not \(2\), let \(R\) be a finitely generated integral \(k\)-algebra, and let

\[
\tau:R\longrightarrow R,\qquad \tau^2=1,
\]

be a nontrivial involution. Write

\[
A=R^\tau,\qquad
R^+=A,\qquad
R^-=\{r\in R:\tau(r)=-r\}.
\]

### Theorem 1 — generic one-bit recovery

Let \(C\in R^-\) be nonzero. Then:

1. \(\operatorname{Frac}(R)/\operatorname{Frac}(A)\) is a quadratic extension.
2. \(C^2\in A\).
3. On the principal open set \(D(C^2)\),
   \[
   R[(C^2)^{-1}]
   =
   A[(C^2)^{-1}]
   \oplus
   C\,A[(C^2)^{-1}].
   \]
4. Consequently the two generic points over the quotient are distinguished by
   \[
   C\longmapsto +C
   \quad\text{or}\quad
   C\longmapsto -C.
   \]

#### Proof

For a finite group acting on an integral domain,
\(\operatorname{Frac}(R^\tau)=\operatorname{Frac}(R)^\tau\): multiplying a
denominator through its finite orbit supplies an invariant denominator.
The fixed field of a nontrivial order-two automorphism has index two, so

\[
[\operatorname{Frac}(R):\operatorname{Frac}(A)]=2.
\]

Since \(\tau(C)=-C\), \(C^2\) is fixed. Every \(r\in R\) has the decomposition

\[
r=\frac{r+\tau r}{2}+\frac{r-\tau r}{2}
=r_++r_-.
\]

For \(r_-\in R^-\), the product \(Cr_-\) is fixed. Hence, after inverting \(C^2\),

\[
r_-=
C\,\frac{Cr_-}{C^2}
\in C\,A[(C^2)^{-1}].
\]

The reverse inclusion is immediate, and even and odd summands intersect trivially in characteristic not \(2\). This proves the decomposition. The involution fixes the first summand and negates the second, so the residual ambiguity is exactly the sign of \(C\). \(\square\)

This is the precise algebraic content behind “one orientation bit.” It does **not** say the odd module is globally principal. It says it becomes principal on any chart where a chosen odd observable does not vanish.

## 1.2 First-observable theorem

Assume \(R\) is graded and \(\tau\) preserves degree. If

\[
(R^-)_{d}=0\quad(d<m),\qquad
(R^-)_{m}=kC,
\]

then no invariant polynomial observable of degree below \(m\) distinguishes the two sheets, while every nonzero degree-\(m\) odd observable is a scalar multiple of \(C\).

For \(m=3\), this is a literal “quadratic forgetting, cubic recovery” theorem:

- degrees \(0,1,2\) contain no orientation observable;
- degree \(3\) contains exactly one, up to scale;
- on \(C\ne0\), its sign labels the two sheets.

## 1.3 Moment version

Let two weighted finite configurations \(X_+\) and \(X_-\) in a vector space \(V\) be exchanged by an involution. Define their moment differences

\[
\Delta M_d
=
\sum_{x\in X_+}w_x x^{\otimes d}
-
\sum_{x\in X_-}w_x x^{\otimes d}.
\]

### Theorem 2 — moment detection

If

\[
\Delta M_0=\Delta M_1=\Delta M_2=0,\qquad
\Delta M_3\ne0,
\]

then:

1. every polynomial statistic of degree at most two has the same weighted value on the two sheets;
2. some cubic statistic distinguishes them;
3. if the permitted symmetry group has a one-dimensional odd cubic subspace, the distinguishing cubic is unique up to scale.

#### Proof

Every degree-\(d\) polynomial is a contraction of its coefficient tensor with moments of degrees at most \(d\). Hence equality through degree two implies equality of every statistic of degree at most two. Since \(\Delta M_3\ne0\), duality of finite-dimensional vector spaces supplies a cubic coefficient tensor pairing nontrivially with it. One-dimensionality gives uniqueness. \(\square\)

This theorem is elementary, but it prevents an important overclaim: “first and second moments forget orientation” means **all polynomial tests of degree at most two** fail, not that every imaginable nonlinear algorithm fails.

## 1.4 Exact Clebsch specialization

For this specialization assume additionally
\(\operatorname{char}k\nmid60\), as in characteristic zero and
\(\mathbf F_{11}\). Let

\[
W=\{(y_1,\dots,y_5)\in k^5:\ e_1(y)=0\}
\]

with \(A_5\) acting by even coordinate permutations. Let an odd permutation \(s\in S_5\setminus A_5\) act through the sign-twisted outer involution

\[
\tau(y)=-P_s y.
\]

Set

\[
\Delta=\prod_{i<j}(y_i-y_j).
\]

The classical alternating-polynomial argument gives

\[
k[W]^{A_5}
=
k[e_2,e_3,e_4,e_5,\Delta]\big/
\left(\Delta^2-\operatorname{Disc}(e_2,e_3,e_4,e_5)\right).
\]

The involution acts by

\[
\tau(e_d)=(-1)^d e_d,\qquad
\tau(\Delta)=-\Delta.
\]

The second formula has two signs: the odd permutation negates the Vandermonde, while scalar multiplication by \(-1\) contributes \((-1)^{10}=+1\).

Therefore:

\[
(R^-)_{0}=(R^-)_{1}=(R^-)_{2}=0,
\qquad
(R^-)_{3}=k e_3.
\]

### Corollary 3 — Clebsch cubic recovery

On the chart \(e_3\ne0\),

\[
R[(e_3^2)^{-1}]
=
A[(e_3^2)^{-1}]
\oplus
e_3 A[(e_3^2)^{-1}],
\]

where \(A=R^\tau\). Thus the generic oriented Clebsch object is recovered from its unoriented quotient by one sign,

\[
e_3=\pm\sqrt{e_3^2}.
\]

If \(\operatorname{char}k\ne3\), then on \(e_1=0\)

\[
e_3=\frac13\sum_{i=1}^5 y_i^3.
\]

This is exactly Hitchin’s marked cubic \(\sigma_3\). Combined with

\[
J_0|_{V_I}=16\sigma_3^2,
\]

it says that Hitchin’s sextic is the orientation-forgetting square, while the marked cubic is the first recovering observable.

## 1.5 Novelty and significance

The invariant-ring ingredients are classical. Stanley explicitly records that alternating polynomials are the Vandermonde times symmetric polynomials and that the alternating-group invariant ring is a rank-two hypersurface over symmetric invariants. The likely-new part is the **specific sign-twisted Clebsch interpretation**, its localization statement, and its identification with the factorization-memory hierarchy.

This is a strong organizing theorem for Paper II/III, but not by itself an Annals-level invariant-theory theorem. It becomes more significant if stated as a reusable criterion and then demonstrated in several nonisomorphic carriers: conic factorization sheets, Hitchin incidence, the \(22\)-row design carrier, and the Hadamard carrier.

# 2. Target 5: the Hitchin–Mathieu–Golay torsor

## 2.1 Three two-valued objects

There are three relevant free \(C_2\)-torsors:

1. the reduced Hitchin fibre
   \[
   F_{\mathrm H}=\{t\in\mathbf F_{11}:t^2-t-1=0\}=\{4,8\};
   \]
2. the determinant torsor
   \[
   T_{11}=\mathrm{PGL}_2(11)/\mathrm{PSL}_2(11);
   \]
3. the parent torsor
   \[
   P_{\mathrm M}=\{H_+,H_-\},
   \]
   where \(H_\pm\cong M_{11}\) are the row and column parents inside the Hadamard/Witt \(M_{12}\).

The banked Clebsch–Hitchin calculation proves that the exchange \(4\leftrightarrow8\) is the nontrivial element of \(T_{11}\). The banked Hadamard result says row–column duality is the outer automorphism exchanging the two \(M_{11}\) parent classes.

## 2.2 The amalgam calculation

Assume the banked subgroup facts

\[
H_+\cap H_-\cong\mathrm{PSL}_2(11),
\qquad
H_\pm\cong M_{11}.
\]

The group orders are

\[
|\mathrm{PSL}_2(11)|=660,\qquad
|M_{11}|=7920,\qquad
|M_{12}|=95040.
\]

Then

\[
|H_+H_-|
=
\frac{|H_+||H_-|}{{|H_+\cap H_-|}}
=
\frac{7920^2}{660}
=95040
=|M_{12}|.
\]

Because \(H_+H_-\subseteq M_{12}\), it follows that

\[
\boxed{H_+H_-=M_{12}}
\]

and hence

\[
\boxed{\langle H_+,H_-\rangle=M_{12}}.
\]

This is a complete proof once the intersection theorem is supplied. No further generation computation is needed.

## 2.3 Marked coherence theorem

### Theorem 4 — Hitchin–Mathieu marked torsor

Under the banked geometric, code, design, and subgroup identifications:

1. \(F_{\mathrm H}\), \(T_{11}\), and \(P_{\mathrm M}\) are free \(C_2\)-torsors.
2. Their acting \(C_2\)'s have a unique group isomorphism, carrying the nontrivial exchange to the nontrivial exchange.
3. Between any two of the torsors there are exactly two equivariant bijections.
4. Choosing one marked sheet/parent determines a unique equivariant bijection among all three.
5. Without that marking, no individual sheet or parent is canonically selected.
6. The two parent stabilizers form the amalgam
   \[
   M_{11}\ \underset{\mathrm{PSL}_2(11)}{*}\ M_{11}
   \twoheadrightarrow M_{12},
   \]
   and the displayed quotient is generated by the two factors.

#### Proof

The first point is the stated input. A group of order two has a unique nonidentity element, so its abstract acting group is unique. If \(X,Y\) are free transitive \(C_2\)-sets, the image of one chosen \(x\in X\) determines an equivariant bijection \(X\to Y\); there are two possible images, hence two bijections. A marking selects one. The amalgam statement follows from the universal property of the free product with amalgamation and the order calculation above. \(\square\)

## 2.4 What this does—and does not—prove

It proves a coherent passage:

\[
\begin{array}{c}
\text{golden root }4\text{ or }8\\
\updownarrow\ \text{one marking}\\
\text{determinant/spinor coset in }T_{11}\\
\updownarrow\ \text{one marking}\\
\text{row or column }M_{11}\text{ parent}.
\end{array}
\]

It does **not** prove an unmarked canonical equality between those sets. Such an equality would contradict the torsor symmetry unless extra structure selected one element. The absence of a canonical selector is not a defect; it is precisely the “one forgotten bit” theorem.

It also separates two kinds of novelty:

- classical: \(M_{12}\), its two degree-\(12\) actions, Hadamard row–column duality, and the outer automorphism;
- plausibly new: identifying the particular reduced Hitchin fibre and the particular factorization-memory orientation carrier with the same marked torsor architecture.

The calculation script verifies the roots \(4,8\), all relevant orders, the product order, and the fact that two free \(C_2\)-torsors admit two—not one—unmarked equivariant bijections.

## 2.5 Best next theorem

The highest-value strengthening is not another abstract torsor statement. It is a **carrier map**:

\[
\text{rank-ten signed cubic tensor}
\longrightarrow
\text{ternary Golay/Hadamard carrier}
\]

whose restriction to the Clebsch four-space is nonzero. By one-dimensionality, that restriction must be proportional to \(e_3\). Such a map would connect the cubic recovery theorem to the \(M_{12}\) object without relying only on a shared two-valued quotient.

# 3. Target 7: continuation rigidity beyond four frames

## 3.1 What the existing graph already reconstructs

For a \(k\)-arc \(K\subset PG(2,q)\), let \(G_K\) be the continuation graph. The banked clique bounds recover:

1. every tangent trace, when
   \[
   q-\binom{k-1}{2}>k(k-2)+1;
   \]
2. the partition of tangent traces into their \(k\) centres, when
   \[
   q+2-k>k\binom{k-2}{2}.
   \]

Hence an abstract graph automorphism induces:

- a permutation of \(K\);
- for each \(p\in K\), a permutation of the tangent traces centred at \(p\);
- preservation of the incidence “continuation \(x\) lies on tangent trace \(px\).”

The remaining problem is to force those permutations to arise from one field automorphism and one projectivity.

## 3.2 Exact reduction to a punctured four-frame

Choose four centres \(F\subset K\). After a projective normalization, the full four-frame coordinates are

\[
x,\qquad y,\qquad \frac{x}{y},\qquad\frac{x-1}{y-1}.
\]

For a bare frame, the simultaneous isotopy equations force all coordinate permutations to be the same field automorphism. For general \(K\), two controlled kinds of puncture occur.

### Missing coordinate classes

At a selected centre there are \(q+1\) lines, \(k-1\) secants through the other selected points, and therefore

\[
q+2-k
\]

tangents to \(K\). A four-frame has \(q-2\) tangents at that centre. Thus precisely

\[
k-4
\]

coordinate classes are missing from each of the four families.

### Missing points

A point legal for the frame but illegal for \(K\) lies on a secant of \(K\) not already among the six frame secants. Since an arc has no three collinear points, these secants are distinct. Their number is

\[
D(k)=\binom{k}{2}-6.
\]

Thus the legal domain is obtained from the frame domain by deleting points on at most \(D(k)\) lines. In particular,

\[
|\Omega\setminus\Omega_K|
\le D(k)(q+1),
\]

so for fixed \(k\) the surviving density tends to \(1\) as \(q\to\infty\).

## 3.3 The reduced functional equations

After normalizing source and target frames, any graph isomorphism supplies partial bijections

\[
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta
\]

on co-large subsets of \(\mathbf F_q\), satisfying on every surviving pair:

\[
\gamma(x/y)=\alpha(x)/\beta(y),
\tag{M}
\]

and

\[
\delta\!\left(\frac{x-1}{y-1}\right)
=
\frac{\alpha(x)-1}{\beta(y)-1}.
\tag{S}
\]

Equation (M) is multiplicative isotopy. By itself it permits the non-Frobenius automorphisms of \(\mathbf F_q^\times\) already seen for triangles. Equation (S) is shifted multiplicative isotopy. Together, on the complete domain, they recover addition and force a field automorphism.

### Theorem 5 — exact reduction

For fixed \(k\), semilinear rigidity of \(G_K\) under the two banked clique inequalities follows from the following statement.

> **Punctured simultaneous-isotopy lemma.** There exists \(Q(k)\) such that, for \(q\ge Q(k)\), any four partial bijections satisfying (M) and (S) after deletion of at most \(k-4\) coordinate classes in each family and at most \(\binom{k}{2}-6\) projective lines extend uniquely to a common field automorphism followed by fractional-linear normalizations.

#### Proof of the reduction

The graph recovers tangent traces and their centre partition by the banked inequalities. Choose four centres and use their recovered traces as four coordinate partitions. Normalize the ordered source and target frames projectively. Each vertex has one label in each of the four partitions, giving the displayed four rational coordinates. An isomorphism induces one bijection of labels in each partition and therefore satisfies (M) and (S). The arc condition gives the exact missing-class and missing-line counts above. If the lemma holds, the induced partial coordinate maps extend semilinearly. The resulting collineation agrees with the graph isomorphism on all legal vertices; uniqueness follows from agreement on a projective frame and a field automorphism. Recovered centre incidence then sends all of \(K\) to the target arc. \(\square\)

This is real progress, but it is not the final theorem because the punctured lemma remains unproved.

## 3.4 A credible route to the missing lemma

The equations fail only on \(O(k^2q)\) of \(q^2\) pairs. Their error rate is therefore

\[
\varepsilon=O(k^2/q).
\]

There is a standard stability theory for approximate homomorphisms of finite groups: if the homomorphism test fails on few pairs, the function is close to an actual homomorphism. Applied in sequence:

1. (M) should force \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) to be close to scalar multiples of one multiplicative-group automorphism;
2. (S) should force that map also to be close to an additive automorphism;
3. a bijection that is both additive and multiplicative is a Frobenius field automorphism;
4. exact agreement on the dense surviving incidence structure should promote “close” to “equal,” because a wrong value corrupts an entire row or column containing \(q-O(k^2)\) surviving points.

This suggests an explicit polynomial threshold, plausibly \(Q(k)=O(k^4)\) with unoptimized constants. That bound is **not proved here**. The right next proof is to write a Pexiderized version of finite-group homomorphism stability for the triple \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\), then repeat it for the shifted equation.

## 3.5 Why this target is potentially the biggest

Closing the lemma would yield a general theorem of the form:

> For every fixed \(k\ge4\), all sufficiently large Desarguesian planes have semilinearly rigid continuation graphs for every \(k\)-arc.

That would turn the four-frame theorem from an isolated exact result into the base case of a general reconstruction principle. It would also be one of the cleanest examples where two individually weak algebraic operations—multiplication and shifted multiplication—jointly force the full field.

The literature located on embedded \(4\)-nets proves constant cross-ratio phenomena, but no theorem matching this dense punctured simultaneous-isotopy extension was found. Partial Latin-square symmetry literature is adjacent, not an obvious hit.

# 4. Target 8: an exact complete repair-port compiler

## 4.1 Delivered implementation

The compiler accepts:

- a generator matrix over a prime field;
- a repair radius;
- one or more target coordinates;
- helper survival probabilities;
- helper-query costs.

For every target it outputs:

1. every exact helper support of size at most the radius;
2. every normalized scalar recovery vector on that support;
3. the minimal support clutter;
4. exact matching and transversal numbers with witnesses;
5. homogeneous reliability coefficients;
6. heterogeneous success probability;
7. an exact optimal adaptive availability-query policy;
8. normalized dual words and the code reconstructed from their span;
9. verification flags and a certificate digest.

## 4.2 Core algebra

Let \(G\) be a \(k\times n\) generator matrix and \(x\) the failed coordinate. For a helper support \(S\), normalize a dual word by \(h_x=1\). Then

\[
Gh^{\mathsf T}=0
\]

becomes

\[
G_S h_S=-G_x.
\]

Every solution with all coordinates of \(h_S\) nonzero has exact support \(S\), and gives

\[
c_x=\sum_{j\in S}(-h_j)c_j.
\]

The compiler solves this affine system exactly over \(\mathbf F_p\) and enumerates its full nonzero fibre. This retains distinctions erased by the support hypergraph.

## 4.3 Clebsch certificate

For the supplied \([6,3,4]_{11}\) Clebsch generator

\[
G=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&1&1&1&0&1\\
10&9&4&8&1&1\\
0&1&7&5&4&7
\end{pmatrix},
\]

the compiler verifies at **each of the six targets**:

- ten minimal repairs;
- every repair has three helpers;
- the support clutter is all \(\binom53=10\) triples;
- every coefficient fibre has one normalized vector;
- \(\nu=1\);
- \(\tau=3\);
- the normalized repair words span a three-dimensional dual;
- the nullspace of that span recovers the original code.

For target \(0\), the homogeneous reliability counts are

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
\text{alive helpers }j&0&1&2&3&4&5\\ \hline
\text{successful states}&0&0&0&10&5&1\\
\text{failed states}&1&5&10&0&0&0.
\end{array}
\]

Thus, with homogeneous helper survival \(s\),

\[
R(s)=10s^3(1-s)^2+5s^4(1-s)+s^5.
\]

For the heterogeneous probabilities in the example input, target \(0\) has success probability

\[
0.9939102642.
\]

The exact adaptive policy has \(141\) decision states, queries helper \(3\) first, and has expected availability-query cost

\[
2.8389887370000007
\]

under the supplied costs and probabilities.

## 4.4 What “optimal policy” means here

The policy minimizes expected helper-query cost until either:

- a full repair support is certified alive, or
- every repair support is hit by a certified-dead helper.

It does not yet optimize:

- subpacketized repair bandwidth;
- helper transmission size;
- parallel latency;
- correlated failures;
- sequential multi-erasure repair;
- service-region load balancing.

Those are extensions, not silently included claims.

## 4.5 Complexity and intended use

The exact compiler is exponential in the bounded inner length:

- helper subsets are enumerated;
- affine coefficient fibres may be enumerated;
- reliability uses \(2^{n-1}\) availability states;
- adaptive policy uses disjoint alive/dead masks.

That is appropriate for the paper’s bounded repair seeds and concatenation theorem: compile a small port once, certify it, and transport it to a positive-density asymptotic family. It is not yet a scalable compiler for thousand-coordinate codes.

## 4.6 Novelty

The ingredients are individually established:

- repair sets correspond to matroid circuits;
- multiple repair alternatives improve availability;
- reliability and hypergraph optimization are standard algorithmic objects.

What appears less standard is the **single exact pipeline** that preserves all three layers simultaneously:

\[
\text{support clutter}
+\text{normalized coefficient fibres}
+\text{availability/cost policy},
\]

and then emits a reconstruction certificate proving that one pointed coefficient port recovers the inner code.

This should be presented as research software accompanying the repair-port paper, not as a claim that Gaussian elimination, hypergraph matching, or dynamic programming is new.

# 5. Recommended execution order

## First: publishable theorem integration

Insert Theorem 1 and Corollary 3 into the factorization-memory/orientation paper. They give a clean algebraic spine:

\[
\text{quotient by }\tau
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{quadratic generic extension}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{first odd generator }e_3.
\]

The moment theorem can then be stated as the observable-level companion.

## Second: state the Mathieu bridge carefully

Use “marked torsor coherence,” not “canonical identification.” Include the one-line order calculation proving that the two \(M_{11}\) parents generate \(M_{12}\). The lack of a canonical unmarked bijection should be advertised as the exact one-bit obstruction.

## Third: attack the punctured isotopy lemma

The proof program should have four lemmas:

1. convert partial isotopy to an approximate multiplicative homomorphism;
2. convert shifted isotopy to approximate additivity;
3. obtain a nearby Frobenius automorphism;
4. upgrade nearby agreement to exact agreement using row/column density.

A finite search for \(k=5\) and moderate \(q\) would be useful, but it does not replace this proof.

## Fourth: harden the compiler

The next implementation steps are:

1. extension fields \(\mathbf F_{p^m}\);
2. coefficient-fibre compression without enumeration;
3. correlated-failure models;
4. policy export as a compact decision diagram;
5. concatenation-port composition and a machine-checkable transfer certificate.

That last step would make the transfer theorem operational: a global code family could ship with a small local proof object rather than only parameter claims.

# 6. Literature audit

## 6.1 What was searched

Searches covered:

- alternating-group invariant rings, anti-invariants, discriminants, and separating invariants;
- Hitchin’s two-icosahedron incidence cover and vector-bundle model;
- \(M_{12}\), its two \(M_{11}\) actions, Hadamard row–column duality, and outer automorphism;

The searches were theorem/abstract/full-text level for the sources used in proofs. Broader result lists were used only to identify candidate sources.

## 6.2 Audit conclusions

- **Target 4:** classical invariant theory contains the ring structure. The Clebsch sign-twist, cubic localization, and “one-bit” packaging appear to be the new contribution.
- **Target 5:** the finite group and Hadamard ingredients are classical. The Hitchin-to-\(T_{11}\)-to-parent-torsor coherence was not found as a prior statement.
- **Target 7:** embedded \(4\)-net cross-ratio theory is relevant, but no dense punctured simultaneous-isotopy extension theorem matching the needed statement was found.
- **Target 8:** circuit/locality and multiple alternatives are classical. No exact compiler with coefficient fibres, reliability, adaptive policy, and code-reconstruction certificates was located in the searched sources.

These are targeted-search conclusions, not definitive novelty certificates.

## 6.3 Potential hits or unresolved literature risks

1. The finite \(4\)-net and partial Latin-square literature may contain a completion theorem under terminology not captured by “punctured isotopy.”
2. Classical \(M_{12}\) subgroup-lattice sources may already record the precise \(M_{11}\cap M_{11}=\mathrm{PSL}_2(11)\) configuration. That would not erase the Hitchin bridge, but it affects how the amalgam should be credited.
3. Distributed-storage simulators may implement pieces of the repair compiler without expressing them as a complete coefficient port.
