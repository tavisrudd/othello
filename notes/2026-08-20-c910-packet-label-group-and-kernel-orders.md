# C910 — the packet-preserving label group and the primary kernel orders

**Lane:** `cubic-threefolds` · **Date:** 2026-08-20

Two open items of the previous pass are closed. The first is the question of
whether the three displayed label permutations exhaust the permutations of the
six labels that preserve the packet of diagonally stable halves — the
"normalizer question" the odd-label report left open. They do, and the group
they generate is the full normalizer of the alternating image. The second is the
pair of orders `2⁴` and `3⁴` of the primary parts of the lattice model of the
isogeny kernel.

## What is now proved

### The packet-preserving label group

Every permutation of the six labels acts on characteristic-two functions of the
labels by precomposition with its inverse; that action preserves the
augmentation hyperplane and the constants, so it descends to the four-dimensional
coefficient heart. `GraphLattices/SixPointLabelHeartMatrix.lean` attaches to each
permutation the matrix of the descended action, defined by its effect on the
normalized representatives of the four coordinate vectors, proves that this
matrix implements the action on every vector of the augmentation hyperplane, and
proves that the assignment is unital and multiplicative. Every such matrix is
therefore invertible, with the matrix of the inverse permutation as its inverse.
The three displayed generators return the three displayed matrices, a word in
them returns the word's matrix, and each of the five transpositions of adjacent
labels returns one displayed matrix.

`GraphLattices/SixPointPacketLabelGroup.lean` then determines the group.

*The criterion.* A label permutation preserves the packet — the vertical copy
and the graphs of `0`, `1`, `W`, `W+1` — exactly when conjugation by its heart
matrix carries `W` to `W` or to `W+1`. The forward direction reads the image of
the graph of `W`: it is the graph of the conjugate, which is not the vertical
copy and, being a packet member, is the graph of one of the four commutant
elements; the conjugate satisfies the same relation `X·X = X+1`, which excludes
`0` and `1`. The reverse direction is a case check on the five members.

*The centralizer.* The permutations whose heart matrix centralizes `W` form a
group of order sixty. This is the orbit-stabilizer theorem applied to the
conjugation action on heart matrices: the orbit of `W` is the displayed family of
twelve matrices. The upper bound comes from closure — conjugation by each of the
five transpositions of adjacent labels permutes the twelve, by a displayed table
of permutations of the index set, and those transpositions generate the whole
symmetric group on the labels — and the lower bound from reaching all twelve from
`W` along that table. Since the symmetric group on six labels has order seven
hundred twenty, the stabilizer has order sixty.

*The centralizer is the alternating image.* The image of the alternating group on
five letters acting on its six Sylow-five subgroups consists of the words in the
displayed translation and involution, and the heart matrix of such a word
centralizes `W` because each generator matrix does. That image already has order
sixty, so the two groups coincide.

*The conclusion.* The packet-preserving group contains the centralizer and the
scaling permutation, which conjugates `W` to `W+1`. Conversely a packet-preserving
permutation either centralizes `W`, hence lies in the alternating image, or is the
scaling permutation times such an element. So the packet-preserving group is
exactly the group generated by the three displayed label permutations. Its order
is one hundred twenty, by orbit-stabilizer again, this time for the subgroup
acting on the two-element orbit of `W`. Finally it equals the normalizer of the
alternating image: a packet-preserving permutation conjugates the centralizer into
itself, and conversely a permutation normalizing the alternating image makes the
conjugate of `W` commute with the two displayed generator matrices, so the
commutant classification and the quadratic relation place that conjugate in
`{W, W+1}`.

### The orders of the primary parts of the kernel

`GraphLattices/SixAxisPrimaryKernelOrders.lean` proves that an internal direct sum
of two submodules has order the product of the two orders, by exhibiting the
addition map from the product to the join as a bijection, and applies it to the
already proved splitting of the lattice model of the isogeny kernel into its two-
and three-primary parts. Each part sits inside the corresponding primary part of
the discriminant group, of order `2⁸` or `3⁸`, so its order divides a power of
that prime; the product of the two orders is `6⁴`, which forces `2⁴` and `3⁴`.
The argument uses no isotropy statement, so it is independent of the maximal
isotropy proved earlier, and it uses no Cauchy or `p`-group input beyond
Lagrange's theorem.

Reviewer terminal `relativeSixAxis_primaryKernelOrders`, on
`lem:relative-six-axis`.

## What the statement is, and what it is not

The group determined here is a group of permutations of six abstract labels
acting on an explicit four-dimensional characteristic-two module. That it is the
full normalizer of the alternating image is a statement about that action. It is
not an identification with the manuscript's normalizer of the six geometrically
constructed dihedral axes, because the six labels are still not identified with
those axes, and it does not construct any geometric Galois action. So the marking
of the actual geometric kernel remains supplied, exactly as before; what has
changed is that the label-side alternative is now closed rather than open.

The orders `2⁴` and `3⁴` are orders of the primary parts of a lattice model built
from explicit integral matrices. No abelian scheme, isogeny, torsion group
scheme, Weil pairing, or geometric commutator pairing is constructed anywhere in
this package, so the identification of these orders with the orders of the
primary parts of the kernel of a relative isogeny is still supplied.

## Mystery ledger

- **Settled: the displayed three-generator group is the whole story on the label
  side.** It is simultaneously the packet-preserving group, the normalizer of the
  alternating image, and a group of order one hundred twenty. The previous pass
  could only say that it was contained in the normalizer and that the packet
  action factors through the sign character. The consequence for marking is
  sharper than the containment: the entire packet action of the label group is a
  sign, so no label-side datum other than a parity can select within the packet.
  Whatever supplies the marking geometrically must have a two-valued shadow on the
  six axes; the natural candidate is the square class of the discriminant of the
  degree-six field over which the axes are defined, that is, whether the Galois
  group of the axes lands in the alternating group. That candidate is neither
  proved nor assumed anywhere.
- **Settled: the per-prime kernel orders.** They follow from the splitting and
  Lagrange's theorem alone. Together with the fact that each primary part of the
  discriminant group is annihilated by its prime, this pins the isomorphism types
  as elementary abelian: the two-primary kernel is four copies of the group of
  order two and the three-primary kernel four copies of the group of order three.
  The isomorphism types themselves are not formalized; only the orders are.
- **Surprise worth recording: the centralizer computation has a combinatorial
  explanation that the package does not yet use.** The fifteen nonzero heart
  vectors are exactly the fifteen pairs of labels, and the orbits of `W` on them
  are exactly the five perfect matchings of the one-factorization already
  displayed in the alternating-action module. So centralizing `W` is the same as
  preserving that one-factorization, which is why the centralizer is the
  alternating image and why the packet-preserving group is its normalizer. The
  present proof reaches the same orders through a twelve-element conjugation orbit
  and never mentions the matchings. Logged on the discovery track.
- **Open, unchanged: the geometric side.** No relative isogeny, torsion group
  scheme, Weil pairing, geometric Galois action, or identification of the six
  labels with the manuscript's dihedral axes is constructed, so the marking of the
  actual kernel and the geometric commutator pairing remain supplied.
