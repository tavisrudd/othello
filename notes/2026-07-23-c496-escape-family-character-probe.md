# C80 — does the C496 quadratic character generalize to the escape family?

**Lane:** `cap` (C80 spine). **Date:** 2026-07-23. Frozen C68b on-conic bucket census is a
read-only, hashed input.

**Verdict:** `NO_CHARACTER_LAW.` C496's `χ` does **not** generalize. At the escape layer — where
the conic scaffold actually lives — no fixed quadratic character of the natural 6-arc invariants
(including the full-PGL cross-ratio multiset, C520's discriminant object) predicts the P/N label at
the orders where P/N mix (q=11 and q=17). The character governed value only at C496's bespoke q=11
edge; the escape-family value law is group-theoretic (A5 smallest-orbit), not character-static.

## Why this is the right test

The recursion-stability probe ([companion](2026-07-23-c496-recursion-stability-probe.md)) showed the
C496 factorization `value = 1_live ⊗ χ(u)` is a depth-0 base fact whose scaffold (5-arc → conic →
edge → `u`) exists **only at the size-4 escape layer** (six general points are not on a conic). So the
only place a `χ` value-law could recur is **across the escape family** (recursion in `q`, not depth).

Each on-conic S4ARENA bucket is a PGL-canonical size-4 residual position `t4 = (t1,t2,t3,t4)` (four
conic parameters; the burned directions `∞,0` are the marked opening pair) with an **exact** game
value P/N. The relevant symmetry is `Stab({∞,0})` (generated by `z↦az`, `z↦1/z`). The probe tests
whether the label is a fixed quadratic character of a canonical invariant, uniformly over
`q∈{11,13,17,19}`.

## Characters tested (all `Stab({∞,0})`- or full-PGL-invariant)

- `k_squares_folded` — `#{t_i square rel to 0}`, folded `k↔4−k` (the global-flip symmetry `z↦(ns)·z`);
- `χ(∏ t_i)`, `χ(∏_{i<j}(t_i−t_j))` (the `t4` discriminant), `χ` of the 6-arc finite discriminant,
  `χ(∏t_i · disc)`;
- `cr_chi_multiset` — the sorted `χ` of the 3 essential cross-ratios of `t4` (full-PGL invariant;
  this is C520's tt#1 resolvent-discriminant square-class object).

Only q=11 and q=17 (the depleted orders) have both labels, so they are the only discriminating tests.

q=17 (the sharpest): `cr_chi_multiset` gives `(-1,-1,1)→{N:3,P:5}`, `(1,1,1)→{N:2}` — mixed. The
non-depleted q=13/19 are all-P, so their apparent "separation" is vacuous and excluded.
