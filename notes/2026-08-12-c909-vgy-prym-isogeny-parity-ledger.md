## Verdict on the requested degree

Van Geemen--Yamauchi, Proposition 3.2, states only that their explicit
elliptic curve \(E''_{a,b}\) is **isogenous** to the order-five elliptic factor
\(E_{a,b}\) of \(J(X_{a,b})\). The proposition does not state the degree or
the induced map on two-torsion. However, the quotient diagram in their
Proposition 3.1 gives a canonical Prym pullback whose degree is forced to be
odd. More precisely, the natural map has degree \(1\) or \(5\); no even
factor can occur. If the independently computed Roulleau axis polarization
\(\deg(\Theta|_E)=5\) is identified with this order-five factor, the natural
pullback has degree \(1\), while its norm has degree \(25\). The source paper
itself does not make that stronger identification.

## Roulleau: exact geometric input

Cached source: Xavier Roulleau, *Genus 2 curve configurations on Fano
surfaces*, arXiv:1002.4467v1, SHA-256
c66706bfa8977656043a8c068d9f2cabc7e72dc0f53eac3fab680ac82172c7bd.

Theorem 11(D) says that a Fano surface \(S\) of type \(D_5\) has five smooth
genus-two curves \(D_1,\ldots,D_5\), with \(D_iD_j=1\) for \(i\ne j\), and that
\[
  D_1+\cdots+D_5
\]
is a fibre of a fibration \(\gamma:S\to E\) onto an elliptic curve. The proof
says the \(D_5\) assertion is obtained by the same Albanese argument as
Lemma 17: the tangent spaces generated by the five curves have a
four-dimensional span in \(H^0(\Omega_S)^*\), hence the Albanese quotient is
one-dimensional. The usual meaning of “fibration” supplies connected fibres;
if this is used in a proof, state the Stein-factorization argument explicitly.

Let \(a:S\hookrightarrow \operatorname{Alb}(S)=J\) be the Albanese embedding.
After choosing the origin of \(E\) at the fibre in Theorem 11(D), the
Albanese universal property gives a homomorphism
\[
  q:J\longrightarrow E,\qquad \gamma=q\circ a,
\]
with connected kernel. Its Rosati dual \(i=q^\dagger:E\to J\) is the
elliptic axis. The divisor identity on \(S\) is
\[
  q^*[0]|_S=D_1+\cdots+D_5.
\]

Roulleau does **not** print \(q\,q^\dagger=[5]\) or the induced polarization
degree. The exact \(d=5\) axis pairing is a later deduction from the six
Roulleau axes, the minimal class of the Fano surface, and the Rosati mixed
intersection identity; it should not be attributed directly to Theorem 11.

## van Geemen--Yamauchi: exact Prym and quotient statements

Cached source: Bert van Geemen--Takuya Yamauchi, *On intermediate Jacobians
of cubic threefolds admitting an automorphism of order five*,
arXiv:1506.05346v3, SHA-256
f263d78728391fc9c1ff836293a484e5caec66b3178ecab3aa1d54b14855baed.

For the standard \(D_5\)-model \(X_{a,b}\), Section 2 constructs the invariant
line \(l\), the genus-six discriminant quintic \(H_{a,b}\), and its genus-eleven
étale double cover \(\mathcal H_{a,b}\). Proposition 2.1 states
\[
 J(X_{a,b})\cong
 \operatorname{Prym}(\mathcal H_{a,b}/H_{a,b})
\]
as principally polarized abelian varieties, with function-field equation
\[
  w^2=1-\frac{xz}{y^2}.
\]

The order-five quotient diagram in Section 3.2/Proposition 3.1 is
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
 \mathcal H_{a,b}&\xrightarrow{5:1}&
 \overline{\mathcal H}_{a,b}=\mathcal H_{a,b}/\widetilde\alpha_5
   &\longrightarrow E'_{a,b},\\
 \downarrow 2&&\downarrow 2\\
 H_{a,b}&\xrightarrow{5:1}&\overline H_{a,b}=H_{a,b}/\alpha_5.
\end{array}
\]
The quotient curves have genera \(3\) and \(2\), respectively, so the right
vertical double cover is étale and its Prym is elliptic. Their explicit
equations are
\[
 \overline{\mathcal H}_{a,b}: r^2=f_4(1-w^2),\qquad
 \overline H_{a,b}:s^2=(t-1)f_4(t),
\]
and the elliptic Prym is represented by
\[
 C_{a,b}: (s')^2=f_4(1-w),
\]
with the printed cubic Weierstrass model \(E''_{a,b}\) in equation (3.2).
The paper explicitly says in equation (3.2) that \(E''_{a,b}\) is this Prym.
Proposition 3.2 then says only
\[
 E''_{a,b}\ \text{is isogenous to}\ E_{a,b},
\]
where \(E_{a,b}\) is the elliptic factor obtained from the order-five action.
The proof identifies the tangent eigenspace but gives no degree, kernel, or
mod-two map.

## Derived odd-degree lemma

Let
\[
 P=\operatorname{Prym}(\mathcal H/H)=J(X),\qquad
 P_0=\operatorname{Prym}(\overline{\mathcal H}/\overline H)=E''.
\]
The degree-five quotient of the two curves induces pullback and norm maps
\[
 \phi=q^*:P_0\longrightarrow P,\qquad
 q_*:P\longrightarrow P_0,\qquad q_*q^*=[5].
\]
Because \(q\) commutes with the involution and the quotient Prym is the
order-five invariant Prym line, the image of \(\phi\) is contained in the
connected elliptic factor \(E\subset P\), and \(\phi\) is an isogeny onto
that factor. The degree assertion follows from polarization scaling:

* for a degree-\(5\) map of curves, \(q^*\Theta_{\mathcal H}=5\Theta_{\overline{\mathcal H}}\);
* for an étale double cover, the Jacobian theta restricts to twice the
  principal Prym polarization;
* after restriction,
  \[
    \phi^*(\Xi_P)=5\Xi_{P_0}.
  \]

Write \(d=\deg(\Xi_P|_E)\), and let \(n=\deg(\phi:P_0\to E)\). Since
\(\Xi_{P_0}\) is principal on the elliptic Prym,
\[
  n\,d=5.
\]
Thus
\[
  (d,n)=(1,5)\quad\text{or}\quad(5,1).
\]
In particular, the canonical Prym-to-factor isogeny is odd. Its norm dual
has degree \(25/n\), also odd. This is a derived Prym-polarization lemma,
not a statement printed in van Geemen--Yamauchi.

The independent six-axis calculation gives \(d=5\) for the Roulleau elliptic
axis (Rosati diagonal \(5\), off-axis \(-1\)). If one proves that this
Roulleau axis is the same primitive order-five factor as the
van Geemen--Yamauchi \(E_{a,b}\), then \(n=1\): \(E''_{a,b}\to E_{a,b}\) is
an isomorphism onto the factor, while \(q_*\) is multiplication by \(5\) in
that identification. Until that geometric identification is printed, the
source-safe claim is only \(n\in\{1,5\}\), hence odd.

## Transfer to two-torsion and the \(r^2=T\) cover

Any odd-degree isogeny \(\phi:E''\to E\) induces an isomorphism
\[
  E''[2]\xrightarrow{\sim}E[2]
\]
of finite étale symplectic local systems: the odd-order kernel meets \(E''[2]\)
trivially, and both sides have rank four. Therefore the projective nonzero
two-torsion packets and the quadratic sign/discriminant covers agree.

The exact C904 specialization of the printed \(E''_{a,b}\) model to the
\(A_5\) cubic line gives the quadratic twist class
\[
 D(T)=(T+27)(T-729/5)
\]
relative to the Tate model
\[
 E_T:y^2+(T+27)xy+(T+27)^2y=x^3,\qquad
 j(E_T)=\frac{(T+27)(T+3)^3}{T}.
\]
Quadratic twisting is by \([-1]\), which acts trivially on 2-torsion, so
\(E''[2]\cong E_T[2]\). Combining with the odd Prym-to-factor isogeny gives
\[
  E[2]\cong E_T[2],
\]
and hence the exotic sign cover is the Tate discriminant resolvent
\[
  r^2=T.
\]
This transfer uses only oddness, not an integral identification of the full
elliptic curves or the fivefold polarization kernel.

## Source boundary / safe wording

Safe:

> Van Geemen--Yamauchi prove an isogeny between the explicit elliptic Prym
> \(E''_{a,b}\) and the order-five elliptic factor. The quotient diagram and
> Prym polarization imply that the natural map has degree \(1\) or \(5\), hence
> odd; therefore it identifies their 2-torsion local systems. The stronger
> degree-one statement follows after matching the factor with the independently
> computed Roulleau axis polarization \(d=5\).

Do not write that van Geemen--Yamauchi themselves prove degree \(5\), degree
\(1\), or a mod-two isomorphism. Their printed Proposition 3.2 says only
“isogenous.”
