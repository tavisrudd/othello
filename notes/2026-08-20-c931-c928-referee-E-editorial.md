# C931 referee E — whole-paper editorial ceiling

## 1. Verdict

**B — minor revision.** The manuscript presents one coherent and apparently publishable theorem package: the abstract splitting is deliberately subordinated to the simultaneous integral placement under \((b_*,e^*)\), and the intersection-cohomology and dual escape statements read as consequences of that placement rather than unrelated add-ons. I found no editorial evidence that a headline is false or substantially pre-empted. Two local changes are required before the priority and integral-intersection-cohomology boundaries are as transparent as the rest of the paper.

## 2. Claim map

- **Theorem 1.1 (printed pp. 2 and 6):** the rank-130 group \(H^3(M,\mathbf Z)\) is described not merely by a split exact sequence but by the fibre-product congruence (3), whose mod-two class is supplied geometrically by the Fano correspondence.
- **Theorem 1.2 (printed pp. 2 and 7):** duality turns the same mod-two glue into a free rank-ten degree-five quotient, with the exceptional Gysin lattice equal to twice that quotient.
- **Corollary 1.3 (printed pp. 2, 7–8):** in degree three, the intersection cohomology of the singular theta divisor is naturally the cohomology of its resolution and inherits the exact sequence and integral lattice of Theorem 1.1.

## 3. Major findings

1. **Coherence and headline.** **Location:** abstract and Introduction, printed pp. 1–3, especially Theorem 1.1 and equations (1)–(3); completion on printed p. 6. **Assertion checked:** whether the paper has one theorem rather than three computations, and whether “integral glue” is the right headline. **Verification:** yes. Section 2 isolates the topological extension, Section 3 identifies the only arithmetic defect, and Section 4 supplies exactly the ten geometric cosets needed to turn those inputs into (3). Sections 5 and 6 are recognizably dual and intrinsic consequences. The text expressly prevents the two most damaging misreadings: equation (1) splits abstractly (printed p. 2), and no surjectivity of the full degree-six Fano transfer is claimed (printed p. 3). **Severity:** none; this is a positive verification. **Smallest adequate remedy:** none.

2. **Priority boundary against Krämer and Faulkner Valiente–Miller Eismeier.** **Location:** Introduction, printed p. 3; end of Section 3, printed p. 5; Section 6, equations (18) and following paragraph, printed pp. 7–8. **Assertion checked:** whether the manuscript separates its contribution from the closest rational and integral predecessors. **Verification/failure:** the Krämer boundary is precise and correct: Krämer, Corollary 6 (source PDF p. 5), works with complex coefficients and obtains the decomposition-theorem skyscraper correction, not the integral fibre-product coset. The FVME boundary is directionally correct but too imprecise. FVME, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 2.10 (printed pp. 2 and 9; source PDF pp. 2 and 10), give a symplectic integral Lefschetz filtration and explicit associated-graded wedge factors. In the \(g=5\), \(\bigwedge^3\to\bigwedge^5\) specialization, the relevant factors are already visibly \(1\) on the primitive-degree-three layer and \(2\) on the primitive-degree-one layer. The manuscript's real novelty is therefore not the bare appearance of \(1^{110},2^{10}\), but the elementary block realization with explicit divided-power representatives and, above all, their geometric realization as the simultaneous lattice glue. Calling FVME only the “nearest general algebraic framework” without an exact theorem-level pointer makes that division of credit less visible than it should be. **Severity:** B/local priority clarification; no theorem or proof mechanism changes. **Smallest adequate remedy:** at the end of Section 3, cite FVME Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 2.10 explicitly and say that their filtration yields the same associated-graded factors after specialization, while the present block proof selects concrete representatives used by Section 4.

3. **Visibility of hypotheses, naturality, and limitations.** **Location:** opening of the Introduction, printed p. 1; Theorem 3.1, printed pp. 4–5; equations (3), (8), and (13)–(14), printed pp. 2, 4, and 6; limitation paragraphs on printed pp. 2–3 and 8. **Assertion checked:** whether these can be recovered without development history. **Verification:** yes, with one useful nuance. “Smooth complex cubic threefold” and the principal polarization are stated immediately; the mod-two fibre-product class is independent of the global sign used to identify the cylinder map, while Theorem 3.1 records symplectic naturality. The split-versus-glue distinction, the ten-endpoint-only Fano claim, and the degree-three-only integral decomposition claim are all conspicuous. **Severity:** none. **Smallest adequate remedy:** optional only—one sentence after (3) could observe explicitly that the congruence class is independent of the global sign because the quotient is mod two.

4. **Compression at the integral intersection-cohomology endpoint.** **Location:** Section 6, printed p. 7, especially equation (17) and the two sentences preceding it. **Assertion checked:** whether a central transition is adequately exposed in an eight-page paper. **Verification debt/failure:** the one-paragraph passage from the integral middle-perversity Deligne sheaf to \(IH^3(\Theta,\mathbf Z)\cong H^3(U,\mathbf Z)\) is too compressed for the corollary's prominence. The allowed E source pack provides only descriptive metadata for Maxim and Friedman, not the exact full-text convention needed to check this integral truncation statement. I therefore do not convert this into a mathematical objection, but it remains an explicit verification debt: the missing source is a theorem-level integral Deligne-sheaf/truncation reference (for example the precise applicable statement in Maxim or Friedman). **Severity:** B/local exposition and sourcing; potentially higher only if the intended convention fails on independent mathematical review. **Smallest adequate remedy:** name the intersection-homology convention, give an exact theorem/section citation, and add two sentences explaining why an isolated singularity in complex dimension four places ordinary degree three below the truncation threshold and why the isomorphism is natural.

5. **Editorial significance and compression as a whole.** **Location:** entire eight-page manuscript, with the proof architecture summarized on printed pp. 2–3. **Assertion checked:** whether brevity has reduced the paper to a special-case calculation. **Verification:** no. The complete-graph Smith calculation alone would not clear a broad significance threshold, but its exact geometric use in the cubic-threefold theta divisor and its intrinsic \(IH^3\) consequence give the paper a conceptual spine. The proofs are terse but unusually well routed; apart from Finding 4 and the quick dualization around equations (15)–(16) on printed p. 7, they remain readable to the intended specialist. **Severity:** none for the default venue; a ceiling limitation for the stretch venue. **Smallest adequate remedy:** for Proceedings, add the local clarifications above and one displayed or verbal exact-dual sequence before (16). Elevation to the stretch venue would require a broader conceptual theorem or a more substantial geometric generalization, not additional prose alone.

## 5. Literature boundary

- **Krämer:** Corollary 6 and its proof establish the complex-coefficient decomposition-theorem correction for the blow-up and the resulting Euler characteristic. They do not determine an integral extension, saturation quotient, divided-power coset, or simultaneous \((b_*,e^*)\)-placement. The manuscript's account on printed pp. 3 and 7–8 is accurate.
- **Faulkner Valiente–Miller Eismeier:** Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 2.10 establish a general symplectically equivariant integral Lefschetz filtration with explicit associated-graded wedge/contraction factors. This substantially anticipates the algebraic shape of the specialization \(L:\bigwedge^3\Lambda\to\bigwedge^5\Lambda\), and should be cited at that precision. It does not supply the cubic-threefold link calculation, the Fano endpoint lifts, the fibre-product placement, the escape lattice, or the identification with \(IH^3(\Theta,\mathbf Z)\).
- **Expertise conditioning:** the public Collège de France profile identifies Claire Voisin's chair as algebraic geometry. I used that only to set a high whole-paper algebraic-geometric significance ceiling; this report neither imitates her style nor attributes any judgment to her.

## 7. Publication recommendation

- **Proceedings of the AMS (default): minor revision, then publish.** The manuscript fits the short-article format and contains a new, memorable integral statement with enough geometric reach to be more than a Smith-form exercise. Acceptance should require the exact FVME boundary and the integral intersection-cohomology citation/explanation; neither repair changes the headline or proof mechanism.
- **Algebraic Geometry (stretch): do not recommend in its present scope.** The result is elegant and credible, but the paper remains a sharply focused computation for one exceptional theta divisor, while the integral exterior-Lefschetz algebra already sits in a recent general framework. The genuinely new geometric glue is valuable, yet on the present eight-page scope it does not convincingly reach the journal's “first-class/high originality” ceiling. A broader theorem explaining when such geometric glue exists, or a substantial family beyond the cubic-threefold case, would be needed; this is a venue-level ceiling judgment, not a D verdict on correctness or priority.
