# The local endomorphism algebra of a stabilizer AME state

**Date:** 2026-08-28  
**Status:** proved research note, with one exact finite-field witness recorded below  
**Scope:** no manuscript edits

## 1. Main result

Let $k=\mathbf F_q$ and, for the physical application in this note, first
assume that $q$ is prime.  Let

\[
 L\subseteq \bigoplus_{i=1}^{2m}P_i,\qquad P_i=k^2,
\]

be the Lagrangian label space of a stabilizer
$\operatorname{AME}(2m,q)$ state.  Define its **local endomorphism
algebra** by

\[
 \operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{loc}}(L)
 =\left\{(T_i)_i\in\prod_i\operatorname{End}_k(P_i):
       \left(\bigoplus_iT_i\right)L\subseteq L\right\}.
\]

This is an intrinsic finite-dimensional $k$-algebra.  It is the object
behind the fundamental-holonomy calculation in the paper.

### Theorem A (intrinsic commutant theorem)

Fix a half-set $B\sqcup B^c$, write
$L=\operatorname{graph}(K_B)$, and denote the $2\times2$ blocks of
$K_B$ by $G_{ij}$, $i\in B$, $j\in B^c$.  Fix $b\in B$ and
$c\in B^c$, and put

\[
 Q_{ij}=G_{bj}G_{ij}^{-1}G_{ic}G_{bc}^{-1}
 \quad(i\ne b, j\ne c).
\]

Evaluation at $b$ is an algebra isomorphism

\[
 \operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{loc}}(L)
 \xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
 E_B:=\operatorname{Cent}_{M_2(k)}\{Q_{ij}:i\ne b, j\ne c\}.
\]

Under this isomorphism, the fixed-party symplectic automorphism group is

\[
 \operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{tr}}(L)
 \cong E_B^1:=\{A\in E_B:\det A=1\}.
\]

In particular, $E_B^1$ consists of units automatically.  Changing
the half-set, base party, or spanning tree changes the displayed copy of
$E_B$ only by the corresponding evaluation isomorphism; the abstract
algebra $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{loc}}(L)$ is chart independent.

#### Proof

A block-diagonal endomorphism $T=(A_i)_{i\in B}\oplus(D_j)_{j\in B^c}$
preserves the graph of $K_B$ exactly when

\[
 A_iG_{ij}=G_{ij}D_j\qquad(i\in B, j\in B^c). \tag{1}
\]

All blocks $G_{ij}$ are invertible by the AME half-set criterion.  Once
$A_b=A$ is fixed, the base row and base column of (1) force

\[
 D_j=G_{bj}^{-1}AG_{bj},\qquad
 A_i=G_{ic}D_cG_{ic}^{-1}. \tag{2}
\]

The remaining equations are equivalent to

\[
 AQ_{ij}=Q_{ij}A.
\]

Thus evaluation at $b$ is bijective onto $E_B$.  Formulas (2) show
that it respects addition and multiplication, so it is an algebra
isomorphism.

If $A\in E_B$ has determinant one, every matrix propagated by (2) also
has determinant one.  In dimension two,
$\operatorname{Sp}_2(k)=\operatorname{SL}_2(k)$, so the resulting family
is locally symplectic.  Conversely every locally symplectic family
evaluates to an element of determinant one.  This proves the group
statement.

The definition of $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{loc}}(L)$ makes chart
independence immediate.  More concretely, evaluation at any party is
injective: transition intertwiners propagate a zero component to every
party.  Evaluation images at two parties are conjugate by any invertible
operator-pushing transition joining them.  A different spanning tree
merely replaces one generating family of closed-path transports by
another and leaves their common centralizer unchanged.  ∎

### Corollary B (the five cases are algebra types)

Exactly one of the following occurs:

| condition on the holonomies | $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{loc}}(L)$ | $\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{tr}}(L)$ | order |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| all scalar | $M_2(k)$ | $\operatorname{SL}_2(q)$ | $q(q^2-1)$ |
| two do not commute | $k$ | center | $d=\gcd(2,q-1)$ |
| commuting, split semisimple nonscalar | $k\times k$ | split norm-one torus | $q-1$ |
| commuting, irreducible nonscalar | $\mathbf F_{q^2}$ | field norm-one torus | $q+1$ |
| commuting, nonsemisimple nonscalar | $k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ | determinant-one dual-number units | $qd$ |

#### Proof

If every holonomy is scalar, its common centralizer is $M_2(k)$.  If a
nonscalar $Q$ occurs, its centralizer is the quadratic algebra $k[Q]$.
If another holonomy fails to commute with $Q$, their common centralizer
is scalar: otherwise a common nonscalar centralizer element $X$ would
force both matrices into the commutative algebra $k[X]$.  If all
holonomies commute, every one lies in $k[Q]$, so their common centralizer
is $k[Q]$.

The three possible quadratic algebras generated by a nonscalar
$2\times2$ matrix are $k\times k$, $\mathbf F_{q^2}$, and the dual
numbers.  The determinant restricts respectively to product, field norm,
and

\[
 \det(aI+bN)=a^2\qquad(N^2=0,\ N\ne0).
\]

Their determinant-one unit groups therefore have orders $q-1$, $q+1$,
and $q\gcd(2,q-1)$.  The scalar determinant-one group has order $d$.
This includes characteristic two without alteration.  ∎

This proves that the existing five-row group classification is the unit
group shadow of a sharper algebra classification.  No finite-subgroup
classification is needed.

## 2. A new low-party theorem

The commutant formulation exposes an extra constraint that is invisible in
the five-case statement.

### Theorem C (forced quadratic algebra for four and six parties)

For a prime-dimensional stabilizer $\operatorname{AME}(2m,q)$ state
with $m=2$ or $m=3$, all fundamental holonomies commute.  Equivalently,

\[
 \dim_k\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{loc}}(L)\ge2.
\]

Thus the scalar-algebra/center-only line of Corollary B cannot occur for
four or six parties.  For $m=3$, the possibilities are precisely the
full matrix algebra, split quadratic algebra, quadratic field, and dual
numbers.

For every prime $q\ge5$, this implies that every stabilizer
$\operatorname{AME}(4,q)$ or $\operatorname{AME}(6,q)$ state has a
noncentral fixed-party transversal symplectic symmetry.

#### Proof

For $m=2$ there is only one fundamental holonomy.

Let $m=3$.  The AME condition makes all nine blocks $G_{ij}$
invertible.  Apply invertible block changes on the left and right to put
the $3\times3$ block matrix into tree-normalized form

\[
 N=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 I&I&I\\
 I&A&B\\
 I&C&D
 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{3}
\]

These changes need not be symplectic, but in dimension two every
invertible change carries the alternating form to a nonzero scalar
multiple of itself.  Hence there are nonzero weights $r_i,s_j\in k$
such that

\[
 N^{\mathsf T}\operatorname{diag}(r_0J,r_1J,r_2J)N
 =-\operatorname{diag}(s_0J,s_1J,s_2J). \tag{4}
\]

The inverse anti-isometry relation gives nonzero weights $t_j$ for
which distinct block rows are orthogonal:

\[
 \sum_{j=0}^2 t_jN_{ij}J N_{kj}^{\mathsf T}=0
 \qquad(i\ne k). \tag{5}
\]

Taking $(i,k)=(0,1)$ in (5) and using the first row of (3) gives

\[
 t_0I+t_1A^{\mathsf T}+t_2B^{\mathsf T}=0.
\]

Thus $B\in\operatorname{span}_k\{I,A\}$.  Similarly,
$D\in\operatorname{span}_k\{I,C\}$.  The orthogonality of block columns
zero and one in (4) gives

\[
 r_0I+r_1A+r_2C=0,
\]

so $C\in\operatorname{span}_k\{I,A\}$, and therefore also
$D\in\operatorname{span}_k\{I,A\}$.  All four non-tree blocks lie in
the same commutative quadratic algebra $k[A]$.  In tree normalization
the fundamental holonomies are their inverses (up to the common base
conjugation), so they commute.

For $q\ge5$, the smallest determinant-one group attached to a quadratic
algebra has order $q-1>\gcd(2,q-1)$, proving the last assertion.  The
small fields retain the algebra statement, although group orders can
coincide: for example the split norm-one group at $q=3$ is just the
center.  ∎

### Importance

This is not merely a proof compression.  It says that six-party AME
stabilizer states are forced onto the exceptional-symmetry locus.  The
“generic state has only central symmetry” heuristic begins, if at all, at
eight parties.  Any genericity statement in the earlier literature memo
must therefore exclude $m\le3$.

## 3. The hidden MDS structures

The algebra $E=\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{loc}}(L)$ acts faithfully on
every local plane $P_i$, and every operator-pushing transition is
$E$-linear after transporting this action between parties.  Projection
onto any half-set is an $E$-linear isomorphism.  This gives the following
standard interpretations.

### Theorem D (exceptional symmetry is extra code linearity)

Assume $q$ is prime.

1. If $E$ contains a split quadratic algebra $k\times k$, then after
   choosing its two local eigendirections,
   
   \[
   L=L_X\oplus L_Z,
   \]
   
   where $L_X$ and $L_Z$ are classical $[2m,m,m+1]_q$ MDS codes on
   complementary local axes.  Lagrangian isotropy makes them weighted
   duals.  This is exactly a locally framed MDS–CSS structure.

2. If $E\cong\mathbf F_{q^2}$, then each $P_i$ is a one-dimensional
   $E$-space and $L$ is an $E$-linear
   $[2m,m,m+1]_{q^2}$ MDS code.  Its trace-symplectic self-duality is
   equivalent to weighted Hermitian self-duality.

3. If $E\cong k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$, then each $P_i$ is the regular
   rank-one module over the dual numbers and $L$ is a free rank-$m$
   code of length $2m$ over that ring.  Every half-set projection is an
   isomorphism.  Thus the Jordan stratum is a nonsemisimple, ring-linear
   analogue of an MDS code, not an accidental centralizer case.

4. If $E=M_2(k)$, then, after local changes of basis,
   
   \[
   L=k^2\otimes C
   \]
   
   for a classical $[2m,m,m+1]_q$ MDS code $C$.  The local alternating
   forms supply nonzero coordinate weights with respect to which $C$ is
   self-dual.  This is the maximally symmetric MDS–CSS case.

#### Proof

For any half-set $S$, the AME projection $L\to\bigoplus_{i\in S}P_i$
commutes with $E$ and is an isomorphism.  Consequently the $E$-module
structure of $L$ is the direct sum of $m$ copies of the local module.

For $E=k\times k$, the two primitive idempotents split every local plane
into two lines and split $L$ into their global images.  Half-set
bijectivity restricts to each summand, so each is an
$[2m,m,m+1]_q$ MDS code.  The ambient alternating form pairs the two
axis systems; isotropy and dimension identify the two codes as weighted
duals.

For $E=\mathbf F_{q^2}$, every faithful two-dimensional $k$-module is
the regular one-dimensional $E$-module.  Hence $L\cong E^m$, and every
half projection is $E$-linear and bijective, which is the MDS property.
After local $E$-coordinates are chosen, every nondegenerate alternating
$k$-form on $E$ has the form

\[
 (x,y)\longmapsto
 \operatorname{Tr}_{E/k}(\kappa_i x y^q),
 \qquad \kappa_i^q=-\kappa_i\ne0.
\]

For $x,y\in L$, isotropy applied to $ax,y$ for every $a\in E$ and
nondegeneracy of the trace pairing imply

\[
 \sum_i\kappa_i x_i y_i^q=0.
\]

All trace-zero $\kappa_i$ differ by factors in $k^\times$, giving a
weighted Hermitian self-duality relation.

The dual-number statement follows in the same way from the fact that its
faithful two-dimensional module is regular; the half projection exhibits
$L$ as free of rank $m$.  No stronger self-duality terminology over
the ring is needed for this conclusion.

Finally, the unique simple $M_2(k)$-module is $k^2$, and every
submodule of a direct sum of copies is $k^2\otimes C$ for a multiplicity
space $C$.  Dimension gives $\dim C=m$, and half-set bijectivity gives
the MDS property.  In compatible local module coordinates, each local
alternating form is $w_iJ$ for some $w_i\ne0$.  Isotropy of
$k^2\otimes C$ is therefore

\[
 \sum_iw_i c_id_i=0\qquad(c,d\in C),
\]

so $C$ is self-dual for the weighted coordinate pairing.  ∎

### Consequence for the relationship between the two papers

The prime-field MDS–CSS torus/full-$\mathrm{SL}_2$ dichotomy is not merely analogous
to two rows of the AME symmetry table.  It is the split-algebra versus
full-matrix-algebra part of the intrinsic endomorphism classification.
The nonsplit field and dual-number cases are the two additional forms of
rank-two linearity available to a general stabilizer AME state.

This is the strongest framing upgrade found in the hour.

## 4. Where generic central symmetry can begin

Let $X_m$ be the variety of anti-symplectic isomorphisms between two
direct sums of $m$ symplectic planes.  It is a torsor for
$\operatorname{Sp}_{2m}$, hence geometrically irreducible.  The AME
block-superregular locus $U_m\subset X_m$ is Zariski open.  On $U_m$,
the condition

\[
 [Q_{ij},Q_{rs}]=0\quad\text{for every pair of fundamental cycles}
\]

is closed after clearing the block-determinant denominators.  Therefore:

### Proposition E (conditional generic-center theorem)

On any nonempty irreducible AME family containing one point with two
noncommuting fundamental holonomies, the locus
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{loc}}(L)=k$ is nonempty Zariski open and
dense.  Equivalently, central fixed-party symmetry is generic on that
family.

Theorem C shows that the hypothesis is impossible for $m\le3$.

### Exact eight-party witness

For $m=4$ over $\mathbf F_7$, take $J_4=\operatorname{diag}(J,J,J,J)$
and

\[
K=\begin{pmatrix}
2&3&1&0&2&0&1&5\\
0&2&1&1&1&3&2&5\\
1&1&3&0&6&4&1&5\\
5&0&2&5&2&2&4&5\\
3&4&4&3&1&6&5&4\\
2&2&5&4&2&3&2&2\\
3&6&3&2&3&2&5&2\\
4&4&3&3&2&3&0&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Exact arithmetic gives $K^{\mathsf T}J_4K=-J_4$.  Every party-aligned
square block minor is nonzero.  For block sizes (1,2,3), the determinant
value multiplicities are respectively

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrrr}
&1&2&3&4&5&6\\\hline
r=1&3&4&2&2&3&2\\
r=2&4&10&6&8&4&4\\
r=3&3&4&2&2&3&2.
\end{array}
\]

With zero-based base row and column $0$, two holonomies are

\[
 Q_{11}=\begin{pmatrix}6&4\\2&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Q_{12}=\begin{pmatrix}6&2\\3&2\end{pmatrix},
\]

and

\[
 [Q_{11},Q_{12}]
 =\begin{pmatrix}1&6\\3&6\end{pmatrix}\ne0.
\]

Thus the generic-center phenomenon is genuinely present by eight parties;
it is not merely allowed by the abstract five-case classification.

## 5. Extension-field boundary

Theorem A uses a special two-dimensional fact.  For $q=p$, conjugating a
base frame through an arbitrary invertible transition preserves
determinant one, and $\operatorname{Sp}_2=\operatorname{SL}_2$.  For
$q=p^e$ treated additively over $\mathbf F_p$, local spaces have
dimension $2e$, and $\operatorname{Sp}_{2e}(\mathbf F_p)$ is not normal
in $\operatorname{GL}_{2e}(\mathbf F_p)$.

The intrinsic algebra $\operatorname{End}_{\mathrm{loc}}(L)$ and its
holonomy-centralizer description still exist.  However, the transversal
symmetry group is not in general just the symplectic units of one evaluated
copy.  It is

\[
 E^\times\cap\bigcap_i M_i^{-1}
 \operatorname{Sp}(P_i)M_i,
\]

where the $M_i$ transport the base evaluation to party $i$.  Equivalently,
it is the unit group of $E$ preserving the full transported family of
alternating forms.  Any extension-field theorem must retain these multiple
form constraints and then add Frobenius-semilinear equivalences separately.

This corrects the overly compressed suggestion that the higher-$e$ group
is simply “the symplectic commutant.”

## 6. Recommended disposition

Theorem A and Corollary B are safe structural replacements for the current
last paragraph of the five-case proof.  Theorem C is a new mathematical
result and materially changes the six-party story.  Theorem D is the main
framing win and should be red-teamed carefully, especially its CSS and
Hermitian terminology, before manuscript integration.

The best next checks are:

1. prove the converse directions in Theorem D with the exact local-Clifford
   and coordinate-weight conventions used by both papers;
2. decide whether the dual-number stratum has a standard self-dual code-over-
   rings formulation or should remain module-theoretic;
3. classify which of the four non-scalar algebra types actually occur for
   each six-party field;
4. formalize Theorem C, whose proof is short and purely linear algebra;
5. test whether the $m=4$ witness can be replaced by a uniform family.

No new hypothesis has been inserted into the all-length theorem.  The only
specialization is explicit: Theorems A–D use prime local dimension when
identifying local symplectic maps with determinant-one $2\times2$ matrices.
