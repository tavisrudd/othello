## 3. Major findings

1. **Integral link calculation verified.** Location: Lemma 2.1, printed p. 3.
   For the circle bundle \(K=S(\mathcal O_X(-1))\), take Euler class \(-h\) and
   use
   \[
   H^q(X,\mathbf Z)=\mathbf Z,0,\mathbf Zh,\mathbf Z^{10},
   \mathbf Z\ell,0,\mathbf Z
   \quad(0\le q\le6),
   \]
   with \(1\mapsto h\), \(h\mapsto3\ell\), and \(\ell\mapsto[\mathrm{pt}]\).
   The Gysin fragments give
   \[
   H^1(K)=H^2(K)=0,
   \qquad H^3(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^3(K),
   \]
   and
   \[
   0\longrightarrow\operatorname{coker}(\mathbf Z
   \xrightarrow{\times3}\mathbf Z)\longrightarrow H^4(K)
   \longrightarrow H^3(X)\longrightarrow0.
   \]
   Since \(H^3(X)=\mathbf Z^{10}\) is free, the last sequence splits as
   groups, giving \(H^4(K)=\mathbf Z^{10}\oplus\mathbf Z/3\). Severity:
   verified, no defect. Remedy: none.

2. **The pair/Mayer--Vietoris mechanism is unchanged and correct over
   \(\mathbf Z\).** Location: Proposition 2.2, displayed (6), printed pp. 3--4.
   Excision at the cone point gives
   \(H^q(\Theta,U;\mathbf Z)=\widetilde H^{q-1}(K;\mathbf Z)\). Thus
   \(H^2(K)=0\) yields
   \[
   0\to H^3(\Theta)\to H^3(U)\to H^3(K).
   \]
   For \(M=U\cup N(X)\), \(H^2(K)=0\) and the isomorphism
   \(H^3(X)\to H^3(K)\) reduce Mayer--Vietoris to a graph: projection induces
   \(H^3(M)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^3(U)\), and under it
   \(\ker e^*=\ker(H^3(U)\to H^3(K))=\operatorname{im}H^3(\Theta)\).
   Composing singular weak Lefschetz with pullback identifies this image with
   \(b^*H^3(J)=b^*\bigwedge^3\Lambda\). Local topology does not prove
   surjectivity of \(e^*\); the manuscript explicitly postpones exactly that
   step to Proposition 4.3. Severity: verified, no defect. Remedy: none.

3. **Both Goresky--MacPherson citations support the uses made of them.**
   Location: after Lemma 2.1, printed p. 3, and Section 6, printed p. 7.

   - In *Stratified Morse Theory*, Part II, Section 1.2, printed p. 153, the
     finite-map Lefschetz theorem gives the required homotopy range. To make
     the application explicit, choose \(m\) such that \(|m\Theta|\) embeds
     \(J\), and take the hyperplane defined by the section \(s^m\), where
     \(s=0\) defines \(\Theta\). Its inverse image has support \(\Theta\); the
     map is proper and finite, so the theorem permits this nongeneric
     hyperplane. With \(\dim_{\mathbf C}J=5\), codimension \(c=1\), and only
     the \(k=0\) locus nonempty, its displayed bound is
     \(5-0-\min(5,0)-1=4\). Hence inclusion is an isomorphism on homotopy
     below degree four (and a surjection in degree four), giving
     \(H^3(J,\mathbf Z)\simeq H^3(\Theta,\mathbf Z)\). Thus the manuscript's
     citation to Part II, Section 1.2 is exact enough and valid integrally.
   - In *Intersection Homology II*, Section 3.1 defines the shifted Deligne
     complex by starting with \(\mathbf Z_U[8]\) and truncating at
     \(\bar m(8)-8\); Section/Theorem 3.5 characterizes that construction and,
     in the PL setting, identifies it with intersection chains. Here
     \[
     \bar m(8)=\left\lfloor\frac{8-2}{2}\right\rfloor=3,
     \qquad
     \tau_{\le-5}Rj_*\mathbf Z_U[8]
       =\bigl(\tau_{\le3}Rj_*\mathbf Z_U\bigr)[8].
     \]
     Therefore the manuscript's unshifted
     \(P_{\bar m}=\tau_{\le3}Rj_*\mathbf Z_U\) is exactly the GM construction,
     not a perverse-normalized complex mislabeled as unshifted. The coefficient
     ring \(\mathbf Z\) satisfies GM's stated regular-Noetherian,
     finite-Krull-dimension hypothesis. Severity: verified, no defect. Remedy:
     none.

4. **Natural map and degree-four boundary verified.** Location: Section 6,
   displayed (17) and the following paragraph, printed pp. 7--8. The standard
   truncation triangle shows
   \[
   \mathbb H^i\!\left(\Theta,\tau_{\le3}Rj_*\mathbf Z_U\right)
   \xrightarrow{\sim}H^i(U,\mathbf Z)\quad(i\le3).
   \]
   The adjunction map \(\mathbf Z_\Theta\to Rj_*\mathbf Z_U\) factors uniquely
   through \(\tau_{\le3}\), so in degree three the ordinary-to-intersection
   map is precisely restriction \(H^3(\Theta)\to H^3(U)\). At the singular
   stalk, \(R^qj_*\mathbf Z_U\) is \(H^q(K,\mathbf Z)\); truncation retains
   \(q\le3\) and removes all of \(H^4(K)\), including its \(\mathbf Z/3\).
   Since the sole singular stratum has even real codimension eight, lower and
   upper middle perversities agree there. No torsion-perversity ambiguity can
   change degree three. Severity: verified, no defect. Remedy: none.
