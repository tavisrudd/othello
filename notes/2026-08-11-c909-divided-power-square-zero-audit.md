# C909 hostile audit: square-zero decompositions and all divided powers

Date: 2026-08-11  
Scope: proposed TT/EJ upgrade from square-zero divisor decompositions; no
manuscript, PDF, mirror, Lean, or commit change

## Verdict: GO with minor theorem repairs

The formal divided-power lemma is correct in a commutative graded ring.
Suppose, after a finite faithfully flat unramified base change (R\to S),

\[
 \theta=\sum_{i=1}^{m} a_iD_i,
 \qquad D_i^2=0,
\]

where the (D_i) are integral divisor classes and (a_i\in S).  Then

\[
 \frac{\theta^k}{k!}
   =\sum_{|I|=k}\left(\prod_{i\in I}a_i\right)
                    \prod_{i\in I}D_i.
\]

Every term with a repeated index vanishes, and the (k!) orderings of each
set (I) cancel exactly.  This identity is taken in rational cohomology;
the right side is integral.  It works at (p=2), with negative coefficients,
and with arbitrary integral/unit coefficients in (S).  No positivity or
division by (2) is involved.  For (k>m) it gives zero.

Thus the proposed statement genuinely implies all intermediate ordinary
divisor-product classes, not only the top cofactor (k=g-1), provided the
descent hypotheses below are included.

## 1. The (p=2) and sign checks

Over any commutative ring, including a dyadic unramified ring, symmetric
rank-one matrices span the symmetric matrices integrally:

\[
 E_{ii}=e_ie_i^{\mathsf t},
 \qquad
 E_{ij}+E_{ji}
  =(e_i+e_j)(e_i+e_j)^{\mathsf t}-E_{ii}-E_{jj}.
\]

The second identity is the required signed combination; no factor (1/2)
appears.  Therefore a full (p^a\operatorname{Sym}) graph block is spanned
over (\mathbf Z_2) by (p^a) rank-one coefficient forms exactly as over
odd (p).  Negative coefficients are harmless: the resulting classes are
integral elements of (\operatorname{NS}), though they need not be effective
divisors.  A statement requiring a positive sum of effective square-zero
divisors would be false or at least unsupported; the theorem must say
“integral linear combination.”

For a rank-one coefficient form (vv^{\mathsf t}) on an elliptic power, the
associated divisor is pulled back from an elliptic quotient and has square
zero in cohomology.  If its descended class (D_v) on the graph quotient
pulls back to (p^a vv^{\mathsf t}), then

\[
 f^*(D_v^2)=(p^a vv^{\mathsf t})^2=0.
\]

Pullback by an isogeny is injective on rational cohomology, and integral
cohomology of a complex torus is torsion-free; hence (D_v^2=0) in integral
cohomology.  This is the missing short bridge from “rank-one form” to
“square-zero divisor class” in the finite-etale block application.

## 2. Faithfully flat descent is a real hypothesis

Let (H_R) be the chosen integral cohomology/Hodge lattice and

\[
 P_R^k=\operatorname{im}
 \bigl(\operatorname{Sym}^k\operatorname{NS}_R\to H_R^{2k}\bigr).
\]

The descent implication is valid in the following precise form:

\[
 P_S^k=P_R^k\otimes_R S,
 \qquad \theta^{[k]}\otimes1\in P_S^k
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \theta^{[k]}\in P_R^k,
\]

because (S/R) is faithfully flat and

\[
 (H_R^{2k}/P_R^k)\otimes_RS
 \simeq H_S^{2k}/P_S^k.
\]

The equality (P_S^k=P_R^k\otimes S), not merely the inclusion, is
load-bearing.  If base change creates new divisor classes and (P_S^k) is
the larger image generated by them, faithful flatness cannot descend
membership to (P_R^k).  Accordingly, “square-zero classes after base
change” must mean classes in the prescribed base-changed coefficient/NS
lattice, or the graph proof must separately establish this product-lattice
base-change equality.

The same argument also descends integrality of `theta^k/k!`: once its
base change equals an element of the integral (P_S^k), faithful flatness
detects membership in the integral (H_R^{2k}).  To pass from local (R=\mathbf
Z_p) statements to a global lattice, membership must be checked at every
prime (or prime-to-(p) equality must be stated); finite generation gives
the intersection of the local lattices.

## 3. Application to finite-etale graph blocks

The finite-etale block theorem supplies, after unramified splitting,

\[
 p^a\bigoplus_\lambda\operatorname{Sym}(L_\lambda^*)
\]

inside the local graph NS lattice.  If the unit block is present, its full
coefficient symmetric lattice must be included as well.  Express the source
polarization coefficient (p^aB) (and the unit blocks) by the signed integral
rank-one identities above.  Each summand is a descended divisor class whose
source pullback has rank one, hence is square-zero in cohomology.  The
polarization itself is therefore an integral linear combination of square-zero
classes over the splitting ring.

Under the exact product-lattice base-change equality in Section 2, the formal
lemma gives

\[
 \frac{\Theta^k}{k!}\in P^k
 \qquad 0\leq k\leq g-1,
\]

and in fact for every (k) (the class is zero once the number of summands is
exhausted).  This is a genuine strengthening of the existing cofactor theorem,
which only constructs the (k=g-1) class by mixed adjugates.  It should be
promoted as a separate “square-zero polarization decomposition” corollary,
not silently folded into the cofactor proof.

The finite-etale theorem alone currently states full block-supported NS forms
and the top mixed-cofactor identity; it does not yet spell out the square-zero
pullback and the exact equality (P_S=P_R\otimes S).  Those are minor proof
repairs if the intended local coefficient-lattice meaning is fixed.  If the
claim instead allows genuinely new geometric divisor classes after field
extension, the descent step is a major gap.

## 4. Chow versus cohomology

The audited divisor-product image is a cohomological/Hodge lattice generated
by (\operatorname{NS}).  The square-zero calculation above is therefore a
cohomology statement.  A Chow-theoretic version requires all of the following:

1. an equality of divisor classes in (\operatorname{CH}^1), not just in
   (\operatorname{NS});
2. (D_i^2=0) in the Chow ring, not merely after the cycle-class map; and
3. Chow product-image descent under the chosen faithfully flat base change.

The graph coefficient calculation supplies none of these automatically.  A
rank-one Neron--Severi class has square zero in cohomology, but a chosen
Picard representative can carry algebraically trivial terms, and the
pullback argument is not an integral Chow injectivity theorem.  The safe
statement is thus ordinary integral cohomology/Hodge membership.  Any Chow
upgrade is a separate theorem and should not be inferred from this lemma.

## Final status / EJ-TT closeout

- **GO:** formal all-(k) divided-power lemma from an integral signed
  square-zero decomposition.
- **MINOR:** add the rank-one-to-square-zero pullback lemma, state signed
  integral combinations, and prove (P_S=P_R\otimes S) for the local graph
  lattice.
- **MAJOR only if scope is widened:** new geometric divisor classes after
  base change or a Chow-ring conclusion; faithful flatness alone does not
  descend either claim.
- **Strength:** under the repaired hypotheses this strictly strengthens the
  finite-etale cofactor theorem from (k=g-1) to every divided power.

**EJ:** finite-etale blocks can support an all-(k) ordinary cohomological
divisor-product corollary.  
**TT:** the factorial disappears by distinct-index expansion, not by any
divided-power theorem; (p=2) and signed coefficients introduce no hidden
denominator.
