## The two field lists

The former single list `finiteBridgeFieldOrders`, present in both the algebra and the
certificate module and forced equal by an `rfl` theorem, served two meanings that the
characteristic-two argument has now separated:

- `PRSRedundancyFive.requiredBridgeFieldOrders = [7, 8, 9, 11, 13, 17, 19]` — the field
  orders whose split-free classification is a logical dependency on the certificate;
- `PRSRedundancyFiveCertificate.certifiedBridgeFieldOrders = [7, 8, 9, 11, 13, 16, 17, 19]`
  — the certificate's own domain below the geometric threshold, which truthfully still
  includes `q = 16`.

Two decidable theorems relate them: the first is contained in the second, and the second
exceeds it only at `16`. Deleting `16` from a single shared list would instead have made
the synthesis theorem vacuous over binary sixteen, since nothing else would have covered
that field.

To keep it non-vacuous, the classification range in
`ExceptionalCoverClassificationInput` now reads
`GeometricClassificationRange characteristic q ∨ q ∈ requiredBridgeFieldOrders`, where
`GeometricClassificationRange characteristic q` is `q ≥ 20 ∨ (characteristic = 2 ∧ q ≥ 16)`.
The former `highField_splitFree_iff_mem_deepFamilies` field is correspondingly
`geometricRange_splitFree_iff_mem_deepFamilies`, and the synthesis theorem's
`hHighField` argument is `hGeometricRange` over the same range. This is exactly what
`prop:r5-bridge` asserts.

  `d₂ + 2d₃ ≤ 4` comes from a different of degree four; wildness costs at least one extra

  `(#Y, d₂, d₃) = (12, 4, 0)` purely by integer arithmetic, without touching the geometry

  is item 4's business, and it is where the encoding reconciliation blocker sits.
