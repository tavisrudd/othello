## The layer that made the reduction possible

The plan assumed the reduction would rest on invariance of the normalized polar parameter, which
`PassantCodeQ13.SymmetricSquareInvariance` already supplies. That is not enough on its own: reducing
a statement about the support family to a representative needs the family itself to be carried to
itself. Four new modules supply that.

`PassantCodeQ13.Equivariance.GroupAction` upgrades the model's map `act` to a group action. The
symmetric square is multiplicative and rescaling a matrix rescales it by the square of the factor,
both polynomial identities in the eight matrix entries and three coordinates; normalization of a
triple is unchanged by rescaling; and the normalized representative of an invertible matrix is again
one of the displayed normalized invertible matrices. Consequently the composition of the maps of two
normalized invertible matrices is the map of their normalized product, and the action on the subtype
of internal points, `orbitMap`, is a bijection satisfying the composition law. No check in the module
ranges over the matrices.

`PassantCodeQ13.Equivariance.SupportInvariance` proves that the decoded minimum-word family is
carried to itself. The argument is structural rather than computational: the encoded family is the
duplicate-free join of four orbits, each of which is the set of encodings of a displayed
representative under all normalized invertible matrices, so acting by a further matrix reindexes the
orbit by the normalized product. The bridge from encodings to decoded sets is a bit-level lemma —
a bit of `encodeSupport` is set exactly at the displayed index of one of its points — which also
gives that the decoded support of a list of internal coordinates is the set of its members. The
invariance is then obtained as an inclusion together with injectivity of the action, so no
enumeration over the 2184 matrices or the 364 supports appears. Its immediate consequences are that
the number of supports through a point and the pair concurrence of two points are unchanged by the
action.

`PassantCodeQ13.Equivariance.Transporter` is the kernel-checked transporter theorem. Two checks
suffice, each over 78 indices. The first verifies that the generated point table carries the first
internal coordinate to each of the 78; the second verifies that the base row of the generated pair
table carries one of the six displayed representative pairs to the pair formed by the first internal
coordinate and the coordinate of each index. Only the base row is needed, because all six
representatives share their first point — the structural fact the previous round's mystery ledger
recorded. Ordered pairs are then reached by composing with the inverse of a point transporter, taken
through the adjugate: the adjugate of a matrix times the matrix is the determinant times the
identity, and the identity acts trivially on a normalized representative. So every ordered pair of
distinct internal points is the image of one of the six representative pairs.

This is what makes the 6006-entry pair table unnecessary as a kernel object. The measurement the
plan asked for is therefore moot: the table does not need an index split, because only 78 of its
entries are read.

## The three leaves

`PassantCodeQ13.Equivariance.MinimumLayerCounts` transports the two membership counts from the
semantic family to counts over the encoded supports, using injectivity of decoding and the bit
characterization, and records how the polar row and the concurrence-eight neighbourhood of a point
transform under the action. `PassantCodeQ13.Equivariance.FusedColorSplit` carries the fused count.

- `unaryDegree_fiftySix` is the count at the first internal point, 56 over the displayed 364
  supports.
- `pairColorEight_recovers_polarRows` becomes the equality of two families each generated by an
  equivariant point function, so it reduces to the agreement of the concurrence-eight neighbourhood
  and the polar row at the first internal point, decided over the 78 internal points.
- `fusedColorSix_splits` is decided at the six representative pairs, each over the 78 middle points.

One measurement matters for anyone repeating this. Stating a check over `minimumSupportCodes`
rather than over the displayed `minimumWordSupports` makes the kernel expand the four orbits — 2184
matrix actions per orbit — and exceeds the memory guard; the same check over the displayed list
finishes in seconds. Stating a check over `InternalPoint` rather than over `Fin 78` reintroduces the
`internalPointIndex` scan the package's third lever forbids. Both were observed as memory kills
before the checks were restated.

## Two further native decisions closed

`decodedSupport_injOn` was a compiled evaluation over all pairs of the 364 decoded sets. It is now
proved: two codes below `2 ^ 78` with the same decoding agree in every bit, since the bits below 78
are the memberships of the decoded sets and the higher bits vanish on both. The only finite content
left is that the displayed supports are below that bound. The duplicate-freeness of the encoded
family, used by the triple-concurrence transport, is likewise now decided over the displayed
supports rather than natively.

## Two anchor decisions closed as well

The relation pattern of the first three anchors and the length of the normalized matrix list were
both compiled evaluations. Neither needs to be: the first is three polar parameters and the second
is the length of a filtered displayed list, and both reduce in the kernel in seconds. The one
compiled evaluation left in that module is the identification of the 2184 anchor images with the
ordered triples carrying the anchor pattern, which stage 5 of the plan owns.
