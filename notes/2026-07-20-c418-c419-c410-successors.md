### `ej3`: reusable secant-concurrency proof mechanism

The restored cubic has an exact general explanation.  For any finite projective plane of order
`q`, put

```text
s_L=|B intersect L|,   m_L=binom(s_L,2),
lambda_B(X)=sum_(L through X) m_L.
```

Classify selected pair-items by the number of distinct supporting secants.  Double counting gives

```text
sum_X binom(lambda_X,1)
  =(q+1) sum_L m_L,

sum_X binom(lambda_X,2)
  =(q+1) sum_L binom(m_L,2) + sum_(L<M) m_L m_M,

sum_X binom(lambda_X,3)
  =(q+1) sum_L binom(m_L,3)
   +sum_(L!=M) binom(m_L,2)m_M
   +C_3(B),
```

where

```text
C_3(B)=sum_X sum_(distinct L1<L2<L3 through X) m_L1 m_L2 m_L3
```

is the weighted number of triples of distinct concurrent secants.  The one-line proof is reusable:
items on one supporting line have `q+1` common points, items on two distinct lines have their
unique intersection, and three distinct lines contribute exactly when they are concurrent.

Consequently equal line-section histograms force the `lambda` moments through degree two.  Their
first possible difference is `C_3`; after converting factorial to raw moments,

```text
Delta sum_X lambda_X^3 = 6 Delta C_3.
```

For the C418 witness,

```text

```

so the raw cubic difference is exactly `6(166-168)=-12`.  This replaces the `ej2` factorization
as the conceptual proof mechanism: the universal package misses two weighted secant-concurrency
triples, and the pointed boundary exposes their quadratic residues.
