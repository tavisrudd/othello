# C682 intrinsic \(E_8\) radial symbol

## Outcome

The previously unexplained scalar cubic
\[
 p(F,h;\xi,\eta)
 =2F\xi^3+5h\xi^2\eta-8000F^3\eta^3
\]
is the intrinsic radial principal symbol of the third Klein transvectant.
The finite matrices multiplying it are not merely gauge-equivalent to
classical \(E_8\) factorizations: divided by \(10\), they are exactly
multiplication by the odd Klein invariant \(t\) on the McKay covariant
module.

Put
\[
 R=\mathbf Q[F,h],\qquad
 t^2=1728F^5-h^3,
\]
with \(\deg(F,h,t)=(12,20,30)\), and normalize
\[
 \mathcal D=\frac1{132}(\,\cdot\,,F)_3.
\]
For \(a\in R\), the invariant \(\mathcal D(a)\) has degree congruent to
\(2\pmod4\), hence lies in the odd summand \(tR\).  Define the scalar radial
operator
\[
 \mathcal R(a)=\frac{\mathcal D(a)}t.
\]
Its complete normal-ordered formula is
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \mathcal R
 =20F\partial_F^3
 50h\partial_F^2\partial_h
 -80000F^3\partial_h^3
 55\partial_F^2.
 \quad}
\]
Consequently
\[
 \boxed{\quad \sigma_3(\mathcal R)=10p. \quad}
\]
This identifies \(p\) without reference to any chosen McKay block or
principal-symbol matrix.

Let \(M_\rho\) be any binary-icosahedral covariant module, viewed as a free
\(R\)-module, and let \(m_t\) denote multiplication by \(t\).  The top
Leibniz term of \(\mathcal D(ag)\), \(a\in R\), \(g\in M_\rho\),
differentiates only \(a\).  Therefore the identification is uniform:
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \sigma_3(\mathcal D|_{M_\rho})=10p\,m_t
 \quad\text{for every McKay block }\rho.
 \quad}
\]
The finite factors \(m_t\) are precisely the classical McKay-node matrix
factorizations of \(t^2=1728F^5-h^3\).  Thus the third Klein transvectant
selects all nine \(E_8\) factorizations through one universal radial cubic;
eight further symbol tables are unnecessary.

## Exact identification on the three-node

On the primitive three-covariant basis, split as
\[
 (g_2,g_{10},g_{18})\sqcup(g_{12},g_{20},g_{28}),
\]
the prior principal-symbol multipliers were the matrices \(A,B\).  Exact
free-basis decomposition now gives
\[
 m_t|_{\mathrm{even}\to\mathrm{odd}}=\frac A{10},
 \qquad
 m_t|_{\mathrm{odd}\to\mathrm{even}}=\frac B{10}.
\]
For example,
\[
\begin{aligned}
tg_2&=-hg_{12}+12Fg_{20},\\
tg_{10}&=\frac15hg_{20}+\frac65Fg_{28},\\
tg_{18}&=24F^3g_{12}+hg_{28},
\end{aligned}
\]
and
\[
\begin{aligned}
tg_{12}&=h^2g_2-60Fhg_{10}+72F^2g_{18},\\
tg_{20}&=144F^4g_2-5h^2g_{10}+6Fhg_{18},\\
tg_{28}&=-24F^3hg_2+1440F^4g_{10}-h^2g_{18}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These identities imply
\[
 m_t^2=t^2I,\qquad AB=BA=100t^2I
\]
without treating the matrix factorization as an accidental determinant
calculation.

## Proof of the radial formula

The third transvectant has differential order at most three.  After division
by \(t\), \(\mathcal R\) has weighted degree \(-24\).  A normal-ordered
coefficient of
\(\partial_F^r\partial_h^s\) must therefore have weight
\[
 12r+20s-24.
\]
For \(r+s\le3\), the semigroup generated by \(12,20\) leaves exactly four
possibilities:
\[
 F\partial_F^3,\qquad
 h\partial_F^2\partial_h,\qquad
 F^3\partial_h^3,\qquad
 \partial_F^2.
\]
There can be no other term.

Direct transvection on \(F^2,F^3,F^2h,h^3\) fixes the four coefficients:
\[
\mathcal R(F^2)=110,\quad
\mathcal R(F^3)=450F,\quad
\mathcal R(F^2h)=210h,\quad
\mathcal R(h^3)=-480000F^3.
\]
Solving gives \(20,50,-80000,55\), respectively.  The exact certificate
also verifies the resulting identity directly on all
\[
 F^ah^b,\qquad 0\le a,b\le6.
\]
The universal McKay-block statement then follows formally from the top
Leibniz term.

## Characteristic web

The binary-cubic discriminant of \(p\) is
\[
 \boxed{\quad
 \operatorname{Disc}_{\xi,\eta}(p)
 =4\,000\,000F^3(h^3-1728F^5)
 =-4\,000\,000F^3t^2.
 \quad}
\]
Thus the radial characteristic three-web acquires repeated directions
exactly over the Klein branch \(t=0\) and the invariant divisor \(F=0\).
This is distinct from the determinant of the module symbol,
whose vanishing records \(p=0\) and \(t=0\) in the cotangent bundle.

## Classical placement

The third transvectant is the binary-form side of the classical
transvectant/Rankin--Cohen bracket formalism.  In those terms, \(p\) is the
reduced radial cubic of the third bracket on the even invariant parameter
ring, and \(m_t\) is its McKay-module coefficient.
