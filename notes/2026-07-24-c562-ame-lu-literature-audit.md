# C562 — LU-rigidity precedence and novelty boundary

**Lane:** `ame-lu`

**Date:** 2026-07-24

**Status:** complete; qualified novelty wording authorized

## Summary verdict

The audit located a direct qubit precedent for both the conclusion and the
proof mechanism.  Rains proves that every automorphism of the qubit
`[[2m,2m-2,2]]` code, `m>=2`, is Clifford by recovering the three diagonal
Pauli axes from a rank-one contraction of

\[
 |0\cdots0\rangle+(-1)^m|1\cdots1\rangle+|2\cdots2\rangle .
\]

Van den Nest--Dehaene--De Moor use this result to prove that every local
factor of an LU equivalence is Clifford whenever the minimal-support
subgroup displays all three Pauli axes at every qubit.  Thus neither
“LU implies LC under a minimal-support axis condition” nor “diagonal tensor
axes force Cliffordness” is new without qualification.

No located predecessor states the finite-field extension used by C560:
the full `q^2-1`-axis Weyl tensor, prime-power local dimension, and the
result that every LU intertwiner between equal-phase CSS states of linear
`[6,3,4]_q` MDS codes is LC.  The closest arbitrary-dimensional AME work
instead permits monomial or Butson local factors in a bounded saturated
regime and does not derive Clifford rigidity.  Recent qudit stabilizer work
found in the audit concerns party-local decomposition or Clifford
classification, not arbitrary single-site LU-to-LC rigidity.

The defensible novelty boundary is therefore a **prime-power-qudit
generalization and MDS/CSS specialization of the Rains--Van den Nest
axis-rigidity mechanism**, not a new LU--LC mechanism in the abstract.
The admitted-pencil statement `LU iff LC iff z` is a new corollary only
relative to C396's separately audited LC classification.

Twelve individually discussed sources were consulted: **one at `full text`,
eight at `partial`, and three at `abstract/metadata only`**.  The negative
remains qualified by the coverage gaps below.

## Closest precedence

### Rains: the diagonal three-axis tensor

Rains, *Quantum codes of minimum distance two*, Theorem 13, identifies the
projector of the qubit `[[2m,2m-2,2]]` code with the diagonal tensor

\[
 v=|0\cdots0\rangle+(-1)^m|1\cdots1\rangle+|2\cdots2\rangle
 \in(\mathbb R^3)^{\otimes 2m}.
\]

A one-leg rank-one contraction shows that every local `SO(3)` factor fixing
`v` is monomial, hence every code automorphism is Clifford.  Corollary 14
then covers equivalences of GF(4)-linear qubit codes under mild distance
hypotheses.  This is the closest proof-level predecessor to C560.

C560 replaces the three real Pauli axes by the `q^2-1` complex Weyl axes and
uses the shortened `[6,3,4]_q` MDS stabilizer subgroup on a four-party
marginal.  That is a genuine extension in alphabet, tensor, and state
family, but its conceptual ancestry should be explicit.

### Van den Nest--Dehaene--De Moor: minimal-support LU rigidity

Their Theorem 1 says that a fully entangled qubit stabilizer state has
`LU=LC` when `X,Y,Z` all occur at every qubit in the subgroup generated by
minimal stabilizer elements.  The proof fixes an arbitrary LU map and shows
each single-qubit factor is Clifford.  Lemma 2 imports the Rains
`[[2m,2m-2,2]]` rigidity statement for minimal supports carrying three
stabilizer elements.

This pre-empts any unqualified claim that C560 first turns intrinsic
minimal-support correlation axes into Cliffordness.  It does not cover a
single `q`-level party for `q>2`, the `F_q` Weyl system, or the MDS
shortening argument.

### Arbitrary-dimensional AME work

Burchardt--Raissi analyze LU/SLOCC equivalence of minimal-support AME
states.  In the saturated `AME(2k,d)` case their bounded regime allows
local monomial or Butson matrices, and their general Proposition 7 gives
support and phase restrictions rather than Clifford rigidity.  Their
dimension bound ceases to settle `AME(6,d)` starting at the range recorded
in their table; C560 uses the additional equal-phase CSS and linear MDS
structure and has no dimension cutoff.

Ramadas--Lakshminarayan prove infinitely many LU classes of phased
minimal-support `AME(6,d)` states whenever the requisite orthogonal arrays
exist.  This does not conflict with C560: their enphased states need not be
equal-phase CSS stabilizer states.  It makes the phrase **equal-phase CSS**
an essential hypothesis, not expository detail.

### Later stabilizer work

The later qubit literature refines the global LU--LC problem, supplies
counterexamples, characterizes qubit local symmetries, and gives recent
graphical or algorithmic LU tests.  The searched prime-dimensional qudit
network paper extends a party-local Clifford decomposition formalism and
proves uniqueness of an entanglement-generating-set decomposition; it does
not prove single-qudit LU-to-LC rigidity.  None of these sources states
C560's finite-field Weyl criterion.

## Authorized manuscript wording

Recommended related-work paragraph:

> Rains proved that the diagonal three-Pauli correlation tensor of a
> qubit `[[2m,2m-2,2]]` code has only Clifford local automorphisms, and
> Van den Nest, Dehaene, and De Moor used this rigidity in their
> minimal-support criterion for qubit stabilizer states.  We extend this
> axis-recovery mechanism to the full finite-field Weyl basis.  For the
> four-party shortening of an equal-phase CSS state associated with a
> linear `[6,3,4]_q` MDS code, the `q^2-1` nonidentity Weyl axes occur
> diagonally and bijectively; an arbitrary product-unitary equivalence
> must therefore normalize every local Weyl system.  To our knowledge,
> this gives the first LU-to-LC rigidity theorem for this six-party
> MDS/CSS family uniformly over prime-power local dimensions.

Recommended portable-criterion wording:

> The proof isolates a prime-power-qudit form of the
> Rains--Van den Nest axis-rigidity argument: it is enough that every
> party lie in a reduced stabilizer support of size at least three whose
> supported subgroup has order `q^2` and projects bijectively onto the
> nonzero Weyl labels at each supported party.

Forbidden or unsupported wording:

- “the first proof that minimal-support stabilizer states satisfy
  `LU=LC`”;
- “a new diagonal-tensor rigidity method” without credit to Rains;
- “the first theorem showing that every LU intertwiner is Clifford”;
- “all six-qudit AME states satisfy `LU=LC`”;
- “all stabilizer states over prime-power dimensions satisfy `LU=LC`”;
- any unqualified priority claim or omission of “equal-phase,” “linear,”
  “MDS,” or “CSS.”

The first recommended paragraph must retain “to our knowledge” unless the
MathSciNet and Google Scholar gaps are closed manually.

## `ej` and Tao closeout

The cheapest high-value upgrade is to make the ancestry part of the proof
story: C560 is not merely adjacent to the qubit minimal-support literature;
its diagonal Weyl tensor is visibly the `q^2-1`-axis successor of Rains's
three-axis tensor.  Naming that bridge strengthens the paper and prevents
an avoidable priority objection.

The second upgrade is a sharper hypothesis boundary.  Ramadas--
Lakshminarayan show that phased minimal-support `AME(6,d)` states split
into infinitely many LU classes.  The headline should therefore retain
“equal-phase CSS states associated with linear MDS codes” at first
statement rather than abbreviating to “six-qudit AME stabilizer tensors”
until the hypotheses have been fixed.

No further task-owned search is likely to improve the verdict cheaply.
Manual MathSciNet and Google Scholar checks remain release-stage gates,
not reasons to broaden C562 recursively.
