# C284 — Polyhedral nonregular coset templates

**Date:** 2026-07-17. **Lane:** `dihedral`. **Status:** COMPLETE.

## Result

The tame polyhedral boundary is finite and explicit. An involution-generated subgroup of type
`A4` is impossible: the involutions of `A4` are the three nonidentity elements of its normal
Klein four subgroup, so they generate at most `V4`. For `S4` and `A5`, every point stabilizer in
the projective-line action is cyclic, and the tables below give every fixed-point-deleted coset
template that can occur.

The regular-table invariant used previously needs one refinement. For an unordered generating
involution triple `T={a,b,c}`, let

- `sigma(T)` be the sorted three pair-product orders; and
- `rho(T)` be the common order of the six products obtained by ordering `a,b,c`.

Exact enumeration proves that all six orders are equal in every `S4` and `A5` class. The pair
`(sigma,rho)` is a complete `Aut(G)`-orbit invariant: the 52 generating triples of `S4` split as
`12+24+4+12` across four classes, and the 380 generating triples of `A5` split as
`120+60+60+60+60+20` across six classes. In particular, the old `A5` signature `(3,5,5)` splits
into two 60-triple classes, `rho=3` and `rho=5`. Their regular values are both zero, so the old
regular table remains numerically correct, but their nonregular templates differ.

## Field-dependent orbit formulas

For a fixed tame embedding, write `epsilon_d=1` when the relevant order-`d` cyclic subgroup is
split (has rational fixed points on `P1(F_q)`), and zero otherwise. Each split maximal cyclic
subgroup type contributes exactly one exceptional orbit: there are respectively `6,4,3`
subgroups of types `C2a,C3,C4` in `S4`, and `15,10,6` subgroups of types `C2,C3,C5` in `A5`;
double-counting their two fixed points gives one orbit of size `|G|/d`.

Thus

```text
S4: m1 = (q+1 - 12 epsilon_2 - 8 epsilon_3 - 6 epsilon_4)/24
    G = (m1 mod 2)t1 xor epsilon_2 t2 xor epsilon_3 t3 xor epsilon_4 t4.

A5: m1 = (q+1 - 30 epsilon_2 - 20 epsilon_3 - 12 epsilon_5)/60
    G = (m1 mod 2)t1 xor epsilon_2 t2 xor epsilon_3 t3 xor epsilon_5 t5.
```

For the tame Dickson embeddings used here, `S4` has `epsilon_2=epsilon_4=1` exactly when
`q=1 (mod 8)` and `epsilon_3=1` exactly when `q=1 (mod 3)`. For `A5`,
`epsilon_2=1` exactly when `q=1 (mod 4)`, while `epsilon_3` and `epsilon_5` are one exactly when
`q=1 (mod 3)` and `q=1 (mod 5)`, respectively. These statements are conditional on the relevant
tame polyhedral embedding existing; they do not assert existence for arbitrary `q`.

## Proof and computation

The proof has three finite-group steps.

1. In a tame subgroup of `PGL2(q)`, a rational point stabilizer is cyclic. Enumerating the cyclic
   subgroup classes of `S4` and `A5`, then using the fixed-point double count above, gives the orbit
   multiplicities and shows that no other transitive projective-line orbit type occurs.
2. The script enumerates every unordered generating involution triple. It independently computes
   its full `Aut(G)`-orbit and asserts that `(sigma,rho)` labels exactly one orbit. For each class
   and cyclic stabilizer it constructs `R(G,K,T)` directly from the coset action and emits its
   exact residual edge list.
3. Every nonregular nimber is computed twice: once by plain memoized Sprague-Grundy recursion with
   connected-component xor, and once with canonicalization under the commuting right action of
   `N_G(K)/K`. All 38 emitted rows agree. A separately constructed matrix/PGL2 implementation then
   checks every generating triple in five `S4` embeddings (`q=7,17,23,31,41`) and five `A5`
   embeddings (`q=11,19,29,31,41`): 2,160 triples total, with both per-orbit and whole-board
   formula checks and zero mismatches.

The newly exposed `A5 (3,5,5; rho=5)` regular graph was also evaluated with the source's
`regular-extra` mode: nimber zero, 11m33s CPU time, 1.5 GB peak memory. The other regular values
are the independently cross-checked C260 results. This extra regular computation uses the valid
right-regular canonicalization but has no second 60-vertex replay in this bundle; it is not used by
any nonregular table entry, and its zero agrees with the pre-existing signature-level row.

This certifies the displayed finite tables and the sampled matrix realizations. The orbit formulas
also use the standard tame cyclic-stabilizer and split/nonsplit facts stated above. The computation
does not classify the full `PSL2/PGL2` escape residual, does not cover wild characteristic, and is
not a Lean proof. The conic replay is an independent graph construction but shares the same
Sprague-Grundy recurrence; the plain-versus-canonical abstract solvers provide the independent
recursion cross-check.
