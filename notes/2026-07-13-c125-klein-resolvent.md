## 1. Klein's actual objects (citations)

Klein, *Lectures on the Ikosahedron* (1884; Morrice transl. 1888,
[archive.org cu31924059413439](https://archive.org/details/cu31924059413439)); modern exposition
with explicit formulas: O. Nash, *On Klein's icosahedral solution of the quintic*,
Expo. Math. 32 (2014), [arXiv:1308.0955](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.0955), eqs (2.3)–(2.7).

- **Icosahedral Möbius group** Γ ≅ A₅ ⊂ PSL(2,ℂ), generated by (Nash (2.3), ε = e^{2πi/5})
  `S = diag(ε³, ε²)` (z ↦ εz) and `T = 5^{-1/2} [[−(ε−ε⁴), ε²−ε³], [ε²−ε³, ε−ε⁴]]`.
  Entries lie in ℤ[ε, 1/√5] ⊂ ℚ(ε) (note √5 = 1+2(ε+ε⁴) ∈ ℚ(ε)).
- **Vertex form, degree 12** (Nash (2.4)): `f = z₁z₂(z₁¹⁰ + 11 z₁⁵z₂⁵ − z₂¹⁰)`, vanishing on the
  12 vertices (the exceptional orbit with stabilizer C₅). Note the integer coefficient **11**.
- **Face form, degree 20**: `H = −(z₁²⁰+z₂²⁰) + 228(z₁¹⁵z₂⁵−z₁⁵z₂¹⁵) − 494 z₁¹⁰z₂¹⁰`
  (Hessian of f, up to 1/121); 20 face centres, stabilizer C₃.
- **Edge form, degree 30**: `T = (z₁³⁰+z₂³⁰) + 522(z₁²⁵z₂⁵−z₁⁵z₂²⁵) − 10005(z₁²⁰z₂¹⁰+z₁¹⁰z₂²⁰)`
  (Jacobian of f,H up to 1/20); 30 edge midpoints, stabilizer C₂.
- **Syzygy** (Nash (2.7)): `H³ + T² = 1728 f⁵`; invariant ring = k[f,H,T] mod this relation.
- **Six diagonals**: Klein I.1.§8 — "the 12 ikosahedral points distribute themselves in pairs in
  6 diameters, which we will describe … as diagonals of the ikosahedron" (the six 5-fold axes;
  antipodal vertex pairs). A₅ permutes them 2-transitively via the exceptional
  A₅ ≅ PSL(2,5) action on the six points of P¹(F₅).
- **The sextic resolvent**: Klein I.4.§15, *"The simplest resolvent of the sixth degree"*
  (Morrice p. 119) — a degree-6 resolvent of the icosahedral equation whose **six roots
  correspond to the six diagonals**; each diagonal carries a quadratic form (the pair of vertex
  linear factors of f it spans), from which Klein builds the root functions. The quintic-side
  counterpart is Part II Ch. IV, *"The problem of the A's and the Jacobian equations of the sixth
  degree"* — degree-6 equations with the same group in the same 2-transitive action, the vehicle
  by which the general quintic reaches the icosahedron. Degree-5 resolvents (the five inscribed
  tetrahedra/octahedra) are the other resolvent family; not our object.

## 2. The match over F₁₁ is a genuine reduction, not an analogy

Since 11 ≡ 1 (mod 5), 11 splits completely in ℚ(ε): ε ↦ 3 and √5 ↦ 4 land in F₁₁ (3⁵ = 1,
4² = 5). Fix the prime 𝔭 | 11 with these values and reduce. All of the following are
**machine-verified** (§5):

1. **Group.** Reduction mod 𝔭 of Klein's literal generators S, T yields a subgroup of order 60
   of PGL(2,11) — faithful because 11 ∤ |A₅| = 60 (the reduction kernel is pro-11) — i.e.
   **A₅ ⊂ PSL(2,11)** acting on the 12 points of P¹(F₁₁) with point stabilizer C₅: the
   icosahedron's vertex action. Up to PGL(2,11)-conjugacy this A₅ is unique (Dickson's subgroup
   classification: PSL(2,q) ⊃ A₅ iff q ≡ ±1 mod 5 or 5 | q, two PSL-classes fused in PGL —
   L.E. Dickson, *Linear Groups*, 1901, §260; cf. Kostant,
   [*The graph of the truncated icosahedron and the last letter of Galois*](https://www.ams.org/notices/199509/kostant.pdf),
   Notices AMS 42 (1995), for A₅ ⊂ PSL(2,11) — the point stabilizer of Galois's exceptional
   degree-11 action).
2. **Vertex form.** f has integer coefficients and its middle coefficient is literally 11, so
   `f mod 11 = z₁z₂(z₁¹⁰ − z₂¹⁰) = z₁¹¹z₂ − z₁z₂¹¹` — squarefree, vanishing on **exactly the
   12 points of P¹(F₁₁)**. Under the Veronese parametrization t ↦ (1:t:t²) of the conic XZ = Y²
   these are exactly the 12 conic points. *The 12 conic points are the mod-11 icosahedron
   vertices — literally the reduced divisor of Klein's degree-12 form.*
3. **Diagonals = missing edges.** The six diagonals of the reduced group (fixed-point pairs of
   its order-5 elements) are, for the choice ε ↦ 3, √5 ↦ 4:
   {0,∞},{1,6},{9,10},{2,4},{3,7},{5,8}; the PGL(2,11) element **g : z ↦ 1/(z+5)** carries them
   to our `witnessMissingEdge` pairs {0,9},{3,4},{1,7},{6,10},{2,∞},{5,8}
   (`Examples.lean:46`, C121). Since C121 measured the arc stabilizer to be exactly order 60,
   g·Γ̄·g⁻¹ **is** our arc-stabilizer A₅ — the reduction of Klein's group, on the nose after one
   projective reparametrization of the conic.
4. **Poles = diagonal quadratics.** PG(2,11) = P(Sym² F₁₁²) canonically: point (x:y:z) ↔ binary
   quadratic x z₁² − 2y z₁z₂ + z z₂², conic = perfect squares, (1:t:t²) ↔ (z₁−tz₂)². Under this
   identification the **polarity pole of the chord through conic points t, t′ is the quadratic
   (z₁−tz₂)(z₁−t′z₂)** — verified to reproduce all six witness arc points from the six pairs.
   So the six arc points ARE the six quadratic factors of f mod 11 grouped by diagonals, i.e.
   **the mod-11 reductions of Klein's six diagonal quadratics**, the geometric objects
   enumerated by the roots of his sextic resolvent (I.4.§15). The A₅ 2-transitivity on our six
   columns (C121, cycle census 1+15+20+24) is the reduction of the classical 2-transitive
   A₅ ≅ PSL(2,5) action on the diagonals.
5. **Face/edge forms and syzygy.** H mod 11 and T mod 11 are squarefree with **no** F₁₁-rational
   roots (faces and edges live in P¹(F₁₂₁): orbits of size 20 and 30 exceed 12); and
   1728 ≡ 1 (mod 11), so the syzygy reduces to `H³ + T² = f⁵`.

So the correspondence is a **reduction of Klein's actual polynomials and group**, coefficient by
coefficient — not merely an isomorphism of abstract A₅-sets.

## 3. Is 11 a good prime? Yes — and uniquely the best one

- **Good**: 11 ∤ |A₅| = 60, so the group reduces faithfully (and the 2-dim representation of the
  binary icosahedral group stays irreducible, 11 ∤ 120); f, H, T all stay squarefree (verified),
  so all three exceptional orbits have good reduction; the bad primes of the icosahedron are
  2, 3, 5 (and √5/ε force ramification bookkeeping only at 5).
- **Rational**: 11 ≡ 1 (mod 5) puts ε and √5 in F₁₁, so the group *and all 12 vertices* are
  F₁₁-rational (for a general good prime the vertices are rational iff p ≡ 1 mod 5).
- **Uniquely optimal**: p = 11 is the **only** prime with p + 1 = 12, i.e. the only prime where
  the 12 vertices can (and do) exhaust P¹(F_p). Mechanism: Klein's coefficient 11 in f dies mod
  11, collapsing f to z₁¹¹z₂ − z₁z₂¹¹, the classical degree-(q+1) form vanishing on all rational
  points. The apex numerology 1728 ≡ 1 (mod 11) is a bonus of the same collapse.

## 4. The precise claimable theorem, and the two caveats

> **Theorem (C125).** Let Γ ⊂ PSL(2, ℚ(ζ₅)) be Klein's icosahedral group in his classical
> coordinates, with vertex form f, face form H, edge form T, and let 𝔭 be a prime of ℚ(ζ₅) above
> 11 (11 splits completely). Then reduction mod 𝔭 is injective on Γ and, under the Veronese
> identification of P¹(F₁₁) with the conic C : XZ = Y² in PG(2,11), carries Γ to a
> PGL(2,11)-conjugate of the arc-stabilizer A₅ of the q=11 witness; the divisor of f mod 𝔭 is
> exactly the 12 F₁₁-points of C; the six diagonals of the icosahedron (the antipodal vertex
> pairs, canonically indexed by the roots of Klein's sextic resolvent, Lectures I.4.§15) reduce
> to the six chords `witnessMissingEdge`; and the six arc points are precisely the poles of these
> chords — equivalently, in P(Sym²) coordinates, the six quadratic factors of f mod 𝔭 grouped by
> diagonals. The reduction is faithful and non-degenerate on every exceptional orbit (f, H, T
> squarefree mod 11), and the icosahedral syzygy reduces to H³ + T² = f⁵.

Status by clause: everything above is **REAL** — either standard (Dickson/Kostant, splitting of
11 in ℚ(ζ₅), pro-p kernel) or verified computationally here at q=11 scale, and all finite parts
are `decide`-grade Lean-able on the C121 plumbing (PGL(2,11) as a Finset).

**Caveat A (wording of "resolvent").** Klein's sextic resolvent is a univariate polynomial in an
auxiliary rational function, with coefficients depending on the icosahedral parameter Z. What
reduces exactly is its *canonical root set* — the six diagonals / diagonal quadratics. Claim
"the six poles are the mod-11 reduction of Klein's six diagonals, the objects the roots of his
sextic resolvent enumerate", **not** "the six poles are the F₁₁-roots of Klein's resolvent
polynomial" (specializing that polynomial at a parameter value is a different statement —
needs-computation, and unnecessary for our purpose).

**Caveat B (extra symmetry mod 11).** Mod 11 the vertex form degenerates to
z₁¹¹z₂ − z₁z₂¹¹, which is (projectively) invariant under **all** of PGL(2,11), not just A₅. So
f mod 11 alone no longer remembers the icosahedron; the A₅ structure is carried by the reduced
group itself — equivalently by H, T, or by the diagonal pairing (our missing-edge matching). The
"12 conic points ↔ degree-12 vertex form" clause is exact but tautological-looking in isolation;
always pair it with the group/diagonal clause.

**Analogy-only residue: none.** The one PGL(2,11) conjugation (choice of 𝔭 and of conic
parameter) is the finite-field analogue of choosing which way to inscribe the icosahedron in the
sphere; it is not a weakening.
