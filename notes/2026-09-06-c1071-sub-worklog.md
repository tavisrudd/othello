> designs.  Alspach and Heinrich~\cite{AlspachHeinrich1990} call a family of
> $s$-edge matchings in $K_n$ a $\operatorname{MATCH}(n,s,\lambda)$ design

The paper does not restate this as a numbered definition; it is imported by citation
in the related-work paragraph, and used at L554.

**Where the design arises** — Theorem `thm:matching-design`, "Concurrency decomposition
and equality rigidity", L540–565:

> \begin{theorem}[Concurrency decomposition and equality rigidity]
> \label{thm:matching-design}
> Suppose $k\ge4$.
> For each $x\notin A$ with $r(x)\ge2$, the secants through $x$ form an
> $r(x)$-edge matching $M_x$ in $K_k$, and
> \[
>  E\bigl(KG(k,2)\bigr)
>    =\mathop{\dot\bigcup}_{x\notin A,\ r(x)\ge2} E(K_{M_x}).
> \]
> Here $K_{M_x}$ is the clique whose vertices are the edges of $M_x$.
>
> If $\Delta_{\cH}(A)=0$, every $M_x$ in this decomposition has
> $m=\lfloor k/2\rfloor$ edges.  The family is simple: a repeated matching
> would repeat each of its pairs of edges, contradicting $\lambda=1$.  Thus
> $(M_x)_{r(x)=m}$ is a simple $\operatorname{MATCH}(k,m,1)$ design.  If
> $Z=\{x\notin A:r(x)=m\}$, then
> \[
>  |Z|=\frac{3\binom{k}{4}}{\binom{m}{2}}
>  =\begin{cases}
>    (k-1)(k-3),&k\ \text{even},\\
>    k(k-2),&k\ \text{odd},
>   \end{cases}
> \]
> and every secant of $A$ contains $k-3$ points of $Z$ when $k$ is even and
> $k-2$ points of $Z$ when $k$ is odd.
> \end{theorem}

**Rank-three realization** — Definition `def:rank-three-realization`, L1240–1248:

> \begin{definition}\label{def:rank-three-realization}
> A \emph{rank-three projective realization} of a
> \(\operatorname{MATCH}(k,m,1)\) design \(\mathcal D\) over a field \(K\)
> is an injective assignment \(i\mapsto p_i\in\PG(2,K)\) whose image is a
> \(k\)-arc and such that, for every matching \(M\in\mathcal D\), the \(m\)
> lines \(p_ip_j\), \(\{i,j\}\in M\), are concurrent.  Two matching designs
> are \emph{isomorphic} when a permutation of their \(k\) vertices carries
> the matching collection of one onto that of the other.
> \end{definition}

### A2. Ten-point realization theorem

Theorem `thm:match-ten-realization`, "Rank-three ten-point classification", L1307–1319:

> \begin{theorem}[Rank-three ten-point classification]
> \label{thm:match-ten-realization}
> Let $K$ be a field.  Among the simple
> $\operatorname{MATCH}(10,5,1)$ designs, only the regular-hyperoval design
> can have a rank-three projective realization.  It has one over $K$ if and
> only if
> \[
>  \operatorname{char}K=2,
>  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
>  \F_8\subseteq K.
> \]
> The other abstract design has no rank-three realization over any field.
> \end{theorem}

Definition of "regular-hyperoval design", L1300–1305:

> Following Alspach and Heinrich's hyperoval construction
> \cite[Theorem~1.2]{AlspachHeinrich1990}, call the
> \(\operatorname{MATCH}(10,5,1)\) class formed by the \(63\) concurrence
> matchings of the regular hyperoval in \(\PG(2,8)\) the
> \emph{regular-hyperoval design}.  It is one of Mathon's two abstract
> classes.

The arc inequation used, and the certificate's stated output, L1369–1390:

> Only a necessary part of the arc open set is needed for the exclusions.
> Indeed,
> \[
>  \det(p_1,p_2,p_9)=x_9,\qquad
>  \det(p_1,p_3,p_9)=x_9-1,
> \]
> so every arc realization has \(x_9(x_9-1)\ne0\).  The certificate
> saturates the concurrency ideal by this product.  This enlarges, rather
> than shrinks, the locus that must be excluded: a unit saturated ideal
> therefore rules out every arc realization without claiming that this one
> product encodes all arc inequations.
>
> The remaining elimination is computer-assisted.  The exact certificate
> summarized in Appendix~\ref{app:rank-three-certificates} gives the following
> mathematical output.  After inverting \(2\), rational module identities make
> both saturated ideals the unit ideal, and their only denominator prime is
> \(2\); hence both designs are excluded in every odd characteristic.  In
> characteristic two the second design again has unit saturated ideal.  For
> the regular-hyperoval design, the surviving triangular basis forces a
> parameter \(t\) with \(t^3+t+1=0\), while the arc inequations force
> \(t\notin\F_2\).  Thus \(\F_8\subseteq K\).  The regular hyperoval over
> \(\F_8\) and scalar extension prove the converse.
> \end{proof}

**What the paper concludes:** existence of a rank-three realization iff
`char K = 2` and `F_8 ⊆ K`. It does NOT currently state projective uniqueness
(the reviewer's item 6 target) — no "projectively equivalent to the embedded
regular `F_8`-hyperoval" sentence appears in this theorem or its proof.

### A4. Evaluation obstruction lemma

Setup for `V` and `ev`, L1574–1579:

> Fix nonzero vector representatives of the
> projective points under consideration.  If $V$ is a finite-dimensional
> linear system of homogeneous forms, write
> $\operatorname{ev}_x\in V^*$ for evaluation at the representative of $x$.
> The zero or nonzero status of this evaluation is independent of the chosen
> representative.

Lemma `lem:evaluation-obstruction`, "Uncovered evaluation obstruction", L1586–1603:

> \begin{lemma}[Uncovered evaluation obstruction]
> \label{lem:evaluation-obstruction}
> Let $A$ and $U$ be finite sets of projective points.  There is no nonzero
> $f\in V$ such that $U\subseteq Z(f)$ and $A\cap Z(f)=\varnothing$ if either
> of the following holds:
> \begin{enumerate}
>  \item the evaluation map
>  $f\mapsto(\operatorname{ev}_u(f))_{u\in U}$ is injective;
>  \item for some $a\in A$, the functional $\operatorname{ev}_a$ belongs to
>  the span of $\{\operatorname{ev}_u:u\in U\}$.
> \end{enumerate}
> \end{lemma}
>
> \begin{proof}
> If $f$ vanishes on $U$, injectivity in the first case gives $f=0$.  In the
> second case, applying a spanning relation for $\operatorname{ev}_a$ to $f$
> gives $f(a)=0$, contrary to $A\cap Z(f)=\varnothing$.
> \end{proof}

Observations: the lemma is stated as one-directional (sufficient only), matching the
reviewer's item 4; `U` is a bare finite set of projective points, `V` a
finite-dimensional linear system of homogeneous forms; `W = {f in V : f|_U = 0}`
does not appear. Obstruction (1) is "evaluation map on `U` injective" — equivalently
`W = 0`. Obstruction (2) is `ev_a in span{ev_u}`.

Companion lemma `lem:line-triangle-obstruction`, L1605–1618:

> \begin{lemma}[Line--triangle obstruction]
> \label{lem:line-triangle-obstruction}
> Suppose that \(U\subseteq\PG(2,K)\) contains three distinct points on a
> line \(L\), together with three noncollinear points outside \(L\).  Then no
> nonzero homogeneous quadratic vanishes on \(U\).
> \end{lemma}

And the Veronese reformulation, L1620–1626:

> For the full system of degree-$d$ forms, the evaluation functionals are the
> degree-$d$ Veronese images of the points, up to nonzero scalar.  Thus the
> first alternative says that the uncovered Veronese images span the dual
> coefficient space; the second supplies a selected Veronese image already in
> their span.  In particular, for the full degree-$d$ system it is enough to
> exhibit $\binom{d+2}{2}$ uncovered points whose Veronese evaluation matrix is
> invertible.  This formulation is not specific to conics.
