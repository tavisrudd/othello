3. **In my own words.**

**Version A.** A signed configuration whose moments cancel through degree two produces an error-detecting quantum code on which separate physical cubic phases act as a coupled logical cubic; conic configurations give explicit invariant-theoretic examples, while a translation construction supplies the same code parameters at every prime at least five. Hessian-rank distributions determine the relevant Pauli expectation magnitudes and prove that the ten-variable example cannot become a product across any nontrivial split under Clifford transformations. For the six-variable example, independent uniform phase noise of rate at most one percent and ideal auxiliary operations give a joint preparation cost below 16.116 raw inputs, against lower bounds of 54 or 36 for the specified independent preparation menus at no worse output error.

**Version B.** I recover essentially the same mathematical result: cancellation of signed quadratic moments yields cubic logical gates, and exact Hessian-rank counts turn the conic examples into distinguishable quantum resources, including the ten-qudit product-exclusion result. The six-qudit code also prepares its target more cheaply than either of two prescribed separate-distillation menus under the stated noise model; this is not a comparison with all possible preparation procedures. A five-dimensional restriction connects the phase to a chordal cubic, although I cannot reconstruct B's two chordal members and their exchange from its exposition alone.

5. **What the preferred version still lacks.**

A needs a compact quantum dictionary at the point where the trade becomes a code: a qudit state as a unit vector, a code as a subspace, logical coordinates as labels for an encoded basis, stabilizer states as the basic resource class, and Clifford operations as the transformations preserving Pauli operators under conjugation. It should then explain why a cubic phase state is a resource for implementing a cubic gate and why this model treats its noisy supply as the cost to minimize.

The highest-value addition would be one worked preparation example, tied to a row of Table 1. Describe the inputs, the stabilizer test, rejection and retry, and the accepted output; define block infidelity as (1-\langle F|\rho_{\mathrm{out}}|F\rangle) for the normalized accepted output. Explain explicitly that the native block produces one six-qudit resource, whereas the separate method assembles that resource from independently purified single-qudit primitives. This would make the quantitative theorem readable without prior familiarity with a “distillation factory.”

Finally, a short takeaway at the start of §4 and relegation of the peripheral optimality vocabulary would preserve the mathematical substance while making the reading route clearer. I do not need elementary explanations of finite fields or invariant theory; I need the links between those familiar calculations and the quantum operations they are claimed to describe.
