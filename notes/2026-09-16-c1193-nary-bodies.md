## Why

The core rule contract admits at most two body atoms (`MAX_BODY`); the Rel lowering binarizes
longer bodies into auxiliary relations (`order_positives`, milestone (a)), materializing every
intermediate join. Soufflé plans n-ary joins natively (C1182, "what Soufflé has that this path
does not"), and C1185 ranked "bodies with more than two atoms" among the evaluator successors.
The programme's step 4 named worst-case-optimal joins (Generic Join / Free Join) as the body
operator. Every intermediate the lowering materializes today is work Soufflé does not do and
memory the C1191 boundary now pays for.

## Deliverable

- Core: rules with up to a declared `MAX_BODY` of at least four atoms, evaluated semi-naively by a
  variable-ordered generic join over the delta atom and the remaining atoms' indexes, without an
  intermediate relation; steps still Tiger records, loop allocation- and call-free; certificates
  unchanged in meaning (one derivation names one rule and its body tuples) and both checkers
  extended to check an n-ary derivation.
- Lowering: binarization becomes a policy, not a necessity. Keep it selectable so the
  binarized program is the measured control for the same source.
- Free Join (leapfrog on the shared variable with hash-trie fallback) is the target shape; a plain
  nested generic join is an acceptable first landing if the measurement says so.

## Acceptance

- A/B: same source, binarized versus n-ary, on the `datalog` cohort and on triangle/path-of-three
