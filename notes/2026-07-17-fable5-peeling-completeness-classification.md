## The question

Fix a locality radius `r`. For a represented matroid `M` (equivalently a linear code with its
Horn/peeling rule system), write `L_r(S)` for the sequential radius-`r` Horn closure of a survivor
set `S`, and `cl(S)` for matroid closure. Define the property:

> **P(r):** for every survivor set `S`, `L_r(S) = cl(S)`.

**Classify the matroids satisfying P(r)** — ideally by excluded minors, failing that by a
structural/composition characterization.

Operationally, `P(r)` says bounded-locality peeling achieves symbol-MAP erasure decoding on every
erasure pattern. It is the matroid-general form of the classical stopping-set-versus-MAP question
for iterative BEC decoding, where no general characterization exists.

## Context: what is already in hand

- **A positive infinite family:** C236 proves the cubic–axis family satisfies `L_3(S) = cl(S)`
  for every seed over every `GF(3^h)`.
- **A maximal negative witness:** the harmonic family contains a seed whose radius-four sequential
  closure is inert while the seed spans — peeling recovers nothing although the information is
  complete.
- **A composition calculus:** C231 (2-sum interface algebra), C233 (finite structural-control
  algebra bounded by radius and interface width), C234 (finite delay syntax). These are the tools a
  closure-property proof will consume.
- **The far side is real and thin:** propagation completeness (PC) and unit-refutation completeness
  are established notions in knowledge compilation; Horn does **not** imply PC (Čepek–Kučera
  counterexample); deciding PC for general CNF is co-NP-hard; the mining pass found nothing on PC
  for matroid-structured Horn systems, with the seam mostly open-access and the founding PC paper
  already in the lit cache. [Mining ledger, L1/L2 — kill/naturality steps run, L3 read unpaid.]

## Motivation

1. **In-program:** C238 names "a theorem beyond matroid 2-sums" as Paper B's promotion gate. This
   classification is that theorem's natural form; it lifts `ports-composition` from a new calculus
   to a result quoted outside the program.
2. **Coding theory:** any characterization — even for a restricted minor-closed class — is the
   first structural answer to stopping-sets-vs-MAP at the matroid level of generality.
3. **Logic/AI:** the contrast statement writes itself if the mathematics cooperates: *PC is
   co-NP-hard for general CNF but structurally characterizable for represented-matroid Horn
   systems.* A dual-audience theorem in the exact sense of the gems method's value predicates.
4. **The witnesses are ours alone:** no other group holds a certified positive family and a
   certified maximal negative witness for the same property.

## Desired outcomes, tiered

- **A+:** excluded-minor characterization of `P(r)` (for fixed small `r`, or uniformly with the
  obstruction set depending on `r`).
- **A:** `P(r)` is minor-closed plus an explicit partial obstruction list containing the harmonic
  witness's minimal inert minor; or a complete characterization within a natural subclass
  (e.g. the class generated by the program's families under 2-sums).
- **B+ (still shippable):** closure/non-closure theorems for minors, duals, and 2-sums, plus the
  co-NP contrast framing — a structural-behavior paper even without full classification.
- **Decisive negative (valuable):** `P(r)` is *not* minor-closed, with a certified counterexample
  pair. This redirects the classification toward 2-sum-generated classes and is itself a citable
  fact about the property's nature.

## Research plan

**Phase 0 — dedupe and vet (gate).** Vet of the gap review and mining ledger; dedupe against C239
and the prior Fable notes; verify the C236/C231/C233/C234 statements against their primary reports.

**Phase 1 — computational probes on existing stock (cheap, decisive ordering information).**
1. Implement a `P(r)` checker for small ground sets (direct closure-vs-`L_r` sweep over survivor
   sets; the certificate infrastructure already computes both sides).
2. Test minor-closedness empirically: for every committed matroid instance with known `P(r)`
   status, check all single-element deletions/contractions. One violation settles Phase 2's shape.
3. Test dual-closedness and 2-sum closure the same way.
4. Locate the *minimal inert witness*: minimize the harmonic negative example under minors to find
   the smallest matroid failing `P(r)`. This is the first candidate excluded minor.

**Phase 2 — closure-property theorems.** Prove or refute, in the order the probes suggest:
contraction-closure, deletion-closure, 2-sum closure (via C231), duality behavior.

**Phase 3 — obstruction mining.** If minor-closed: enumerate minor-minimal non-`P(r)` matroids by
exhaustive search over small ground sets (standard matroid databases up to 9 elements suffice for
a first obstruction census), against the Phase-1 checker.

**Phase 4 — the characterization.** Either the excluded-minor statement with a finite (or
`r`-indexed) obstruction list, or the subclass characterization; write the PC/co-NP contrast as
the second framing. Feed the result into `ports-composition` per its promotion gate.

**Phase 5 — formalization.** The objects are finite and already inside the certificate discipline.
An excluded-minor theorem decomposes into (a) closure lemmas (inductive, Lean-friendly), and
(b) finitely many obstruction checks (pure `decide`-style certificates). This is among the most
formalizable classification statements available to the program.

## Starting theorems and proof spines

All items below are **candidate** lemmas with proposed spines — none is proved, and the spines are
plausibility sketches to be validated or discarded in Phase 2.

**Candidate Lemma 1 (contraction).** *If `M` satisfies `P(r)`, so does `M/e`.*
Spine: survivor sets of `M/e` correspond to survivor sets of `M` containing `e` as known; a
radius-`r` peeling step in `M/e` lifts to a radius-`≤ r+1` step in `M` using `e`; the delta between
`r` and `r+1` is where the spine can fail, and where the finite delay syntax (C234) should absorb
the slack. Failure mode to watch: contraction can *create* short rules that `M` lacked, so the
converse direction (peeling in `M` projecting to `M/e`) is the safer half.

**Candidate Lemma 2 (deletion).** *If `M` satisfies `P(r)`, so does `M \ e`.*
Spine: deletion removes a helper; `L_r` in `M\e` is computed with a subset of `M`'s rules, so the
inclusion `L_r^{M\e}(S) ⊆ L_r^{M}(S)` is immediate, but `cl` also shrinks. The dangerous case is a
survivor set whose only short derivations in `M` route through `e`. Expect this lemma to be the
one that *fails* if `P(r)` is not minor-closed; Phase 1's probe should target exactly this
configuration (elements lying on many short circuits).

**Candidate Lemma 3 (2-sum).** *If `M₁`, `M₂` satisfy `P(r)`, so does their 2-sum.*
Spine: C231's interface algebra reduces cross-boundary derivations to a scalar budget on the
basepoint; a peeling schedule for the 2-sum interleaves schedules on the parts, exchanging the
basepoint's status at most boundedly often (C233's control algebra bounds the exchanges by radius
and interface width). This is the lemma the existing machinery was practically built to prove, and
the natural first Phase-2 target.

**Candidate Theorem 4 (contrast).** *Deciding `P(r)` for general Horn systems is co-NP-hard;
deciding it for represented matroids over a fixed field lies in [class to be determined].*
Spine: hardness imports from the PC literature (Čepek–Kučera line); the upper bound rides on
whatever structure Phases 2–3 expose — an excluded-minor theorem with a finite list gives a
polynomial-time minor-check via matroid-minor testing within the represented class.

**Candidate Theorem 5 (the classification).** Shape depends on Phase 1. The two live shapes:
(a) *`M` satisfies `P(r)` iff `M` has no minor in the finite list `X_r`*, with the minimal inert
harmonic minor as the first member; (b) *the `P(r)` matroids are exactly the 2-sum closure of a
base class* (positive families + degenerate cases), if minor-closedness fails.

## Risks and unknowns

- The property may depend on the representation, not only the matroid (the Horn rule system sees
  field structure; C236's uniformity over `GF(3^h)` is encouraging but not decisive). Phase 1
  should include one same-matroid different-representation probe before the classification
  language is fixed.
- The radius parameter may not stabilize: `P(r)` could be strict in `r` forever, making the clean
  statement `P := ∃r P(r)` or a per-`r` family of theorems. Either is publishable; the writing
  differs.
- The PC contrast requires the L3 read of the PC founding papers (priced, in cache) before any
  claim of novelty on the logic side is made.
