# C907 (B=C=0) mixed-star initial-form certificate

**Lane:** `clebsch`

**Status:** exact maximal (B=C=0) boundary-star calculation; not a full
compactification fan.

## Scope

This certificate closes every torus-orbit initial system for which both
`B,C` tend to zero, while the three `y` coordinates take arbitrary toric
weights.  It includes all weights with some `p_i>2` or `p_i<0`; those weights
are not an automatic empty face.  It does not cover a face where `B` or `C`
has valuation zero, the translated `B=1` or `C=1` charts, or a normalization
of a global compactification.

Write, after a finite ramified base change clearing the rational weights,

\[
 y_i=\delta^{-p_i}x_i,\qquad
 B=\delta^\beta b,\qquad C=\delta^\gamma c,
 \qquad \beta,\gamma>0,
\]

with all `x_i,b,c` torus units.  Put `p=p_1+p_2+p_3` and

\[
m=\max\{2,p_1,p_2,p_3,\beta+\gamma-p\}.
\tag{1}
\]

The transformed graph equation is the monomial-normalized saturated principal
equation

\[
\begin{aligned}
G={}&Xbc\left(\delta^mL-
 \sum_i\delta^{m-p_i}x_i
 -\delta^{m-(\beta+\gamma-p)}\frac Q{Xbc}\\
&\hspace{82pt}-\delta^{m-2}
 (1-\delta^\beta b)(1-\delta^\gamma c)\right)=0,
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

where `X=x_1x_2x_3`.  On the dense torus it is exactly
`delta^m Xbc(L-F_delta)=0`.  A toric chart chooses the corresponding monomial
multiple of the pulled-back cleared graph.  At least one displayed coefficient
has `delta`-order zero by (1), so $G$ is not divisible by `delta`; after
localizing at `Xbc`, its ideal is already saturated by `delta` and by every
remaining torus unit.  The reduced central induced graph is

\[
 H=\sum_{p_i=m}x_i+
 \mathbf1_{\beta+\gamma-p=m}\frac Q{Xbc}+
 \mathbf1_{m=2}.
\tag{3}
\]

It contains no $L$.  This is the required order: graph saturation, central
reduction, then the relative tangent-Fitting calculation.

## Exact alternative

For every cone in this star, one and only one of the following occurs.

1. If (3) is a single torus monomial or a nonzero constant, its saturated
   graph ideal is the unit ideal: the cone is **empty**.
2. Otherwise (3) has at least two monomials.  If its polar monomial occurs,
   $D_bH=-Q/(Xbc)$ is a unit on the torus.  If not, any occurring $x_i$
   has $D_{x_i}H=x_i$, also a unit.  Thus the central graph is smooth,
   hence reduced and normal; the total graph is smooth near that stratum,
   and $L$ is a free cotangent
   summand.  Its reduced-stratum relative tangent-Fitting ideal is `(1)`:
   the cone is **free**.

There is no residual case in this star, because `B,C` vanish whereas the
marked residual Rees center has `B=C=1`.

This exhausts the full `B=C=0` star: a bounded graph arc must have at least
two terms at the maximal pole order (1), exactly the support tested above.
The assertion is about its central initial graph.  It does not claim that a
finite global fan, normal crossing model, or collar system has been built.

## Finite replay

The fixed support package has the five possible leading monomials

\[
 x_1,x_2,x_3, Q/(Xbc),1.
\]

The generator enumerates all 31 nonempty support masks, clears the unit denominator,
saturates first by `Xbc`, then computes the tangent-logarithmic critical
ideal of the **reduced initial graph** and saturates it by the same units.
It reports exactly 5 empty one-monomial/constant masks and 26 free masks;
there are no holds.  Setting `Q=1` is harmless because nonzero `Q` is a
torus coefficient unit.

## Consequence for outside weights

The previously unhandled regimes are explicitly free whenever their leading
support is nonempty.  Within this `B=C=0` star, examples are:

\[
 p_1=P>2,\quad p_2=p_3=0,\quad \beta=\gamma=P,
\]

whose leading equation is $x_1+Q/(Xbc)=0$.  If
`p_1=-P`, `p_2=p_3=0`, and `0<P<2`, take
`beta+gamma=2-P`; then the leading equation is
$1+Q/(Xbc)=0$.  For $P\ge2$ that particular negative-weight direction has
the polar monomial alone and is empty.  Formula (3), rather than a bound on
the $p_i$, is the uniform certificate.

## Boundary

This calculation does not replace the translated finite-pole charts, the
compact residual `B=C=1` chart, their overlaps, the normalization of arbitrary
global initial schemes, or topological collars.
