## Step 1 — group verification in code

### What the order-2092 search actually enumerates

The campaign's live arm has three representations, all four-block and all scoring the same law
at a different level of the quotient ladder `Z/522 -> Z/174 -> Z/87 -> Z/58 -> Z/29`.

| Object | Rust type | Where | What an entry is |
|---------------------|--------------------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| `q29` margin shell  | `[[i8; 29]; 4]`          | `src/order6_margin_tabu.rs:57-63`    | even coset sum of signs, `-18..18`      |
| `q174` count state  | `[[u8; 174]; 4]`         | `src/order6_phase_two.rs:180`        | count of `+1` in a fibre of three, `0..3` |
| carrier sign matrix | `[[i8; 522]; 4]`         | `src/order6_carrier.rs:147`          | one sign, `+-1`                         |

The objective is the same function at every level: the combined periodic autocorrelation of the
four blocks against a target that is **flat off the zero shift**. At the carrier it is `2088` at
shift zero and `-4` at all others (`src/order6_carrier.rs:143-144`, checked by `const _`
assertions); at `q29` it is `2020` and `-72` (`src/order6_margin_tabu.rs:66-68`), and the general
aggregation law is `quotient_targets` at `src/order6_phase_two.rs:1258`. The scoring functions
verified against are `direct_carrier_score` (`src/order6_carrier.rs:878`) and `direct_score`
(`src/order6_margin_tabu.rs:489`).

The flatness of the off-zero target is the single load-bearing fact for the whole group: it is
what lets a multiplier permute the shift classes without changing the score.

### Which transformations the existing code already treats as invariances

`src/order6_galois.rs` is the only place the campaign reasons about the group, and it does so to
*exclude* elements from its move set, not to quotient by them:

- `src/order6_galois.rs:27-31` — "the shift `s` drops out entirely: **a per-block shift is an
  exact symmetry of the whole objective** and can never be a move."
- `src/order6_galois.rs:36-39` — "The diagonal of the per-block group — one multiplier applied to
  all four blocks at once — permutes the whole combined correlation and so leaves the objective
  identically fixed. It is a relabelling of the state, not a move."

Those are exactly two of the five families below. Nothing in the campaign uses either as a
quotient: the tabu step (`src/order6_margin_tabu.rs:338`), the carrier step and the corpus writers
all store raw states.

The **multiplier program** is a different mechanism and is not an invariance of the search at all.
`src/multiplier_shard_window.rs` and `src/projected_orbit_min_cost.rs` implement it: a shard
*assumes* each `X_i` is fixed by the subgroup generated by one unit of `Z/522`, then decides the
level identity `sum_i sum_c B_{i,c}^2 = 1043 + 520 (f - 1)` as a subset-sum over projected orbits.
That is the lossy direction — it restricts the search to a fixed-point set `X^H` and an empty
shard says nothing about states outside `X^H`. The card records it as closed: 148 of 167
nontrivial units are emptied, and the surviving assumptions are weak. Quotienting, by contrast,
requires no `X_i` to be invariant under anything.

### The group that is actually valid for the code's representation

Five families, each verified in code by applying it to states and re-scoring with the campaign's
own direct scorers (`src/order6_orbit_canonical.rs`, tests
`every_generator_family_fixes_the_q29_objective` and
`every_generator_family_fixes_the_carrier_objective`, both passing):

| # | Family | Why it fixes the score | Count at level `N` |
|---|---------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|------------------------|
| 1 | per-block translation     | each `A_b` is shift-invariant                    | `N^4`                  |
| 2 | per-block index inversion | `A_b(-g) = A_b(g)`; independent across blocks    | `2^4`                  |
| 3 | common multiplier         | permutes shift classes; off-zero target is flat  | `|U(Z/N)| / 2` further |
| 4 | per-block value negation  | `A_b` is quadratic in `y_b`                      | `2^4`                  |
| 5 | block permutation         | the score is a symmetric sum over blocks         | `4! = 24`              |

Family 3 is counted after family 2 because `-1` is a unit, so the diagonal inversion is already
in family 2. Families 1-3 compose as `p -> t (eps_b p) + s_b` with `t` common and `(eps_b, s_b)`
per block; that set is closed, which is what makes the canonical form below exact.

**Negative control, also in code.** A multiplier applied to *one* block is not a symmetry unless
it is `+-1` (test `a_single_block_multiplier_is_not_a_symmetry`). This is the same fact
`order6_galois` relies on when it prices per-block twists as genuine moves, so the group does not
extend in that direction.

### Group order, against the C1151 part B claim

Two scopes, both exact groups, differing in which declared constraint they must also preserve.
`OrbitScope::Objective` fixes the score and nothing else. `OrbitScope::SeedScope` additionally
preserves the campaign's declared seeding law `signed_sum == (block == 0)`
(`src/q29_inventory_scope.rs:494`), which pins block zero: value negation flips a row sum, so it
is admitted on blocks one to three only, and the block permutation is the `S3` on those blocks.

| Level | Scope | Verified order | C1151 part B claim | Ratio |
|-----------|-----------|----------------------|--------------------|-----------|
| carrier   | objective | `522^4 * 516,096`    | `522^4 * 6`        | `86,016x` |
| carrier   | seed      | `522^4 * 64,512`     | `522^4 * 6`        | `10,752x` |
| `q29`     | objective | `29^4 * 86,016`      | —                  | —         |
| `q29`     | seed      | `29^4 * 10,752`      | —                  | —         |

Gated by `the_group_orders_are_the_declared_products`. `|U(Z/522)| = 168` and `|U(Z/29)| = 28`,
both computed rather than asserted.

**Verdict on the claim.** The C1151 part B derivation (`2026-09-12-c1151-part-b...md` §3.5) is
**confirmed and is a strict undercount**. Its part (i), independent translation, and part (iii),
permutation of the three equal blocks, are both real. Its part (ii), common unit multiplication,
is real but was not carried into the arithmetic. Two further families it does not name —
independent per-block index inversion and independent per-block value negation — are each worth a
factor of up to sixteen. Its stated worry, that "the Goethals--Seidel array and the border impose
extra conditions that kill (iii)", does not arise in the code: the block permutation is a symmetry
of the objective as written, and the only thing distinguishing block zero is the declared seeding
row-sum law, which costs the factor of four between the two scopes above, not the whole family.

**Where the multiplier program is lossy, exactly.** It searches `X^H` for `H` generated by a unit;
a solution whose four blocks are not individually `H`-invariant is unreachable, and the card's own
provenance rule then confines an empty shard to that shard. The quotient here replaces the
assumption with a canonical form: it never removes a state from the search space, only decides
which representative of an orbit is visited.

### Why that is the expected answer, and what it does say

Every banked state was measured for its exact stabiliser, and the stabiliser is trivial almost
everywhere:

| Level | Scope | `|G|` | States with trivial stabiliser |
|-------|-----------|-----------------------|--------------------------------|
| 29    | objective | `60,837,482,496`      | 672 of 675                     |
| 29    | seed      | `7,604,685,312`       | 672 of 675                     |
| 174   | objective | `157,690,754,629,632` | 100 of 100                     |
| 522   | objective | `38,318,853,375,000,576` | 72 of 72                    |
| 522   | seed      | `4,789,856,671,875,072`  | 72 of 72                    |

The three exceptions at `q29` have stabilisers of order two. So the group acts essentially freely
on the states this campaign banks, and each orbit holds about `6.1e10` states at `q29` and about
`3.8e16` at the carrier. Two consequences point in opposite directions and both are real:

- **For enumeration the factor is the whole group order.** An exhaustive arm that visits one
  representative per orbit does `|G|` times less work, and with trivial stabilisers that factor is
  not eroded. This is the case for canonical augmentation on any *enumerating* arm.
- **For corpus dedup and restart seeding the factor is worthless.** Two independent states collide
  only if one lies in the other's orbit, and an orbit is a vanishing fraction of a space large
  enough that the `q174` fibre alone holds about `2^1322` exact solutions. A zero collision count
  over 847 banked states is the arithmetically expected result, not a surprise, and no larger
  corpus would change it.

## Step 3 — the smallest measurable prototype

### What was built

Opt-in orbit dedup on `q29` restart seeding, on the spike branch only.

- `OrbitSeedFilter` (`src/order6_orbit_canonical.rs`): a fixed-capacity exact orbit filter. Its
  key is the whole canonical state, never a digest, so a refusal means the two seeds genuinely
  share an orbit. The table is preallocated and never grows; a full table stops filtering and
  counts the overflow rather than reallocating or silently narrowing the search.
- `tabu_order6_margin_outer_epochs` (`src/order6_margin_tabu.rs`) takes an optional filter and
  applies it **at the cold epoch boundary**, where the sampler and the deadline test already live.
  A refused draw skips the epoch and redraws. The full-neighbourhood tabu step, its Gram
  maintenance and its kick are untouched: no line inside `Q29MarginTabu::step` changed, and the
  filter allocates nothing after construction.
- `order6 margin-tabu --orbit-dedup --orbit-capacity --orbit-scope` exposes it. With the flag off
  the code path is the retained control's exactly.

## Step 4 — conclusions

### Group verification: claim against code

| C1151 part B §3.5 claim | Verdict in code |
|---------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| Independent translation of each block, `522^4`     | Confirmed; `order6_galois.rs:27-31` already says so  |
| Common unit multiplication is a symmetry           | Confirmed; `order6_galois.rs:36-39` already says so, but it was not carried into the claimed order |
| Permutation of the three equal blocks, `3!`        | Confirmed, and understated: the objective admits all `4!` |
| "the Goethals--Seidel array and the border impose extra conditions that kill (iii)" | Does not happen; the only thing distinguishing block zero is the declared seeding row-sum law |
| Per-block index inversion                          | Not named in the claim; is a symmetry, `2^4`, independent across blocks |
| Per-block value negation                           | Not named in the claim; is a symmetry, `2^4`         |
| Claimed order `522^4 * 6`                          | Verified order `522^4 * 516,096` at the carrier, `86,016` times larger |
| Per-block multiplier other than `+-1`              | Verified **not** a symmetry — the negative control    |

The declared seeding scope is not a lossy restriction. A zero of the objective forces exactly one
block to have block sum `+-2`; negating that block and permuting it into position zero always
reaches `signed_sum == (block == 0)`, so the scope is a complete transversal of the negation and
block-permutation families and its index is exactly eight. **The campaign is already quotienting
by a factor of eight without naming it, and the remaining factor of `4.8e15` at the carrier is
unexploited.**

### Orbit counts

847 banked states at three levels; **zero orbit merges beyond plain equality** at every level and
in both scopes. Restart runs add about 660 more restarts and 290 epoch best states, all
orbit-distinct. The group acts with a trivial stabiliser on 844 of the 847 banked states, so each
orbit holds the full group order.

### Prototype

Opt-in orbit dedup on `q29` restart seeding: distinct starting points unchanged, best margin
unchanged, cost at most 0.8% of steps at campaign-default epochs and indistinguishable from zero
at short epochs. The inner tabu loop is untouched. Exact commands are in the Step 3 replay block.

### What is not claimed

- **No search-space reduction was demonstrated.** The quotient was measured on corpus dedup and
  restart seeding, where it is worthless, and not on an enumerating arm, where the factor lives.
- **The verified group is a lower bound on the symmetry group, not its full description.** Five
  families are proved symmetries and per-block multipliers other than `+-1` are proved not to be.
  Nothing rules out a non-affine permutation of `Z/N` that preserves every block autocorrelation.
- **`|G|` is not the reduction factor for a constrained enumeration.** An arm that enumerates a
  set `S` cut out by extra conditions gains only the subgroup carrying `S` into itself, which can
  be far smaller. The card's `q18` arm enumerates 1,984,512 sealed source interfaces against a
  group of order `7.6e9` at `q29`, so most of the group cannot act inside that set at all. Any
  claimed enumeration speedup has to measure the acting subgroup first.
- **No negative coverage is claimed anywhere.** No enumeration was re-run, no shard was closed, and
  the zero collision counts are statements about the named files and runs only.
- **Nothing in the campaign's evidence was modified.** The Williamson and pair-join model, the
  banked 14,800 margin and the multiplier program are untouched; the live authority worktree was
  read and never written.

### Recommended next allocation

**Do not build corpus dedup or restart-seed dedup into the campaign.** Measured worthless, twice.
The prototype stays opt-in on the spike branch as the instrument that proved it.

The one place the quotient can still pay is an **enumerating** arm, and it needs a measurement
before it needs an implementation. In priority order:

1. **Measure the acting subgroup of one enumeration before writing any canonical augmentation.**
   Take the exhaustive two-transfer census — the smallest banked exact enumeration, and C1151 part
   B experiment F's own target — and compute how many group elements carry its enumerated set into
   itself. That number, not `|G|`, is the available factor. If it is small the whole row closes as
   a measured negative; if it is large, canonical augmentation is worth building and its gate is
   McKay's coverage theorem, reproducing the original census up to the equivalence rather than
   approximating it. This is the highest-expected-value successor and it is cheap.
2. **Sampled orbit revisiting inside one tabu run.** The census answered whether independent
   restarts collide; it did not answer whether one run re-enters an orbit it already visited, which
   is the question C1151 part B §3.5(c) actually raised about tabu tenure. Canonicalising every
   step is forbidden — it would destroy the incremental delta structure — but canonicalising every
   `2^k`-th visited state is cold, cheap and answers it. Gate: a measured revisit rate, with the
   hot loop untouched.
3. **Only then, and only if (1) says the factor is real, the canonical augmentation itself.**

`q18` and `q29` enumeration arms are the natural targets for (1) because their groups are smallest
and their enumerations are the ones the card says grant negative coverage.
