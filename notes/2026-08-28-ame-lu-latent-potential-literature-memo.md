## Executive assessment

The cleanup exposed a stronger organizing object than the paper currently advertises: an AME stabilizer label space in a half-set chart is a **rank-two symplectic local system** (equivalently, a small quiver representation) whose edge transports are the invertible blocks of the systematic matrix and whose fundamental four-cycle holonomies determine fixed-party local equivalence and symmetry.

That interpretation is not cosmetic. It compresses several results into one standard algebraic statement:

> In prime local dimension, the fixed-party transversal symmetry group is the norm-one unit group of the commutant algebra generated by the four-cycle holonomies.

The five symmetry cases already proved in the paper are then exactly the five possible commutant types for a two-dimensional representation: the full matrix algebra on the maximally symmetric locus, scalars on the generic locus, and the three two-dimensional commutative algebras (split semisimple, nonsplit field, and dual numbers) on the intermediate loci. This is the strongest immediate upgrade. It turns an order table into a structure theorem, gives a natural moduli stratification, and points directly toward extension fields.

The next two best opportunities are:

1. prove that the generic AME stabilizer state has only central fixed-party symmetry, with the exceptional symmetry types forming explicit proper algebraic strata; and
2. determine what extra code structure is forced by each non-generic commutant type—especially whether the nonsplit field case is precisely hidden extension-field linearity and whether the dual-number case is excluded or sharply constrained by the AME all-minor condition.

Those are potentially paper-level results. By contrast, improving the unknown-party algorithm and extracting a robust certification protocol are promising but less immediate: the former meets graph/code-isomorphism barriers, and the latter needs a genuinely quantitative stability argument rather than another exact lemma.

My overall judgment is that the recent mathematical pass was a **large conceptual upgrade** even before any new theorem is added. It changed the work from a collection of exact AME-specific tests into the beginning of a representation-theoretic classification. The manuscript should remain frozen until the first two claims below are proved or killed; premature reframing would risk overpromising.

## Literature benchmark

### Closest current local-equivalence work

The strongest direct comparison is the 2025 Claudet–Perdrix program. Their [STACS paper](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2025.27) gives a complete graphical characterization of LU equivalence of qubit graph states using generalized local complementations. Their [ICALP paper](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ICALP.2025.59) bounds the required level logarithmically and obtains a quasi-polynomial recognition algorithm; it also proves LU=LC through 19 qubits. A subsequent [2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25219) pushes the minimum LU-not-LC counterexample result to 27 qubits and connects the obstruction to triorthogonal/Reed–Muller structure.

[Burchardt–de Jong–Vandré](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03961) independently reduce qubit stabilizer LU equivalence to modular linear equations after deriving a constrained form for the local unitaries. Their method is constructive in practice but does not claim a general polynomial runtime; their enumeration verifies equality of LU and LC orbit counts through 11 qubits.

The classical baseline remains the minimal-support method of [Van den Nest–Dehaene–De Moor](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0411115) and [Zeng–Chung–Cross–Chuang](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0611214): suitable coverage by minimal stabilizer supports forces local factors to be Clifford. The 27-qubit counterexample of [Ji–Chen–Wei–Ying](https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.1266) shows why such rigidity cannot be assumed for arbitrary stabilizer states. [Englbrecht–Kraus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.07106) place local stabilizer-state symmetries in a broader group-theoretic setting and explain their connection to transversal gates.

The AME results occupy a genuinely sharper special regime. The AME projection property makes every half-set a systematic chart; minimum-support subgroups are not merely a cover but an optimal direct-sum generating family; and the local-equivalence test reduces to simultaneous conjugacy of a fixed family of $2\times2$ holonomies in prime dimension. Thus the right comparison is:

- general qubit graph states: complete but quasi-polynomial graphical recognition;
- general stabilizer states: constructive modular systems, without a proved general polynomial bound in the cited algorithm;
- AME stabilizer states here: fixed-label recognition by explicit finite-field linear algebra, with an exact symmetry classification.

This algorithmic contrast is already a substantive result. It should not be inflated into an unrestricted polynomial-time LU algorithm: the paper fixes parties and uses AME rigidity.

### Adjacent AME and code literature

[Huber–Grassl](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2020-06-18-284/) show that quantum MDS weight distributions are determined by code parameters and derive bounds using exact enumerator identities. This supports treating AME/QMDS states as a rigid class, but it does not give the local-equivalence or symmetry classification here.

[Ramadas–Lakshminarayan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.04096) construct scalar LU invariants for general AME states arising from orthogonal arrays and exhibit infinite LU families. Their work reinforces the need to distinguish complete orbit data from a selected scalar invariant. It does not replace the stabilizer-specific transport classification.

[Tan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.19677) studies qutrit AME states, their LU orbits, and their relation to transversal symmetries. It is the closest recent AME-specific conceptual neighbor and makes careful novelty separation important: the present advantage is the all-length additive stabilizer theorem and the algorithmic atlas, not the general observation that AME symmetries encode transversal operations.

[Sarvepalli](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.1171) relates representable symplectic matroids to linear quantum codes and connects self-dual Lagrangian matroids to secret sharing. The current AME systematic criterion resembles a uniform representable symplectic object, but the paper permits arbitrary additive $\mathbf F_p$-linear label spaces with $2e$-dimensional party blocks. The standard term would therefore be a representable symplectic polymatroid or a block symplectic matroid, not an ordinary $\mathbf F_q$-linear symplectic matroid. This is an avenue, not a ready-made identification.

### What the award-winning comparisons actually show

The two closest 2025 LU papers are top-venue papers, but I found no official record that either received its conference best-paper award. They should not be called award winners.

The nearest useful award benchmarks are deliberately broader:

- [Panteleev–Kalachev](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03654), officially listed as a [STOC 2022 Best Paper](https://www.sigact.sigact.hosting.acm.org/prizes/best_paper.html), turns an algebraic code construction into a decisive asymptotic QEC result.
- [Gupta–He–O’Donnell](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.11355), marked in the official [QIP 2026 programme](https://app.onlineexpo.com/events/qip-2026/en/schedules/9f9349c5-a18a-45be-a1cd-aa3e1f890414/export/pdf) as Best Student Paper, converts a structural question about pure states into a sharp operational certification theorem using $O(n\varepsilon^{-1}\log(1/\delta))$ copies and only single-qubit measurements.

These papers are not precedence for the AME theorem. They are useful quality controls. Award-level framing tends to combine a clean structural idea with a memorable consequence: a solved existence problem, an asymptotic bound, or an explicit operational algorithm. For the present work, the most plausible such consequences are the commutant classification plus either a generic-symmetry theorem or a robust certification bound.

## The latent master theorem

Fix a half-set (B). In systematic form the label space is

\[
L=\{(x,K_Bx):x\in V_B\},
\]

where $K_B$ is anti-symplectic and every party-aligned square block required by AME is invertible. Write its $2\times2$ blocks in prime dimension as transports from the parties of $B$ to those of $B^c$. Choosing one row and one column as a tree trivialization leaves $(m-1)^2$ fundamental four-cycle holonomies. A change of local Clifford frames is a gauge transformation; after tree normalization, it acts by simultaneous conjugation on the holonomies.

Let $\mathcal A_B\subseteq M_2(\mathbf F_q)$ be the unital algebra generated by these holonomies and let

\[
\mathcal E_B=\operatorname{Cent}_{M_2(\mathbf F_q)}(\mathcal A_B).
\]

The fixed-party linear symmetries are the determinant-one units of $\mathcal E_B$, up to the already-understood central/projective conventions. The five existing cases become:

| generated/commutant behavior | standard algebraic type | determinant-one symmetry |
|---|---|---|
| holonomies scalar | $M_2(\mathbf F_q)$ | $\mathrm{SL}_2(q)$ |
| holonomies generate a noncommutative algebra | $\mathbf F_q$ | center |
| common split eigenspaces | $\mathbf F_q\times\mathbf F_q$ | split torus, order $q-1$ |
| irreducible common quadratic algebra | $\mathbf F_{q^2}$ | norm-one torus, order $q+1$ |
| common nonsemisimple flag | $\mathbf F_q[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ | Jordan centralizer, order $q\,\gcd(2,q-1)$ |

This formulation has four advantages.

First, it explains why there are exactly five cases; they are not a fortuitous finite subgroup list. Second, it makes the group order a corollary of the algebra type. Third, it identifies the symmetry computation with a standard endomorphism-ring computation. For $q=p^e$, the intrinsic endomorphism algebra still exists, but the symmetry group must preserve every symplectic form transported from the parties to the base frame. It is generally an intersection of several transported symplectic groups inside the unit group of the algebra, not a single “symplectic commutant.”

The principal proof obligation is invariance under changing the half-set and tree normalization. The symmetry group is intrinsically invariant, but the generated algebra may be presented differently. The clean statement may need Morita-equivalence language, or simply an explicit conjugacy theorem for $\mathcal E_B$. This should be proved before adopting “local system” terminology in the manuscript.

There is a tempting invariant-theory corollary. For quiver representations over characteristic zero, traces along oriented cycles generate the invariant ring (the Le Bruyn–Procesi theorem; see this [modern account](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.08527)). For simultaneous $2\times2$ conjugacy one expects a short trace-word separating family. However, finite-field matrix invariants can require additional generators; see [Campbell et al., *Invariants of 2×2-Matrices over Finite Fields*](https://doi.org/10.1006/ffta.2002.0359). Therefore the safe research question is to find a finite-field separating set for this restricted holonomy locus—not to claim that cycle traces alone are complete.

## Ranked theorem program

### Tier 1: immediate, high-value mathematics

#### 1. Commutant-algebra classification

Prove the master theorem above and derive the five symmetry groups from the algebra classification. This should shorten the current proof while strengthening its conceptual content and auditability.

**Success criterion:** one intrinsic theorem, one elementary classification lemma for unital subalgebras/commutants of $M_2(\mathbf F_q)$, and the existing order table as a corollary.

**Red-team issue:** the central symmetry order in even characteristic and the distinction between linear, projective, and unitary lifts must remain explicit. Do not silently identify $\mathrm{Sp}_2(q)$, $\mathrm{SL}_2(q)$, and their projective images where kernels differ.

#### 2. Generic minimal symmetry and exceptional strata

The AME condition is a finite nonvanishing condition on party-aligned block minors, hence a Zariski-open locus inside the anti-symplectic systematic matrices. Having a nonscalar commuting endomorphism is an algebraic condition. This strongly suggests:

> On every nonempty irreducible AME component containing one transport system with two noncommuting holonomies, the generic fixed-party symmetry is the center; the split, nonsplit, Jordan, and maximally symmetric cases lie in proper algebraic subvarieties.

The hypothesis cannot hold for $m\le3$: the same-day note proves that the
four- and six-party anti-symplectic block relations force all fundamental
holonomies to commute. An exact eight-party example over $\mathbf F_7$
shows that the generic-center regime is nonempty from $m=4$ onward.

Over finite fields, a quantitative version could bound the exceptional proportion by (O_m(1/q)), after degrees and component hypotheses are controlled.

**Why it matters:** it converts the classification into a distributional theorem: high symmetry is exceptional, and the exceptional mechanisms are named.

**Red-team issue:** “generic” over a finite field needs a geometric ambient family and nonemptiness. One example proves properness only on the component containing it. Reducibility and small fields cannot be swept aside.

#### 3. Hidden structure forced by each exceptional algebra

The algebra types force extra code structure:

- split algebra: two invariant local axes and a CSS decomposition into paired classical MDS codes;
- nonsplit field algebra: a canonical $\mathbf F_{q^2}$-linear, weighted Hermitian self-dual MDS code;
- dual numbers: a free block-MDS module over $\mathbf F_q[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$;
- full matrix commutant: a weighted self-dual classical MDS code tensored with the local symplectic plane.

The remaining sharp goal is to package the converse directions with the exact local-Clifford and coordinate-weight conventions used in the papers, and to determine which strata occur for each parameter set.

**Red-team issue:** do not identify the split stratum with CSS or the nonsplit stratum with extension-field linearity without checking compatibility across every party and with the symplectic form. A local invariant algebra need not globalize automatically.

### Tier 2: strong but requires a new idea

#### 4. Extension-field commutant theorem

For $q=p^e$, the relevant local symplectic space has dimension $2e$ over $\mathbf F_p$. The same transport construction gives an intrinsic endomorphism algebra inside $M_{2e}(\mathbf F_p)$. Fixed-party symmetries are the units that preserve the full transported family of alternating forms. Artin–Wedderburn theory organizes the semisimple part; the radical and these simultaneous form constraints control the actual group.

A useful theorem would classify possible commutants under the AME all-minor condition, or at least give a polynomial algorithm that outputs the algebra decomposition and symmetry group order. This would replace ad hoc extension-field caveats with a standard algebraic framework.

**Risk:** the number of algebra types grows rapidly with $e$, and semilinear Frobenius equivalences are not captured by an $\mathbf F_p$-linear commutant alone. Aim first for a structure/algorithm theorem, not a closed five-case list.

#### 5. Robust reconstruction and certification

The optimal decomposition

\[
L=\bigoplus_{j\notin B}L(B\cup\{j\})
\]

splits any stabilizer-character discrepancy into (m) independent (q^2)-character problems. This is the best latent route to robustness. Instead of accumulating worst-case phase error over a large stabilizer generating set, use character orthogonality or Parseval estimates on the direct-sum factors.

Possible targets are:

- a sharper stability radius for recovering local Clifford axes from approximate conjugation;
- a noise-robust test for membership in an AME stabilizer LU orbit;
- a certification protocol whose settings or sample complexity scales with the (m) minimum-support factors rather than the full stabilizer group.

The QIP 2026 certification result is the right benchmark: it states an operational promise, supplies an explicit measurement model, and proves both sample complexity and a limitation on nonadaptivity. A credible AME theorem likewise needs a noise model, observable data, completeness/soundness, and a quantitative bound.

**Risk:** exact half-supported phase correction does not imply a small correction under perturbation. The current character obstruction gives identifiability, not conditioning.

#### 6. Unknown-party canonization

The current factorial search over party permutations is structurally crude. The determinant data and four-cycle holonomies define a bounded-arity colored relational structure, suggesting reduction to colored graph/hypergraph isomorphism and quasipolynomial canonization. A cautious target is

\[
\binom{2m}{m}\,\exp((\log m)^{O(1)})
\]

by enumerating the image of one half and canonizing the residual transport system, or better an intrinsic all-half construction that avoids half enumeration.

This would align the algorithmic story with Claudet–Perdrix while preserving the stronger fixed-label result.

**Risk:** linear code equivalence is graph-isomorphism hard; see [Petrank–Roth](https://coding.yonsei.ac.kr/publications/international_journal/pdf/1596no.pdf). A polynomial unknown-party claim is not currently justified. Arbitrary party permutations also move the chosen half and alter the systematic chart.

### Tier 3: valuable secondary directions

#### 7. Affine phase torsor and extension cocycle

Unique phase correction on any prescribed half gives a canonical set-theoretic lift after choosing that half and a Clifford section. The failure of multiplicativity is an (L)-valued two-cocycle. Computing this cocycle could decide when

\[
1\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow \Gamma\longrightarrow \operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{tr}}(L)\longrightarrow1
\]

splits on each symmetry stratum and could connect the AME result cleanly to Heisenberg/metaplectic lifting phenomena in the MDS–CSS work.

This is conceptually strong but should follow, not precede, the commutant theorem.

#### 8. Symplectic polymatroid and secret-sharing formulation

AME gives the uniform block-rank function

\[
r(S)=2e\min(|S|,m).
\]

The all-block-minor criterion is therefore a representation of a uniform block symplectic polymatroid. This may provide a standard combinatorial home for the systematic theorem and clarify threshold secret sharing.

The gain is mainly unification unless it yields a new representability obstruction, construction theorem, or access-structure result. Avoid presenting ordinary symplectic-matroid terminology as if it already covered arbitrary additive party blocks.

#### 9. Construction by block-superregular anti-symplectic matrices

The systematic theorem turns AME construction into a concrete matrix problem: construct an anti-symplectic block matrix for which every required party-aligned square block is invertible. This invites probabilistic existence estimates, explicit Cauchy/Vandermonde-type constructions, and searches for genuinely additive non-$\mathbf F_q$-linear examples.

Quantum MDS bounds limit the parameter range, so the target should be new constructions or a clean equivalence theorem, not an implication that arbitrary long AMEs will result.

## What not to claim

1. **Not a solution to general stabilizer LU equivalence.** AME projection rigidity and fixed party labels are essential.
2. **Not LU=LC in general.** The exact local-Clifford reduction is special to this minimum-support geometry; 27-qubit counterexamples remain decisive.
3. **Not yet a complete scalar-invariant theorem.** Determinants and four-cycle holonomies are complete as operator-valued simultaneous-conjugacy data; finite-field trace data require a separate separating-invariant proof.
4. **Not yet polynomial under arbitrary party permutations.** Code-equivalence/GI barriers are real.
5. **No automatic extension-field linearity.** An $\mathbf F_p$-linear commutant containing a field is evidence of additional module structure, but compatibility and globalization must be proved.
6. **No robustness from exactness alone.** The direct-sum support theorem improves the coordinate system for a stability proof; it is not itself a stability estimate.
7. **No award claim for the direct 2025 comparison papers.** Their value is topical and technical, not an award designation.

## Recommended research sequence

### First sprint: prove or kill the commutant theorem

1. Rewrite the existing fixed-label equations abstractly as an endomorphism condition for the transport representation.
2. Prove chart and spanning-tree invariance of the commutant up to conjugacy.
3. Classify unital subalgebras and centralizers in $M_2(\mathbf F_q)$, including characteristic two.
4. Recover all five groups and orders from determinant-one units.
5. Test the formulation on every computed example and compare it with the current theorem statement.

This is low-risk and likely yields a real compression even if no further classification succeeds.

### Second sprint: interrogate the exceptional strata

1. Search existing examples by commutant type.
2. Test whether split type coincides with a CSS-like axis decomposition.
3. Test whether nonsplit type produces a global $\mathbf F_{q^2}$-linear/Hermitian model.
4. Search aggressively for dual-number AMEs; if none occur, derive an obstruction from block-superregularity.
5. Exhibit one absolutely irreducible example per relevant component to support generic-central-symmetry statements.

### Third sprint: choose one consequence

- If algebraic geometry is clean: prove generic minimal symmetry and a finite-field exceptional-count bound.
- If character estimates are clean: prove a robust orbit/symmetry certification theorem.
- If canonization is clean: improve the unknown-party complexity and state the GI boundary precisely.

Only after one of these succeeds should the manuscript be reconsidered. The commutant theorem alone is probably an integrated upgrade; the broader directions should not be added as speculative narrative unless they produce a proved statement.

## Bottom line

The deepest latent potential is a change of mathematical level:

\[
\text{block equations and a five-row table}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\text{endomorphism algebra of a rank-two symplectic transport system}.
\]

That change explains the existing classification, generalizes naturally, and creates three serious research questions: the geometry of symmetry strata, hidden linear structure on exceptional strata, and robust reconstruction from the optimal minimum-support decomposition. Among them, the first two are the best balance of novelty, feasibility, and fit with the present paper.
