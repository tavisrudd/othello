# Module 56. Double-normal factorization kills the projected variation

**Packet part:** Module 56. Stable index:
`notes/2026-08-19-c925-modular-direct-qdm-proof-packet.md`

**Status:** the two-normal algebraic theorem below is proved. It derives the
Module 54 projected Stokes-row vanishing from two separately typed one-wall
can/var factorizations. This is a genuine reduction of the analytic target:
one no longer needs a two-wall Stokes matrix or a global pure-block Stokes
morphism. The remaining occurrence theorem is the identification of the
actual fixed-phase inverse-Laplace can/var blocks with these same one-wall
Thom/divided-rank packages in one common receiver. Existing sources supply
the pieces separately, but not that identification.

## 56.1 The exact two-normal calculation

Let \(R\) be a discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal \(\mathfrak m\),
fraction field \(K\), and residue field \(k\). Let \(V\) and \(P_i\),
\(i\in\{1,2\}\), be finite free \(R\)-modules. Fix a common raw row

\[
                         r:V\longrightarrow R.                 \tag{56.1}
\]

For each wall let

\[
 V\xrightarrow{\ c_i\ }P_i
   \xrightarrow{\ v_i\ }V,
 \qquad
 v_ic_i=1-T_i,                                                 \tag{56.2}
\]

where \(T_i\) is the named local operation. Suppose that there are a
nonzero normal parameter \(a_i\in\mathfrak m\), a unit
\(u_i\in R^\times\), and a normalized row

\[
                         \rho_i:P_i\longrightarrow R           \tag{56.3}
\]

such that

\[
               rv_i=a_i\rho_i,
             \qquad
               \rho_ic_i=u_i r.                               \tag{56.4}
\]

The first equation says that ordinary rank vanishes to positive normal
order on the embedded variation packet and that \(\rho_i\) is its divided
row. The second says that the normalized variation map recovers the same
raw rank row, up to a unit. Both equations are one-wall statements.

### Theorem 56.1 -- double-normal projected-variation law

Under (56.1)--(56.4), for \(i\ne j\),

\[
                    \rho_jc_jv_i=a_i u_j\rho_i.                \tag{56.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
       (\rho_jc_jv_i)\otimes_R k=0.                            \tag{56.6}
\]

If an exact closed reader identifies
\(\bar v_i:\bar P_i\xrightarrow{\sim}
\operatorname{im}(1-\bar T_i)\), identifies \(\bar c_i\) with the actual
can map (so that \(\bar v_i\bar c_i=1-\bar T_i\)), and identifies the
Module 54 row with \(\bar\rho_i\bar v_i^{-1}\), with the same paths and
direction, then (56.6) is exactly

\[
 \rho_j(1-T_j)|_{\operatorname{im}(1-T_i)}=0.                 \tag{56.7}
\]

#### Proof

Compose the second equation for wall \(j\) with \(v_i\), then use the first
equation for wall \(i\):

\[
        \rho_jc_jv_i=u_jrv_i=a_i u_j\rho_i.
\]

Because \(a_i\in\mathfrak m\), reduction modulo \(\mathfrak m\) gives zero.
The last assertion is precisely the stated exact-reader identification.
\(\square\)

The formula is directed: the normal factor comes from the **source** wall
\(i\), while the unit comes from the **landing** wall \(j\). Exchanging the
walls proves the reverse directed equation. No commutation of \(T_1,T_2\)
is used.

### Corollary 56.1A -- alternating cross words multiply their normal factors

For a sequence of wall labels \(i_0,\ldots,i_n\),

\[
 \rho_{i_n}(c_{i_n}v_{i_{n-1}})\cdots
     (c_{i_1}v_{i_0})
 =\left(\prod_{\ell=1}^{n}u_{i_\ell}a_{i_{\ell-1}}\right)
   \rho_{i_0}.                                                \tag{56.7a}
\]

Thus every nonempty word generated by these typed cross maps has closed row
zero. This is only a row statement; it does not apply after inserting an
arbitrary endomorphism between cross maps, and it does not say that the full
word or its full Stokes block vanishes.

The proof is repeated substitution of (56.4). This is the multiplicative
normal-order analogue of the Writer law in Modules 43 and 53.

## 56.2 Why this is the right extension of the existing one-wall source

Proposition 46.1 already proves the model identities for a Thom image. If

\[
                  P_i=\theta_i A_i,
       \qquad \theta_i=\lambda_{-1}(q_i^{-1}E_i),              \tag{56.8}
\]

then equivariant rank satisfies

\[
      r(v_i x)=a_i\rho_i(x),
      \qquad
      \rho_i(\theta_i b)=r(b),
      \qquad
      a_i=(1-q_i^{-1})^{\operatorname{rk}E_i}.                 \tag{56.9}
\]

Thus (56.4) is not a disguised two-wall hypothesis: it is exactly the
one-wall divided-rank theorem, twice, provided \(c_i\) is the Thom/variation
map and \(v_i\) is its actual packet embedding in one receiver. Theorem
56.1 then performs all two-wall algebra formally.

This also explains the sparse-shadow perspective. Ordinary rank forgets
each supported packet to positive normal order. Dividing by the landing
normal factor reconstructs its one visible row, but a vector arriving from
another supported packet still carries the source normal factor and remains
invisible on the closed fibre.

One sufficient new geometric adapter is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\begin{gathered}
 \text{one common occurrence receiver }(V,r),\\
 \text{two actual can/var factorizations satisfying (56.4), and}\\
 \text{one exact fixed-phase closed reader.}
\end{gathered}}                                                \tag{56.10}
\]

It avoids an independent computation of a common two-wall Stokes matrix and
is stronger than two unrelated one-wall decompositions. It is not asserted
to contain less information: common can/var data determine the corresponding
pairwise Stokes block. For one directed conclusion, only the source equation
\(rv_i=a_i\rho_i\), the landing equation \(\rho_jc_j=b_jr\), and
\(a_i\in\mathfrak m\) are used. The symmetric package is retained because
the application needs both directions and one-wall provenance.

There is also a cheap falsifier for any proposed one-wall realization:

\[
                    r(1-T_i)=a_i u_i r.                       \tag{56.10a}
\]

It follows immediately from (56.2)--(56.4), and must hold in the actual
occurrence receiver before the double-normal theorem can be invoked.

## 56.3 The tempting spectral-morphism shortcut is not automatic

Tony Yue Yu and Shaowu Zhang prove two highly relevant functorial results:

1. Proposition 2.6(4) of *Topological Laplace Transform and Decomposition of
   nc-Hodge Structures* says that an **already existing** morphism of
   co-Stokes structures is graded on every good interval.
2. Their Theorem 8.13 identifies the asymptotic lift of the formal spectral
   decomposition of an algebraic exponential-type connection with the
   straight-Gabrielov-path vanishing-cycle decomposition in a non-anti-Stokes
   direction.

These results identify blocks and make genuine morphisms respect them. They
do not extend a covector on one formal block to a global Stokes morphism.
For two one-dimensional blocks and

\[
             S=1+\iota_j b\pi_i,\qquad b\ne0,                 \tag{56.11}
\]

the row \(\rho=(0,1)\) is pure in the \(j\)-block for one splitting, but

\[
                         \rho S-\rho=b\pi_i\ne0.               \tag{56.12}
\]

It therefore does not define a morphism to a pure \(j\)-object. Requiring
such a morphism without constructing it independently is essentially to
assume the incoming projected variations vanish.

There is also a separate can/var typing issue. Yu--Zhang's vanishing-cycle
block is a quotient \(F_{c_0}/F_{c_i}\); Module 54 uses
\(\operatorname{im}(1-T_i)\) inside a common nearby-cycle space. A
middle-extension can/var comparison, with the same paths, ordering,
direction, and monodromy normalization, is needed to identify them. Theorem
8.13 is objectwise and does not supply that map or the Gamma/rank row.

Consequently Yu--Zhang gives a useful **block reader** once an algebraic
exponential-type occurrence and its morphisms are known, but it does not by
itself prove the Gamma-to-formal point-line identity (35.14).

## 56.4 What the VGIT/GKZ sources contribute

Spenko--Van den Bergh, Proposition 12.6, gives an explicit wall map on the
specialized Grothendieck-group schober. On a moved projective generator its
rank coefficient is

\[
 \sum_{\varnothing\ne J\subseteq\mathcal J}
   (-1)^{|J|+1}\prod_{j\in J}z_j
       =1-\prod_{j\in\mathcal J}(1-z_j).                       \tag{56.13}
\]

Thus the moved map has coefficient \(1-\prod_j(1-z_j)\), while its
identity-minus-map rank defect has coefficient \(\prod_j(1-z_j)\), up to
the chosen wall orientation. The latter has positive normal order at the
integral point \(z_j=1\). This is the correct source shape for (56.4), not a
DVR can/var factorization. Their Theorem 13.1,
however, identifies the schober perverse sheaf with the GKZ system only at a
nonresonant parameter. It does not identify the normalized integral jet or
the actual fixed-phase cubic QDM packet.

Equation (56.13) is only a coefficient on a moved projective generator. It
does not say that the full chamber map preserves the generic rank kernel or
acts by one scalar on the full rank coimage: common generators have
coefficient one while the moved generator has coefficient (56.13). The
actual variation-packet restriction and the two equations in (56.4) are
still required. Thus Proposition 12.6 is evidence for, not a proof of, the
`CanVarFactor` constructor.

Gu--Yu--Yu, Theorem 6.2, supplies a connection- and pairing-compatible
formal QDM decomposition in its oriented flip case \(r_+<r_-\), under the
paper's setup hypotheses. This extends the one-wall QDM source beyond the
blowup calculation and covers the correspondingly oriented flip types in
the unit pilot; the balanced/flop case belongs to their separate Theorem
6.6. These results do not give a common
analytic inverse-Laplace receiver for two consecutive walls, a Gamma
integral row, or the can/var identification in (56.10).

Therefore the sources now meet immediately on either side of one precise
bridge:

\[
\begin{CD}
 \text{VGIT/GKZ or Thom one-wall factorization}
   @>{\text{(56.4)}}>>
 \text{double-normal row theorem}\cr
 @V{\text{actual QDM Fourier--Laplace/can--var reader}}VV
 @VV{\text{Theorem 56.1}}V\cr
 \text{fixed-phase Malgrange blocks}
   @>>>
 \text{projected variation zero.}
\end{CD}                                                       \tag{56.14}
\]

The vertical arrow is the remaining theorem. It must be occurrence-indexed;
the canonically completed eight-weight model is still only a toric pilot,
not an automatically identified AKMW/QDM occurrence.

## 56.5 Make the remaining illegal states unrepresentable upstream

Write a factor witness as the fully indexed dependent record

\[
 \mathsf{Factor}(o,\gamma,\chi,R,V,r,i,a_i,c_i,v_i,\rho_i,T_i),
                                                               \tag{56.15a}
\]

whose private constructor contains (56.2)--(56.4), and let
\(\mathsf{Reader}(d;F_i,F_j)\) take the **actual factor values** as indices.
It contains the exact closed-base-change isomorphisms
\(\bar v_i:\bar P_i\cong\operatorname{im}(1-\bar T_i)\), the can-map and
row identities above, and the chosen path/order/direction. Then extend the
Module 55 producer boundary with the trusted smart constructor

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf{doubleNormal}_{i\to j}:{}&
 \mathsf{CommonRawRow}(o,\gamma,\chi,R,V,r)\\
&\to(F_i:\mathsf{Factor}(o,\gamma,\chi,R,V,r,i,
                         a_i,c_i,v_i,\rho_i,T_i))\\
&\to(F_j:\mathsf{Factor}(o,\gamma,\chi,R,V,r,j,
                         a_j,c_j,v_j,\rho_j,T_j))\\
&\to\mathsf{Reader}(i\to j;F_i,F_j)\\
&\to\mathsf{ProjectedVariationZero}(o,\gamma,\chi,i\to j).
                                                                    \tag{56.15}
\end{aligned}
\]

The private factor constructor contains the equations, not merely an
operator with a label saying “Thom”. Its indices force the two factors to
use the same occurrence, trait, receiver, raw row, based loop, and phase
character. The positive-order index has no zero-order constructor. Because
the reader is indexed by the factor **values**, it identifies their actual
maps and rows, not just isomorphic abstract packets.

With this interface the following states cannot reach the Module 54
consumer:

1. two one-wall packages in different receivers;
2. a unit normal factor or a missing source normal order;
3. a Thom map which is not the actual \(c_i\), or an embedding which is not
   the actual \(v_i\);
4. a quotient vanishing-cycle block silently substituted for an image block;
5. a nonresonant GKZ packet silently specialized at the integral point;
6. the completed toric pilot silently identified with an actual AKMW
   occurrence; or
7. a pure formal covector silently promoted to a global Stokes morphism.

The types do not prove that (56.15) is inhabited. They reduce the trusted
geometric implementation to the single vertical adapter in (56.14).
They also cannot certify epistemic provenance: the implementer could choose
rows after inspecting the desired answer. Constructor privacy narrows the
trusted code surface, while source provenance and noncircularity remain
explicit audit obligations.

The executable Haskell replay models the same boundary with promoted factor
and map identifiers. That first-order encoding checks index mismatch and
direction errors; the value-indexed \(\mathsf{Reader}(d;F_i,F_j)\) above is
the stronger dependent specification required in a proof assistant.

## 56.6 Sharp hostile tests

The following load-bearing weakenings have small countermodels.

- If \(a_i\) is a unit, take \(R=\mathbb Q[[s]]\), all modules and rows
  one-dimensional, \(c_i=1\), \(v_i=2\), \(a_i=2\), and \(T_i=-1\).
  Then the cross row is \(2\), nonzero on the closed fibre.
- If \(\rho_jc_j=u_jr\) is dropped, a free choice of \(c_j\) can make the
  cross row arbitrary even when \(rv_i\in\mathfrak m\).
- If the two factors use different raw rows, the second row need not vanish
  on the first embedded packet.
- If the closed reader is not exact, the torsion module \(R/(s)\) is
  generically invisible and closed-visible.
- If only formal block purity is retained, (56.11)--(56.12) gives a nonzero
  incoming Stokes coefficient.

These are not failures of Theorem 56.1; they are exact falsifiers for
weakening its producer contract.

## 56.8 EJ/TT and mystery ledger

**EJ.** The new law is a second-order orthogonality theorem made from two
first-order optics. Each wall exposes one divided row; entering it through a
different positive-normal wall leaves one uncancelled normal factor. The
two-wall Stokes matrix is unnecessary.

**TT.** Do not call a pure formal block vector a Stokes morphism, and do not
call a Thom operator a variation map without the exact occurrence reader.
The theorem is powerful precisely because its two equations are typed as
one-wall source facts and the only remaining bridge is named.

## Boundary

The first genuine \((1,1)\) overlap no longer needs a bespoke two-wall
analytic vanishing theorem if its two incident one-wall QDM packages can be
read as normalized can/var factorizations in one receiver. The directed
covector then vanishes by the identity (56.5). The currently missing theorem
is narrower and better typed: identify the actual fixed-phase
inverse-Laplace packets, maps, and divided rows with those one-wall packages,
with exact closed base change and the chosen direction. No unconditional
\(m=2\) or all-\(m\) result is claimed.
