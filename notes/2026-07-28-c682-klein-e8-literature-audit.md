# C682 Klein \(E_8\) operator: quick literature audit

## Verdict

This is a bounded positioning audit, not a source-deep priority closure.
Zero sources were read at `full text` depth; six were read at `partial`
depth and one previously cached source was reused at its recorded `partial`
depth.

The audit changes the novelty boundary in two places.

1. The \(3\)-covariant Hilbert numerator
   \[
   t^2+t^{10}+t^{12}+t^{18}+t^{20}+t^{28}
   \]
   is classical McKay--Kostant data.  Suter prints this exact polynomial in
   his \(E_8\) table, with denominator
   \((1-t^{12})(1-t^{20})\).  The free-module structure is also part of the
   standard maximal-Cohen--Macaulay module-of-covariants picture.  Neither
   the six degrees nor freeness over a homogeneous parameter ring should be
   advertised as new.
2. The principal \(3\)-by-\(3\) blocks satisfy the stronger identities
   \[
   AB=BA=-100(h^3-1728F^5)I_3,\qquad
   B=\frac12\operatorname{adj}(A).
   \]
   Thus they are a matrix factorization of the \(E_8\) plane branch, and
   the complete principal symbol satisfies
   \[
   \sigma_3(\mathcal D)^2=100t^2p^2I_6,\qquad
   \det(\lambda I-\sigma_3(\mathcal D))
      =(\lambda^2-100t^2p^2)^3.
   \]
   Explicit \(E_8\) matrix factorizations attached to the McKay nodes are
   classical and tabulated in the literature.  The determinant
   \(-10^6p^6t^6\) is therefore not best positioned as a freestanding new
   factorization phenomenon.

The strongest surviving candidate is more conceptual:

> the third Klein transvectant on the complete \(3\)-covariant module
> realizes the \(3\)-node \(E_8\) matrix factorization as its principal
> symbol, with a single scalar cubic \(p\); its return algebra first fails
> to saturate the binary-icosahedral commutant at degree \(22\), where the
> missing line is the Koszul syzygy \(hg_2+Fg_{10}\).

No predecessor for that combined statement was located in this quick pass.
The explicit matrix-factorization comparison is now complete in
`notes/2026-07-28-c682-klein-e8-graded-gauge.md`: after
\(Y=-h/12,\ Z=F\), C682's blocks are graded-gauge equivalent over
\(\mathbf Z[1/30]\) to the unprimed tabulated three-node.  The sentence
must still retain “to our knowledge” until the
invariant-differential-operator literature has received a source-deep

## Closest literature

### McKay--Kostant Poincaré series

Suter's \(E_8\) example gives
\[
P_i(t)=\frac{z_i(t)}{(1-t^{12})(1-t^{20})}
\]
and prints, for the three-dimensional node,
\[
z_i(t)=t^2+t^{10}+t^{12}+t^{18}+t^{20}+t^{28}.
\]
He attributes the general numerator theorem to Kostant.  Kostant's paper
develops the individual Poincaré series for the restrictions
\(\operatorname{Sym}^n(\mathbf C^2)|_\Gamma\) using the affine Dynkin
root system.  This decisively removes novelty from the six degrees and
their Molien numerator.

### Modules of covariants and \(E_8\) matrix factorizations

Curto--Morrison review the explicit McKay correspondence: the
\(\rho\)-isotypic component of \(\mathbf C[s,t]\) is
\(M_\rho\otimes V_\rho\), where \(M_\rho\) is a maximal
Cohen--Macaulay module over \(\mathbf C[s,t]^G\), and the associated
presentations are matrix factorizations.  Their appendix lists the two
length-three \(E_8\) factorizations explicitly.  They attribute the
construction to Gonzalez-Sprinberg--Verdier and explain the subsequent
matrix-factorization interpretation.

Direct multiplication of C682's displayed matrices gives
\[
AB=BA=-100(h^3-1728F^5)I_3.
\]
Therefore C682 has not discovered the existence of a matrix factorization
for the \(3\)-node; it has apparently found a particularly sparse,
transvectant-selected gauge for the unprimed three-dimensional McKay node.
The later exact comparison gives
\[
LJAJR=\psi_3,\qquad
R^{-1}JBJL^{-1}=-172800\phi_3
\]
after \(Y=-h/12,\ Z=F\), with compatible degree-zero gauges on the
\((2,10,18)\) and \((12,20,28)\) free summands.  This closes the
identification step and leaves source-depth, rather than isomorphism class,
as the next literature gate.

Omer independently tabulates the eight indecomposable \(E_8\)
factorizations in a different gauge.  This is corroboration and a useful
comparison source, not the historical priority source.

### Centralizer algebras

Barnes--Benkart--Halverson study
\[
Z_k(G)=\operatorname{End}_G(V^{\otimes k})
\]
for finite \(G<SU_2\), with bases and dimensions controlled by walks on
the affine Dynkin graph.  This is close vocabulary but a different
centralizer: C682 concerns \(\operatorname{End}_G(\operatorname{Sym}^nV)\)
and the subalgebra generated by the degree-shifting third transvectant and
its adjoint.  Their paper does not preempt the degree-\(22\) failure in the
sections inspected.

Kostant already determines every multiplicity in
\(\operatorname{Sym}^nV|_G\), so the \(0,2,1\) input is classical data.
The conclusion that all closed transvectant excursions fail to mix the
doubled \(3\) was not located.

### Transvectants and invariant operators

Olver--Sanders treat transvectants as the basic classical invariant
bidifferential operators and connect them to Rankin--Cohen brackets,
star products, and Moyal brackets.  Hence neither the third transvectant
nor its interpretation as a symplectic bidifferential operator is new.
The C682 novelty question begins only after fixing the Klein dodecic,
passing to the \(3\)-covariant MCM module, and computing the induced finite
Weyl matrix and scalar principal cubic.

Kramer constructs a different binary-icosahedral invariant generalized
Casimir.  C682's exact rank-eight commutator comparison already proves that
the first return is not a polynomial in Kramer's operator and Euler degree.
No conflict was found there.
