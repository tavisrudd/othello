## A noncircular local propagation lemma

The defect condition alone is insufficient. The following lemma gives the
correct ascending-induction mechanism.

### Lemma

Let \(M\) be a finite-dimensional Hermitian space, let \(L\subset M\) be a
hyperplane, and let

\[
 \mathcal E_L=P_L\operatorname{End}(M)P_L
 \simeq\operatorname{End}(L)
\]

be the full algebra supported on \(L\). If \(T=T^\dagger\) and

\[
 (1-P_L)TP_L\ne0,
\]

then the unital algebra generated by \(\mathcal E_L\) and \(T\) is
\(\operatorname{End}(M)\).

### Proof

Choose a unit vector \(e\) spanning \(L^\perp\). The nonzero cross block
\((1-P_L)TP_L\) supplies a nonzero map \(L\to\mathbf Ce\). Composing it on
the right with the matrix units in \(\operatorname{End}(L)\) supplies every
map \(L\to\mathbf Ce\). Since \(T\) is self-adjoint, the opposite cross
block supplies every map \(\mathbf Ce\to L\). Their products supply the
matrix unit on \(\mathbf Ce\), while \(\mathcal E_L\) already contains all
matrix units on \(L\). These are all matrix units of \(M\).
\(\square\)

Apply this on one isotypic multiplicity space \(M_{\rho,n}\). If the degree
\(n-6\) corner is already full, pushing it through
\(\Delta_{n-6}\) supplies the full algebra on

\[
 L=\operatorname{im}\Delta_{n-6}.
\]

If \(\Delta_{n-6}\) is surjective, the degree \(n\) corner is immediately
full. If it has hyperplane image, take

\[
 T=\Delta_n^\dagger\Delta_n.
\]

The lemma proves the degree \(n\) corner once its cross block relative to
\(L\) is nonzero. This uses only the lower neighbor, so induction by degree
is well founded; no assumption about the degree \(n+6\) corner is needed.

## How the global defect enters

At a strict multiplicity peak, suppose both adjacent maps have maximal rank.
Then \(L=\operatorname{im}\Delta_{n-6}\) is a hyperplane, and
\(\ker\Delta_n\) is a line. Two independent degeneracies can occur:

1. \(\ker\Delta_n=L^\perp\). This is exactly a two-sided defect
   \(K_{\rho,n}\ne0\).
2. \(\ker\Delta_n\subset L\). Then
   \(\Delta_n\Delta_{n-6}\) loses rank, even though the two-sided defect can
   vanish.

If neither degeneracy occurs, \(T\) has a nonzero cross block and the lemma
closes the corner. Equivalently, at a strict peak it is enough to prove:

- maximal rank for each one-step block;
- no relevant two-sided defect;
- maximal rank for the two-step block
  \(\Delta_n\Delta_{n-6}\).

This explains why the two-sided defect is high-value but not by itself a
complete substitute for all polynomial minors. It separates the desired
all-weight statement into two geometrically meaningful transversality
conditions rather than an opaque generated-algebra determinant.

At degree \(22\), the first degeneracy occurs: the standard
\(\mathbf3\)-isotypic multiplicity space has a dark line orthogonal to the
lower image. The supported lower algebra therefore cannot reach that line,
and the full corner loses exactly the observed two dimensions.
