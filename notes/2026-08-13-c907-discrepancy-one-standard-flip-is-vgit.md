# C907 — discrepancy-one standard flips satisfy the Gu--Yu--Yu VGIT criterion

Date: 2026-08-13

Status: exact source-extension theorem for geometric coverage.  Every smooth projective standard
flip of type `(1,2)` over a smooth connected complex curve is a `D`-flip
satisfying Gu--Yu--Yu Lemma 6.9.  The primitive polarization is the canonical
bundle; fibrewise `O(1)` normal generation gives degree-`(+/-1)` generation
of the wall algebra; and the standard common-blowup model makes the
nonzero-degree ideal the prime ideal of the wall.  Corollary 6.12 therefore
proves the QDM decomposition, and the C907 simple-wall argument gives the
primitive-sixth Gamma-rank identity in that wall's fixed-sector receiver.

This closes the Gu--Yu--Yu **coverage** gap for the curve `(1,2)` row.  It
does not turn the wall-local receiver identity into the intrinsic row needed
to compose adjacent walls.  Nor does it cover an arbitrary global point
`(1,3)` wall; only the explicit split/disconnected point model is presently
proved.

## 1. Canonical divisor is the primitive polarization

Let `X_- dashrightarrow X_+` be a standard flip of type `(1,2)` with smooth
curve wall `S`.  In Gu--Yu--Yu Definition 3.4,

\[
 P_-=P_S(V_-),\quad \operatorname{rk}V_-=3,
 \qquad
 N_{P_-/X_-}=\psi_-^*V_+\otimes O_{P_-}(-1),
 \quad \operatorname{rk}V_+=2.                    \tag{1}
\]

On a fibre `P^2`, adjunction gives

\[
 K_{X_-}|_{P^2}=O(-3)\otimes O(2)=O(-1).           \tag{2}
\]

On the other side, a fibre of `P_+=P_S(V_+)` is `P^1` and its normal has
rank three, so

\[
 K_{X_+}|_{P^1}=O(-2)\otimes O(3)=O(1).            \tag{3}
\]

The contractions are small, hence canonical divisors correspond under the
isomorphism in codimension one.  Set `D=K_{X_-}`.  Equations (2)--(3) show
that `O(-D)` is relatively ample on `X_-`, the transform `O(K_X+)` is
relatively ample on `X_+`, and both restrict to primitive `O(1)`
polarizations.  Thus this is a `D`-flip and Lemma 6.9(iii) holds.  Smoothness
makes the canonical bundles Cartier; discrepancy one makes the fibre degree
exactly one.

## 2. Degree-one generation

Let `U_0=Spec R_0` be an affine chart of `X_0`, with inverse images `U_±`,
and put

\[
 R_n=H^0(U_-,O(nD))=H^0(U_+,O(nK_{X_+})),
 \qquad R=\bigoplus_{n\in Z}R_n.                   \tag{4}
\]

For `m>=0`, `O(-mD)` restricts to `O_{P^2}(m)` on exceptional fibres and to
a one-dimensional space on point fibres.  Higher fibre cohomology vanishes.
Formal functions and cohomology-and-base-change therefore reduce the
completed multiplication map

\[
 \operatorname{Sym}^m(q_{-,*}O(-D))
 \longrightarrow q_{-,*}O(-mD)                    \tag{5}
\]

to fibrewise multiplication.  On `P^2` this is
`Sym^m H^0(O(1))->H^0(O(m))`, which is surjective; off `S` it is
multiplication in a one-dimensional space.  The completed cokernel vanishes
everywhere, so Nakayama and faithful completion give surjectivity.  Hence
`R_{<=0}` is generated by `R_{-1}`.

The same argument on `X_+`, using fibres `P^1` and `O(1)`, proves that
`R_{>=0}` is generated by `R_1`.  This is Lemma 6.9(i).

## 3. The nonzero-degree ideal is the wall ideal

Let

\[
 I=\langle R_n:n\ne0\rangle\subset R,
 \qquad I_0=I\cap R_0.                              \tag{6}
\]

Away from `S`, both contractions are isomorphisms and the two canonical
polarizations are dual trivial lines locally.  Products of degree `1` and
`-1` sections generate the unit, so `V(I)` is supported over `S`.

Along `S`, the standard common blowup has

\[
 E=P_S(V_+)\times_SP_S(V_-),\qquad
 N_{E/\widetilde X}=O(-1,-1).                      \tag{7}
\]

Its Rees description identifies the conormal of `S in X_0` with the
degree-`(1,-1)` products of the two normal-coordinate bundles.  On the
associated graded, `I_0` is generated by all products `x_i y_j`, the local
standard determinantal model.  More intrinsically, after trivializing a line
pulled back from the affine base, the two primitive polarizations satisfy

\[
 \pi_-^*O(-K_{X_-})\otimes\pi_+^*O(K_{X_+})
 \cong O_{\widetilde X}(-E).                        \tag{8}
\]

Indeed both sides restrict to `O_E(1,1)` and agree away from `E`; the only
possible difference is pulled back from `U_0`, hence is trivial after the
chosen affine localization.  Since `widetilde X=Bl_S X_0`, pushforward of
`O(-mE)` is the `m`-th power of the scheme-theoretic ideal of `S`.  Therefore
products of degree `1` and `-1` sections generate the full Rees ideal, not
merely its associated-graded radical.  It follows that

\[
 R/I\cong O_S.                                      \tag{9}
\]

On a connected affine chart of a smooth curve, `O_S` is a domain.  Thus `I`
is prime, proving Lemma 6.9(ii).  A finite disconnected wall is handled
componentwise; its global ideal is radical with prime component ideals.

## 4. QDM and rank consequence

Gu--Yu--Yu Lemma 6.9 makes the master smooth and semi-free, and Corollary
6.12 proves

\[
 QDM(X_- )\cong QDM(X_+)\oplus QDM(S)              \tag{10}
\]

over their reduced Laurent-Novikov base, intertwining the full connections
and Poincare pairings.

The C907 simple-VGIT point-row theorem applies in a fixed wall receiver.  A point in the common open
has ambient coordinate one; the curve summand is supported and has rank
zero.  Shen--Shoemaker's oriented asymptotics and the fixed-sector receiver
give

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathfrak r_{X_-}|_{P_6(X_-)}\ne0
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \mathfrak r_{X_+}|_{P_6(X_+)}\ne0.}               \tag{11}
\]

Equation (11) is wall-local in exactly the sense of
`2026-08-13-c907-simple-vgit-rank-theorem.md`: it does not identify the two
incident large-radius/Stokes realizations at a later peak.

## 5. Boundary

Discrepancy one is load-bearing.  If `r_- - r_+=nu>1`, the canonical
polarization restricts as `O(nu)` and does not supply Lemma 6.9(iii).  The
one point `(1,3)` case is handled by an explicit simple-VGIT model.  An
arbitrary global standard `(1,3)` flip still has nonprimitive discrepancy and
remains a genuine weighted/index issue.

The theorem assumes the exact standard common-blowup geometry of Definition
3.4.  An AKMW pi-nonsingular elementary piece must still be shown to
globalize to that form.

## EJ / TT / AA

- **EJ:** discrepancy one turns `K_X` itself into the missing degree-one wall
  coordinate; no auxiliary polarization or root stack is needed.
- **TT:** relative ampleness alone gives generation only after a Veronese.
  The fibre degree-one calculation (2)--(3) is what makes the master smooth.
- **AA:** audit the precise toric meaning of AKMW pi-nonsingularity.  A unit
  circuit would reduce the remaining geometric problem to the point `(1,3)`
  wall and the separate receiver-coherence gate.
