## Reusable hypersurface-lift theorem

Let `G` act on a projective scheme `X`, let `Q` be a `G`-linearized regular section cutting out a
hypersurface `C`, and let

```text
0 -> A --Q--> B -> R -> 0
```

be the induced exact sequence of section modules (with the character twist included in `A` when
`Q` is only semi-invariant).  If `s in R^G`, then:

1. every lift `p in B` of `s` defines a cocycle
   `c_p(g)=Q^(-1)(g.p-p) in A`;
2. its class `[c_p]=delta(s) in H^1(G,A)` is independent of `p`;
3. the affine fibre of lifts of `s` is the affine `G`-space
   `g star a=g.a+c_p(g)`;
4. `[c_p]=0` exactly when the fibre has a `G`-fixed lift;
5. any `G`-equivariant functor on `B` remains covariant on the absolute lifts, while a functor on
   `A` applied after choosing an origin descends only if it is invariant under the translations
   generated by `c_p`.

The proof is the connecting-homomorphism calculation.  The cocycle identity follows by expanding
`(gh).p-p`; changing `p` to `p+Qa` adds `g.a-a`; and a coboundary is exactly a translation to an
affine fixed point.  The last clause is functoriality, not a new cohomology assertion.

For C417, `A=H^0(O(m-2))`, `B=H^0(O(m))`, and the certified affine fixed-point rank gaps prove
that `delta(s)` is nonzero on both frozen families.  This is the portable theorem exported by the
task.  It applies to any equivariant hypersurface factorization family, not only the three Coxeter
fields.

For the two frozen defining-characteristic cases there is a stronger local corollary:

```text
dim H^1(C_p,A)=2,
dim H^1(C_p,A)^(N_PSL(C_p))=1,
res(delta(s)) spans that fixed line.
```

The cyclic norm is zero on `A`; the coboundary ranks are `4` and `8`, leaving two cohomology
dimensions.  Exact normalizer action then cuts them to one.  This prevents the overclaim that the
Sylow class itself is unique while giving an intrinsic local-normalizer recognition theorem.

The global computation is sharper still:

| type | group | `dim Z^1` | `dim B^1` | `dim H^1` |
|:---:|:---:|---:|---:|---:|
| B3 | `PSL_2(7)` | 6 | 5 | 1 |
| B3 | `PGL_2(7)` | 7 | 6 | 1 |
| H3 | `PSL_2(11)` | 10 | 9 | 1 |
| H3 | `PGL_2(11)` | 11 | 10 | 1 |

Thus C417's nonzero class generates the entire global first cohomology in all four cases.  The
normalizer-fixed local line is exactly the restriction of the unique global line, not merely a
distinguished candidate.

## Proof-tool exports

The connecting-class package gives six reusable proof moves.

1. **Equivariant-origin obstruction.**  To disprove a canonical normalization of common
   hypersurface lifts, show that `delta(s)` is nonzero.  This replaces coordinate-by-coordinate
   failed covariance tests by one affine fixed-point or cohomology calculation.
2. **Uniqueness by one nonzero witness.**  If `H^1(G,A)` is one-dimensional, any two nontrivial
   affine lift constructions represent the same extension up to scalar.  One normalized
   configuration then fixes the scalar.  In C417 this applies to both inner and full groups.
3. **Sylow-normalizer detection.**  Restrict to a cyclic Sylow subgroup, compute the much smaller
   `H^1(C_p,A)`, and retain its normalizer-fixed part.  At B3/H3 this recovers the unique global
   line from a two-dimensional local space.
4. **Projective-cover recognition.**  Homogenize an affine orbit by `x |-> (1,x)`.  A constant
   socle kernel, prime-to-`p` point stabilizer, and 2-transitive action identify the permutation
   module as `P_G(1)` and the image as `P_G(1)/soc(P_G(1))`.
5. **First-moment descent.**  Vanishing through degree `r-1` proves origin independence of the
   degree-`r` signed moment by the binomial formula, with no invariant-theory census.
6. **Coboundary no-go test.**  Pairwise differences are fixed by every change of origin.  If two
   proposed relation lines already differ on that submodule, no affine base change or Rees
   filtration generated by it can identify them.

For C416, move 2 can pin down any nontrivial **affine defect extension** up to scalar.  It does not
identify the full section-level Fourier operator: the local linear Hom space remains
16-dimensional until a Hecke, pairing, multiplication, or incidence constraint cuts it down.

### C509 tangent-cocycle lemma

Let the unipotent radical `U=(F_q,+)` of a Borel act on an affine tangent parameter by

```text
u star z = z + d u.
```

The translation `c_d(u)=du` is a cocycle.  Torus normalization selects the scalar-map line among
additive cocycles.  If `d != 0` in `F_q`, translation by `dU=F_q` is transitive; if `d=0`, the
origin is fixed and the Borel torus separates zero from the nonzero orbit.  Thus the orbit split is
controlled exactly by `char(F_q)|d`.

For C509, `d=6`, recovering its ordinary transitive tangent fibre and its characteristic-two/three
`1+(q-1)` split.  At redundancies eight and nine the predicted coefficients are `7` and `8`; once
the corresponding tangent-coordinate formula is proved, the modular split follows formally with
no new orbit enumeration.

## Moment descent and polarization

For a signed finite family `nu=sum_i w_i delta_(x_i)` in an affine space, define symmetric moments
`mu_r(a)=sum_i w_i(x_i-a)^(sym r)`.  The binomial identity is

```text
mu_r(a+t)
  = sum_(j=0)^r binom(r,j) (-t)^(sym(r-j)) sym mu_j(a).
```

Hence if `mu_0=...=mu_(r-1)=0`, then the first surviving `mu_r` is independent of the origin.
Conversely, the displayed lower moments are exactly the translation correction terms.  For B3 and
H3 the exact checker verifies, for every matching base,

```text
mu_0=mu_1=mu_2=0,                 mu_3 != 0.
```

This proves the base independence of C406's cubic without pretending the whole quotient
configuration is linear.  Classical design-trade incidence cancellation is adjacent terminology,
but the proof needed here is just polarization of an affine signed measure.  No novelty is claimed
for that identity.

## Projective evaluation and complementary bundles

A degree-`d` homogeneous form is a section of `O_X(d)`.  Its value at a projective point lies in
the corresponding one-dimensional fibre; a scalar coordinate requires a representative or a
trivialization, while its zero divisor is canonical.  Thus the correct objects are

```text
Phi_M in H^0(X,O(h-1)),            P_M in H^0(X,O(h+1)),
h=(q-1)/2.
```

Their scalar characters are complementary because `(h-1)+(h+1)=q-1`.  On `F_q^*` the two
evaluation characters are dual, which is the right source for the twisted projective Fourier
kernel.  This does **not** trivialize `O_X(q-1)` as a geometric line bundle; it trivializes only
the scalar character on rational representatives.  Sheet parity remains the separate external
`PGL/PSL` sign, as required by the T1 falsifier.

Projective evaluation-code papers commonly choose one normalized representative for every point.
That convention makes a coordinate code, but the bundle formulation explains why zero evaluation
is intrinsic and nonzero values have degree-dependent rescaling.  C406's product depth therefore
uses the canonical output (a zero divisor), while a direct scalar comparison of quotient and
product evaluations would silently mix two line bundles.
