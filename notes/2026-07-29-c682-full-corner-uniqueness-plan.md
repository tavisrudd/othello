# C682 full-corner uniqueness plan

Date: 2026-07-29

## Objective

Prove, or reduce to the first exact obstruction, the all-weight statement

\[
\operatorname{Alg}\langle
\Delta_n^\dagger\Delta_n,\,
(\Delta_n^2)^\dagger\Delta_n^2,\,
\Delta_{n-6}\Delta_{n-6}^\dagger
\rangle
=\operatorname{End}_{2.A_5}(\operatorname{Sym}^n)
\]

for every \(n\ne22\), with the known exact \(8<10\) failure at degree \(22\).

The primary route is maximal rank plus induction on isotypic multiplicity.
It should avoid factoring the 21 parameterized generated-algebra minors.

## Gate A: all-weight maximal rank

For an irreducible \(2.A_5\)-module \(\rho\), write

\[
M_{\rho,n}=\operatorname{Hom}_{2.A_5}
(\rho,\operatorname{Sym}^n),\qquad
D_{\rho,n}:M_{\rho,n}\longrightarrow M_{\rho,n+6}.
\]

The first target is

\[
\operatorname{rank}D_{\rho,n}
=\min(\dim M_{\rho,n},\dim M_{\rho,n+6})
\quad\text{for all }\rho,n.
\]

Because \(\Delta_n\) is equivariant, every block has the representation-
theoretic lower bound

\[
\dim\ker\Delta_n\ge
\sum_\rho \dim(\rho)
\max(m_\rho(n)-m_\rho(n+6),0).
\]

Equality of the total dimensions proves maximal rank on every block
simultaneously. The observed candidate kernel series is

\[
\sum_{n\ge0}\dim\ker\Delta_n\,t^n
=\frac{
1+2t+3t^2+3t^6+3t^{10}+2t^{11}+t^{12}
}{1-t^{20}}.
\]

Execution order:

1. Prove the forced-defect series from the affine-\(E_8\) McKay
   multiplicities.
2. Prove the same series for the polynomial kernel of the third-order
   transvectant.
3. Prefer a scalar proof: dehomogenize to a third-order ODE or use the five
   mod-\(5\) tridiagonal chains and their boundary determinants.
4. Use the order-three bound to rule out hidden solutions.

Falsifier: any degree where the exact kernel exceeds the forced McKay
defect. Record the first degree, isotype candidates, and exact basis.

## Gate B: supported-algebra propagation

Let \(M=M_{\rho,n}\) and

\[
L=\operatorname{im}D_{\rho,n-6},\qquad
U=\operatorname{im}D_{\rho,n}^\dagger.
\]

If the neighboring corners are full, path operators supply the full
supported algebras

\[
\operatorname{End}(L),\qquad \operatorname{End}(U).
\]

Prove the finite-dimensional lemma:

> If \(L+U=M\), neither subspace is zero, and \(L\not\perp U\), then the
> unital algebra generated by the full algebras supported on \(L\) and
> \(U\) is \(\operatorname{End}(M)\).

The all-weight defect theorem already gives \(L+U=M\) for \(n>52\).
At a codimension-two peak,

\[
\dim L=m-2,\qquad\dim U=m-1.
\]

For \(m\ge4\), dimension alone forbids \(L\perp U\), since
\(\dim L+\dim U=2m-3>m\). The \(m=3\) bases must be checked exactly.

Falsifier: a peak above degree \(52\) where a neighboring corner is not
available under the proposed induction, or a low-dimensional orthogonal
pair.

## Gate C: well-founded multiplicity induction

Induct first on multiplicity \(m\), then along components joined by
maximal-rank square edges.

1. Multiplicity-zero and multiplicity-one corners are automatic.
2. At a strict peak, both neighboring multiplicities are smaller; the
   outer induction supplies their full corners.
3. Equal-multiplicity maximal-rank edges are isomorphisms and transport a
   full corner between adjacent degrees.
4. Every remaining off-peak vertex must connect, through square edges, to
   either an already closed peak or a smaller-multiplicity boundary.

This graph statement must be proved from the nine Kostant numerators and
the semigroup \(\langle12,20\rangle\), not extrapolated from a finite scan.
The induction must explicitly isolate degree \(22\), so its failed
\(\mathbf3\)-corner is never used to prove a later corner.

Falsifier: the first equal-multiplicity component with no smaller boundary,
or one whose only boundary passes through the failed degree-\(22\)
\(\mathbf3\)-corner.

## Acceptance gate

The all-weight full-corner theorem may be claimed only if:

1. every \(D_{\rho,n}\) has maximal rank;
2. the supported-two-subspace lemma is proved;
3. the multiplicity graph gives a noncircular induction for all nine
   modules;
4. every low-dimensional orthogonality and degree-\(22\) interaction is
   checked exactly;
5. the three displayed local returns really contain the pushed/pulled
   supported algebras required by the induction, or the theorem is stated
   for the full path corner instead.

The last distinction is essential. Neighboring *full path corners* push
all of \(\operatorname{End}(L)\) and \(\operatorname{End}(U)\), but the
three local return operators need not contain those supported algebras
without an additional word-reduction argument. If this gate fails, record
whether the proof closes the full graded path corner but not the
three-generator local algebra.
