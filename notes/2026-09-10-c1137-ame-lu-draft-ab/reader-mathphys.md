# Cold mathematical-physics reader: X versus Y

I read X first, then Y, with chronology and intended winner withheld. I read the complete extracted texts, including proofs, appendices, disclosures and references (X: 40 pages; Y: 43 pages), in bounded chunks. I inspected rendered X pages 1, 7 and 22, and Y pages 3, 8, 17, 31, 37 and 38. My perspective is mathematical physics: linear algebra, operators, tensors and spectral arguments are familiar; stabilizer verification and coding theory are not my specialties. This is a full reading, not an independent formal verification. Cited sources were not independently checked; no new literature research was conducted.

## Summary and contribution

Both versions prove that every local factor of a product-unitary conversion between even-party stabilizer AME states is Clifford, for arbitrary additive prime-power stabilizers. The decisive mechanism is especially attractive: minimum-support stabilizer groups contain every one-site Weyl label exactly once, and rank-one contractions recover the axes of the resulting diagonal operator tensor. A continuous equivalence problem becomes finite symplectic linear algebra.

The second headline is quantitative: sufficiently small state-vector defect identifies an exact symmetry and controls the collective residual generators. Cleaning, Fourier concentration, a discrete overlap gap and a balanced-cut estimate serve distinguishable purposes. The larger, dimension- and party-independent transition-compatibility radius does not solve the remaining character correction. This distinction is stated honestly and is central to understanding what has actually been proved.

The common secondary results include constructive recognition, an intrinsic endomorphism algebra, forced extra structure for four and six parties, marginal determination, stochastic conversion and encoder consequences. Y additionally develops optimal complementary marginal tests, weighted and nonstabilizer refinements, and a sufficient observable condition for entering the robust theorem.

## Significance and scope

The exact proof and the quantitative theorem plausibly justify a substantial mathematical quantum-information paper. The strongest appeal to my specialty is that the exact theorem is explained by elementary tensor geometry, while the residual estimate connects local generators to a physically meaningful state distance. The finite centralizer classification is also rewarding: it converts an apparently large symmetry problem into a small algebra.

Y's marginal-test contribution earns its space in the body. The four-qutrit character change on page 14 is subsequently detected by three of four tests on page 17. This makes the otherwise abstract distinction between label space and character experimentally interpretable. The model is appropriately limited to support-restricted binary tests allowing joint operations within the subset. It is not automatically a result about laboratory setting counts or fully local measurements.

The priority discussion is detailed, but my assessment of novelty remains conditional on its accuracy. Neither version pretends that tensor decomposition uniqueness or the qualitative cleaning obstruction is new. The explicit exclusion of an AME-existence claim from the radius scaling is also welcome.

## Correctness and uncertainty

I found no substantive mathematical counterexample or model contradiction. I could follow and locally check the support squeeze, tensor-axis argument, half-set graph form, centralizer propagation, stochastic polar-decomposition argument, Fourier concentration and spectral residual estimates. I checked the normalization logic distinguishing normalized Hilbert–Schmidt distance from the unnormalized collective Frobenius norm. The final bound has the expected square-root local-dimension factor.

The coding-specific entry to Lemma 6.1 remains the point where an adjacent reader relies most on the supplied dictionary and standard inputs. The scalar compression of a supported operator on a correctable region is understandable, but proving cleaning from the stated symplectic restriction property would require an extra reconstruction step. This is a confidence boundary, not an identified false claim.

Y's Proposition 4.6 is convincing: any m library subgroups span, so a nontrivial character passes at most m−1 tests; a one-site error gives the matching support-inclusion upper bound. Appendix C.2 is more demanding but its opposite-half error-basis orthogonality and spectral-projection summation are intelligible without stabilizer-verification expertise. The proof carefully avoids assuming that the two entire stars commute. The marginal-certified rounding corollary correctly retains a product-unitary preparation promise and distinguishes a confidence bound from a raw observed frequency.

One definite production defect occurs in Y: equation numbers (4.1) and (4.2) are reused on page 17, although they already denote the support formula on page 9 and transition equation on page 11. The reference to Equation (4.2) in Corollary 6.11 consequently becomes ambiguous. This should be fixed before circulation. I found no mathematical defect requiring a new theorem or a changed measurement model.

## Exposition and organization

Y communicates the real headlines better. Its introduction places quantitative rounding immediately after exact rigidity (pages 2–3), then marks the encoder theorem as a consequence. X places the encoder consequence before the quantitative question (page 2). For a reader arriving because of the title, Y establishes the governing pair of results more directly.

The major improvement is Y's “Reading the paper” paragraph on page 6. It offers a definite route through Sections 2, 3 and 6 and explicitly makes the classification refinements optional. X's organization paragraph mostly lists contents. Y is three pages longer, but a selected first reading is easier to manage.

There is a small qualification to that skip promise. The proof of Theorem 6.7 explicitly invokes the Pauli-compatible Choi argument in Section 4 (Y page 28; developed on pages 18–19). Proposition 6.9 invokes transition classification and phase correction from Section 4 as well. An adjacent reader can accept these as black boxes, but “these sections can be skipped” should specify the short facts to import or give precise return links. This is most relevant when reading the robust proof in full rather than only its global-rounding core.

The shared tensor picture is useful, not decorative. X puts Figure 1 before the tensor lemma on page 7; Y puts it at the start of page 8, after the lemma. I slightly prefer X's placement because the visual primes the proof. The shared defect-well schematic (X page 22; Y page 23) is clear but less useful than a diagram of the three correctable regions would be. Its extensive caveats prevent overinterpretation yet consume space without helping reconstruct the cleaning step.

The most enjoyable shared passages are the short support proof, the four-qutrit/Frobenius example and the two-uniform variance argument. Appendix B's Fisher-metric interpretation is particularly natural for a mathematical physicist, although appropriately optional. The heaviest cognitive load comes from serially presenting all of recognition, five algebra types, code interpretations, certification, stochastic conversion and rounding before closure. Y contains that load better through navigation, but neither abstract needs the larger transition-radius constant and detailed character caveat in addition to both main constants and the recognition output specification.

## Major and minor comments

1. Make the advertised main-proof route genuinely self-contained at the level of prerequisites: supply a compact Choi/Pauli-compatibility dictionary and precise links for the later transition theorem. A three-region support sketch would help more than another general roadmap.
2. Keep Y's complementary test theorem and short observable corollary in the main text. They connect the state-distance premise to observables and illuminate the character issue. Keep weighted optimization and arbitrary-AME refinements optional in Appendix C; their full generality is not a third headline for this title.
3. Reduce introductory repetition. The same radius scaling, distinction from approximate error correction, family-existence qualification and character obstruction recur in the abstract, introduction, Section 6 and conclusion. Preserve the qualifications at their theorem statements; summarize them more lightly elsewhere.

Minor points: repair Y's duplicate equation numbers; complete the bibliographic identifiers for entries currently ending simply in “Version 1/2/3”; replace “former list of unrelated group orders” in Section 5 with a description independent of any manuscript history; consider stating the normalized-distance convention before its first abstract use. The detailed formalization disclosure is appropriately separated, and it does not falsely advertise full theorem verification.

## Recommendation and A/B verdict

I recommend publication after a focused expository and production revision, subject to the journal's specialist verification of the cited coding inputs and priority claims. I would not request a new mathematical direction or expansion of the test model.

**Preference: Y, moderately.** It gives exact and robust rigidity their correct order, offers better safe skips, and makes marginal certification answer a concrete question about the robust theorem. X is shorter and occasionally places a figure better, but its shorter length does not compensate for weaker reader routing. Y's extra material increases value without demanding a new specialty, provided its appendix remains optional.
