**Verdict**: five findings, one substantive and structural. Probe 1a is a good pencil probe — the
central argument that `state = u` is ill-typed is correct and load-bearing, and the
signature-collapse proposition and its proof are sound, which matters because the whole plan
revision rests on them and no probe had independently checked them until now. The substantive
finding is a contradiction between two of its own sections that nobody caught for the rest of the
task. § 2 recommends "**sorts, not a context language**" for the arity bound, which puts each pinned
support set in its own sort; § 5 item 1 then promises that the congruence on intervened states is
"what prunes probe 3's contingency search". Those cannot both hold. A compiled class never spans
sorts, and a contingency candidate's pinned support *is* its sort, so two different contingency sets
are never in the same class and the promised pruning can only ever act across the values of one
fixed support. That is exactly the ceiling probe 3 hit five hours later without recognising it —
verifier fractions flat at 66% to 73% and pruning falling to zero on the larger rows — and it was
derivable at pencil time from the two sections sitting three pages apart. Secondarily: the
closed-form state count is a uniform-domain, all-variables-intervenable special case presented as
the general formula, and one of the seven fixtures probe 1 shipped already falls outside it; the
"exactly when" in § 1's exception is sufficient rather than necessary; and the kill criterion as
written could not have fired.

## 1. The signature collapse and its proof — sound

This is the pencil result the plan revision rests on, so it is worth stating that it survives
checking.

**The proposition.** For the hard-intervention vocabulary, with `x = (u, ∅)` and `x' = (u', ∅)`,
`x ~ x'` in the compiled presentation iff `O(u, I) = O(u', I)` for every admissible partial
assignment `I`.

**The proof works.** Bisimulation on a deterministic labelled system is "for every word `w`, the
observation after `w` agrees". Reading a word `w` from `(u, ∅)` lands at `(u, I_w)` where `I_w` is
the final overwrite of `w`, and the two facts the proof needs are both true: `I_w` depends only on
`w` and not on `u`, because the exogenous coordinate is never written; and `w ↦ I_w` is surjective
onto `{I : |dom I| ≤ k}`, because any word listing `I`'s assignments produces `I`. So the
observation behaviour of `x` is exactly the function `I ↦ O(u, I)`, and two roots are bisimilar
exactly when those functions agree. The corollary — `~_{≤k}` refines monotonically because the
arity-`k` signature is a prefix of the arity-`k+1` one — follows from `enumerate_supports` emitting
supports shortest-first, which my probe 1 review verified in the implementation.

**One qualification the proof does not state, and it is the one that matters downstream.** The
proposition is about roots. The generalization to arbitrary intervened states is *not* "two states
are equivalent iff `O(u, I') = O(u', I')` for every admissible `I'`". The correct statement is that
`(u, I)` and `(u', I'')` are equivalent iff `dom(I) = dom(I'')` and `O(u, I ⊕ w) = O(u', I'' ⊕ w)`
for every admissible word effect `w` — indexed by the *overwrite*, not by the absolute pin set,
because a word cannot unpin a coordinate and the untouched coordinates keep each state's own values.
I found this the hard way while rebuilding the class oracle in Python for the probe 3 review: keyed
on the absolute pin set my congruence pruned 36 candidates where the compiler prunes 15, and it
matched exactly only after being re-indexed by the word effect. § 5 item 1 makes a claim about
exactly these states and does not carry the qualification.

## 2. § 2's sort structure forecloses § 5 item 1's promise — defect, and it is the one that cost the task

§ 2 sets the sort structure and recommends it over the alternative:

> **Sort structure.** One sort per pinned *support set*: `Sort_S = Val(U) × Val(S)` … This is cleaner
> than the alternative of encoding the bound as a `FiniteContextLanguage` over generator words, which
> would restrict admissible words but still demand generators total on the full unbounded carrier.
> Recommendation: **sorts, not a context language**, for the arity bound.

§ 5 item 1 then names the payoff:

> **The congruence on intervened states.** The signature partition says nothing about pairs
> `(u, I)`, `(u', I')` with non-empty pins. The compiler gives class equality for all of them, which
> means two contingency sets in the same class have identical futures under every subsequent edit.
> **This, not the quotient on `Val(U)`, is what prunes probe 3's contingency search**.

The two are incompatible. In a typed presentation the generators are declared per source sort, so
states in different sorts have different outgoing generator index sets and a label-respecting
bisimulation cannot identify them; `CompiledObservation` makes this concrete by carrying one
`SortRange` per sort in `class_ranges`. A contingency candidate in probe 3 is a
`(cause, witness, setting)` triple whose pinned support is `cause ∪ witness` — which is precisely
the sort. So two candidates with *different contingency sets* are in different sorts and are never in
the same class. The congruence can only identify candidates sharing a support and differing in the
pinned values.

The consequence is quantitative and it is probe 3's result. The candidate count for a fixed cause is
dominated by the choice of witness set, `C(|pool| − |X|, |W|)`, which is the axis the congruence
cannot touch; the compressible axis is the settings of one support, whose size is `d^{|X|} − 1`. For
the dominant layer — singleton causes over binary variables — that factor is one, so no compaction is
possible at all. Probe 3 measured a verifier fraction of 58% on its one real row and predicted it
would "improve sharply with model size"; my probe 3 review measured it flat at 66% to 73% from five
to eleven voters with pruning falling to zero on the larger rows. The ceiling was in probe 1a § 2 and
the promise was in probe 1a § 5, three pages apart, at pencil time, before any code.

**What the pencil probe should have done with it.** Not necessarily change the recommendation — the
memory argument for sorts is real and correct, since the context-language route materializes
`|U| · (d+1)^n` states against the sorts route's `|U| · π_k`. What is missing is the trade: the sorts
route buys memory and gives up any possibility of identifying states with different pinned domains,
and it is exactly that identification that item 1 was selling. Naming the trade would have made probe
3's compaction threshold visibly unreachable before it was predeclared at "under 10%".

## 3. The closed-form state count is a special case presented as the general one — defect, no arithmetic effect

§ 3 writes, with `n = |V|` and uniform domain size `d`:

```
σ_k  =  Σ_{j ≤ k} C(n, j)            (number of sorts)
π_k  =  Σ_{j ≤ k} C(n, j) d^j        (pin configurations)
|X_k| = |U| · π_k                    (materialized states)
```

Two hypotheses are unstated, and the implementation is more general than the formula in both.

**Sorts range over the intervenable set, not over `V`.** `enumerate_supports(&self.intervenable, arity)`
enumerates subsets of the declared intervenable variables, so the correct binomial is `C(m, j)` with
`m = |intervenable|`. On every fixture in the task `m = n`, so nothing computed is wrong. But the
distinction is not academic: probe 3's second lane defect was `enumerate_supports` generating
positions into the intervenable list and using them as variable ids, a bug that is invisible exactly
when `m = n` and the vocabulary is `0..n`. The closed form as written encodes the same assumption
the bug depended on.

**Domains need not be uniform.** `sort_states` multiplies the actual per-variable domain sizes, so
the general form is `π_k = Σ_{|S| ≤ k} ∏_{v ∈ S} d_v`. The task already ships a fixture outside the
uniform form: `identity-predicted-loss` has domains 2 and 3, giving `π_2 = 1 + (2 + 3) + 6 = 12` and
`6 × 12 = 72` states, which is what the report prints — and which no single `d` in
`1 + 2d + d²` reproduces (`d = 2` gives 9, `d = 3` gives 16).

The `k = n` specialization `|X| = |U| · (d + 1)^n` is correct, and every arithmetic instance probe 1a
computed from these formulas is right; I recomputed all of them in the probe 1 review and found one
slip, in the `n = 30` row.

## 4. § 1's "exactly when" is sufficient, not necessary — wording problem

The load-bearing argument in § 1 is correct and is the reason the plan was revised: `do(V := a)`
does not act on `u`, the only function on `Val(U)` it induces is the identity, and a presentation
whose every generator is the identity computes plain observational equivalence. That kills the first
plan's lowering and the killing is clean.

The exception is stated as an equivalence: "`state = u` remains closed **exactly when** every
intervenable `V` is exogenous in disguise: `pa(V) = {U_V}` for a dedicated exogenous parent and
`f_V` a bijection." Sufficiency is right, including the dedication requirement without which moving
`U_V` would move other variables. Necessity is not: a variable with two dedicated exogenous parents
and a surjective mechanism is also closed, since some setting of those coordinates realizes each
value of `V` without disturbing anything else. The general condition is that for each intervenable
`V` and each value, some modification of dedicated exogenous coordinates achieves it while leaving
every other endogenous variable alone. Nothing in the task depends on the difference — all four named
fixtures intervene on non-roots and are excluded either way.

## 6. The kill criterion could not have fired — finding

§ 6 states it: "if the arity-bounded state count is impractical at every `k` producing a non-trivial
quotient, the flat lowering is dead and the spike jumps to probe 7."

As written this cannot fire on any small model. `k = 1` produces a non-trivial quotient on essentially
any fixture with more than one class, and `|U| · (1 + Σ_v d_v)` states is practical for any `|U|`
worth writing down. To fire, *every* `k` with a non-trivial quotient would have to be impractical,
and the smallest such `k` never is. The probe duly reports "it does not fire", and the report is
right about that; what is missing is the observation that the criterion was not capable of firing,
which is the same shape as probe 8's "word closure non-vacuous, measured" — a threshold met by any
nonzero outcome.

The conclusion is nonetheless robust, and in an unexpected direction. The criterion was evaluated
against probe 1a's own memory model, which my probe 1 review showed to be six to nine times too
pessimistic in the arity-bounded regime. That error pushes toward firing, and it still did not fire.
So the substantive verdict — the flat lowering is viable — survives its own defect with room to
spare, and the recommendation it drove, promoting probe 7 from gated to expected, survives on the
argument that actually carries it: a canonical exogenous space is `∏_V d^{d^{|pa(V)|}}`, which for
ten binary variables with two parents each is about `1.1 × 10^12` and fails by twelve orders of
magnitude, a distance no constant of order ten touches.

## 7. What was checked and found sound

- **The `state = u` refutation** (§ 4 above), which is the probe's reason to exist and is correct.
- **The signature-collapse proposition and proof** (§ 1), independently checked here for the first
  time.
- **The arity bound by omitting extending generators at the boundary**, needing no sink state and no
  padding, which is legal because a typed presentation declares generators per source sort. Probe 1
  implements it exactly this way and gates it with the eight-variable boundary fixture.
- **The memory argument for sorts over a context language**: the context-language encoding would
  demand generators total on the unbounded `|U| · (d + 1)^n` carrier, against `|U| · π_k` for sorts.
  Real, and correct as far as it goes (§ 2 above for what it omits).
- **The transition tables dominating the states**, which is right in form — the per-state cost is
  `4 · (generators per state + 1)` bytes and not 4 — and wrong in the constant, per the probe 1 review.
- **Every arithmetic instance except one**, recomputed in the probe 1 review: `n = 3, 4, 5` at
  `1.1e5`, `5.3e6`, `2.5e8`; `π_k` for `n = 20` at `k = 2` and `k = 3`; `n = 10` at `k = 3` and full
  arity; `16^10 ≈ 1.1e12`; the 164-bytes-per-state figure for `n = 20, d = 2` as an interior
  constant; and `16,384 × 801 ≈ 1.3e7`. The exception is the `n = 30, k = 2` row, where `π_2` is
  1,801 rather than 1,741.
- **"Memory binds before the id width does"** survives the constant correction: even at the corrected
  ceiling of about `7e7` states the `u32` state id is not the binding limit. What the probe did not
  anticipate is that for the audited certificate policy neither is the transition table — the core
  repair note records `ExhaustivePairAudit` reaching 6.9 GB at 205,056 states, so the practical cap
  is the certificate's memory and not the presentation's.
- **The instruction that probe 1 must report `|X_k|` and the transition bytes rather than `|U|`**,
  which probe 1 obeyed everywhere except the per-state column.

**Verdict.** Probe 1a did the job a pencil probe exists to do: it killed an ill-typed lowering before
any code was written, and its two load-bearing results — the `state = u` refutation and the
signature-collapse proposition — are correct, the latter checked independently here for the first
time. Its cost model has one wrong constant, documented in the probe 1 review, and one under-stated
closed form. The finding worth carrying is structural rather than arithmetic: the sort structure it
recommended in § 2 makes the contingency pruning it promised in § 5 impossible across contingency
sets, which is the ceiling probe 3 spent a session hitting, and the trade between memory and
cross-support identification was available at pencil time and never named. That is the failure mode a
pencil probe is supposed to prevent, appearing inside the pencil probe itself.
