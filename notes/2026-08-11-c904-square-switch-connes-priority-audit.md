# C904 signed square-switch to Connes \(B\): priority audit

Date: 2026-08-11

Status: bounded claim-specific literature/priority audit; no manuscript or
Lean edits

## Executive verdict

The broad slogan **“a determinant antisymmetrizer becomes a signed sum of
cyclic words” is classical and must not be claimed as new**.  Procesi gives
the exact dictionary: a permutation is sent, cycle by cycle, to a product of
traces of words, and the total antisymmetrizer is sent to the fundamental
trace identity.  Generic signed Pluecker/Laplace straightening is classical
as well, and modern Frobenius Brauer categories already encode closed
labelled circles by cocenter (cyclic-word) data.

The other endpoint is independently classical.  Diagram categories act on
Hochschild complexes of symmetric or commutative Frobenius algebras and
recover the BV/Connes operation.  For the particular truncated algebra
\(R=k[u]/u^h\), Speirs's Lemma 4 prints the weightwise formula

\[
 B(z_m)=\pm m z'_m
\]

when \(h\nmid m\), together with the exceptional \(h\mid m\) groups.  Thus
neither the coefficient \(m\), nor the cyclic interpretation of the
truncated-polynomial torsion, is available for a novelty claim.

What this bounded audit did **not** locate is the exact composite needed by
C904:

\[
 \text{integral Frobenius--Hankel mixed cofactor}
 \longrightarrow
 \text{principal open-chain quotient}
 \longrightarrow
 \bigl(HH_0(R)\xrightarrow B HH_1(R)\bigr),
\]

with the first arrow proved well-defined after the **complete determinant
antisymmetrizer**, preserving the integral lattice and its loop contractions.
That bridge remains plausibly new.  It is more precise than, and is not
proved by, any of the classical pieces above.

There is a crucial correction to the proposed terminology.  A local
“signed square-switch descent” is not a standard Brauer relation and, in the
current C904 model, it is false term by term.  Abstract Brauer diagrams are a
basis; Pluecker straightening gives a three-term signed identity only after a
determinant/minor realization; and the existing local cyclic-readout
counterexample shows that the readout does not kill each switch.  The viable
new statement is therefore a **global antisymmetrizer-to-cyclic obstruction
lemma**, not a local square-switch quotient theorem.

This audit discusses eight primary sources.  Two were read at full text and
six at the specified partial depth below.  Its negative is bounded: no exact
predecessor was located in the primary sources and keyword families recorded
in §6, but this was not an exhaustive citation-graph closure.

## 1. Exact classical ancestry

### 1.1 Generic determinant/Pluecker straightening

Swan's Theorems 2.6, 3.1, and 4.1 give generalized Laplace relations and a
straightening law for one or two minors with coefficients \(\pm1\).
Corollary 5.5 says that, for a generic square matrix, all linear relations
among Laplace products follow from the generalized Laplace relations of
Theorem 2.6.

This fully preempts the following level of claim:

> signed local exchanges among generic determinant matchings are generated
> by Pluecker/Laplace straightening.

It does **not** prove the C904 statement.  The Hankel/Frobenius substitution
identifies many entries of the generic matrix and may create additional
relations; the labels are tensors in \(R\otimes R\); self-adjointness imposes
another quotient; and no cyclic or Hochschild target appears in Swan.

### 1.2 Antisymmetrizers already produce cyclic trace words

Procesi's construction immediately before Theorem 4.3 sends a permutation

\[
 \sigma=(i_1\cdots i_a)(j_1\cdots j_b)\cdots
\]

to

\[
 \operatorname{Tr}(X_{i_1}\cdots X_{i_a})
 \operatorname{Tr}(X_{j_1}\cdots X_{j_b})\cdots.
\]

Theorem 4.3 identifies trace identities with the appropriate Young-ideal
kernel and sends the one-column antisymmetrizer to the fundamental trace
identity.  Theorem 4.5 says that the trace-identity ideal is generated by
substitutions into these fundamental identities.

This is the closest classical precedent.  It preempts any unqualified
phrase such as “we discover a map from determinant antisymmetry to cyclic
words.”  The remaining distinctions are load-bearing:

- Procesi works with \(GL_n\) matrix invariants in characteristic zero, not
  the integral Frobenius--Hankel specialization;
- his output is a **product of ordinary matrix traces**, not a Hochschild
  chain or the map \(B:HH_0\to HH_1\);
- closed cycles, rather than a selected marked open chain, are the native
  objects;
- no integral primitivity or \(p\mid h\) loop obstruction is addressed.

The eventual paper should cite Procesi at the exact point where the complete
antisymmetrizer is reorganized by cycle structure.  The new theorem, if
proved, should be presented as an integral Frobenius-Hankel/open-chain
refinement of that dictionary.

### 1.3 Brauer and Frobenius-Brauer diagrams

Lehrer--Zhang's Theorem 2.6 presents the free Brauer category by generators
and relations; its diagram set is a free basis.  Their Theorem 4.8 identifies,
in characteristic zero, the kernel of the orthogonal/symplectic contraction
functor by total symmetrizer or antisymmetrizer data; Theorems 5.9 and 6.10
give explicit symplectic and orthogonal kernel generators.  Theorem 7.3's
positive-characteristic extension assumes a large-characteristic bound and
its proof localizes at a factorial.

The consequence for C904 is sharp: a square switch is **not** an abstract
Brauer-category equality.  It appears only after imposing a representation
kernel or determinant realization, and its normalization depends on rank,
orthogonal versus symplectic convention, and characteristic.

McSween--Savage supply the closest labelled version.  They define the
cocenter

\[
 C(A)=A/[A,A]
\]

and the bubble algebra \(\operatorname{Sym}(C(A)[x])\).  Their Theorem 4.7
says that oriented affine Frobenius-Brauer Hom spaces are free over this
bubble algebra on decorated reduced matchings.  Their Example 3.4 includes
the truncated current algebra \(A=k[t]/t^\ell\).

Thus “closed labelled loops are cyclic-word/cocenter data” is also classical.
But the theorem is over a field, oriented, and static: it does not construct
Connes \(B\), select a marked open chain from an adjugate, or retain the
integral torsion of a closed Frobenius loop.

Samchuck-Schnarch treats the unoriented affine Frobenius-Brauer setting and
lists truncated polynomial algebras among the examples.  Proposition 3.8
shows exactly how changing the Frobenius trace changes the affine dot by an
even central unit.  The proposed general affine basis is still Conjecture
4.4, although spanning is established from the displayed relations.  This is
strong negative evidence against treating the desired integral labelled
straightening as a routine consequence of an existing unoriented basis
theorem.

## 2. Exact classical ancestry on the Hochschild side

Tradler--Zeinalian's Theorem 3.3 makes cyclic Sullivan chord diagrams act on
normalized Hochschild cochains of a finite-dimensional algebra with a
nondegenerate invariant inner product; Corollary 3.4 obtains the BV
structure.  Their Theorem 4.3 extends the action to general Sullivan chord
diagrams in the commutative case.  These are the standard Frobenius/chord
diagram operations behind the dual of Connes \(B\).

Klamt's Theorem 2.3 gives a dg-functor from looped diagrams to natural
operations on Hochschild complexes of commutative Frobenius dg-algebras.
Proposition 2.11 identifies a displayed looped diagram with the BV/dual
string-topology operation.  The defining diagram relations are edge
collapse, valence, reordering, and loop-sliding relations.  They are not the
three-term Pluecker relations of a determinant cofactor.

Speirs provides the exact truncated-polynomial normalization.  In §3,
Proposition 3 computes \(HH_*(k[u]/u^h)\) over a general commutative unital
base ring and exhibits the two-periodic resolution with maps

\[
 y-x,
 \qquad
 \frac{y^h-x^h}{y-x}.
\]

Lemma 4 decomposes by total weight \(m\) and states that, for \(h\nmid m\),
Connes \(B\) carries a generator in even degree to \(m\) times a generator in
odd degree, up to sign; for \(h\mid m\), the exceptional groups are \(k/hk\)
and \(k[h]\), and \(B\) is zero.  Lemma 6 restates the same calculation on the
cyclic bar summands.

Accordingly, the C904 equality

\[
 B(u^m)=m u^{m-1}du

\]

and its \(p\)-primary denominator are standard consequences of cyclic
homology.  The new work can only be the proof that the *determinant cofactor
lattice* reaches this standard complex with exactly the claimed integral
image.

## 3. Priority line for the proposed theorem

The following claims are **preempted** and should be credited rather than
advertised as new:

1. generic signed Pluecker/Laplace straightening of determinant minors;
2. permutation cycles indexing products of cyclic trace words;
3. the total antisymmetrizer producing the fundamental trace identity;
4. matchings and contraction kernels in Brauer invariant theory;
5. closed Frobenius-labelled loops carrying cocenter/cyclic-word labels;
6. chord/loop diagrams inducing Hochschild and BV/Connes operations;
7. the coefficient \(m\) in Connes \(B\) for \(k[u]/u^h\).

The strongest presently unpreempted formulation located in this audit is:

> **Integral Frobenius--Hankel antisymmetrizer theorem.**  On the image of
> the complete multilinear adjugate for the self-adjoint truncated
> Frobenius algebra \(R=O[u]/u^h\), the principal marked-open-chain
> obstruction is well defined, agrees on the one-carry associated graded
> with \(B:HH_0(R)\to HH_1(R)\), and has the claimed primitive image after
> all closed-loop contractions.

That statement is not in any source located here.  It is also **not yet
proved** by the current local-switch model.  Priority language should remain
“no predecessor located in a bounded audit” until the global descent lemma
exists and receives a forward-citation closure.

## 4. Normalization and proof hazards

1. **Three terms, not two.**  A Pluecker square relation is a signed
   three-term identity.  A two-term switch requires a separately proved
   vanishing, quotient, or fixed-endpoint specialization.
2. **Global versus local.**  The complete determinant antisymmetrizer may
   admit a cyclic reorganization even though no individual local switch is
   killed.  The existing C904 counterexample rules out using the latter as
   the proof of the former.
3. **Brauer loop parameter versus Frobenius Euler element.**  The scalar
   Brauer parameter \(\delta\) is not the C904 loop value.  With
   \(\lambda(u^{h-1})=1\), the Frobenius Euler element is
   \(e_R=h u^{h-1}\).  It is nonprimitive exactly when a prime divides \(h\).
4. **Trace rescaling.**  Replacing \(\lambda\) by \(\lambda(u_0\,\cdot)\)
   for a central unit changes the dual basis/teleporter.  Proposition 3.8 of
   Samchuck-Schnarch shows that the affine dot is correspondingly rescaled.
   A basis-free theorem must prove that its obstruction is invariant under
   this conjugate normalization.
5. **Connes signs.**  Chain conventions use variants of
   \(B=(1-t)sN\).  On \(HH_0\), the universal-derivative identification is
   canonical only after fixing normalized chains and the \(du\) generator;
   Speirs states the weight coefficient only up to sign.  The cochain BV
   operator in chord-diagram sources is the Frobenius transpose of chain
   \(B\), with a degree shift and possible Koszul sign.
6. **Generic versus Hankel.**  Swan's independence theorem is for a generic
   matrix.  Hankel specialization, truncation \(u^h=0\), and
   transpose/self-adjoint invariance can introduce extra relations.  The
   special fibre cannot inherit generic straightening injectivity without a
   proof.
7. **Fields erase the desired obstruction.**  The Frobenius-Brauer basis
   sources work over fields, commonly with characteristic restrictions.  The
   C904 phenomenon is an integral or \(p\)-local saturation defect occurring
   precisely where \(p\mid h\).  Tensoring with a field can make the crucial
   cokernel disappear.
8. **Closed-loop splitting is illegal at the wall.**  Discarding closed
   cycles would require dividing by their Euler value; this is exactly the
   nonunit \(h\) whose valuation the theorem is meant to detect.
9. **Procesi traces are not Connes chains.**  A product of scalar traces is
   a degree-zero invariant.  Passing to one marked position and interpreting
   the result as \(B\) is an additional construction, not a terminological
   identification.
10. **Base-change trace.**  Any unramified extension or residue-degree trace
    can multiply evaluations and alter primitivity.  The theorem must state
    whether it is over \(O\), its residue field, or after scalar restriction.

## 5. Acceptance gate for a genuinely new result

Before novelty can be attached to the bridge, a proof should print the
following data.

1. The free integral module of labelled matchings and the exact submodule of
   determinant/Laplace relations.
2. The global cyclic-readout formula on the complete antisymmetrizer, with
   an explicit proof that it is independent of every straightening choice.
3. The projection to the principal open-chain associated graded, including
   every closed-loop Euler factor rather than deleting loops over a field.
4. A commuting square with the two-periodic diagonal resolution and Connes
   \(B\), including the sign convention.
5. An integral primitivity/saturation argument, not merely equality after
   tensoring with \(\mathbf Q\) or a residue field.
6. Small-rank replay showing both the positive cases and the known local
   switch counterexample.  The theorem should explain why the global sum
   survives the counterexample.

If these gates close, the priority claim can be narrow and strong: the new
result is the **integral open-chain refinement of the classical
antisymmetrizer-to-trace dictionary**, specialized to a truncated symmetric
Frobenius/Hankel algebra.
