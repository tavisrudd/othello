\section{Sharp limits of coarser recovery invariants}
\label{sec:separations}

The preceding sections identify exact information for three different tasks.
Here we show that two tempting coarsenings lose essential information.  The
first separation rules out the complete RGHW hierarchy as a reliability
invariant at every quotient rank: minimum support sizes do not retain the
overlap pattern of recovery sets.  The second rules out even the full abstract
nested pair, together with the same unlabelled helper supports, as a
confinement invariant: its ambient presentation can change $d(I^\perp)$ and
the prescribed-coset costs in~\eqref{eq:weighted-subspace-cost}.

First consider a one-dimensional recovered space.  On the helper set
$\{0,\ldots,8\}$, let
\[
 \begin{split}
  \mathcal A&=\{158,026,045,013,478\},\\
  \mathcal B&=\{168,237,078,015,124\},
 \end{split}
\]
where, for example, $158$ denotes $\{1,5,8\}$.  Both are realizable as the
minimum recovery sets at a distinguished coordinate of a represented
$[10,4,6]_q$ code over a common finite prime field.
Here a \emph{clutter} means a family of sets none of which contains another;
in this example it is the five inclusion-minimal recovery supports.

\begin{proposition}[Rank-one support-overlap separation]
\label{prop:rank-one-reliability-separation}
\coverage{absent}%
The two distinguished coordinates above have the same minimum helper cost
$M_1=3$.  Under independent homogeneous helper survival with probability
$s$, their radius-three repair reliabilities are
\begin{align}
 R_{\mathcal A}(s)&=5s^3-7s^5-s^6+5s^7-s^9,
 \label{eq:reliability-A}\\
 R_{\mathcal B}(s)&=5s^3-7s^5-2s^6+8s^7-3s^8.
 \label{eq:reliability-B}
\end{align}
In particular, the complete rank-one relative-weight hierarchy does not
determine bounded repair reliability.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The five triples in each clutter are the minimum recovery sets, so both
minimum costs are three.  For $k=1,\ldots,5$, the numbers of $k$-edge
subfamilies according to union size are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
k&\mathcal A&\mathcal B\\ \hline
1&5u^3&5u^3\\
2&7u^5+3u^6&7u^5+3u^6\\
3&2u^6+6u^7+2u^8&u^6+8u^7+u^8\\
4&u^7+2u^8+2u^9&4u^8+u^9\\
5&u^9&u^9.
\end{array}
\]
Inclusion--exclusion for the five edge-containment events gives
\eqref{eq:reliability-A} and~\eqref{eq:reliability-B}.

We now give the representation argument.  Its purpose is to realize exactly
the five listed triples as collinear triples; after lifting, adjoining the
distinguished target $x$ turns exactly those triples into four-column circuits.

\paragraph{The two plane arrangements.}
Over an infinite field $K$, choose
five projective lines $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_5$.  For
$\mathcal A$, make $\ell_2,\ell_3,\ell_4$ concurrent and label
\[
 0=\ell_2\cap\ell_3\cap\ell_4,\quad
 1=\ell_1\cap\ell_4,\quad
 4=\ell_3\cap\ell_5,\quad
 5=\ell_1\cap\ell_3,\quad
 8=\ell_1\cap\ell_5.
\]
Choose $2,6$ on $\ell_2$, $3$ on $\ell_4$, and $7$ on $\ell_5$.
For $\mathcal B$, make $\ell_1,\ell_4,\ell_5$ concurrent at $1$, put
\[
 8=\ell_1\cap\ell_3,\quad
 7=\ell_2\cap\ell_3,\quad
 0=\ell_3\cap\ell_4,\quad
 2=\ell_2\cap\ell_5,
\]
and choose $6,3,5,4$ on $\ell_1,\ell_2,\ell_4,\ell_5$, respectively.
The incidence skeleton is
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
 & \text{three concurrent lines} & \text{other prescribed intersections}\\ \hline
\mathcal A & \ell_2,\ell_3,\ell_4\text{ at }0
  & 1=\ell_1\cap\ell_4,\ 4=\ell_3\cap\ell_5,\
    5=\ell_1\cap\ell_3,\ 8=\ell_1\cap\ell_5\\
\mathcal B & \ell_1,\ell_4,\ell_5\text{ at }1
  & 8=\ell_1\cap\ell_3,\ 7=\ell_2\cap\ell_3,\
    0=\ell_3\cap\ell_4,\ 2=\ell_2\cap\ell_5
\end{array}
\]
At each free choice, avoid the finitely many unwanted joining lines.  The
unwanted lines are precisely those that would create an additional collinear
triple.  The displayed triples are therefore exactly the collinear triples.

\paragraph{Generic lift.}
Choose representatives $p_i\in K^3$ of either plane configuration, let
$x=(1,0,0,0)$, and lift helper $i$ to $h_i=(t_i,p_i)\in K^4$.  Dependence of
the lifts over any displayed line and dependence of any four helper lifts are
proper linear conditions on the $t_i$.  For a displayed line, its three
quotient vectors have a unique dependence up to scalar, and the same
coefficients give one nonzero linear equation in the $t_i$.  For four helper
points, the quotient vectors span $K^3$, so their lifted determinant is a
nonzero linear form in the four first coordinates.  Avoiding this finite union
makes every helper triple and quadruple independent, while the dependent
four-sets through $x$ are exactly $\{x\}\cup A$ for the five prescribed
triples $A$.

\paragraph{Finite-field realization and parameters.}
Choose the construction over $\mathbb Q$ and reduce both
configurations modulo one prime avoiding the finitely many nonzero
determinants.  The two row-span codes then have rank four, dual distance four,
and minimum recovery sets exactly $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$.  A
hyperplane not containing $x$ contains at most three helpers because every
four helpers are independent.  A hyperplane containing $x$ contains at most
one displayed collinear triple, and such a triple gives four columns in a
hyperplane.  Thus the maximum hyperplane intersection has size four, so both
codes have distance $10-4=6$.
\proves{prop:rank-one-reliability-separation}%
\end{proof}

The rank-one separation extends to every quotient rank without changing any
relative generalized weight.  For a nested pair $K\subseteq D\leq\F_q^J$
with $\dim(D/K)=\ell$, define its radius-$R$ full-quotient reliability by
\[
 \Pr\{\exists L\leq D:\ L+K=D,\ |\supp L|\leq R,
                     \ \supp L\subseteq S\},
\]
where $S\subseteq J$ is the random set of independently surviving helpers,
each present with probability $s$.
The condition $L+K=D$ says that the surviving helper equations recover the
entire quotient $D/K$; the radius $R$ bounds their union support $|\supp L|$.

\begin{theorem}[The complete RGHW hierarchy does not determine reliability]
\label{thm:rghw-reliability-separation}
\coverage{absent}%
For every $\ell\geq1$, there are two nested code pairs of quotient dimension
$\ell$ with identical relative generalized Hamming weights
$M_1,\ldots,M_\ell$ but different bounded reliability laws for recovery of
the full quotient.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $\ell=1$, use Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-one-reliability-separation}.
For $\ell>1$, let $K_0\subset D_0$ be either of its two associated rank-one
pairs.  On disjoint helper blocks, add the same $\ell-1$ pairs
\[
 0\subset\langle\mathbf1_{L_i}\rangle
 \qquad(1\leq i\leq\ell-1),
\]
where each padding line has the unique support of size $L_i$.  Thus
\[
 K=K_0\oplus0\oplus\cdots\oplus0,
 \qquad
 D=D_0\oplus\langle\mathbf1_{L_1}\rangle\oplus\cdots
       \oplus\langle\mathbf1_{L_{\ell-1}}\rangle.
\]
For a direct sum of rank-one quotient pairs, the $t$th relative weight is the
sum of the $t$ smallest component costs.  Both constructions therefore have
the same hierarchy, obtained from the common multiset
$\{3,L_1,\ldots,L_{\ell-1}\}$.

To verify the direct-sum formula, project a feasible helper subspace to each
quotient component.  A $t$-dimensional quotient image has nonzero projection
to at least $t$ of the one-dimensional components.  On disjoint helper
blocks, each active projection pays at least that component's minimum cost,
so the total support is at least the sum of the $t$ smallest costs.  The
direct sum of minimum words from the $t$ cheapest components attains the
bound.

Set $R=3+\sum_iL_i$.  A recovery subspace of full quotient rank projects
nontrivially to every padding component and hence uses its entire forced
support.  Removing those supports leaves a radius-three recovery set in the
base component.  Conversely, any base radius-three recovery set together
with the padding generators recovers the full quotient at radius $R$.  Thus
\[
 R_{\mathrm{full},R}(s)
 =s^{\sum_iL_i}R_{\mathrm{base},3}(s).
\]
The two polynomials remain different by
\eqref{eq:reliability-A}--\eqref{eq:reliability-B}.

Every pair in the construction is realized by an inner code.  Put
$V=\F_q^J/K$, let $\pi:\F_q^J\to V$ be the quotient map, and choose an
isomorphism $\alpha:\F_q^\ell\to D/K\leq V$.  The generator map
\[
 G:\F_q^\ell\oplus\F_q^J\longrightarrow V,
 \qquad G(a,x)=\alpha(a)+\pi(x),
\]
uses the first $\ell$ coordinates as targets, $J$ as helpers, and has message
space $V$.  Its helper kernel is $K$ and the helper preimage of the target span
is $D$, so its associated nested pair is $K\subseteq D$.
\uses{prop:rank-one-reliability-separation}%
\proves{thm:rghw-reliability-separation}%
\end{proof}

The second separation fixes the abstract nested pair itself.  This is not a
row-basis change of one inner code: by
Proposition~\ref{prop:puncture-shorten-pair}, such a change leaves
$(D_P,K_P)$ unchanged.  Here a \emph{coefficient presentation} means an
ambient inner dual code, target coordinates, and target coefficient map that
realize a prescribed abstract pair $K\subseteq D$ as its
shortening--puncturing pair.  Different such realizations may have different
ambient dual codes and distances, so the examples below cannot be related by
changing the row basis of one encoder.  Let
\[
 K=0,\qquad
 D=\langle u,v\rangle\leq\F_2^3,\qquad
 u=100,\quad v=011.
\]
The nonzero helper words have weights $1,2,3$, so
$(M_1,M_2)=(1,3)$.

\begin{proposition}[The abstract nested pair does not determine confinement]
\label{prop:coefficient-presentation}
\coverage{absent}%
Present the target space first by
\[
 10\mapsto u,\qquad01\mapsto v,\qquad11\mapsto u+v,
\]
and then by
\[
 11\mapsto u,\qquad10\mapsto v,\qquad01\mapsto u+v.
\]
The two inner codes have the same associated nested pair, the same unlabelled
set of helper supports, and the same complete relative-weight
hierarchy.  Their inner-dual weight multisets are respectively
$\{2,3,5\}$ and $\{3,3,4\}$.  In every outer-distance regime where the
collapse theorem applies, their additive costs are
respectively
\[
 (M_1+d(I^\perp),M_2+d(I^\perp))=(3,5)
 \qquad\text{and}\qquad
 (M_1+d(I^\perp),M_2+d(I^\perp))=(4,6).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the graph-code realization
\[
 I^\perp=\{(-\alpha^{-1}(x),x):x\in D\},
\]
where $\alpha$ is the displayed identification from the two-dimensional
target space to $D$.  Over $\F_2$ the minus sign is invisible.  The three
nonzero graph words are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c|c}
 & \text{target coefficient} & \text{helper word}
 & \text{target/helper weights} & \text{total weight}\\ \hline
I_1 & 10 & 100 & 1+1 & 2\\
    & 01 & 011 & 1+2 & 3\\
    & 11 & 111 & 2+3 & 5\\ \hline
I_2 & 11 & 100 & 2+1 & 3\\
    & 10 & 011 & 1+2 & 3\\
    & 01 & 111 & 1+3 & 4
\end{array}
\]
Thus $d(I^\perp)$ is two in the first
presentation and three in the second.  Adding this distance to
$(M_1,M_2)=(1,3)$ gives the two threshold sequences by
Theorem~\ref{thm:ranked-confinement}.
\uses{thm:ranked-confinement}%
\proves{prop:coefficient-presentation}%
\end{proof}

These are limits on representation, not on executability.  Keeping the
functional labels avoids the second loss, and keeping minimizing lifts permits
the exact recursion to return the original coefficient systems.  The next
section turns that closure law into an optimizer.
