\section{Prescribed positive-density realization}
\label{sec:realization}

The pointed zero-functional sector persists even when outer dual distance
grows.

\begin{theorem}[Prescribed represented recovery structures]
\label{thm:prescribed}
Let $O_N\leq L^N$ be $L$-linear outer codes with
$d(O_N^\perp)\to\infty$, and let $C_N=O_N\circ I$.  For all sufficiently
large $N$, every pointed dual witness through $(j,x)$ of weight at most
$r+1$ is confined to block $j$ if and only if
\[
 r+1<z_x(I).
\]
Under this condition the radius-$r$ helper supports and normalized equations
at $x$ occur on the designated
class of $N$ targets $(j,x)$, a class of density exactly $1/m$ in $C_N$.
Other inner coordinate types may carry isomorphic recovery data as well.  More
precisely, at every block
\[
 \cH_{(j,x)}^{(\leq r)}(C_N)
   =\operatorname{embed}_j\bigl(\cH_x^{(\leq r)}(I)\bigr),
 \qquad
 \cP_{(j,x)}^{(\leq r)}(C_N)
   =\operatorname{embed}_j\bigl(\cP_x^{(\leq r)}(I)\bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every \(\F_q\)-linear functional on \(L\) has a unique form
\(a\mapsto\Tr_{L/\F_q}(ba)\), by nondegeneracy of the trace pairing.  Let
\(\beta=(\beta_j)\) lie in the functional dual of the outer code,
and write \(\beta_j(a)=\Tr(b_ja)\).  Since \(O_N\) is \(L\)-linear, applying
\(\beta\) to every scalar multiple \(ca\) of every \(a\in O_N\) shows
\[
 \Tr\!\left(c\sum_j b_ja_j\right)=0
 \quad\text{for every }c\in L.
\]
Trace nondegeneracy gives \((b_j)\in O_N^\perp\).  Conversely, every outer
dual word gives such a functional tuple.  The trace representation is
coordinatewise injective, so it preserves support exactly.  Thus the
functional-dual support distance is \(d(O_N^\perp)\).

Fix \(r\).  For all sufficiently large \(N\),
\(d(O_N^\perp)\ge r+2\).  Every realization of a nonzero functional tuple
then has weight at least \(r+2\): each nonzero functional requires a nonzero
inner block.  The exact pointed decomposition of
Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} consequently leaves only the zero-functional
sector in the bounded range.  For \(N\ge2\), its exact cost is the
block-independent quantity \(z_x(I)\).  Hence confinement through every
\((j,x)\) holds exactly when \(r+1<z_x(I)\).

If \(r+1<z_x(I)\), Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} identifies both exact helper
supports and normalized equations by zero-extension.  If
\(r+1\ge z_x(I)\), the attained pointed inner-dual minimum at \(x\), together
with a minimum nonzero inner-dual word in a second block, gives a
zero-functional pointed nonembedded witness of weight at most \(r+1\).
This proves necessity as well as sufficiency.  Finally, the \(N\) designated
targets \((j,x)\) among the \(mN\) concatenated coordinates form a class of
density exactly \(1/m\), irrespective of any additional occurrences.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Concatenated parameters]
\label{prop:concat-parameters}
If \(I\) has parameters \([m,k,d]_q\) and
\(O_N\leq L^N\) has \(L\)-dimension \(K_N\) and symbol distance \(D_N\),
then \(C_N=O_N\circ I\) has length \(mN\), \(\F_q\)-dimension \(kK_N\),
and minimum distance at least \(dD_N\).  In particular, if
\[
 \frac{K_N}{N}\longrightarrow R>0,
 \qquad
 \liminf_N\frac{D_N}{N}\geq\delta>0,
\]
then the concatenated family has asymptotic rate \(kR/m\) and relative
distance at least \(d\delta/m\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The blockwise inner encoder is injective, so restriction of scalars multiplies
the outer \(L\)-dimension by \([L:\F_q]=k\).  A nonzero outer word has at
least \(D_N\) nonzero symbols, and each nonzero symbol encodes to an inner
word of weight at least \(d\).  The finite parameter statements follow;
division by \(mN\) gives the asymptotic claims.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Positive-density MDS coefficient fingerprints]
\label{cor:mds-fingerprints}
Let \(I\) be an \([m,k]_q\) MDS code with \(1\leq k<m\), and fix \(x\).
Its radius-\(k\) exact helper-support family is the complete \(k\)-uniform
clutter on the other coordinates, while its minimum-weight normalized
recovery equations reconstruct \(I\), and \(z_x(I)=2(k+1)\).  Both layers
occur on a designated target class
of density \(1/m\) in every sufficiently long
concatenation by an outer family with dual distance tending to infinity.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The minimum-weight assertions are Theorem~\ref{thm:mds-reconstruction}.  Every
minimum dual support through \(x\) has size \(k+1\), so
\(\mu_x(0)=d(I^\perp)=k+1\) and
\[
 z_x(I)=2(k+1)>k+1.
\]
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:prescribed} with \(r=k\).  The helper-support family depends
only on the MDS parameters; the transferred normalized coefficients retain
the represented inner code.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Positive-density Clebsch recovery equations]
\label{cor:clebsch-port}
Let $I$ be the $[6,3,4]_{11}$ Clebsch hexagon code
\cite{RuddClebschRigidity2026}, and fix a coordinate $x$.
The minimal radius-three repair clutter at $x$ is the complete
three-uniform hypergraph on the other five coordinates.  Hence it has ten
minimal repair supports,
\[
 \nu_x^{(\leq3)}=1,\qquad \tau_x^{(\leq3)}=3,
\]
and homogeneous helper-survival reliability
\[
 10s^3(1-s)^2+5s^4(1-s)+s^5.
\]
Already the radius-three equations at $x$ determine $I$.
Moreover $z_x(I)=8$, so both the minimum-weight radius-three recovery equations
and the full radius-five equation family occur on a
designated target class of density
$1/6$ in an asymptotically
good fixed-$\F_{11}$ family.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The dual $I^\perp$ is again an $[6,3,4]_{11}$ MDS code.  Every four-set
containing $x$ is therefore the support of a dual word, unique up to scalar.
The minimal helper sets are consequently all $\binom53=10$ triples on the
other coordinates.  Their matching and transversal numbers are one and
three, and recovery succeeds exactly when at least three of the five
helpers survive, giving the displayed polynomial.

At radius three the common-core star of minimum normalized dual words is
already a basis of \(I^\perp\), by
Corollary~\ref{cor:mds-fingerprints}.  Thus the minimum-weight normalized
recovery equations, and hence also the full radius-five family, recover
\(I^\perp\) and \(I\).

Finally, the minimum dual-word weight through $x$ is four, so
\[
 z_x(I)=\mu_x(0)+d(I^\perp)=4+4=8.
\]
Taking $r=5$ gives $r+1=6<8$.  Theorem~\ref{thm:prescribed} therefore
confines every full pointed witness to its inner block and reproduces the
coefficient fibers at one of the six coordinate types in every block.
Choosing asymptotically good outer codes over $\F_{11^3}$ gives the claimed
fixed-$\F_{11}$ family and density.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The support clutter and reliability polynomial in
Corollary~\ref{cor:clebsch-port} are shared by every $[6,3,4]$ MDS code.
The Clebsch-specific information lies in the scalar coefficient fibers:
they recover the inner code that is characterized geometrically in
\cite{RuddClebschRigidity2026}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
Corollary~\ref{cor:mds-fingerprints} applies without a new transfer argument
to every represented MDS inner code.  This includes the usual arc and
projective Reed--Solomon presentations, and the classical MDS code
presentations underlying AME constructions, whenever their displayed field
and dimension hypotheses hold.  Their minimum exact helper-support families
are generic MDS data; only their normalized equations distinguish the represented
geometric or quantum-code realization.
\end{remark}

This is a represented-recovery theorem: it does not say that every abstract
hypergraph is linearly representable.  It yields standard scaled asymptotic
regions.  If $Q=q^k$, random $L$-linear outer codes of rate $R$ can have
relative distances $\delta,\delta^\perp$ whenever
\[
 H_Q(\delta)<1-R,
 \qquad H_Q(\delta^\perp)<R.
\]
For an inner $[m,k,d]_q$ code the concatenated parameters satisfy
\[
 R_{\rm concat}\to\frac{k}{m}R,
 \qquad
 \delta_{\rm concat}\geq\frac{d}{m}\delta.
\]
Indeed, for a random $L$-linear outer code the expected numbers of nonzero
words and dual words below the two cutoffs have exponential rates
$R+H_Q(\delta)-1$ and $H_Q(\delta^\perp)-R$, respectively, so both vanish
simultaneously; this is the random-linear-code form of the
Gilbert--Varshamov argument \cite[Chapter~17]{MacWilliamsSloane1977}.
When $Q$ is square, algebraic-geometry outer codes similarly give, for
$\gamma=1/A(Q)$ and $\gamma<R<1-\gamma$,
\[
 \delta(O_N)\geq1-R-\gamma,
 \qquad
 \delta(O_N^\perp)\geq R-\gamma,
 \qquad
 \delta_{\rm concat}\geq\frac{d}{m}(1-R-\gamma).
\]
These are the classical GV and TVZ ingredients.  For the completed
$[20,4,9]_9$ seed, $Q=6561$ and $\gamma\leq1/80$, recovering the concrete
rate-$1/10$ family as one instance
\cite[Chapters~2 and~7]{Stichtenoth2009}.  The stronger self-dual family used
for that concrete instance is precisely Stichtenoth's theorem over
\(\F_{6561}\) \cite[Theorem~1.6(ii)]{Stichtenoth2005}.
