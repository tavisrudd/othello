\subsection{Relation to adjacent formulations}
\label{sec:related-work}

RGHWs and RDLPs describe support and access thresholds for nested codes
\cite{LuoEtAl2005,KuriharaUyematsuMatsumoto2012,GeilEtAl2014,SanJose2025,
Geil2026}; our identity $\mu_t(P)=M_t(D_P,K_P)$ specializes this theory to the
canonical target/helper pair.  Recovery-set formulations retain supports but
generally discard the normalized coefficients and functional labels coupling
blocks \cite{GopalanEtAl2012,PamiesEtAl2013,RawatMazumdarVishwanath2015,
AbdelGhaffarWeber2017,MarquezCorbellaEtAl2019,GruicaEtAl2026}.  Disjoint
simplex recovery likewise optimizes support families rather than labelled
transfer \cite{CheeEtAl2024}.  Regenerating codes instead optimize download and
subpacketization \cite{ShahEtAl2010,YeBarg2016,GuruswamiWootters2016,
MatthewsEtAl2026}.

Service-rate regions allocate requests among recovery sets under capacities
\cite{AktasEtAl2021,AlfaranoEtAl2024}; we transfer only the radius-bounded
region generated by preserved supports.  Generalized coset weights minimize
supports of prescribed-coset representatives, while generalized covering
radii maximize the analogous quantity over coset tuples
\cite{ZhangEtAl2019,ElimelechFirerSchwartz2021}.  Our finite objective also
retains target normalization and the functional labels imposed by the outer
code.

Concatenation and matrix-product constructions produce LRCs with prescribed
parameters \cite{LiuEtAl2022,GalindoEtAl2023v1,JinFu2026}; Jin and Fu, for
example, fix a binary $[3,2,2]$ inner code and choose $\F_4$ outer codes.  We
allow an arbitrary inner code and target split and identify the exact finite
obstruction to preserving every bounded dual recovery equation.  Outer
distance is needed only to collapse that obstruction to the RGHW formula.  We
make no minimum-bandwidth claim.

The functional-dual decomposition is classical, and min--sum elimination also
appears in concatenated decoding \cite{Forney1966,ChenLingXing2005,
AjiMcEliece2000,GuruswamiSudan2002,BlomqvistEtAl2020}.  Symbol-indexed soft
information, presentation-dependent input--output enumerators, and message
weights retain labels for decoding or enumeration
\cite{LoskotBeaulieu2006,Nogin2005}; our target-normalized costs instead
identify the first nonconfined support.

Contextual equivalence and congruence are established ideas in automata and
finite-interface decomposition.  Weighted-automata minimization identifies
states by their future weighted behaviour \cite{Mohri2009}, and analogous
Myhill--Nerode statements exist for recognizable tree series
\cite{Maletti2008}.  In coding and represented-matroid settings, minimal tree
realizations and finite equivalence across bounded-width separations likewise
compress interface information \cite{Kashyap2007,FunkMayhewNewman2019}.
We import none of those theorems.  Here the contexts are outer linear codes,
the response is the exact target-normalized recovery-support cost, and
Theorem~\ref{prop:rank-bounded-outer-tests} proves a radius-controlled
separator bound and bounded witness cover.  The witness-propagating min--sum
composition is Theorem~\ref{prop:prescribed-coset-composition}.
