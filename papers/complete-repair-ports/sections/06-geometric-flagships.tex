\section{The projective-simplex family}
\label{sec:projective-simplex}

The relative-weight hierarchy can be computed exactly for a non-MDS family.
Assume $m\geq2$, put
\[
 N=\frac{q^m-1}{q-1},
\]
and let $A$ be an $m\times N$ matrix containing one representative of every
point of $\operatorname{PG}(m-1,q)$.  Define the inner code by
\begin{equation}
 I_m^\perp=\{(a,aA):a\in\F_q^m\}.
 \label{eq:projective-inner-dual}
\end{equation}
Take the first $m$ coordinates as targets and the projective coordinates as
helpers.  The associated nested pair is isomorphic to
\[
 0\subseteq S_m=\{aA:a\in\F_q^m\},
\]
where $S_m$ is the $q$-ary simplex code.

\begin{theorem}[Projective-simplex thresholds]
\label{thm:projective-thresholds}
\coverage{absent}%
For $1\leq t\leq m$,
\begin{equation}
 M_t(S_m,0)=\frac{q^m-q^{m-t}}{q-1},
 \label{eq:simplex-weights}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
 d(I_m^\perp)=q^{m-1}+1.
 \label{eq:projective-dual-distance}
\end{equation}
Hence the first nonconfined helper cost at recovered dimension $t$ is
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_t=\frac{q^m-q^{m-t}}{q-1}+q^{m-1}+1.
 \label{eq:projective-thresholds}
\end{equation}
The family is non-MDS for $m\geq2$; for $m\geq3$ the successive increments
of $\Gamma_t$ are unequal.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The target restriction of a word $(a,aA)\in I_m^\perp$ is $a$, and no
nonzero dual word has zero target restriction.  Hence normalized recovery
systems correspond, with support preserved, to subcodes of $S_m$; this proves
the asserted identification of the associated pair.

Let $U\leq\F_q^m$ have dimension $t$.  The common zero coordinates of the
subcode $UA$ are the projective points in $U^\perp$, a subspace of projective
dimension $m-t-1$.  Thus
\[
 |\supp(UA)|=N-\frac{q^{m-t}-1}{q-1}
 =\frac{q^m-q^{m-t}}{q-1},
\]
independently of $U$.  This proves~\eqref{eq:simplex-weights}.  Every
nonzero simplex word $aA$ has weight $q^{m-1}$, while a nonzero target vector
$a$ has weight at least one.  Equality is attained by a coordinate vector,
which proves~\eqref{eq:projective-dual-distance}.  The confinement formula is
Theorem~\ref{thm:ranked-confinement}.  The non-MDS and increment assertions
follow from the displayed parameters.
\uses{thm:ranked-confinement}%
\end{proof}

This family also gives an exact stochastic law at every recovered dimension.
Let each helper survive independently with probability $s$, and let $F$ be
the set of failed projective points.  The available recovery equations are
indexed by
\[
 \{a\in\F_q^m:aA|_F=0\}
 =\bigl(\operatorname{span}F\bigr)^\perp,
\]
which has dimension $m-\rank(F)$.

\begin{theorem}[Projective rank reliability]
\label{thm:projective-reliability}
\coverage{absent}%
The probability of retaining at least $t$ independent target recovery
equations is
\begin{equation}
 R_t(s)=
 \sum_{\substack{F\subseteq\operatorname{PG}(m-1,q)\\
                  \rank(F)\leq m-t}}
 (1-s)^{|F|}s^{N-|F|}.
 \label{eq:projective-rank-reliability}
\end{equation}
Writing $N_v=(q^v-1)/(q-1)$, this equals
\begin{equation}
 R_t(s)=
 \sum_{u=0}^{m-t}\qbinom{m}{u}
 \sum_{v=0}^{u}\qbinom{u}{v}
 (-1)^{u-v}q^{\binom{u-v}{2}}s^{N-N_v}.
 \label{eq:projective-mobius-reliability}
\end{equation}
Its endpoint terms are
\begin{align}
 R_t(s)&=\qbinom{m}{t}s^{M_t}+O(s^{M_t+1})
 &&(s\to0),
 \label{eq:rare-survival}\\
 1-R_t(s)&=B_{m,m-t+1}(1-s)^{m-t+1}
            +O((1-s)^{m-t+2})
 &&(s\to1),
 \label{eq:rare-failure}
\end{align}
where
\[
 B_{m,r}=\qbinom{m}{r}
          \frac{|\operatorname{GL}(r,q)|}{r!(q-1)^r}
\]
is the number of unordered projective $r$-frames.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Recovery of $t$ independent target combinations is possible exactly when
$m-\rank(F)\geq t$, which gives~\eqref{eq:projective-rank-reliability}.
For a vector subspace $U\leq\F_q^m$, let $\mathbb P(U)$ denote its projective
points.  M\"obius inversion in the subspace lattice gives the total
probability of failed sets spanning a fixed $u$-space $U$ as
\[
 \sum_{V\leq U}\mu(V,U)s^{N-N_{\dim V}},
 \qquad
 \mu(V,U)=(-1)^{u-v}q^{\binom{u-v}{2}},
 \qquad v=\dim V.
\]
Here the total weight of all failed sets contained in $\mathbb P(V)$ is
\[
 \sum_{F\subseteq\mathbb P(V)}(1-s)^{|F|}s^{N-|F|}
 =s^{N-N_{\dim V}},
\]
which accounts for the power of $s$ before inversion.
There are $\qbinom{m}{u}$ choices for $U$ and $\qbinom{u}{v}$ subspaces
$V\leq U$ of dimension $v$.  Summing over $u\leq m-t$ proves
\eqref{eq:projective-mobius-reliability}.

For $s\to0$, a smallest surviving set that permits rank-$t$ recovery is the
complement of the points in an $(m-t)$-dimensional vector subspace.  It has
size $M_t$, and there are $\qbinom{m}{m-t}=\qbinom{m}{t}$ such subspaces.
For $s\to1$, failure first occurs when the failed points span dimension
$m-t+1$.  A smallest such set is a projective frame of that size.  Counting
its span and then its unordered projective bases gives $B_{m,m-t+1}$.
\end{proof}

At the same helper length, the simplex presentation is characterized by its
first recovery cost.

\begin{proposition}[Equality in the first projective bound]
\label{prop:projective-uniqueness}
\coverage{absent}%
Let $B$ be any full-row-rank $m\times N$ matrix over $\F_q$, with
$N=(q^m-1)/(q-1)$.  Then
\[
 \min_{0\ne a\in\F_q^m}\wt(aB)\leq q^{m-1}.
\]
Equality holds if and only if, up to column scaling and permutation, the
columns of $B$ represent every point of $\operatorname{PG}(m-1,q)$ exactly
once.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each nonzero column, the fraction of nonzero $a\in\F_q^m$ for which its
entry in $aB$ is nonzero is
\[
 \frac{q^{m-1}(q-1)}{q^m-1}.
\]
Averaging $\wt(aB)$ over $a\ne0$ gives at most $q^{m-1}$, strictly less if
$B$ has a zero column.  If the minimum attains this average, every nonzero
linear combination has weight $q^{m-1}$ and every projective hyperplane
contains total column multiplicity
$(q^{m-1}-1)/(q-1)$.

Let $c$ be the vector of projective column multiplicities.  The
point--hyperplane incidence matrix is square, and its real Gram matrix has
the form $(r-\lambda)I+\lambda J$ with $r>\lambda$.  It is nonsingular, so
the constant hyperplane-sum equations have the unique solution
$c=\mathbf1$.  Conversely, the simplex matrix has constant nonzero weight
$q^{m-1}$.
\end{proof}
