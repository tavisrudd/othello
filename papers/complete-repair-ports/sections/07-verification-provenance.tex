\label{sec:verification}

The source and evidence identities are listed in
\texttt{verification/artifact-versions.json}.  It records SHA-256 hashes of
the Ergodis source files described here, the three retained benchmark records
and their raw samples, and the executable and runner identities reported by
those records.  Ergodis's publication repository is
\href{https://github.com/tavisrudd/ergodis}{\texttt{tavisrudd/ergodis}}.
The unchanged Lean sources are also available in the
\href{https://github.com/tavisrudd/compositional-recovery/tree/2d90a8b9cbb8721ea984fcd0684589096d40f5fe/lean}{versioned paper companion}.

\paragraph{Executable evidence.}
The independent \texttt{ergodis-verify} crate has no optimizer dependency.
Its bounded binary-composition checker establishes a specified
necessary-coordinate restriction by direct finite checking.  Its separate
min-plus transition checker checks supplied four-by-four summaries against
a retained tree, with linear storage and logarithmic update work, using
bounded saturating arithmetic.  This checks summary composition and
replacement.  Source-model lowering and domain optimality need their own
justification.  These contracts are separate from the paper's formal coverage.

\subsection{Measured performance and solver choice}

The measurements below are the previously recorded recovery benchmark
snapshot, not a measurement of every current engine.  They assess engineering reach and the
point at which a general solver becomes preferable; they are not mathematical evidence or the
principal contribution of the software.  The structural comparison is how
interface width, target rank, field size, distinct block types, frontier size,
and query reuse affect total cost.  Compilation, repeated queries, witness
expansion, and verification must be charged separately; a fast retained-state
query does not imply a fast construction of that state.

The specialized dynamic programs are most useful when compilation exposes a
small algebraic state space.  They are not replacements for a general
constraint solver when the residual model contains unrelated side
constraints.  The appropriate deployment rule is therefore:
\[
 \text{algebraic compilation}
 \longrightarrow
 \begin{cases}
   \text{specialized exact DP},&\text{when the compiled state is sufficient},\\
   \text{CP-SAT},&\text{for the remaining general constraints}.
 \end{cases}
\]
CP-SAT combines constraint programming, satisfiability, and linear-relaxation
methods~\cite{PerronEtAl2023}; it is therefore an appropriate end-to-end
baseline.  To distinguish the cost of compilation from that of the residual
engine, a \emph{structured CP-SAT} control should receive the same symmetry
reduction and algebraic preprocessing as Ergodis.  The displayed comparisons
measure the complete approaches; they do not separately attribute the gains
to preprocessing and the residual engine.

\paragraph{Measurement protocol.}
The retained application measurements use external wall time on an AMD
Ryzen AI 9 HX 370 under Linux.  Each sample starts a fresh process and includes
implementation startup and state or model construction; dependency installation
is excluded.  Paired programs use logical CPU 3 in rotated order.  A cold
sample performs one solve.  A warm sample performs eight solves on each side,
and its external wall time is divided by eight.  Peak memory is the process
high-water resident set size.

Table~\ref{tab:ergodis-applications} uses seven paired rounds, recorded in
\texttt{application-readme-ab.json}.  The tower and Hamming-outer comparisons
use three paired rounds with a 400-second process limit, recorded separately
in \texttt{application-long-cold.json} and
\texttt{application-long-warm.json}.  Reported times are sample medians;
paired ratios use the unrounded observations.  Timeout comparisons give lower
bounds, not completed control timings.  These records supersede the earlier
in-process and amortized-kernel protocols for the measurements displayed here.
Their exact identities and protocol fields are recorded in the paper's
\texttt{verification/artifact-versions.json}.

\paragraph{Tower composition.}
The represented tower benchmark applies the composition theorem: at depth six and fanout
four, ergodis expands an exact coefficient witness over $4096$ leaves in a
$4.559$ ms cold-process median.  Direct CP-SAT exceeded $400$ s in each of
three paired rounds, giving a speedup greater than $87{,}743$; for equal
eight-solve batches the bound is greater than $45{,}103$.  The labelled-table
control has not been rerun under this corrected protocol.

The same compilation pattern has been tested through six bundled application
examples.  These are separate matched comparisons, not a ranking of unlike
tasks.  Each row below states the question and the exact output checked against
its formulation-specific open-source control.  The cold-process gaps range
from $34$-fold to $190$-fold.
The comparisons do not include commercial solvers or claim a universal
ranking.

As a code-native check, ergodis also analyzes Jin and Fu's published
$\F_4$ Hamming-outer family~\cite[Corollary~5.4]{JinFu2026}.  For its binary
$[4095,2718,6;2]$ LRC, all backends obtain the same exact confinement cost.
Three corrected cold-process pairs give a $442.573$ ms ergodis median and a
$270.939$ s direct-CP-SAT median.  The paired speedup is $657.88$ .  Each equal eight-solve direct-CP-SAT batch exceeded
$400$ s, giving a separate warm speedup greater than $196.50$.  The
labelled-table model has not been rerun under this protocol.  Exact
inputs, outputs, tool versions, peak memory,
replay commands, and raw timing samples are in the corrected application evidence
in the Ergodis benchmark records identified above.  Their content hashes
distinguish the measured snapshot from subsequent development.

General-purpose constraint solvers have also been used to construct binary
codes~\cite{KochemazovEtAl2026}, and repair dependency graphs and scheduling
predate this work~\cite{LinEtAl2021RepairBoost}.  The contribution here is the
structure-aware compilation of linear-recovery fibres, with exact witnesses,
before a specialized dynamic program or a residual solver is invoked.
