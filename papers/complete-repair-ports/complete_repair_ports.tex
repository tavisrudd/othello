\begin{abstract}
Let $I\leq\mathbb F_q^E$ be a represented inner code, let $P\subseteq E$ be a
target set, and put $J=E\setminus P$.  The target and helper parts of a
generator matrix determine a nested pair
$K_P\subseteq D_P\leq\mathbb F_q^J$.  We identify its relative generalized
Hamming weight $M_t(D_P,K_P)$ with the minimum helper union needed to recover
$t$ independent target combinations.  For any linear outer code $O$ with at
least two blocks and $d(O^\perp)>r+1$, every such
recovery system using at most $r$ helpers is confined to its inner block
exactly when
\[
 r<M_t(D_P,K_P)+d(I^\perp).
\]
Below this threshold, concatenation preserves every bounded normalized dual
recovery equation, its exact helper support, and every inclusion-minimal
recovery set.

Consequences include an exact best-target generalized-weight formula, exact
MDS thresholds, transfer of bounded service-rate regions, and
positive-density realization in asymptotically good fixed-alphabet families.
We construct pairs with the same complete relative-weight hierarchy but
different bounded repair reliabilities, and presentations of the same nested
pair with different confinement thresholds.  For a projective-simplex family,
\[
 M_t=\frac{q^m-q^{m-t}}{q-1}
\]
and the recovery probability is an explicit projective-rank distribution.
The one-coordinate criterion $r+1<z_x(I)$ is the rank-one case.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Locality asks how many helpers recover one erased coordinate.  Cooperative
locality and $(r,\delta)$-locality ask for recovery after several erasures, and
availability asks for disjoint choices of helpers.  Generalized Hamming
weights give global lower bounds for cooperative recovery
\cite{RawatMazumdarVishwanath2015,GopalanEtAl2012,PamiesEtAl2013,
AbdelGhaffarWeber2017}.  Recovery sets and
coordinate-indexed recovery structures record more local information
\cite{MarquezCorbellaEtAl2019}, but support data alone discard the dual
coefficients that implement the recovery equations.

This paper identifies the relative generalized Hamming weights of the
associated nested pair in the generator matrix as the exact fixed-target
helper costs.  The same quantities are then the sharp obstructions to exact
transfer through concatenation.  Relative generalized Hamming weights and
the relative dimension/length profile were introduced for nested codes by
Luo, Mitrpant, Vinck, and Chen, who interpreted them as access thresholds in
a wiretap model~\cite{LuoEtAl2005}; later work develops their
ramp-secret-sharing and algorithmic roles
\cite{GeilEtAl2014,SanJose2025,Geil2026}.  The result here is the canonical
nested pair attached to a represented target/helper split, its exact recovery
interpretation, and its sharp concatenation role, including transfer of every
minimizing equation rather than only the numerical weight.

Let $q$ be a prime power.  Let $I\leq\F_q^E$ have a full-row-rank generator matrix
$G=(G_P\mid G_J)$, where $P$ is the target set and $J=E\setminus P$.  Put
\[
 U_P=\operatorname{im}G_P,
 \qquad W_P=U_P\cap\operatorname{im}G_J,
\]
and define the associated nested code pair
\[
 K_P=\ker G_J
 \subseteq
 D_P=\{a\in\F_q^J:G_Ja\in U_P\}.
\]
We assume $0<\dim I<|E|$, so both the represented message space and
$I^\perp$ are nonzero.
We use the convention $d(0)=\infty$ for the zero code.
The quotient $D_P/K_P\cong W_P$ is exactly the space of target linear
combinations recoverable from the helper coordinates.
Here $t$ is the dimension of a recovered subspace of $W_P$, not the number
of erased coordinate positions.  Recovering all values on $P$ is the special
case $U_P\subseteq\operatorname{im}G_J$ and $t=\dim U_P$; dependent target
columns may make this dimension smaller than $|P|$.

\begin{theorem}[Main theorem]
\label{thm:main}
\coverage{absent}%
\uses{thm:relative-weight-recovery, thm:ranked-confinement}%
Let $\ell=\dim W_P\geq1$ and $1\leq t\leq\ell$.  The minimum cardinality of
the union of helpers in a system recovering a $t$-dimensional subspace of
$W_P$ is
\[
 \mu_t(P)=M_t(D_P,K_P).
\]
Let $I$ be used as the represented inner code in a concatenation with an
$L$-linear outer code $O\leq L^N$, where $L$ is the message space of $I$.
For every nonnegative integer $r$, if $N\geq2$ and
$d(O^\perp)>r+1$, then the concatenation confines every recovery system using
at most $r$ helpers for every internally recoverable $t$-dimensional target
subspace to its target inner block if and only if
\[
 r<M_t(D_P,K_P)+d(I^\perp).
\]
When the inequality holds, zero-extension gives a bijection on the normalized
recovery-equation systems and their exact helper supports.
Consequently, for any outer family with dual distance tending to infinity,
the same statement holds for all sufficiently long concatenations at every
fixed $r$.
\end{theorem}

The first assertion follows by identifying a recovery system with a subspace
$A\leq D_P$ disjoint from $K_P$.  Passing between this form and the standard
definition of $M_t(D_P,K_P)$ uses a complement to $A\cap K_P$.  The second
assertion combines that identity with a fixed-demand dual decomposition: a
nonconfined recovery system consists of its least inner realization together
with a nonzero inner-dual perturbation in another block.  The finite outer
gate removes every nonzero outer-functional alternative; dual-distance growth
makes that gate automatic at fixed radius.

The same bijection yields the later transfer results.  It preserves every
block-local statistic determined by normalized recovery systems of cost at
most $r$.  After forgetting coefficients, it also preserves statistics
determined by inclusion-minimal helper supports, including bounded repair
reliability and the bounded service-rate region.  This statement is
block-local: the upward-closed recovery-set family in the full concatenated
coordinate set also contains irrelevant cross-block supersets.

The theorem contains the previous one-coordinate criterion.  If $P=\{x\}$
and $t=1$, then $M_1(D_P,K_P)$ is the least number of helpers in a dual word
through $x$, and
\[
 z_x(I)=M_1(D_P,K_P)+1+d(I^\perp).
\]
Thus $r<M_1+d(I^\perp)$ is exactly $r+1<z_x(I)$.

Concatenation has long been used to construct LRCs with prescribed parameter
properties \cite{LiuEtAl2022,JinFu2026}.  Jin and Fu fix a binary $[3,2,2]$
inner code and choose $\F_4$ outer codes to obtain parameter-optimal binary
locality-two codes.  Here the inner representation is arbitrary, and the
question is the sharp obstruction to preserving every bounded dual recovery
equation.  We make no minimum-bandwidth claim.
