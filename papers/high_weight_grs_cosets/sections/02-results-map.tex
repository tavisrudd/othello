\section{Results at a glance}
\label{sec:results-map}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} classifies every coset of weight at least \(r-1\).
Its conclusion is independent of the placement of the deleted set \(A\) and
of all nonzero GRS column multipliers.

Appending a syndrome direction as a parity-check column raises its coset
weight by one.  The preceding table therefore gives all one-column
extensions with Singleton defect at most one:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
d_S(f)&d(\ker[H_S\mid f])&\text{extension class}\\ \hline
r&r+1&\text{MDS}\\
r-1&r&\text{NMDS}\\
\leq r-2&\leq r-1&\text{defect at least two}.
\end{array}
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:family-aggregate-nmds} then counts the minimum supports in
each NMDS family and determines its complete aggregate weight enumerator.

In arbitrary characteristic the exact Lucas-carrier arithmetic can be more
complicated, but the general conclusion remains short:
\[
 d_S(f)\geq r-1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 f\in\cP_r\cup\cM^{\max}_{r,p}.
\]
The carrier is explicit from adjacent zero entries in Pascal row \(r-2\).
The fixed R5--R7 classifications decide its arithmetic in the ranges stated
in Section~\ref{sec:fixed-level-roadmap}; they sharpen the uniform theorem in
small fields but are not inputs to it.

\subsection{Why the classification closes}

The proof has three steps.  First, the Hankel dictionary converts a
weight-\((r-2)\) representation into a split squarefree binary form.  Second,
the recursive carrier theorem identifies exactly when every contraction to
the terminal R5 system is trapped.  Third, outside that carrier, one
degree-six selector chooses all contraction roots simultaneously; the exact
R5 pencil count supplies the remaining cubic while avoiding those roots and
the deleted set \(A\).
