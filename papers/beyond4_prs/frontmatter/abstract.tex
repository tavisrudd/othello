We classify projective Reed--Solomon deep-hole syndromes through redundancy
ten and prove a recursive obstruction theorem at arbitrary redundancy.  A
syndrome is split-free precisely when its two-row Hankel kernel contains no
completely split squarefree form.  Coherent polar contraction removes a
prospective root while retaining it as a forbidden marker, so a lower witness
lifts without acquiring a double root.

For redundancy \(r\geq6\) and
\[
 q\geq6r-15+\lfloor2\sqrt{6r-17}\rfloor,
\]
every split-free syndrome lies in the union of the catalecticant rank-two
scheme and one maximal adjacent-zero Lucas carrier.  The proof determines the
reduced terminal components in every characteristic, shows that dense
squarefree-marker contractions select a single component, and proves by
Pascal nesting that all modular descendants merge into the one linear
carrier.  If the characteristic exceeds \(r-1\), the Lucas carrier is empty,
giving the exact tangent/conjugate-secant classification and
\(q(q+1)^2/2\) deep-hole directions.

The fixed-level consequences are complete classifications at redundancies
five and six for every \(q\geq7\), a complete split-free classification at
redundancy seven (a deep-hole classification for \(q\geq11\)), and exact
deep-hole classifications at redundancies eight, nine, and ten for
\(q\geq43,53,59\), respectively.  At the first fresh higher Lucas carrier,
in characteristic two and redundancy ten, an exact final-pair
Artin--Schreier criterion proves that every carrier point is shallow over
every admissible field.  Finite certificates close the bounded fields and a
direct-locus replay independently reconstructs the redundancy-seven finite
classification relative to its stated shared inputs.
