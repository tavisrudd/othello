We classify, up to projective-semilinear equivalence, the one-column
maximum-distance-separable extensions of projective Reed--Solomon codes at
redundancy five over every prime power \(q\geq7\), and determine their exact
projective counts.  Equivalently, we classify the deep-hole syndrome
directions in the first case beyond the known classification through
redundancy four.  Besides the tangent and conjugate-secant families, the
classification contains an osculating-pair family selected by \(q\bmod3\)
when the characteristic is not three, a nucleus point and a wild
Artin--Schreier family in characteristic three, and sporadic orbits exactly
for \(q\in\{7,8,9,11,13,17,19\}\).

A syndrome determines a Hankel linear system of binary forms and is split-free
precisely when this system contains no squarefree form that splits completely
over \(\mathbb F_q\).  At redundancy five the system is a pencil of cubics.
Cyclic descent produces the arithmetic families; in the \(S_3\) case an
integral off-diagonal fiber square of arithmetic genus one supplies the point
bound.  Exact certificate computations settle the remaining fields.

For higher redundancies we introduce coherent polar flags: iterated
contractions that retain every removed root as a forbidden marker, exactly the
information required to lift squarefree witnesses.  This gives complete
split-free classifications at redundancies six and seven.  At redundancy six
they are deep-hole classifications for every \(q\geq7\); at redundancy seven
they become so whenever the covering radius is six, in particular for
\(q\geq11\).  At arbitrary redundancy \(r\), a catalecticant-rowspace
reduction and an integral Grassmannian calculation show that every recursively
pointed contained component is persistent or modular.  Consequently every
split-free syndrome lies in these loci once
\[
q\geq6r-15+\lfloor2\sqrt{6r-17}\rfloor .
\]
