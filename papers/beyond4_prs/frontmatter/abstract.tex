We classify the projective deep-hole syndrome directions of projective
Reed--Solomon codes at redundancy five over every prime power \(q\geq7\), the
first case beyond the known classification through redundancy four, and
determine their exact projective counts.  The classification contains the
persistent tangent and conjugate-secant families, arithmetic osculating-pair
families governed by \(q\bmod3\), characteristic-three nucleus and
Artin--Schreier families, and sporadic orbits occurring exactly for
\(q\in\{7,8,9,11,13,17,19\}\).

The proof associates to each syndrome a Hankel linear system of binary forms.
The syndrome is split-free precisely when this system contains no squarefree
form that splits completely over \(\mathbb F_q\).  At redundancy five the
system is a pencil of cubics; its generic case is controlled by an integral
off-diagonal fiber square of arithmetic genus one.

For higher redundancies we introduce coherent polar flags: iterated
contractions that retain every removed root as a forbidden marker.  This
retained information is exactly what permits squarefree witnesses to lift.
The method gives complete split-free classifications at redundancies six and
seven.  At redundancy six these are deep-hole classifications for every
\(q\geq7\); at redundancy seven they become deep-hole classifications whenever
the covering radius is six, in particular for \(q\geq11\).  At arbitrary
redundancy \(r\), a catalecticant-rowspace reduction and an integral
Grassmannian calculation show that every contained component is persistent or
modular.  Consequently, outside these components every syndrome is shallow
once
\[
q\geq6r-15+\lfloor2\sqrt{6r-17}\rfloor .
\]
