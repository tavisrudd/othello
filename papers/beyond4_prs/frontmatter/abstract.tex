We classify, up to projective-semilinear equivalence, the deep-hole syndrome
directions of projective Reed--Solomon codes at redundancies five and six over
every prime power \(q\geq7\), with exact projective counts.  At redundancy
seven we give the complete split-free classification over the same range; it
is a deep-hole classification whenever the covering radius is six, in
particular for \(q\geq11\).
At redundancy five the list contains, besides the tangent and
conjugate-secant families, an osculating-pair family selected by \(q\bmod3\),
a characteristic-three nucleus point and wild Artin--Schreier family, and
sporadic orbits exactly for
\(q\in\{7,8,9,11,13,17,19\}\).  A syndrome determines a Hankel linear system
of binary forms and is split-free precisely when the system contains no
completely split squarefree form.  For cubic pencils, cyclic descent produces
the arithmetic families, a genus-one fiber square controls the \(S_3\) case,
and exact certificates settle the remaining fields.
The balanced \(q=8\) row gives \(1116\) \([10,5,6]_8\) MDS
extensions and hence minimum-support \(\operatorname{AME}(10,8)\) states and
\([[9,1,5]]_8\) quantum MDS codes.
For redundancies six and seven we introduce coherent polar flags.  A contraction
removes a prospective root; retaining it as a forbidden marker is exactly what
prevents a lower squarefree witness from lifting with a double root.
Complete contained-component calculations and one- and two-marker point
counts give the uniform ranges \(q\geq29\) and \(q\geq37\), respectively.
Finite certificates close the remaining fields and record the exceptional
orbits, stabilizers, and Frobenius fusions.  At redundancy seven a second
direct-locus enumerator independently reconstructs the split-free set and
orbit partition.  No contained-component claim is made at arbitrary
redundancy.
