\begin{abstract}
Does an arbitrary local change of basis identify more equal-phase AME
states than Clifford transformations do?  Let \(C\) be a linear
\([2m,m,m+1]_q\) MDS code and let
\(\lvert\Psi_C\rangle=q^{-m/2}\sum_{c\in C}\lvert c\rangle\) be its
equal-phase CSS state.  For every prime power \(q\) and \(m\geq2\), every
product-unitary intertwiner between two such states is local Clifford.
Thus every transversal conversion between the associated
\([[2m-1,1,m]]_q\) quantum MDS codes is Clifford factor by factor, and no
code in the family admits a transversal non-Clifford logical unitary.
For odd prime \(q\), generalized Reed--Solomon codes of every even length
\(2m\leq q+1\) attain exactly the full projective one-qudit Clifford
group.  The proof recovers local Weyl axes from \((m+1)\)-party marginals:
shortening \(C\) and \(C^\perp\) makes the entire reduced operator
diagonal on the full \(q^2\)-element Weyl basis, including the identity,
and rank-one contractions identify its axes.

For \(m=3\), we combine this rigidity with a pencil of six-point arcs.
A degree-eight quotient \(z\) classifies projective, monomial-code,
local-Clifford, and local-unitary equivalence.  With any party taken as
the input of a \([[5,1,3]]_q\) encoder, the logical symplectic group is
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\) on the conic/GRS locus and the split torus off it.
Fixed-copy scalar contractions are generically constant, whereas exact
marginal and four-copy witnesses detect special strata.
\end{abstract}
