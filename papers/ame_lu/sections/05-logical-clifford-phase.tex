\section{The logical-Clifford phase}\label{sec:logical}

Regard any one party of an \(\AME(6,q)\) stabilizer tensor as an input and
the other five as outputs.  This is a \([[5,1,3]]_q\) encoding isometry.
A tensor symmetry fixing every party induces a one-qudit logical
symplectic transformation in that view.

\begin{theorem}[Logical phase]\label{thm:logical-phase}
For an equal-phase CSS state from a six-arc over a field of odd order, the
fixed-party symplectic kernel is
\[
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{SL}_2(q),&\text{if the arc is conic/GRS},\\
T=\{\diag(a,a^{-1}):a\in\F_q^\times\},&\text{if the arc is nonconic}.
\end{cases}                                             \tag{5.1}
\]
Thus every encoder view of a GRS tensor realizes the full one-qudit
logical Clifford group from fixed-party symmetries.  Off the GRS locus,
the fixed-party logical group is the split torus; adding party-moving
isodualities gives its normalizer.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every four-party support carries the two-dimensional shortened label
plane \((3.2)\), and projection to the Pauli plane of any retained party
is an isomorphism.  For a fixed party permutation, one anchor block in
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\) therefore forces the other five local symplectic
blocks through overlaps of the shortened supports.  Satisfying all
support relations is necessary and sufficient for preserving the full
Lagrangian.

Diagonal anchor blocks always propagate and give \(T\).  If an anchor has
a nonzero off-diagonal entry, the overlap equations force a
coordinatewise scaling equivalence between \(C\) and \(C^\perp\).  In the
arc language, the labelled six-arc must be fixed by Gale association.
For six points in \(\PP^2\), that fixed divisor is precisely the conic
locus.  On a conic, the usual GRS dual multipliers propagate every anchor
in \(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\); off the conic, only diagonal anchors survive.
\end{proof}

The distinction is operational rather than a classical automorphism
count.  Including logical Paulis, the projective logical Clifford group
has order \(q^2\lvert\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\rvert\) on the GRS locus, whereas
the non-GRS encoder view has \(q^2\cdot2(q-1)\) after the party-moving
normalizer is included.  At \(q=11\), these orders are \(159720\) and
\(2420\), respectively.

The MDS property also makes minimum operator pushing uniform.  For any
input party and nonidentity Pauli label, each of the ten four-sets
containing that party gives a unique stabilizer representative supported
there.  Removing the input leaves a weight-three output Pauli.  Thus each
input Pauli has exactly ten minimum-support pushes, although the symmetry
orbits of those pushes can distinguish finer projective classes.

Arithmetic symmetry jumps do not change the phase.  The
characteristic-\(17\) tetrahedral specialization lies on the GRS boundary
and retains the full \(\mathrm{SL}_2(17)\) kernel.  The
characteristic-\(31\) non-GRS \(A_5\) specialization retains only the
split torus.  These are symmetry enhancements inside the two phases, not
exceptions to \((5.1)\).
