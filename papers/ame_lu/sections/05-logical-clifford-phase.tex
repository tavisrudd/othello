\section{The logical-Clifford phase}\label{sec:logical}

Regard any one party of an \(\AME(6,q)\) stabilizer tensor as an input and
the other five as outputs.  This is a \([[5,1,3]]_q\) encoding isometry.
The kernel of the party action on Clifford tensor symmetries is
\[
 \mathcal K_C=\{(F_1,\ldots,F_6)\in\mathrm{SL}_2(q)^6:
                 (\bigoplus_iF_i)L_C=L_C\}.
\]
For an encoder view, the \emph{fixed-party symplectic kernel} means the
image of \(\mathcal K_C\) on its input block.

\begin{theorem}[Logical phase]\label{thm:logical-phase}
For an equal-phase CSS state from a six-arc over an odd prime field, the
fixed-party symplectic kernel is
\[
\begin{cases}
\mathrm{SL}_2(q),&\text{if the arc is conic/GRS},\\
T=\{\diag(a,a^{-1}):a\in\F_q^\times\},&\text{if the arc is nonconic}.
\end{cases}                                             \tag{5.1}
\]
Thus every encoder view of a GRS tensor realizes the full one-qudit
logical Clifford group from fixed-party symmetries.  Off the GRS locus,
the fixed-party logical group is the split torus.  Any party-moving
isoduality that exchanges the CSS \(X\)- and \(Z\)-spaces acts by the
nontrivial Weyl-group element and generates the normalizer of \(T\)
together with \(T\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every four-party support carries the two-dimensional shortened label
plane \((3.2)\), and projection to the Pauli plane of any retained party
is an isomorphism.  If \(F_i\) is fixed at one party, requiring
\(\bigoplus_iF_i\) to carry each shortened plane to itself therefore
determines the other five blocks through overlapping four-sets.  These
conditions are also sufficient: the shortened planes span \(L_C\),
because the weight-four shortened words span both three-dimensional MDS
codes \(C\) and \(C^\perp\).

The common diagonal anchor \(\diag(a,a^{-1})\) preserves
\(C_X\oplus C^\perp_Z\), so \(T\) is always present.  Write a propagated
collection as
\[
 F_i=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_i&\beta_i\\
                     \gamma_i&\delta_i\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 D_\alpha=\diag(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_6),
 \quad\text{and similarly for }D_\beta,D_\gamma,D_\delta .
\]
Preservation of \(L_C=C_X\oplus C^\perp_Z\) is equivalent to
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
D_\alpha C\subseteq C,&D_\gamma C\subseteq C^\perp,\\
D_\beta C^\perp\subseteq C,&D_\delta C^\perp\subseteq C^\perp.
\end{array}                                             \tag{5.2}
\]
Apply these inclusions to the unique shortened word on each four-set.
On two four-sets meeting in three parties, comparison through the
projection isomorphisms shows that an off-diagonal coefficient vanishes
at one party if and only if it vanishes at all parties.  Hence a
nondiagonal propagated collection makes \(D_\gamma\) or \(D_\beta\)
nonsingular, and \((5.2)\), together with equality of dimensions, gives
\(D_\gamma C=C^\perp\) or \(D_\beta C^\perp=C\).  Conversely, such a
diagonal code--dual equivalence satisfies \((5.2)\) for the two
elementary unipotent anchors; together with \(T\) they generate
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\).

It remains to identify when the diagonal equivalence
\(C\sim C^\perp\) occurs.  A parity-check matrix for \(C\) and a kernel
basis for it represent associated, or Gale-dual, ordered six-arcs.
The classical self-association theorem for six points in
\(\PP^2\) says that these two sextuples are projectively equivalent
exactly when the six original columns lie on a conic
\cite[Section~2.3]{HowardMillsonSnowdenVakil2008}.  Equivalently, solving
the multipliers in \((5.2)\) leaves the determinant of the six quadratic
evaluation vectors.  Since the columns form an arc, the conic is
nonsingular.  On it, the standard GRS dual multipliers realize the
code--dual equivalence and hence every anchor in
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\); off it, the preceding implication excludes every
nondiagonal anchor.

Finally, an isoduality interchanges the two summands \(C_X\) and
\(C^\perp_Z\), so its input block swaps the two Weyl axes.  It conjugates
\(a\) to \(a^{-1}\) in \(T\), giving \(N(T)\) whenever such a
party-moving symmetry is present.
\end{proof}

This is an operational distinction, not a classical automorphism count.
Including logical Paulis, the projective logical Clifford group has order
\(q^2\lvert\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\rvert\) on the GRS locus.  At \(q=11\), the
two non-GRS pencil classes have party-moving isodualities, so their
logical group is \(q^2N(T)\); the GRS and non-GRS orders are \(159720\)
and \(2420\), respectively.

The MDS property also makes minimum operator pushing uniform.  For any
input party and nonidentity Pauli label, each of the ten four-sets
containing that party gives a unique stabilizer representative supported
there.  Removing the input leaves a weight-three output Pauli.  Thus each
input Pauli has exactly ten minimum-support pushes, although the symmetry
orbits of those pushes can distinguish finer projective classes.  This
uses only the projection isomorphism for the shortened planes, not the
finite group calculation.

Arithmetic symmetry jumps do not change the phase.  The
characteristic-\(17\) tetrahedral specialization lies on the GRS boundary
and therefore retains the full \(\mathrm{SL}_2(17)\) kernel.  The
characteristic-\(31\) \(A_5\) specialization is non-GRS and therefore
retains only the split torus.  These are symmetry enhancements inside the
two phases, not exceptions to \((5.1)\).
