\section{Introduction}

An absolutely maximally entangled state has maximally mixed reductions on
every subsystem no larger than half the parties.  A linear
\([2m,m,m+1]_q\) MDS code supplies such a state at minimum support:
if \(C\leq\F_q^{2m}\), then
\[
  \lvert\Psi_C\rangle
    =q^{-m/2}\sum_{c\in C}\lvert c\rangle                       \tag{1.1}
\]
is \(\AME(2m,q)\); conversely, the equal-phase state of a linear
\([2m,m]_q\) code is AME only if the code is MDS.  This code--state
dictionary is standard
\cite{RaissiGogolinRieraAcin2018}.  Our question is how much of the code
geometry survives arbitrary single-party changes of basis.

The local-unitary versus local-Clifford problem is false for general
stabilizer states \cite{JiChenWeiYing2008}.  It is therefore important that
the following result concerns a specific MDS/CSS family and says more than
equality of two orbit partitions.

\begin{theorem}[Local-unitary rigidity]\label{thm:lu-lc-rigidity}
Let \(q\) be a prime power, let \(m\geq2\), and let
\(C,D\leq\F_q^{2m}\) be linear \([2m,m,m+1]_q\) MDS codes.
Suppose that, for a party permutation \(\pi\), single-qudit unitaries
\(U_1,\ldots,U_{2m}\), and a phase \(\theta\),
\[
 (U_1\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{2m})\pi\lvert\Psi_C\rangle
   =e^{i\theta}\lvert\Psi_D\rangle .
\]
Then every \(U_i\) normalizes the finite-field Weyl system.  In particular,
the displayed equivalence is local Clifford.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Transversal Clifford no-go]
\label{cor:transversal-clifford}
Let \(V:\mathbb C^q\to(\mathbb C^q)^{\otimes(2m-1)}\) be an encoding
isometry whose normalized Choi state is \(\lvert\Psi_C\rangle\) as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:lu-lc-rigidity}.  Thus \(V\) encodes the associated
\([[2m-1,1,m]]_q\) quantum MDS code.  If
\[
 (U_1\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{2m-1})V=VL
\]
for a logical unitary \(L\), then \(L\) and every \(U_i\) are Clifford.
In particular, this code admits no transversal non-Clifford logical
unitary.
\end{corollary}

The mechanism is visible in one reduced operator.  Retain any \(m+1\)
parties and shorten both \(C\) and \(C^\perp\) on the other \(m-1\).
Each shortening is \([m+1,1,m+1]_q\), and together they give a
\(q^2\)-element stabilizer subgroup.  At every retained party its
\(q^2-1\) nonidentity elements run bijectively through the nonidentity
Weyl operators.  After the identity term is removed, the reduced state
is a diagonal \((m+1)\)-way tensor on the full Weyl basis.  A contraction
of this tensor has rank one exactly on a coordinate axis.  Those axes
are intrinsic, so any product unitary carrying one reduced state to
another must permute the local Weyl axes.  This is precisely the
Clifford condition.  The corollary follows by applying the theorem to
the Choi state of a transversal logical action.

Rains used the corresponding three-Pauli tensor to determine the
automorphisms of qubit distance-two codes \cite[Theorem~13]{Rains1999},
and Van den Nest, Dehaene, and De Moor used that rigidity in their
minimal-support criterion for qubit stabilizer states
\cite{VanDenNestDehaeneDeMoor2005}.  Our argument is a finite-field
extension of this axis-recovery mechanism.  The contribution here is its
uniform realization for equal-phase CSS states from linear
\([2m,m,m+1]_q\) MDS codes, together with the transversal corollary.
We do not claim a new LU--LC mechanism in the abstract.
The qualifier is essential: arbitrary phased minimal-support
\(\AME(6,d)\) states form infinitely many LU classes
\cite{RamadasLakshminarayan2025}, and neither nonlinear orthogonal arrays
nor general stabilizer states are covered here.

For the rest of the paper we specialize to \(m=3\).  Our main application
is the ordered pencil
\[
H_t=\bigl((0,1,1-t),(0,1,t-1),(1,1-t,0),(1,t-1,0),
          (1,0,-t),(1,0,t)\bigr).                              \tag{1.2}
\]
On its admitted odd-prime-field non-GRS locus, define
\[
\begin{split}
 A(t)&=-4t(t-1)^2,\\
 B(t)&=(t^2-t+1)(t^2-3t+1),\\
 z(t)&=\bigl(B(t)/A(t)\bigr)^2 .
\end{split}                                                     \tag{1.3}
\]
Exact bracket identities show that \(z\) is the degree-eight projective
quotient of the pencil.  Theorem~\ref{thm:lu-lc-rigidity} upgrades its
local-Clifford classification to the following complete local result.

\begin{corollary}[Classification of the admitted pencil]
\label{cor:lu-lc-pencil}
For admitted parameters \(t,u\) over a prime field \(\F_q\) with \(q\)
odd, the following are equivalent:
\[
 H_t\sim_{\mathrm{proj}}H_u
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \ker H_t\sim_{\mathrm{mon}}\ker H_u
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \Psi_t\sim_{\LC}\Psi_u
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \Psi_t\sim_{\LU}\Psi_u
 \Longleftrightarrow z(t)=z(u),
\]
where party permutations are allowed.
\end{corollary}

The second main result gives this geometry a direct coding-theoretic
interpretation over odd prime fields.  Regard any one tensor leg as the
input of the associated \([[5,1,3]]_q\) encoder.  The fixed-party product-Clifford symmetries
induce the full logical \(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\) precisely when the six-arc is
conic/GRS; otherwise they induce only the split torus.  Thus the
nineteenth-century self-association condition for six points controls
which one-qudit logical Clifford gates are available without moving
parties.

Quantum Reed--Solomon codes and their encoding circuits were developed
by Grassl, Geiselmann, and Beth
\cite{GrasslGeiselmannBeth1999}.  Aharonov and Ben-Or constructed
fault-tolerant coordinatewise gates for polynomial codes, with degree
reduction when a gate changes the polynomial degree
\cite[Section~5]{AharonovBenOr2008}; Gottesman constructed the full
logical Clifford group for any prime-dimensional stabilizer code using
ancillas and Pauli measurements \cite{Gottesman1999}.  These results
establish that GRS and polynomial encoders support fault-tolerant
logical gates, but they do not identify the fixed-party product-Clifford
kernel.  The GRS half of Theorem~\ref{thm:logical-phase} upgrades those
constructions to the whole fixed-party \(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\) symmetry.
Its converse classifies the obstruction among all six-arcs:
self-association is exactly the condition for the kernel to exceed the
split torus.  Our literature search for this comparison covered quantum
Reed--Solomon and polynomial-code gate constructions, general
prime-dimensional stabilizer Clifford synthesis, and restrictions on
transversal gate sets.  We claim this equivalence, not priority for the
GRS gate constructions themselves.  Eastin and Knill's
restriction on universal transversal sets gives the general boundary
\cite{EastinKnill2009}.

Classically, we use Coble's
association theory \cite{Coble1922} through the modern treatment of
Dolgachev and Ortland \cite[Chapter~III]{DolgachevOrtland1988}; for
finite-projective arc background see Segre, Hirschfeld, and the modern
survey of Ball and Lavrauw
\cite{Segre1955,Hirschfeld1998,BallLavrauw2019}.

The rest of the paper separates classification from explicit scalar
certificates.  Section~\ref{sec:dictionary} fixes the arc, code, Weyl, and
AME conventions.  Section~\ref{sec:rigidity} proves the headline theorem
and its transversal corollary.
Section~\ref{sec:pencil} proves the \(z\)-classification, and
Section~\ref{sec:logical} proves the logical-Clifford phase theorem:
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\) on the GRS locus and a split torus off it.
Section~\ref{sec:invariants} gives a uniform H3/GRS marginal separator, an
exact four-copy \(q=13\) witness, and the generic-constancy obstruction for
fixed-copy scalars.  Appendix~\ref{sec:transport} calculates the
exceptional divisor of that witness, redundant given
Corollary~\ref{cor:lu-lc-pencil}; it is not part of the
classification.
Section~\ref{sec:verification} records the computational trust
boundary and the precise scope of all results.
