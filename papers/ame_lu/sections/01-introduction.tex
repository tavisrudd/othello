\section{Introduction}

An absolutely maximally entangled state has maximally mixed reductions on
every subsystem no larger than half the parties.  Fix a prime power
\(q=p^e\) and the standard additive \(q\)-dimensional Weyl system.  We ask
whether a stabilizer \(\AME(2m,q)\) state retains its local Weyl frames
under arbitrary single-party changes of basis.

The assertion that local-unitary (LU) equivalence implies local-Clifford
(LC) equivalence fails for general stabilizer states
\cite{JiChenWeiYing2008}.  Maximal entanglement supplies the missing
rigidity.

Throughout, \emph{transversal} means a tensor product of one-site physical
operators with the party labels fixed.  A \emph{fixed-party} symmetry has
this form; a \emph{party-moving} symmetry additionally permutes tensor
factors.  LU and LC equivalence allow a party permutation when it is
displayed explicitly.  Logical groups called fixed-party below exclude
such permutations.

\begin{theorem}[Local-unitary rigidity]\label{thm:lu-lc-rigidity}
Let \(q=p^e\), let \(m\geq2\), and let
\(\lvert\psi\rangle,\lvert\phi\rangle\) be stabilizer
\(\AME(2m,q)\) states for the standard additive Weyl system.
Suppose that, for a party permutation \(\pi\), single-qudit unitaries
\(U_1,\ldots,U_{2m}\), and a phase \(\theta\),
\[
 (U_1\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{2m})\pi\lvert\psi\rangle
   =e^{i\theta}\lvert\phi\rangle .
\]
Then every \(U_i\) normalizes the finite-field Weyl system.  In particular,
the displayed equivalence is local Clifford.
\end{theorem}

The mechanism is visible in any \(m+1\)-party reduced operator.  Stabilizer
labels supported on those parties form a group of order \(q^2\), and
projection to the local Pauli-label group at every party in the marginal is
bijective.  The marginal is therefore diagonal on the complete local Weyl
basis, with nonzero coefficients.  A rank-one contraction recovers exactly
one Weyl axis, so the tensor intrinsically determines every local Weyl
frame.  Product conjugation must permute those frames and is consequently
Clifford.  The argument uses neither CSS form, equal phases, classical
linearity, nor \(\F_q\)-linearity of the stabilizer labels.
Thus the proof has four steps:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{AME support squeeze}\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{full-Weyl marginal}\\
\Longrightarrow\ \text{intrinsic local axes}
\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{local Clifford factors}.
\end{gathered}
\]

\begin{corollary}[Transversal Clifford no-go]
\label{cor:transversal-clifford}
Let \(m\geq2\), and let \(V_\psi,V_\phi:\mathbb C^q\to
(\mathbb C^q)^{\otimes(2m-1)}\) be encoding isometries whose normalized
Choi states are stabilizer \(\AME(2m,q)\) states
\(\lvert\psi\rangle,\lvert\phi\rangle\).  They encode the associated
\([[2m-1,1,m]]_q\) quantum MDS codes.  If
\[
 (U_1\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{2m-1})V_\psi=V_\phi L
\]
for a logical unitary \(L\), then \(L\) and every \(U_i\) are Clifford.
Thus every transversal conversion between two codes in the family is
Clifford factor by factor.  Taking \(V_\psi=V_\phi\), no such code admits a
transversal non-Clifford logical unitary.
\end{corollary}

The support bijections also retain how local Weyl frames on different
parties fit together.  On an \((m+1)\)-party support \(A\), let
\(p_i:L_\psi(A)\to\F_q^2\) be the bijective projection at party \(i\).
The maps \(M_A(i,j)=p_jp_i^{-1}\) push a Weyl label from \(i\) to \(j\).
Their collection over all \(A,i,j\) is the
\emph{minimum-support atlas}.  Local trace-symplectic frame changes are
compatible precisely when they intertwine every \(M_A(i,j)\).  Here a
projective product-unitary group means the image of the product-unitary
symmetries in the projective unitary group of the full tensor product.
Schematically, overlapping supports turn operator pushing into a loop,
\[
 P_i\xrightarrow{\,M_A(i,j)\,}P_j
 \xrightarrow{\,M_B(j,k)\,}P_k
 \xrightarrow{\,M_C(k,i)\,}P_i ,
\]
whose composite is an atlas holonomy.

\begin{theorem}[Minimum-support atlas classification]
\label{thm:atlas-classification}
Let \(m\geq2\).  After a fixed party relabelling, two stabilizer
\(\AME(2m,q)\) states are
LU equivalent exactly when their minimum-support operator-pushing atlases
are equivalent by local symplectic changes of Weyl frame.  For one state,
let \(\mathcal G_\psi\) be its group of compatible local symplectic gauges,
let \(\Gamma_\psi\) be its fixed-party projective product-unitary symmetry
group, and identify its projective Pauli stabilizer with the label group
\(L_\psi\).  Then
\[
 1\longrightarrow L_\psi\longrightarrow\Gamma_\psi
   \longrightarrow\mathcal G_\psi\longrightarrow1
\]
is exact.  If \(q\) is prime, choosing base transports identifies
\(\mathcal G_\psi\) with the centralizer in \(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\) of the
atlas holonomies.
\end{theorem}

Thus an a priori continuous LU-equivalence problem reduces to finite
symplectic linear algebra on Weyl frames recovered from the state itself.

Rains recovered the three Pauli axes from a diagonal correlation tensor,
and Van den Nest, Dehaene, and De Moor used this mechanism in a
minimal-support LU-to-LC criterion for qubit stabilizer states
\cite[Theorem~13]{Rains1999}
\cite[Theorem~1 and Corollary~1]{VanDenNestDehaeneDeMoor2005}.
Corollary~\ref{cor:recognition-generation} gives a criterion of the same
kind at every local dimension.  Through each party, it asks for marginals
of both states whose recovered Weyl labels generate the local label group.
The states and coefficients are otherwise arbitrary: no stabilizer, CSS,
or linearity hypothesis enters.  For fully entangled qubit stabilizer states
on at least three parties, the minimal-support lemma identifies this
generation condition with the hypothesis of Van den Nest, Dehaene, and De
Moor.  The criterion therefore unifies their two proof branches rather than
strengthening their theorem in local dimension two.

The distinction appears above local dimension two.  The label group of a
minimal support embeds in the local label group at each of its parties.  In
the qubit case it therefore has only the two nontrivial sizes just described;
in larger dimension it may be intermediate.  Such subgroups occur naturally
in CSS states, where a minimum-weight \(X\)-support carrying no \(Z\)-word
can contribute a line of labels.  Proposition~\ref{prop:stabilizer-ame-support}
verifies the stronger, full-Weyl hypothesis for stabilizer AME states over
every prime power.

The axis-recovery linear algebra is classical
\cite{Harshman1970,Kruskal1977}.  Chang and Jing introduced the general
Kruskal-type tensor-uniqueness method into the qubit LU problem
\cite{ChangJing2022}; their generic coefficient tensors yield LU invariants,
not a Clifford conclusion.  Only the additive label structure is used, so
the criterion also applies to the integer-modulus Weyl system on
\(\mathbb C^d\) for arbitrary \(d\).  Section~\ref{subsec:recognition}
gives the proof and its sharp two-party and nongenerating boundaries.
Van den Nest, Dehaene, and De Moor also gave a finite invariant set
characterizing LC equivalence of arbitrary binary stabilizer states
\cite{VanDenNestDehaeneDeMoor2005Invariants}.  Our atlas theorem is not a
first general LC-classification result: it uses the AME support profile to
replace a global invariant list by an all-prime-power operator-pushing
criterion and an explicit symmetry-group centralizer.
Tan relates the LU orbits and local symmetries of even-party AME tensors
to quantum-MDS codes and computes the full local symmetry group of the
canonical four-qutrit AME state \cite[Theorems~3.5, 3.6, and 5.3]{Tan2026}.
The local factors in Tan's displayed generators are qutrit Cliffords, so
that calculation contains the \(q=3,m=2\) automorphism subcase above.
Our contributions are the uniform all-prime-power, all-\(m\),
arbitrary-additive intertwiner theorem and the exact minimum-support-atlas
classification of its residual Clifford symmetries.  The AME condition
itself supplies both the full-Weyl marginal required by the axis argument
and the minimum-support generation required by the atlas theorem.

Equivalently, the support bijection is a minimal-support Pauli form of
perfect-tensor operator pushing: every one-party Weyl operator has a unique
product-Weyl representative on any chosen \(m\) of the other parties.
Perfect-tensor operator pushing and the associated
\([[2m-1,1,m]]\) encoding interpretation were introduced in the
holographic-code setting by Pastawski, Yoshida, Harlow, and Preskill
\cite[Eq.~(2.3) and Section~5.7]{PastawskiYoshidaHarlowPreskill2015}.
Overlapping minimum supports compare the recovered local Weyl-label
frames.  Theorem~\ref{thm:atlas-classification} makes their transition
atlas a complete classification reduction rather than only an obstruction.
For six parties, explicit holonomy conjugacy data recover the pencil
coordinate \(z\).
The boundary \(m\geq2\) is sharp: a Bell pair has non-Clifford
\(U\otimes\overline U\) symmetries.  Its pair marginal is pure rather than
maximally mixed, which is exactly the failure that the second-moment
computation of Section~\ref{sec:two-uniform} detects.

Rigidity determines which product operations are possible; the
maximum-distance-separable (MDS) Calderbank--Shor--Steane (CSS) model
determines which Clifford operations actually occur.  If
\(C\leq\F_q^{2m}\) is a linear \([2m,m,m+1]_q\) MDS code, then
\[
  \lvert\Psi_C\rangle
    =q^{-m/2}\sum_{c\in C}\lvert c\rangle                       \tag{1.1}
\]
is a stabilizer \(\AME(2m,q)\) state.  Conversely, an equal-phase state
of a linear \([2m,m]_q\) code is AME only if the code is MDS
\cite{RaissiGogolinRieraAcin2018}.  Here the MDS--CSS geometry is no
longer needed for rigidity; it makes the exact fixed-party projective
logical image computable.

\begin{corollary}[Diagonal-isodual transversal logical group]
\label{cor:diagonal-isodual-transversal-group}
Let \(q\) be an odd prime, let \(m\geq2\), and let
\(C\leq\F_q^{2m}\) be a linear \([2m,m,m+1]_q\) MDS code.  The
projective group of logical unitaries of the associated
\([[2m-1,1,m]]_q\) quantum MDS code that admit tensor-product physical
implementations is
\[
\begin{cases}
 \F_q^2\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_2(q),
  &\text{if \(SC=C^\perp\) for a nonsingular diagonal \(S\),}\\
 \F_q^2\rtimes T,
  &\text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\]
where \(T=\{\diag(a,a^{-1}):a\in\F_q^\times\}\).
\end{corollary}

Put
\[
 \mathcal D(C,C^\perp)
 =\{s\in\F_q^{2m}:\diag(s)C\subseteq C^\perp\}.
\]
The branch condition is the nullity test
\(\dim\mathcal D(C,C^\perp)=1\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:diagonal-multiplier-line} proves that this dimension
is zero or one and that every nonzero vector in the space has full support
and is unique up to scalar.

The nullity-one condition governs the two branches above.  In odd
characteristic the \(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\) factor has coherent Weil lifts,
while the affine projective group retains the usual Heisenberg central
extension; Section~\ref{sec:exact-logical} separates these lift questions.
Generalized and extended generalized Reed--Solomon codes are diagonally
isodual, but the condition is not confined to that family: self-dual
non-GRS MDS constructions are known \cite{ZhuLiao2024}.

For example, an extended \([8,4,5]_7\) Reed--Solomon code gives an
\(\AME(8,7)\) tensor and a \([[7,1,4]]_7\) quantum MDS code whose
projective transversal logical group has order
\(7^2\lvert\mathrm{SL}_2(7)\rvert=16464\).  No non-Clifford
transversal gate enlarges this group.

For binary stabilizer codes, Wirthm\"uller determines the identity
component of the local-unitary automorphism group and gives a protected-qubit
criterion for finiteness of the induced automorphism group
\cite[Theorem~7 and Corollary~11]{Wirthmuller2011}.  Our result instead
determines every local factor inside the prime-power stabilizer-AME class.

Finiteness alone needs much less than either hypothesis.
Section~\ref{sec:two-uniform} proves that a pure state whose two-party
marginals are all maximally mixed --- any local dimension, no stabilizer
structure, no maximal entanglement --- has finite product-unitary symmetry
group modulo phase, and that the defect of a product unitary grows
quadratically away from that group, with an explicit ratio constant
independent of the number of parties.  This local estimate is stated in
generator coordinates: the admissible \(\ell^1\) radius is governed by the
state's uniformity order, remaining bounded in the order-three family and
growing linearly for an \(\AME(2m,q)\) state.  It is not a defect ball.
The stabilizer hypothesis then sharpens
discreteness into uniform separation: because overlaps of stabilizer states
are quantized, a product Clifford is either an exact symmetry or has defect
at least \((2-2p^{-1/2})^{1/2}\), a constant depending on the
characteristic and on nothing else.  Rounding a near-symmetry to a Clifford
and invoking that gap gives a defect-only threshold below which every
approximate symmetry factors through an exact one; unlike the generator
radius, this threshold is exponentially small in the number of parties.
This splits the
rigidity phenomenon into an
entanglement half, which forces discreteness, and an algebraic half, which
names the finite group.  It sharpens the limitation of the theorems above
without removing it.  Arbitrary phased minimal-support \(\AME(6,d)\) states
still form infinitely many LU classes
\cite{RamadasLakshminarayan2025}, and nonlinear orthogonal arrays and
nonstabilizer AME states are still unclassified here; but every one of
those states has a finite product-symmetry group modulo phase, so the
stabilizer hypothesis is doing only the work of identifying which finite
group appears.

The geometric applications in the remainder of the paper specialize to
\(m=3\).  Our main explicit application is the ordered pencil
\[
H_t=\bigl((0,1,1-t),(0,1,t-1),(1,1-t,0),(1,t-1,0),
          (1,0,-t),(1,0,t)\bigr).                              \tag{1.2}
\]
On its admitted odd-field non-GRS locus, define
\[
\begin{split}
 A(t)&=-4t(t-1)^2,\\
 B(t)&=(t^2-t+1)(t^2-3t+1),\\
 z(t)&=\bigl(B(t)/A(t)\bigr)^2 .
\end{split}                                                     \tag{1.3}
\]
Exact bracket identities show that \(z\) is the degree-eight projective
quotient of the pencil.  Theorem~\ref{thm:lu-lc-rigidity} upgrades its
local-Clifford classification to the following complete local result.

\begin{corollary}[Classification of the admitted pencil]
\label{cor:lu-lc-pencil}
Let \(t,u\) lie in the nondegenerate locus \((7.1)\) over an odd prime
field \(\F_q\).  Write \(\sim_{\mathrm{proj}}\) for projective equivalence
of the ordered six-arcs up to a party permutation and
\(\sim_{\mathrm{mon}}\) for monomial equivalence of their codes.  Then
\[
 H_t\sim_{\mathrm{proj}}H_u
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \ker H_t\sim_{\mathrm{mon}}\ker H_u
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \Psi_t\sim_{\LC}\Psi_u
 \Longleftrightarrow
 \Psi_t\sim_{\LU}\Psi_u
 \Longleftrightarrow z(t)=z(u),
\]
where party permutations are allowed.
\end{corollary}

The exact diagonal-isodual group dichotomy above holds for every \(m\).
For six parties it has a geometric coding-theoretic interpretation over
odd prime fields.  Regard any one tensor leg as the
input of the associated \([[5,1,3]]_q\) encoder.  The fixed-party product-Clifford symmetries
induce the full logical \(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\) precisely when the six-arc is
conic/GRS; otherwise they induce only the split torus.  Thus the
nineteenth-century self-association condition for six points controls
which one-qudit logical Clifford gates are available without moving
parties.  Including logical Paulis, the exact fixed-party projective
logical group is \(\F_q^2\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\) on the GRS locus and
\(\F_q^2\rtimes T\) off it.

Quantum Reed--Solomon and polynomial-code constructions provide the
standard coding setting
\cite{GrasslGeiselmannBeth1999,AharonovBenOr2008,Gottesman1999}.
Closest to our fixed-party question are restrictions on diagonal gates
between qubit codes \cite[Propositions~3 and~4]{AndersonJochymOConnor2014},
the endomorphism-algebra classification of multiblock diagonal Clifford
gates \cite[Theorems~5.5 and~6.1]{DasuBurton2025}, and automorphism methods
that recover SWAP-transversal logical Cliffords and their Pauli corrections
\cite{SayginelEtAl2025}.  Other locality models, including fold-transversal
and \(2\)-local layers, can realize larger groups
\cite{TansuwannontChanTakagi2026,Holmes2026}; fully transversal gates remain
subject to the Eastin--Knill obstruction and, for codes with several logical
qubits, cannot realize the full logical Clifford group
\cite{EastinKnill2009,ChakrabortyGottesman2026}.

Our coordinates may carry independent one-qudit factors, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:transversal-clifford} also treats conversions between
different one-logical-qudit quantum-MDS encoders.  The MDS--CSS
specialization identifies the exact fixed-party projective image for every
half-dimensional MDS code over an odd prime field.  Its dividing condition,
diagonal code--dual equivalence, also occurs beyond GRS codes in
algebraic-geometry MDS families \cite{ZhuZhao2026}.

Classically, we use Coble's
association theory \cite{Coble1922} through the modern treatment of
Dolgachev and Ortland \cite[Chapter~III]{DolgachevOrtland1988}; for
finite-projective arc background see Segre, Hirschfeld, and the modern
survey of Ball and Lavrauw
\cite{Segre1955,Hirschfeld1998,BallLavrauw2019}.
Over characteristic zero, the GIT quotient of six ordered points in
\(\mathbb P^2\) is a double cover of \(\mathbb P^4\), with Gale
association exchanging the sheets and the self-associated locus forming
the Igusa-quartic branch divisor
\cite[Section~2.3]{HowardMillsonSnowdenVakil2008}.  That divisor is dual
to the Segre cubic parametrizing six points on \(\mathbb P^1\).  Our
six-arc phase theorem is the finite-field, fixed-label slice of this
picture: the branch condition becomes diagonal isoduality, and the
Veronese determinant detects the conic/GRS locus.  This interpretation is
not used in the proof.

Sections~\ref{sec:dictionary}--\ref{sec:atlas} contain the main line: the
arc--code--AME conventions, LU-to-LC rigidity, and its structural
completion by the minimum-support atlas.  Section~\ref{sec:two-uniform}
develops discreteness and quantitative stability, while
Section~\ref{sec:exact-logical} determines the exact MDS--CSS logical
images and their lifts.  The next three sections develop the six-party
applications and contrasts.  Section~\ref{sec:pencil} proves the
\(z\)-classification; Section~\ref{sec:logical} proves the
logical-Clifford phase theorem; and Section~\ref{sec:invariants} compares
operator-valued rigidity with the generic blindness of fixed-copy scalar
invariants.  Section~\ref{sec:verification} records the trust boundary,
and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} closes with the exact extension-field
boundary.  The appendices contain the detailed \(H_3\) and
\(q=13\) scalar certificates, optional transport calculations, and finite
party-permutation examples; none is used in the uniform rigidity or atlas
theorems.
