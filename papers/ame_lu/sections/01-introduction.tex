\section{Introduction}

An absolutely maximally entangled state has maximally mixed reductions on
every subsystem no larger than half the parties.  Fix a prime power
\(q=p^e\) and the standard additive Weyl system on \(\mathbb C^q\).  We
ask how much of those local Weyl coordinates survives an arbitrary
single-party change of basis.

Local-unitary (LU) equivalence does not imply local-Clifford (LC)
equivalence for general stabilizer states \cite{JiChenWeiYing2008}.
Maximal entanglement changes the answer completely.

\begin{theorem}[Local-unitary rigidity]\label{thm:lu-lc-rigidity}
Let \(q=p^e\), let \(m\geq2\), and let
\(\lvert\psi\rangle,\lvert\phi\rangle\) be stabilizer
\(\AME(2m,q)\) states for the standard additive Weyl system.  Suppose
that, for a party permutation \(\pi\), single-qudit unitaries
\(U_1,\ldots,U_{2m}\), and a phase \(\theta\),
\[
 (U_1\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{2m})\pi\lvert\psi\rangle
   =e^{i\theta}\lvert\phi\rangle .
\]
Then every \(U_i\) normalizes the Weyl system.  Thus the displayed
equivalence is local Clifford.
\end{theorem}

The proof reads one \((m+1)\)-party reduced operator.  Stabilizer labels
supported there form a group of order \(q^2\), and projection to the
label space at any retained party is bijective.  The nonidentity part of
the marginal is therefore diagonal on the complete local Weyl basis, with
no missing axis.  Rank-one contractions recover those axes intrinsically,
so product conjugation must permute them.  The argument is independent of
CSS form, equal phases, classical linearity, and \(\F_q\)-linearity of the
stabilizer labels:
\[
 \text{AME support squeeze}\ \Longrightarrow\
 \text{full-Weyl marginal}\ \Longrightarrow\
 \text{intrinsic axes}\ \Longrightarrow\
 \text{Clifford factors}.                              \tag{1.1}
\]

The approximate problem cannot use this proof naively.  The nonidentity
part of one minimum-support marginal has exponentially small
Hilbert--Schmidt norm.  Instead, choose one tensor leg as the logical input
of the associated \([[2m-1,1,m]]_q\) code.  Its physical parties split
into three correctable regions.  A nested commutator localizes on one
region, scalar compression controls leakage, and Weyl--Fourier
concentration recovers a Clifford on the logical leg.  Repeating this with
each party as input gives the following global statement.

\begin{theorem}[Quantitative rounding]\label{thm:quantitative-rounding}
Let \(\lvert\psi\rangle\) be a stabilizer \(\AME(n,q)\) state with
\(n=2m\), \(m\geq2\), and let \(U=\bigotimes_iU_i\).  Write
\[
 \varepsilon(U)=\min_{|z|=1}
   \lVert U\lvert\psi\rangle-z\lvert\psi\rangle\rVert
\]
and put
\[
 \tau_p=\min\left\{\frac13,\frac{\sin(\pi/p)}{2\sqrt2}\right\},
 \qquad d_p=(2-2p^{-1/2})^{1/2},
\]
\[
 R^{\mathrm{clean}}_{n,q}=
 \min\left\{\frac{\tau_p}{8},\frac1{4\sqrt{2q}},
             \frac1{8\pi\sqrt n},
             \frac{d_p}{1+4\pi\sqrt n}\right\}.       \tag{1.2}
\]
If \(\varepsilon(U)<R^{\mathrm{clean}}_{n,q}\), then, up to phase,
\[
 U=g\bigotimes_{i=1}^n e^{ih_i},\qquad g\in G(\psi),
 \qquad
 \left(\sum_i\lVert h_i\rVert_F^2\right)^{1/2}
   \leq\pi\sqrt q\,\varepsilon(U),                     \tag{1.3}
\]
where the \(h_i\) are traceless Hermitian with spectral spread at most
\(\pi\).  Before the exact branch is selected, each \(U_i\) is within
normalized Hilbert--Schmidt distance \(8\varepsilon(U)\) of a one-qudit
Clifford.  Uniformly over prime powers and existing AME states,
\[
 R^{\mathrm{clean}}_{n,q}
 =\Theta\!\left(\min\{p^{-1},q^{-1/2},n^{-1/2}\}\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

The exact three-cleanable-region Clifford obstruction is due to
Pastawski and Yoshida \cite[Lemma~5]{PastawskiYoshida2015}, building on
stabilizer cleaning \cite[Lemma~1]{BravyiTerhal2009} and the localization
method behind transversal-gate hierarchy bounds
\cite{BravyiKonig2013,JochymOConnorKubicaYoder2018}.  The new step here is
quantitative: an implementation with leakage \(\varepsilon\) puts every
nested logical Weyl commutator within \(8\varepsilon\) of a scalar; a
finite Fourier argument then rounds it to a Clifford.  Quantized overlaps
of stabilizer states separate distinct product-Clifford branches by
\(d_p\), after which the AME second moment gives the collective residual
bound in \((1.3)\).  The theorem is a certified lower bound on the
rounding radius, not an optimality claim.

The support bijections retain more than the individual Weyl axes.  For an
\((m+1)\)-party support \(A\), projection at party \(i\) gives a
bijection \(p_i:L_\psi(A)\to\F_q^2\).  The transition maps
\(M_A(i,j)=p_jp_i^{-1}\) form the \emph{minimum-support atlas}.  Their
holonomies record how the recovered frames fit together.

\begin{theorem}[Minimum-support atlas classification]
\label{thm:atlas-classification}
After a fixed party relabelling, two stabilizer \(\AME(2m,q)\) states are
LU equivalent exactly when their minimum-support atlases are equivalent by
local trace-symplectic changes of Weyl frame.  For one state, if
\(\mathcal G_\psi\) is the group of compatible local symplectic gauges and
\(\Gamma_\psi\) its fixed-party projective product-unitary symmetry group,
then
\[
 1\longrightarrow L_\psi\longrightarrow\Gamma_\psi
  \longrightarrow\mathcal G_\psi\longrightarrow1
\]
is exact.  When \(q\) is prime, \(\mathcal G_\psi\) is the centralizer in
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\) of the atlas holonomies after base transports are
chosen.
\end{theorem}

At the dimension-only radius
\[
 \min\left\{\frac{\tau_p}{8},
   \frac{2\sin(\pi/p)}{4+64\sqrt q}\right\},           \tag{1.4}
\]
the symplectic maps recovered by cleaning already satisfy every exact
atlas transition.  This still does not select the exact affine branch.
Localized commutators cancel Weyl-translation phases, so they see the
stabilizer label space but not its character.  The resulting exact
product-Pauli correction need not be locally close to the rounded product
Clifford.  This affine-character obstruction is the precise missing input
between robust linear compatibility and a party-count-independent global
entry radius.

The encoder consequence of exact rigidity is immediate and operational.
Throughout, \emph{transversal} means a tensor product of one-site physical
operators with party labels fixed.

\begin{corollary}[Transversal Clifford no-go]
\label{cor:transversal-clifford}
Let \(V_\psi,V_\phi:\mathbb C^q\to
(\mathbb C^q)^{\otimes(2m-1)}\) be encoders whose normalized Choi states
are stabilizer \(\AME(2m,q)\) states.  If
\[
 (U_1\otimes\cdots\otimes U_{2m-1})V_\psi=V_\phi L,
\]
then \(L\) and every \(U_i\) are Clifford.  In particular, no code in
this family admits a transversal non-Clifford logical unitary.
\end{corollary}

Rains recovered the three Pauli axes from a diagonal correlation tensor,
and Van den Nest, Dehaene, and De Moor used that mechanism in a
minimal-support LU-to-LC criterion for qubit stabilizer states
\cite[Theorem~13]{Rains1999}
\cite[Theorem~1 and Corollary~1]{VanDenNestDehaeneDeMoor2005}.
Tan computes the full local symmetry group of the canonical four-qutrit
AME state \cite[Theorems~3.5, 3.6, and 5.3]{Tan2026}.  The exact theorem
above treats every prime power, every \(m\geq2\), and arbitrary additive
stabilizer labels; the Bell-pair boundary \(m=1\) is sharp because
\(U\otimes\overline U\) supplies non-Clifford symmetries.  Perfect-tensor
operator pushing and the associated encoder interpretation come from the
holographic-code construction of Pastawski, Yoshida, Harlow, and Preskill
\cite[Eq.~(2.3) and Section~5.7]{PastawskiYoshidaHarlowPreskill2015}.
The atlas theorem turns those local pushing maps into a complete invariant
for this state class.

The proof is organized around the two causal chains just described.
Section~\ref{sec:dictionary} fixes the additive stabilizer conventions and
derives the AME support formula.  Sections~\ref{sec:rigidity} and
\ref{sec:atlas} prove exact rigidity, the atlas classification, and the
encoder corollary.  Section~\ref{sec:quantitative} proves cleaning-based
rounding, robust atlas compatibility, and the affine-character obstruction.
Section~\ref{sec:verification} states the trust boundary, and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} records the scaling consequences.  The
appendices contain partial-Weyl recognition, detailed two- and
\(k\)-uniform stability, and the weaker single-marginal and aggregate
rounding mechanisms.
