\section{Verification and scope}\label{sec:verification}

The rigidity theorem, the arc--AME dictionary, and generic constancy are
conceptual proofs in the text.  The projective quotient uses displayed
bracket identities and projectivities.  Its transition-map invariance and
collision argument are proved in Section 4, while the 450 exact rational
functions are certificate-supported.
Section~\ref{sec:rigidity} proves the general MDS diagonal-multiplier
criterion.  Section~\ref{sec:logical} combines it with Gale duality to
obtain the six-arc phase theorem.  The party groups,
factor sets, complements, and torus actions in
Corollary~\ref{cor:computed-party-splitting} are certificate-supported
finite examples, not inputs to the uniform theorem.
Appendix~\ref{sec:scalar-certificates} proves the marginal trace/rank and
incidence bridges; the \(H_3\) determinants and \(q=13\) contractions are
certificate-supported.
Appendix~\ref{sec:transport} proves the systematic transport reduction; the cycle-cover
determinants, assignment exhaustion, exact minors, and double-coset
support sets are certificate-supported.

The reproducibility package is in \texttt{supplement/}.  From that
directory,
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
\texttt{python3 verify.py}
 &\text{checks all seventeen byte counts and SHA-256 digests},\\
\texttt{python3 verify.py --replay}
 &\text{regenerates all eight canonical JSON certificates in memory}.
\end{array}
\]
The full replay uses only the Python standard library, deterministic
integer, rational, polynomial, and finite-field arithmetic, and canonical
JSON serialization.  It includes separately implemented checks appropriate
to each claim: CSS versus direct-Lagrangian shortening, symbolic versus
finite projectivity, chord concurrency versus stabilizer ranks, quotient
minors versus finite-field elimination, and cycle-cover versus
fraction-free transport determinants.  These checks belong to one
paper-owned Python package.  They provide independent implementations
within that package, not an independent external reproduction.  The exact
files, entry points, hashes, domains, expected
outputs, and negative scopes are recorded in
\texttt{supplement/EVIDENCE.md} and
\texttt{supplement/EVIDENCE-MANIFEST.json}.
On the reference machine the digest check takes under a second, the full
evidence replay about six minutes, and each Lean aggregate under ten
seconds.

From the repository root, the exact formal replay is
\begin{verbatim}
python3 lean/scripts/lean-build-queue.py run \
  RelativeConicArcs.Gates.AMELUAggregate \
  RelativeConicArcs.Gates.AMELUAggregateAxioms \
  --profile single --threads 1 \
  --aggregate RelativeConicArcs.Gates.AMELUAggregateAxioms
\end{verbatim}
The checked release is then reproduced from \texttt{papers/ame\_lu/} by
\begin{verbatim}
make release-check
\end{verbatim}
The release identifier is content based:
\texttt{release/RELEASE-MANIFEST.json} records the SHA-256 digest of every
public byte and every file in the recursive formal dependency closure,
including the Lean toolchain and build definitions.  The manifest, rather
than a mutable working-tree name, is the immutable identity of the
companion release.

\paragraph{Claim-to-trust map.}
\noindent\textbf{LU-to-LC rigidity, atlas classification, and the
transversal no-go.}
The manuscript gives complete proofs.  Lean kernel-checks the support,
transition, marginal, covariance, axis-recovery, and holonomy cores; the
manuscript proves the exact projective atlas extension.

\noindent\textbf{Discreteness and quantitative stability.}
The manuscript gives complete proofs.  No certificate and no Lean module
supports Section~\ref{sec:two-uniform}; the numerical checks that first
accompanied these statements are not used and are not part of the
supplement.  Two thresholds behave differently and the difference is
recorded in the statements: \(\tau_p\) in
Proposition~\ref{prop:quantitative-intertwiner} is explicit, while
\(\varepsilon_0\) in Corollary~\ref{cor:approximate-decomposition} comes
from a compactness argument and is not exhibited.
Proposition~\ref{prop:stability-region} proves that no such threshold can
be uniform in the party count.

\noindent\textbf{Pencil and six-arc classification.}
The manuscript proof uses exact certificates for the finite holonomy
calculation.  The Lean interface is conditional and names its geometric and
holonomy inputs.

\noindent\textbf{\(H_3\) and \(q=13\) separators.}
The manuscript proof uses exact certificates for the determinants and
contraction ranks.  The finite graph core is unconditional in Lean; the
trace/covariance bridges and exact ranks are named inputs.

\noindent\textbf{Diagonal-isodual logical groups.}
The manuscript gives the proof, and the multiplier theorem is unconditional
in Lean.  The formal exact-carrier terminal names the propagation interface
as a hypothesis.

\noindent\textbf{Clebsch extremal \(X\)-syndromes.}
The coset-state, stabilizer-character, and one-representative-per-support
lemma is proved in the manuscript and kernel checked in Lean.  The
nonsingular conic, 120 syndromes, and transitive
\(C_{10}\times A_5\) action are imported from the cited companion Clebsch
theorem; they are not outputs of this paper's certificates or Lean aggregate.

\noindent\textbf{Twelve computed party-extension splittings and transport.}
Exact certificates supply the finite complements and transport matrices.
Lean proves the abstract splitting algebra once a complement is supplied.

The corresponding certificate entry points and formal theorem names appear
in the claim crosswalk in \texttt{supplement/EVIDENCE.md}.

The trusted boundary is Python integer arithmetic, the exact-arithmetic
implementations in the generators, deterministic Gaussian elimination,
and the mathematical dictionaries connecting arcs, codes, stabilizers,
and tensor contractions.  The certificates check the bounded
enumerations and symbolic identities that they record.  They do not prove
the conceptual correspondence between the implemented objects and the
paper definitions; that correspondence is supplied in the preceding
sections.

The Lean~4 artifact uses toolchain \texttt{v4.32.0-rc1}; no aggregate module
uses \texttt{sorry} or an unsafe declaration.  Its aggregate contains
unconditional formal proofs
of the support squeeze, minimum-support generation, marginal and
axis-recovery cores, atlas equivalence, abstract holonomy centralizers,
the Choi/transversal bridge, the diagonal-multiplier theorem, and the
abstract projective-extension algebra.  The ordinary kernel-checked
declarations use only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and
\texttt{Quot.sound}; three finite graph cardinalities use
\texttt{native\_decide} and are reported separately by the axiom terminal.
All other finite evidence is replayed by the Python supplement rather than
imported as a Lean axiom.

The geometric classification, exact logical propagation, finite
contractions, concrete party complements, and transport-orbit inputs enter
conditional interfaces.  The manuscript proves or the supplement
certificate-checks those inputs as recorded in the claim-to-trust map above;
importing the aggregate does not turn them into unconditional Lean theorems.
The exact module-to-claim crosswalk and each named hypothesis are recorded
in \texttt{supplement/EVIDENCE.md}.

The extension-field scalar statements in
Proposition~\ref{prop:frobenius-sector-divisors} are unconditional in
Lean.  The aggregate audit checks the frame-ratio
factorization, explicit Gale multiplier and odd-characteristic zero
criterion, same-exponent sector disjointness, and automorphism equivariance
of \(G,A,B,z\) and the admitted locus.  Its separate Galois-\(z\) orbit
terminal is conditional on two named bridges: extraction of a projective
Frobenius sector from an additive Clifford equivalence, and construction of
a Clifford from a Galois match.  Neither bridge is used as a paper theorem.

Finally, none of the results classifies nonlinear orthogonal arrays,
arbitrary nonstabilizer AME tensors, or stabilizer states that are not AME.
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-uniform-discrete} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:two-uniform-stability} apply to all of them, but they
bound symmetry groups rather than classify states, and they do not identify
the finite group; Remark~\ref{rem:two-uniform-not-clifford} exhibits a
\(2\)-uniform state whose symmetry group contains a non-Clifford element.
We do not claim that four
copies are globally minimal, that \(z=2,4/9\) are orbit exceptions, or
that a fixed copy degree separates all classes as \(q\) varies.  The
logical-group theorem and the quantum part of the pencil classification
are restricted to odd prime fields.  Over extension fields, Frobenius
basis permutations are additional local Cliffords.  The scalar \(z\)
is Frobenius-covariant, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:frobenius-sector-divisors} determines the two
divisors attached to each field automorphism, but the two additive-Clifford
bridges needed for a full orbit classification are outside the theorem
package.  The minimum-support atlas theorem remains exact over extension
fields, with local blocks in \(\mathrm{Sp}_{2e}(\F_p)\); its holonomy form
requires both a centralizing base block and the propagated symplectic
membership conditions, and does not imply a semilinear classification.
Theorem~\ref{thm:lu-h3-grs} is restricted to the stated
odd residue-field reductions, and the detector exceptions described in
Appendix~\ref{sec:transport} do not alter the
characteristic-uniform rigidity theorem.
