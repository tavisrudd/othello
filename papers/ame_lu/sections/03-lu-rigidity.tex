\section{Shortened Weyl tensors and LU rigidity}\label{sec:rigidity}

We first isolate the linear-algebra statement that recovers a diagonal
tensor's coordinate axes.  It is the full-Weyl counterpart of the
three-Pauli argument in \cite{Rains1999,VanDenNestDehaeneDeMoor2005}.

\begin{lemma}[Diagonal-tensor axes]\label{lem:diagonal-axes}
Let \(r\geq3\), let \(E_1,\ldots,E_r\) be \(N\)-dimensional complex inner
product spaces, and choose orthonormal bases
\(\{e_{ij}:1\leq j\leq N\}\).  If
\[
 T=\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_j
 e_{1j}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{rj},\qquad \lambda_j\ne0,       \tag{3.1}
\]
then the coordinate axes in every \(E_i\) are intrinsic to \(T\).
Consequently, a product of invertible linear maps carrying one tensor of
the form \((3.1)\) to another is monomial in the displayed bases.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Contract the first factor against
\(x=\sum_jx_je_{1j}^*\).  The result is
\[
 T_x=\sum_j\lambda_jx_j e_{2j}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{rj}.
\]
Flattening from \(E_2\) to \(E_3\otimes\cdots\otimes E_r\) gives a matrix
of rank \(\#\{j:x_j\ne0\}\).  Thus \(T_x\) has rank one exactly when
\(x\) lies on a coordinate axis.  Product equivalence preserves this
rank-one contraction locus, and hence permutes the axes in the first
factor.  Apply the same argument in every factor.
\end{proof}

Let \(C\leq\F_q^{2m}\) be \([2m,m,m+1]_q\) MDS, and fix a party set
\(S\) of size \(m+1\).  Shortening \(C\) on the \(m-1\) coordinates
outside \(S\) gives an \([m+1,1,m+1]_q\) MDS code.  The dual
\(C^\perp\) has the same parameters, so its corresponding shortening
does as well.  Choose nonzero shortened words
\(c^S\in C\) and \(h^S\in C^\perp\).  Each has support exactly \(S\),
and
\[
 L_C(S)=
 \{(ac^S,bh^S):a,b\in\F_q\}.                            \tag{3.2}
\]
At a party \(i\in S\), projection of this plane to the local Pauli labels
is
\[
 (a,b)\longmapsto(ac_i^S,bh_i^S),
\]
which is invertible because both coordinates are nonzero.  Thus the
\(q^2-1\) nonidentity labels in \(L_C(S)\) run bijectively through the
nonidentity Weyl labels at every party of \(S\).

\begin{proposition}[A shortened marginal fixes the local Weyl axes]
\label{prop:marginal-axes}
Let \(C,D\leq\F_q^{2m}\) be linear \([2m,m,m+1]_q\) MDS codes.  If a
product unitary on an \((m+1)\)-set \(S\) carries \(\rho^C_S\) to
\(\rho^D_S\), then every local factor on \(S\) is Clifford.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For a general equal-phase CSS state, the same stabilizer partial-trace
calculation as in \((2.1)\) gives
\[
 \rho^C_S=q^{-|S|}\sum_{\ell\in L_C(S)}W_\ell .
\]
Subtract the identity term and view the result as a tensor in the
\(m+1\) traceless local Hilbert--Schmidt spaces, each of dimension
\(q^2-1\).  Conjugation preserves these spaces.  By \((3.2)\), after
indexing the nonzero label \(v=(a,b)\), this tensor has the form
\[
 T^C_S=\sum_{v\in\F_q^2\setminus\{0\}}\lambda_v
 \bigotimes_{i\in S}W_{L_iv},                                  \tag{3.3}
\]
where every \(L_i\) is invertible and every coefficient \(\lambda_v\) is
nonzero.  This is a diagonal tensor with \(N=q^2-1\).

Local conjugation fixes the identity and acts unitarily on each
Hilbert--Schmidt space.  Lemma~\ref{lem:diagonal-axes} therefore forces
each local adjoint action to permute the axes
\(\mathbb CW_v\), \(v\ne0\).  It also fixes the identity axis, so the
underlying unitary normalizes the projective Weyl group.  This is the
single-qudit Clifford condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:lu-lc-rigidity}]
Absorb the party permutation by relabelling \(D\).  Partial trace of the
\(2m\)-party equivalence gives a product-unitary equivalence
\(\rho^C_S\sim\rho^D_S\) for every \((m+1)\)-set \(S\).  Given a party
\(i\), choose such a set containing it and apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:marginal-axes}.  Thus every \(U_i\) is Clifford.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:transversal-clifford}]
Let \(\lvert\Phi\rangle=q^{-1/2}\sum_x\lvert x,x\rangle\), and use the
Choi convention
\(\lvert\Psi_C\rangle=(I\otimes V)\lvert\Phi\rangle\).
Writing \(U_{\mathrm{phys}}=\bigotimes_iU_i\), the relation
\(U_{\mathrm{phys}}V=VL\) gives
\[
 (I\otimes U_{\mathrm{phys}})\lvert\Psi_C\rangle
   =(L^T\otimes I)\lvert\Psi_C\rangle .
\]
Hence
\[
 \bigl((L^T)^{-1}\otimes U_{\mathrm{phys}}\bigr)
   \lvert\Psi_C\rangle=\lvert\Psi_C\rangle .
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:lu-lc-rigidity} makes every displayed local factor
Clifford.  Entrywise conjugation preserves the finite-field Weyl group,
since \(\overline{X(a)}=X(a)\) and
\(\overline{Z(b)}=Z(-b)\); it therefore preserves the Clifford
normalizer.  Taking adjoints preserves the normalizer as well, and
\(U^T=(\overline U)^\dagger\).  Thus transposition preserves the
finite-field Clifford group, and \(L\) is Clifford.
\end{proof}

The theorem needs only a cover by retained sets of size \(m+1\); choosing
one such set through each party is enough.  More generally, the proof
applies whenever every party belongs to a support of size at least three
whose supported stabilizer subgroup has order \(q^2\) and projects
bijectively onto all nonzero local Weyl labels.
