\section{Four-party Weyl tensors and LU rigidity}\label{sec:rigidity}

We first isolate the linear-algebra statement that recovers a diagonal
tensor's coordinate axes.  It is the full-Weyl counterpart of the
three-Pauli argument in \cite{Rains1999,VanDenNestDehaeneDeMoor2005}.

\begin{lemma}[Diagonal-tensor axes]\label{lem:diagonal-axes}
Let \(r\geq3\), let \(E_1,\ldots,E_r\) be \(N\)-dimensional complex inner
product spaces, and choose orthonormal bases
\(\{e_{ij}:1\leq j\leq N\}\).  If
\[
 T=\sum_{j=1}^N\lambda_j
 e_{1j}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{rj},\qquad \lambda_j\ne0,       \tag{3.1}
\]
then the coordinate axes in every \(E_i\) are intrinsic to \(T\).
Consequently, a product of invertible linear maps carrying one tensor of
the form \((3.1)\) to another is monomial in the displayed bases.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Contract the first factor against
\(x=\sum_jx_je_{1j}^*\).  The result is
\[
 T_x=\sum_j\lambda_jx_j e_{2j}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{rj}.
\]
Flattening from \(E_2\) to \(E_3\otimes\cdots\otimes E_r\) gives a matrix
of rank \(\#\{j:x_j\ne0\}\).  Thus \(T_x\) has rank one exactly when
\(x\) lies on a coordinate axis.  Product equivalence preserves this
rank-one contraction locus, and hence permutes the axes in the first
factor.  Apply the same argument in every factor.
\end{proof}

Fix a four-party set \(S\).  Shortening an \([6,3,4]\) MDS code on the two
coordinates outside \(S\) leaves a one-dimensional code.  Choose nonzero
shortened words \(c^S\in C\) and \(h^S\in C^\perp\).  Each has support
exactly \(S\), and
\[
 L_C(S)=
 \{(ac^S,bh^S):a,b\in\F_q\}.                            \tag{3.2}
\]
At a party \(i\in S\), projection of this plane to the local Pauli labels
is
\[
 (a,b)\longmapsto(ac_i^S,bh_i^S),
\]
which is invertible because both coordinates are nonzero.  Thus the
\(q^2-1\) nonidentity labels in \(L_C(S)\) run bijectively through the
nonidentity Weyl labels at every party of \(S\).

\begin{proposition}[A four-party marginal fixes the local Weyl axes]
\label{prop:marginal-axes}
If a product unitary on \(S\) carries \(\rho^C_S\) to \(\rho^D_S\), then
every one of its four local factors is Clifford.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Subtract the identity term from \((2.1)\) and view the result as a tensor
in the four traceless local Hilbert--Schmidt spaces, each of dimension
\(q^2-1\).  Conjugation preserves these spaces.  By \((3.2)\), after
indexing the nonzero label \(v=(a,b)\), the tensor has the form
\[
 T^C_S=\sum_{v\in\F_q^2\setminus\{0\}}\lambda_v
 W_{L_1v}\otimes W_{L_2v}\otimes W_{L_3v}\otimes W_{L_4v},     \tag{3.3}
\]
where every \(L_i\) is invertible and every coefficient \(\lambda_v\) is
nonzero.  This is a diagonal tensor with \(N=q^2-1\).

Local conjugation fixes the identity and acts unitarily on each
Hilbert--Schmidt space.  Lemma~\ref{lem:diagonal-axes} therefore forces
each local adjoint action to permute the axes
\(\mathbb CW_v\), \(v\ne0\).  It also fixes the identity axis, so the
underlying unitary normalizes the projective Weyl group.  This is the
single-qudit Clifford condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:lu-lc-rigidity}]
Absorb the party permutation by relabelling \(D\).  Partial trace of the
six-party equivalence gives a product-unitary equivalence
\(\rho^C_S\sim\rho^D_S\) for every four-set \(S\).  Given a party \(i\),
choose a four-set containing it and apply
Proposition~\ref{prop:marginal-axes}.  Thus every \(U_i\) is Clifford.
\end{proof}

Only two marginals are needed if their supports cover all six parties;
for example \(S=\{1,2,3,4\}\) and \(S'=\{1,2,5,6\}\).  More generally, the
proof applies whenever every party belongs to a support of size at least
three whose supported stabilizer subgroup has order \(q^2\) and projects
bijectively onto all nonzero local Weyl labels.  We use only the
\([6,3,4]\) MDS specialization.
