\section{Arithmetic specialization}
\label{sec:arithmetic}

The remaining core argument has three interfaces.  Each must be proved with
an explicit integral normalization.

\begin{enumerate}
\item The two golden fibres must be identified with the two icosahedral
parents \(A_5^-\) and \(A_5^+\), including the comparison matrices and
their common \(A_4\).
\item The order-four exchanger must be computed together with its spinor
square class and its reduction modulo \(11\).
\item The reduced exchanger must agree with the finite deck involution
\(T_{11}\), not merely have the same order or interchange the same two
unlabelled sets.
\end{enumerate}

The same distinction applies to the Mathieu formulation.  Two
\(M_{11}\) parents, their \(\PSL_2(11)\) intersection, and Hadamard
row--column exchange give a marked-torsor corollary only after their
carriers have been identified with the reduced Hitchin construction.
Group orders and abstract conjugacy classes do not supply this
identification.  This conditional claim is \claimid{HC-5}.

\paragraph{Integral boundary.}
The abstract quadratic cover gives, on its nonbranch locus,
\[
 \#X_f(\F_q)=1+\chi_q(5J(f)).
\]
Its geometric interpretation over finite fields currently holds only away
from an unspecified finite set of primes.  The final theorem will name the
largest justified integral base supplied by an explicit model; it will not
replace that finite exceptional set by \(p\ne2,5\) without proof.
