\section{Introduction}

The Clebsch matching configuration has two sheets.  Forgetting the sheet
retains the unordered matching data; recovering it requires an odd
observable.  Over \(\F_{11}\) that first observable is a signed cubic tensor
on a ten-dimensional matching quotient.  We ask whether the orientation and
its cubic have a characteristic-zero source.

Hitchin's incidence between harmonic cubics and ordered icosahedra supplies
the double cover.  Let \(H\) be the rational seven-space of harmonic cubics,
let \(J_0\) denote Hitchin's invariant sextic, and write
\[
 V=\{(y_1,\ldots,y_5):\textstyle\sum_i y_i=0\},\qquad
 \sigma_3(y)=\frac13\sum_i y_i^3
\]
for the Clebsch four-space and its invariant cubic.  The following theorem
gives the arithmetic and harmonic realizations of the same cubic line.

\begin{theorem}[Arithmetic orientation and harmonic realization]
\label{thm:main}
\claimid{MAIN-1}.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The function field of Hitchin's ordered-icosahedron incidence cover is
\[
 \Q(\mathbf P(H))\bigl(\sqrt{5J_0}\bigr),
\qquad J_0|_V=16\sigma_3^2.
\]
Hence its restriction to \(D(\sigma_3)\subset\mathbf P(V)\) is the constant
golden torsor
\[
 D(\sigma_3)\times_{\Spec\Q}\Spec\Q(\sqrt5)
 \longrightarrow D(\sigma_3).
\]
The explicit fibre over \(xyz\) has good reduction at \(11\): its Galois
exchanger reduces to the nontrivial class
\[
 T_{11}=\operatorname{PGL}_2(11)/\operatorname{PSL}_2(11),
\]
while an explicit \(A_5\)-equivariant identification places the signed
matching cubic on the line \(\langle\sigma_3\rangle\).
\item The zonal degree-six harmonics on the ten axes through opposite
icosahedral faces contain \(V\) as their Petersen \((-2)\)-eigenspace.
Label the unit face axes by pairs \(\{i,j\}\), and put
\[
 F_y(\omega)=\sum_{i<j}(y_i+y_j)P_6(u_{ij}\mathbin{\cdot}\omega),
 \qquad
 P_6(s)=\frac{231s^6-315s^4+105s^2-5}{16}.
\]
On this four-space the spherical Gaunt cubic is
\[
 \frac1{4\pi}\int_{S^2}F_y(\omega)^3\,d\omega
 =-\frac{784000}{1247103}\sigma_3(y).
\]
Equivalently, the Clebsch cubic line is the four-channel restriction of the
standard degree-six Steinhardt cubic.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The first assertion determines the rational square class of Hitchin's known
degree-two cover by evaluating its golden fibre.  It does not claim an
integral incidence model at every prime: the abstract equation
\(z^2=5J_{\mathbf Z}\) is etale on \(D(10J_{\mathbf Z})\), while comparison
with the geometric incidence variety is proved only after inverting an
unspecified integer.  The statement at \(11\) concerns the explicitly
integral golden fibre and exchanger, so it does not depend on a global
good-reduction claim.

The second assertion gives an exact analytic realization on the
four-dimensional nontrivial summand of the face-axis module.  The displayed
characteristic-zero scalar has denominator divisible by \(11\).  The
cross-characteristic conclusion is therefore equality of the integral line
\(\langle\sigma_3\rangle\), not reduction of one scalar.

The proof has four steps.  Section~\ref{sec:cover} fixes the square class of
the rational cover and separates its three integral boundaries.
Section~\ref{sec:cubic} constructs the equivariant finite tensor bridge.
Section~\ref{sec:arithmetic} computes the golden exchanger and its spinor
class at \(11\).  Section~\ref{sec:harmonic} constructs the degree-six
channel and evaluates its Gaunt cubic.  Section~\ref{sec:consequences}
states the exact common-line conclusion, and
Section~\ref{sec:verification} records which explicit data receive
independent exact audits.
