\section{Introduction}

Let
\[
 H=\ker\!\left(\partial_x^2+\partial_y^2+\partial_z^2:
 \Q[x,y,z]_3\longrightarrow\Q[x,y,z]_1\right)
\]
be the rational seven-space of harmonic cubics.  Hitchin associated to a
general \([f]\in\mathbf P(H)\) two icosahedral six-axis configurations:
equivalently, two Mukai--Umemura isotropic three-planes \(U\subset H\) with
\(f\perp U\) \cite{HitchinIcosahedron,HitchinBundles}.  The incidence
variety of pairs \(([f],U)\) is therefore generically a degree-two cover of
\(\mathbf P(H)\).  We determine its rational square class and then study a
separate occurrence of the same Clebsch invariant in degree-six spherical
harmonics.

Let \(J_0\) denote Hitchin's irreducible invariant sextic, with his
normalization, and write
\[
 V=\{(y_1,\ldots,y_5):\textstyle\sum_i y_i=0\},\qquad
 \sigma_3(y)=\frac13\sum_i y_i^3
\]
for the Clebsch four-space and its invariant cubic.
Here and below, \(D(\sigma_3)\) is the principal open set
\(\{\sigma_3\ne0\}\).

\begin{theorem}[Arithmetic incidence cover]
\label{thm:arithmetic-main}
The function field of Hitchin's incidence cover is
\[
 \Q(\mathbf P(H))\bigl(\sqrt{5J_0}\bigr),
\qquad J_0|_V=16\sigma_3^2.
\]
Hence its restriction to \(D(\sigma_3)\subset\mathbf P(V)\) is the constant
golden torsor
\[
 D(\sigma_3)\times_{\Spec\Q}\Spec\Q(\sqrt5)
 \longrightarrow D(\sigma_3).
\]
The fibre over \(xyz\) consists of two conjugate icosahedral configurations
defined over \(\Q(\sqrt5)\).  Their Galois exchanger has good reduction at
\(11\), where its spinor class is the nontrivial element of
\[
\operatorname{PGL}_2(11)/\operatorname{PSL}_2(11).
\]
\end{theorem}

The last sentence concerns only the displayed golden fibre and its
exchanger; it is not a good-reduction statement for the global geometric
incidence cover.

\begin{theorem}[Degree-six harmonic restriction]
\label{thm:harmonic-main}
Let \(u_{ij}\), \(1\leq i<j\leq5\), be the explicitly labelled unit axes
through opposite icosahedral faces in \eqref{eq:face-axis-labels}, and set
\[
 F_y(\omega)=\sum_{i<j}(y_i+y_j)P_6(u_{ij}\mathbin{\cdot}\omega),
 \qquad
 P_6(s)=\frac{231s^6-315s^4+105s^2-5}{16}.
\]
The coefficient vectors \((y_i+y_j)_{i<j}\) form the Petersen
\((-2)\)-eigenspace, and their zonal harmonics give an injective copy of
\(V\) in \(\mathcal H_6\).  With
\(\langle f,g\rangle=(4\pi)^{-1}\int_{S^2}fg\), the spherical cubic on this
copy is
\[
 \frac1{4\pi}\int_{S^2}F_y(\omega)^3\,d\omega
 =-\frac{784000}{1247103}\sigma_3(y).
\]
\end{theorem}

Hitchin proves the generic degree, identifies the branch sextic, computes
its Clebsch restriction, and classifies the fibre over \(xyz\)
\cite[\S\S3, 9--10]{HitchinIcosahedron}
\cite[\S\S4--5]{HitchinBundles}.  The arithmetic theorem fixes the remaining
rational constant in that square class and computes the spinor class of the
explicit specialization.  The Mukai--Umemura threefold supplies the
incidence source \cite{MukaiUmemura}; Dye's finite theorem gives the
complementary square-\(5\) criterion for Clebsch hexagons
\cite{DyeClebsch}.  The abstract
equation \(z^2=5J_{\mathbf Z}\) is etale on \(D(10J_{\mathbf Z})\), but its
comparison with the geometric incidence variety is proved only after
inverting an unspecified integer.

The harmonic theorem is new and logically independent of the incidence
cover.  It places the classical degree-six bond-order observable
\cite{SNR1981,SNR1983} on the same four-space and computes its exact
restriction.  The coefficient
\(-784000/1247103\) has denominator divisible by \(11\), and this paper
constructs no common integral lattice from which the two realizations could
be obtained by base change.  The relation proved here is their common base
four-space and invariant polynomial, not a specialization between them.
