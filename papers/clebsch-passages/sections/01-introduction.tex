\section{Introduction}

The Clebsch matching configuration has two sheets.  Forgetting the sheet
retains the unordered matching data; recovering it requires an odd
observable.  Paper~II constructs such an observable over \(\F_{11}\): a
signed cubic tensor on a ten-dimensional matching quotient.  The question
here is arithmetic.  Does this finite orientation arise as a specialization
of a characteristic-zero double cover?

Hitchin's ordered-icosahedron incidence supplies the geometric cover.  Its
rational function field has the form
\[
 w^2=5J_0.
\]
On the Clebsch four-space the invariant \(J_0\) restricts to
\(16\sigma_3^2\).  Away from \(\sigma_3=0\), division by \(4\sigma_3\)
then leaves the constant quadratic torsor
\[
 \Spec\Q(\sqrt5)\longrightarrow\Spec\Q.
\]
The factor \(5\) records the orientation field; the cubic records the first
place at which orientation becomes visible.

\paragraph{Principal target.}
The central theorem will identify the golden Clebsch orientation torsor
with this Hitchin fibre and prove that its deck exchange reduces modulo
\(11\) to the finite sheet involution \(T_{11}\).  This statement is
partitioned in the trust ledger as \claimid{HC-1}, \claimid{HC-2},
\claimid{HC-3}, and \claimid{HC-4}.  The tensor clause \claimid{HC-3} is
certified, and the rational cover and specialization clauses are proved.
The geometric incidence comparison in positive characteristic is asserted
only outside an unspecified finite set of primes; the present integral
equations do not justify the sharper claim \(p\ne2,5\).

\paragraph{Harmonics.}
The axes through opposite icosahedral faces give a canonical
ten-dimensional subspace of the degree-six spherical harmonics.  Its
four-dimensional summand is the Clebsch module, and the standard Gaunt
cubic restricts there to a nonzero multiple of \(\sigma_3\).  This
identifies the orientation observable with a symmetry-resolved
Steinhardt-type cubic, not merely with an analogous invariant.
