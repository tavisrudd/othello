\section{Introduction}

Hitchin associated to a general harmonic cubic two icosahedral
six-axis configurations \cite{HitchinIcosahedron,HitchinBundles}.  Retaining
one of the two configurations gives a degree-two incidence cover of the
projective seven-space of harmonic cubics.  We determine its rational square
class and then study a separate occurrence of the same Clebsch invariant in
degree-six spherical harmonics.

Let \(H\) be the rational seven-space of harmonic cubics, let \(J_0\) denote
Hitchin's invariant sextic, and write
\[
 V=\{(y_1,\ldots,y_5):\textstyle\sum_i y_i=0\},\qquad
 \sigma_3(y)=\frac13\sum_i y_i^3
\]
for the Clebsch four-space and its invariant cubic.

\begin{theorem}[Arithmetic incidence cover]
\label{thm:arithmetic-main}
The function field of Hitchin's incidence cover is
\[
 \Q(\mathbf P(H))\bigl(\sqrt{5J_0}\bigr),
\qquad J_0|_V=16\sigma_3^2.
\]
Hence its restriction to \(D(\sigma_3)\subset\mathbf P(V)\) is the constant
golden torsor
\[
 D(\sigma_3)\times_{\Spec\Q}\Spec\Q(\sqrt5)
 \longrightarrow D(\sigma_3).
\]
The fibre over \(xyz\) consists of two conjugate icosahedral configurations
defined over \(\Q(\sqrt5)\).  Their Galois exchanger has good reduction at
\(11\), where its spinor class is the nontrivial element of
\[
\operatorname{PGL}_2(11)/\operatorname{PSL}_2(11).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Degree-six harmonic restriction]
\label{thm:harmonic-main}
Let \(u_{ij}\), \(1\leq i<j\leq5\), be the explicitly labelled unit axes
through opposite icosahedral faces in \eqref{eq:face-axis-labels}, and set
\[
 F_y(\omega)=\sum_{i<j}(y_i+y_j)P_6(u_{ij}\mathbin{\cdot}\omega),
 \qquad
 P_6(s)=\frac{231s^6-315s^4+105s^2-5}{16}.
\]
The coefficient vectors \((y_i+y_j)_{i<j}\) form the Petersen
\((-2)\)-eigenspace, and their zonal harmonics give an injective copy of
\(V\) in \(\mathcal H_6\).  With
\(\langle f,g\rangle=(4\pi)^{-1}\int_{S^2}fg\), the spherical cubic on this
copy is
\[
 \frac1{4\pi}\int_{S^2}F_y(\omega)^3\,d\omega
 =-\frac{784000}{1247103}\sigma_3(y).
\]
\end{theorem}

The arithmetic theorem fixes the unknown constant in the square class of
Hitchin's known cover by evaluating one golden fibre.  Its statement at
\(11\) concerns only that explicit fibre and exchanger.  The abstract
equation \(z^2=5J_{\mathbf Z}\) is etale on \(D(10J_{\mathbf Z})\), but its
comparison with the geometric incidence variety is proved only after
inverting an unspecified integer.

The harmonic theorem uses the same polynomial \(\sigma_3\) on \(V\), but it
is logically independent of the incidence cover.  The coefficient
\(-784000/1247103\) has denominator divisible by \(11\), and this paper
constructs no common integral lattice from which the two realizations could
be obtained by base change.  The relation proved here is their common base
four-space and invariant polynomial, not a specialization between them.
