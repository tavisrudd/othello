\section{The degree-six harmonic realization}
\label{sec:harmonic}

Put \(\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\).  The following vectors have squared norm \(3\);
their projective classes are the ten axes through opposite faces of an
icosahedron:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:face-axis-labels}
\begin{aligned}
v_{12}&=(-1,-1,-1),&
v_{13}&=(-1,-1,1),&
v_{14}&=(-1,1,-1),&
v_{15}&=(-1,1,1),\\
v_{34}&=(0,-\phi^{-1},-\phi),&
v_{25}&=(0,-\phi^{-1},\phi),&
v_{45}&=(-\phi^{-1},-\phi,0),\\
v_{23}&=(-\phi^{-1},\phi,0),&
v_{35}&=(-\phi,0,-\phi^{-1}),&
v_{24}&=(\phi,0,-\phi^{-1}).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
We use the unit representatives \(u_{ij}=v_{ij}/\sqrt3\).  Two labels are
adjacent when the pairs are disjoint.  This is the Petersen graph
\(KG(5,2)\), and the labeling is geometric because, for distinct axes,
\[
 (u_{ij}\cdot u_{kl})^2=
 \begin{cases}
 5/9,&\{i,j\}\cap\{k,l\}=\varnothing,\\
 1/9,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Replacing \(\phi\) by its conjugate gives the conjugate embedded
configuration.  The abstract Petersen incidence and the formulas below are
unchanged after the corresponding relabeling.  We do not claim that this
displayed embedding of its four-space is defined over \(\Q\).

The zonal degree-six harmonics define
\[
 L:\mathbb R^{10}\longrightarrow\mathcal H_6,\qquad
 L(a)(\omega)=\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}P_6(u_{ij}\cdot\omega),
\]
where
\[
 P_6(s)=\frac1{16}
 \left(231s^6-315s^4+105s^2-5\right).
\]
We equip \(\mathcal H_6\) with the normalized spherical inner product
\[
 \langle f,g\rangle=\frac1{4\pi}\int_{S^2}f(\omega)g(\omega)\,d\omega.
\]

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:harmonic-main}}

Let \(A\) be the Petersen adjacency matrix in the labeling
\eqref{eq:face-axis-labels}.  Direct evaluation gives the reproducing-kernel
matrix
\[
 K_{ef}=P_6(u_e\cdot u_f)
 =\frac1{243}(196I+47J-112A).
\]
The addition theorem for spherical harmonics gives
\[
 G_{ef}
 =\left\langle P_6(u_e\cdot-),P_6(u_f\cdot-)\right\rangle
 =\frac1{13}K_{ef}.
\]
Thus \(G\), not \(K\), is the Gram matrix for the stated inner product.
Its eigenvalues on
\(\mathbb R^{10}\simeq\mathbf1\oplus V_4\oplus V_5\) are respectively
\[
 \frac{110}{1053},\qquad
 \frac{140}{1053},\qquad
 \frac{28}{1053}.
\]
They are positive, so \(L\) is injective.

The Petersen eigenvalues are \(3,-2,1\) on
\(\mathbf1,V_4,V_5\).  For \(a_{ij}=y_i+y_j\) with \(\sum_i y_i=0\),
\[
 (Aa)_{ij}
 =
 \sum_{\{k,l\}\cap\{i,j\}=\varnothing}(y_k+y_l)
 =-2(y_i+y_j).
\]
These vectors form the four-dimensional \((-2)\)-eigenspace.

This identifies the abstract bridge uniquely.  The action of \(A_5\) on
five letters is two-transitive, so an endomorphism of \(\Q^5\) commuting
with \(A_5\) has one constant diagonal entry and one constant off-diagonal
entry.  Its restriction to \(V\) is therefore scalar.  Consequently, once
the five plane triples are identified, every \(A_5\)-equivariant comparison
between the arithmetic coordinate module and the Petersen coefficient
module is a scalar multiple of
\[
 y\longmapsto (y_i+y_j)_{i<j}.
\]
The inverse on its image is
\[
 y_i=\frac13\sum_{j\ne i}a_{ij}.
\]
Requiring the comparison to preserve \(\sigma_3\) forces the scalar to
have cube one, hence to equal one over \(\Q(\sqrt5)\).  The abstract
comparison is therefore canonical with the stated normalization.  It
still supplies no map between the two ambient harmonic spaces.

The cubic integral is \(A_5\)-invariant on \(V_4\).  The invariant cubic
line is generated by \(\sigma_3\), so one nonzero value determines the
scalar.  At \(y=(4,-1,-1,-1,-1)\), exact spherical moments give
\[
 \frac1{4\pi}\int F_y^2=\frac{2800}{351},\qquad
 \frac1{4\pi}\int F_y^3=-\frac{15680000}{1247103},
\]
while \(\sigma_3(y)=20\).  This proves the coefficient in
Theorem~\ref{thm:harmonic-main}.  The moments follow by expanding \(P_6\)
and applying
\[
 \frac1{4\pi}\int_{S^2}
 \omega_1^{2a}\omega_2^{2b}\omega_3^{2c}\,d\omega
 =
 \frac{(2a-1)!!(2b-1)!!(2c-1)!!}
 {(2a+2b+2c+1)!!};
\]
monomials with an odd exponent integrate to zero.

\begin{remark}
In the orthonormal Condon--Shortley convention, write
\(F=\sum_{m=-6}^6q_{6m}Y_{6m}\) and
\[
 W_6(F)=
 \sum_{m_1+m_2+m_3=0}
 \begin{pmatrix}6&6&6\\m_1&m_2&m_3\end{pmatrix}
 q_{6m_1}q_{6m_2}q_{6m_3}.
\]
The Gaunt formula and
\(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}6&6&6\\0&0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
=-20/\sqrt{46189}\) give
\[
 \int_{S^2}F^3\,d\omega
 =-\frac{130}{\sqrt{3553\pi}}\,W_6(F).
\]
This is the standard unnormalized degree-six bond-order invariant
\cite{SNR1981,SNR1983,DLMF}.
\end{remark}
