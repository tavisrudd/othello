\section{Conclusion}

Hitchin's two conjugate Clebsch charts live over \(\Q(\sqrt5)\), where the
branch sextic becomes \(16\sigma_3^2\).  Their common rational point
\([xyz]\) lies in the finite etale locus of the rational incidence model.
Their descended union has a nonsplit two-branch singularity there; its
normalization fibre and conductor recover \(\Q(\sqrt5)/\Q\).  The
fixed-configuration sections map this normalization model canonically to the
incidence normalization, whose complete reduced fibre determines the
remaining rational square class.
The explicit configurations also have a well-defined specialization at
\(11\), without requiring a global integral incidence model there.

The golden orientation passes next to the unique regular two-graph on the
six axes.  Its tight frame forces \(C^2=5I\); triangle holonomy, the diagonal
of \(*\!\bigwedge^3C\), the commutator Pfaffian, and the cross-golden
determinant are the same cubic.  The six outer translates are the Joubert
coordinates on the Segre cubic, their centered determinants are the
Segre--Igusa polar coordinates, and a coordinate section is the diagonal
Clebsch cubic surface.  The cross-block calculation also characterizes the
six-axis case: it is the unique nontrivial symmetric conference order whose
balanced exchange spectrum is cut-independent.  In higher orders the
classical aligned-four-set design fixes the second-moment mean and variance
but not the individual spectrum.  Its marked blocks nevertheless recover
every conference signing from order ten onward, up to switching and global
negation, and quadratically many selected determinant tests suffice.  One
triangle calibration restores the orientation used by the cubic shadows.
This operator stage is the memory retained after the incidence cover has
selected a golden orientation.

The degree-six face-axis construction returns to \(\sigma_3\): the
normalized spherical cubic restricts to it on the Petersen four-space.
After the golden axes, outer marking, plane triples, chart lift, and Petersen
labels are fixed, the selected sheet is assigned the relative source
\([C_{\mathfrak m},Z_{\mathfrak m}]_{\rm or}\); deck exchange gives its
opposite, and the primitive Petersen map carries its sign to the degree-six
Gaunt cubic.  The sheet alone does not determine the marking or lift.  This
is a relative comparison of orientation sources, not an equality between
\(Z_{\mathfrak m}\) and \(\sigma_3\) or a map between their ambient harmonic
spaces.  Determining the exact finite set of primes
that must be inverted for the geometric incidence comparison remains a
concrete arithmetic problem: it is the integral identification, flatness,
normality, and Stein-base-change problem isolated in
Section~\ref{sec:cover}, not an unexplained defect of \(z^2=5J_0\).
