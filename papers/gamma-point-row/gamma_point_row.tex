\begin{abstract}
The Gamma integral structure turns the class of a point into the flat Euler
covector which reads ordinary rank.  We prove that the zero or nonzero
restriction of this row to a fixed formal-monodromy primary packet is a
birational invariant of smooth projective varieties.  The proof uses one
smooth projective equivariant completion of a W{\l}odarczyk cobordism rather
than a sequence of chamberwise comparisons.  A common-open orbit cylinder
gives one point row at the two endpoints.  Rotation localization kills every
intermediate fixed stratum.  The remaining two endpoint gauged series are
the opposite residue expansions of one balanced Mellin--Barnes kernel;
after Rees homogenization, a finite quotient of their common source carries
the formal primary decomposition needed to compare the rows.

For a smooth cubic threefold \(X\), we isolate the only cubic-specific input.
Cai's small quantum-connection matrices give a rank-two block with
primitive-sixth formal monodromy.  We reconstruct its indicial polynomial
directly, and an elementary \({}_2F_3\) Barnes calculation shows that the
Gamma point row is nonzero on both primitive-sixth lines.  Quantum K\"unneth
then makes the packet nonzero on \(X\times\mathbf P^m\), whereas
\(\mathbf P^{m+3}\) has no primitive-sixth packet.  Birational invariance
therefore implies that \(X\times\mathbf P^m\) is irrational for every
\(m\geq0\).  We also prove exact
one-wall point identities and give countermodels showing why formal
monodromy, pairing, integrality, and localized Fourier support alone cannot
replace the common-master argument.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Gamma class; quantum cohomology; birational wall
crossing; VGIT; ordinary flop; Stokes matrix; Fourier--Laplace transform;
GKZ system.
