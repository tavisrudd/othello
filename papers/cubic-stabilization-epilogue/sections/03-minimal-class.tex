\section{Integral divisor products and the primitive minimal class}
\label{sec:minimal-class}

Let \((A,\Theta)\) be a principally polarized abelian variety of dimension
\(g\).  Put
\[
 P_A^k=\operatorname{im}\!\left(
 \operatorname{Sym}^k\NS(A)\longrightarrow H^{2k}(A,\Z)
 \right),
 \qquad
 \gamma_A=\frac{\Theta^{g-1}}{(g-1)!}.
\]
The class \(D^k/k!\) of a divisor on an abelian variety is integral.  The
question here is whether these divided powers already belong to the smaller
lattice \(P_A^k\) generated by ordinary products of integral divisors.

For quotients of elliptic powers, this question is controlled by the
valuation pattern of the descended coefficient forms.  The main local result
of this section says that finite-\'etale graph slopes force all divided powers
of the prescribed graph divisor lattice into ordinary products, in every
degree.  The proof has two steps: the graph equations produce a symmetric
matrix-of-ideals lattice, and a midpoint inequality makes that lattice
rank-one generated.  Rank-one coefficient divisors have square zero, so their
divided powers expand without factorials.  The six-axis application supplies
the primitive minimal class needed below.

\subsection{A local graph theorem}

Work over a complete unramified discrete valuation ring \(O\), with
uniformizer \(p\).  Fix an elliptic curve \(E\), an isogeny
\(q:E^n\to Y\) to a principally polarized abelian variety, and extend the
\(p\)-adic coefficient lattices to \(O\).  A symmetric matrix
\(A\in\operatorname{Sym}_n(O)\) denotes the standard coefficient divisor
\(D_A\) on \(E^n\), generated by coordinate and Poincare divisors.  Define
the prescribed graph-divisor lattice and its ordinary product image by
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 N_{\rm gr}(q,O)
 &=\{D\in\NS(Y)\otimes O:q^*D=D_A
       \text{ for some }A\in\operatorname{Sym}_n(O)\},\\
 P_{\rm gr}^k
 &=\operatorname{im}\!\left(
   \operatorname{Sym}^kN_{\rm gr}(q,O)\longrightarrow
   H^{2k}(Y,\mathbf Z_p)\otimes O\right).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3.1}
\]
Because \(q^*\) is injective on torsion-free cohomology, we identify
\(N_{\rm gr}(q,O)\) with the symmetric matrices whose coefficient divisors
descend through \(q\).  This is the prescribed lattice even when \(E\) has
complex multiplication; no extra CM divisor is included.

Suppose the pullback coefficient polarization on the elliptic-power source is
\[
 G=PB,\qquad
 P=\bigoplus_a p^a I_{M_a},\qquad
 B=\mathop{\perp}_a B_a,
 \tag{3.2}
\]
where each \(B_a\) is unimodular.  A \emph{marked finite-\'etale graph
presentation} consists of a symplectic ruling of the primary polarization
kernel in which \(\ker q\) is the graph of an endomorphism \(T\), with the
following properties: \(T\) preserves the
depth summands \(M_a\),
\[
 T^{\mathsf t}B^{-1}=B^{-1}T
 \quad\text{or, equivalently,}\quad BT^{\mathsf t}=TB,
 \tag{3.3}
 \label{eq:graph-dual-self-adjoint}
\]
and the algebra generated by \(T\) modulo \(p^a\) is finite etale on every
positive-depth summand.  Thus adjointness is taken for the dual coefficient
form \(B^{-1}\).  No etaleness is required on \(M_0\).

After a finite unramified extension splitting these etale algebras, the
positive-depth summands decompose orthogonally into blocks
\[
 L_i=L_{a_i,\lambda_i},\qquad
 T_i=t_iI+p^{a_i}S_i,
 \tag{3.4}
 \label{eq:graph-split-block}
\]
with \(S_i\) integral.  Indeed, completeness lifts the primitive idempotents
of each split finite-\'etale quotient to idempotents in the algebra generated
by \(T\).  They are polynomials in \(T\), so
\eqref{eq:graph-dual-self-adjoint} makes them self-adjoint for
\(B^{-1}\).  Their images are therefore mutually orthogonal for \(B^{-1}\),
and hence for \(B\); on each image, \(T\) is scalar modulo the corresponding
power \(p^{a_i}\).  We keep the unit part as one block.  Write
\(\delta_{ij}=v_p(t_j-t_i)\), with \(v_p(0)=\infty\), for two
positive-depth blocks.

\begin{lemma}[The graph coefficient lattice]
\label{lem:graph-coefficient-lattice}
\coverage{fragment}%
\lean{graphCoefficient_graphCoordinate_block_identity,%
  graphCoefficient_dualForm_adjoint_iff,%
  graphCoefficient_slopeCommutator_expansion,%
  graphCoefficient_scalarDifference_divisibility,%
  graphCoefficient_commutatorDepth_iff_truncated,%
  graphCoefficient_crossDepth_iff_splitSlopeCommutator,%
  graphCoefficient_rectangular_crossDepth_iff_splitSlopeCommutator,%
  graphCoefficient_rectangular_unitPositive_commutator,%
  graphCoefficient_symmetricFiniteBlockFamily_depth_iff_descent,%
  graphCoefficient_crossDepth_intersection_and_midpoint,%
  graphCoefficient_crossDepth_eq_of_effectiveSlopeDifference_eq,%
  graphCoefficient_crossDepth_eq_of_dvr_scalar_lifts,%
  graphCoefficient_unitCrossDepth,%
  graphCoefficient_flattenedSplitPresentation_iff_descent,%
  graphCoefficient_afterSplitBasisChange_iff_descent,%
  graphCoefficient_splitPresentation_rankOneGenerated}%
In the split basis, the coefficient lattice of descended graph divisors is
the symmetric matrix-of-ideals lattice whose diagonal block \(i\) has depth
\(p^{a_i}\) and whose \((i,j)\)-block has depth
\[
 e_{ij}=\max\{a_i,a_j,a_i+a_j-\delta_{ij}\}.
 \tag{3.5}
 \label{eq:graph-cross-depth}
\]
For the unit block and a positive-depth block \(j\), one has
\(e_{0j}=a_j\).
In particular,
\[
 e_{ij}\geq \max(a_i,a_j)\geq
 \left\lceil\frac{a_i+a_j}{2}\right\rceil.
 \tag{3.6}
 \label{eq:graph-midpoint-bound}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose the symplectic basis determined by the marked ruling and the depth
decomposition.  Adjoining the graph generators to the source homology
lattice gives the quotient lattice, and in these coordinates the
change-of-lattice matrix is
\[
 C=\begin{pmatrix}P^{-1}&P^{-1}T\\0&I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
A coefficient alternating form descends precisely when its matrix transported
by \(C\) is integral.  For a symmetric source coefficient \(A\), direct
multiplication gives
\[
 C\begin{pmatrix}0&A\\-A&0\end{pmatrix}C^{\mathsf t}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 P^{-1}(AT^{\mathsf t}-TA)P^{-1}&P^{-1}A\\
 -AP^{-1}&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{3.7}
 \label{eq:graph-descent-matrix}
\]
Thus descent is equivalent to the integrality of the three displayed
nonzero blocks.  Here \(A=A^{\mathsf t}\) is a symmetric coefficient
bilinear form in this normalization.  On two positive-depth blocks
\(L_i\times L_j\), the two off-diagonal blocks first
force \(A_{ij}\) to have depth \(\max(a_i,a_j)\).  Substituting
\(T_i=t_iI+p^{a_i}S_i\) into the upper-left block leaves the additional
condition
\[
 p^{-a_i-a_j}(t_j-t_i)A_{ij}\in M_{r_i\times r_j}(O);
\]
the terms containing \(S_i,S_j\) are already integral.  Intersecting the two
conditions gives \eqref{eq:graph-cross-depth}.  Changing a scalar lift cannot
change its maximum.
Pairing the unsplit unit block with \(L_j\) gives \(e_{0j}=a_j\) directly
from \eqref{eq:graph-descent-matrix}, and
\eqref{eq:graph-midpoint-bound} follows.
\end{proof}

The inequality \eqref{eq:graph-midpoint-bound} is the valuation shadow of the tropical positive
semidefinite cone studied by Yu~\cite{YuTropical}.  We need the following
signed integral lift of that inequality; in particular, no division by two
is permitted.

\begin{lemma}[Rank-one generation over a DVR]
\label{lem:dvr-rank-one}
\coverage{complete}%
\lean{rankOne_mixed_coefficient_identity, rankOne_midpoint_exponents,%
  rankOne_cross_coefficient_decomposition,%
  rankOne_weightedPair_decomposition_of_midpoint,%
  rankOne_finiteMatrix_generation_of_pairwise_midpoint,%
  rankOne_finiteMatrix_generated_iff_pairwise_midpoint_of_dvr}%
Let \(L\) be a symmetric matrix-of-ideals lattice over \(O\), with diagonal
depths \(p^{a_i}\) and cross depths \(p^{e_{ij}}\).  Then \(L\) is generated
by its rank-one forms if and only if
\[
 2e_{ij}\geq a_i+a_j\qquad(i\ne j).
 \tag{3.8}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(c vv^{\mathsf t}\in L\), the two diagonal conditions imply
\[
 v_p(cv_iv_j)\geq
 \left\lceil\frac{a_i+a_j}{2}\right\rceil.
\]
Hence rank-one forms cannot generate a shallower cross ideal.

Conversely, fix a generator \(p^e(uv^{\mathsf t}+vu^{\mathsf t})\) of a
cross block.  Choose \(x\geq a_i\) with \(y=2e-x\geq a_j\).  The integers
\(x,y\) have the same parity.  If \(t=\min(x,y)\), write
\(x=t+2r\) and \(y=t+2s\).  Then
\[
 p^t(p^ru+p^sv)(p^ru+p^sv)^{\mathsf t}
 -p^xuu^{\mathsf t}-p^yvv^{\mathsf t}
 =p^e(uv^{\mathsf t}+vu^{\mathsf t}).
 \tag{3.9}
\]
All three rank-one terms lie in \(L\).  Together with the diagonal rank-one
forms, these identities generate \(L\), without division by two.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[All-degree graph saturation]
\label{thm:all-degree-graph-saturation}
\coverage{fragment}%
\lean{squareZero_sum_pow_eq_factorial_mul_squarefreeProductSum,%
  ordinaryDivisorProductSubmodule,%
  squarefreeProductSum_mem_ordinaryDivisorProductSubmodule,%
  decomposableTwoForm_squareZero,%
  ellipticSource_rankOne_decomposable_and_squareZero,%
  rankOne_squareZero_of_injectivePullback,%
  rankOne_allDegree_squareZeroAssembly,%
  faithfullyFlat_tensorQuotient_zero_implies_mem,%
  rankOne_allDegree_integralProductMember_of_faithfullyFlatQuotient,%
  rankOne_allDegree_of_injectivePullback_and_faithfullyFlatDescent,%
  rankOne_allDegree_of_canonicalEllipticSourcePullback_and_faithfullyFlatDescent,%
  rankOne_allDegree_ordinaryProduct_of_canonicalEllipticSourcePullback,%
  graphLattice_coefficientExtension_preserves_member_and_rankOne,%
  graphLattice_splitCoordinateBasisChange_rankOne_and_recovery,%
  ordinaryDivisorProducts_baseChange_and_faithfullyFlatReflection,%
  rankOne_allDegree_dividedPower_of_faithfullyFlatCoefficientExtension,%
  rankOne_allDegree_dividedPower_of_splitGraphDVR,%
  rankOne_allDegree_dividedPower_of_splitGraphDVR_afterBasisChange,%
  rankOne_allDegree_dividedPower_of_markedGraphDescent_afterBasisChange}%
For a marked finite-\'etale graph presentation, every divided power of a class
in its prescribed graph divisor lattice is an ordinary integral divisor
product.  Equivalently, after localization at \(p\), the divided-power
envelope of that lattice and its ordinary product image agree in every
degree.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
After the unramified splitting extension, Lemmas
\ref{lem:graph-coefficient-lattice} and~\ref{lem:dvr-rank-one} express every
class in the extended graph divisor lattice as a finite sum
\(D=R_1+\cdots+R_m\) of rank-one coefficient classes.  These are classes in
the coefficient extension of the original lattice, not newly descended line
bundles.  On the elliptic-power source the alternating form of each \(R_i\)
has rank two, hence \(R_i^2=0\).  Pullback by the isogeny is injective on
torsion-free cohomology, so the same identity holds on the quotient.
Therefore
\[
 \frac{D^k}{k!}=\sum_{1\leq i_1<\cdots<i_k\leq m}
 R_{i_1}\cdots R_{i_k},
 \tag{3.10}
 \label{eq:graph-divided-power-expansion}
\]
an \(O'\)-linear combination of ordinary products over the splitting ring
\(O'\).  More precisely,
\[
 P_{\mathrm{gr}}^k\otimes_OO'
 =\operatorname{im}\!\left(
 \operatorname{Sym}^k(N_{\mathrm{gr}}\otimes_OO')
 \longrightarrow H^{2k}\otimes_OO'\right).
\]
Faithful flatness descends the quotient of these finite lattices, and hence
descends \eqref{eq:graph-divided-power-expansion} to \(O\), and therefore to
\(\Z_p\) in the six-axis application.
\end{proof}

The theorem concerns the divisor lattice selected by the marked
elliptic-power presentation.  If \(E\) has no complex multiplication, this
is the full Neron--Severi lattice.  At a CM fibre extra divisor classes need
not satisfy the graph hypotheses.  The statement is cohomological: it
neither constructs Chow divided powers nor asserts that every integral Hodge
class is a divisor product.

For comparison with the classical rational theory, Milne proves that the
Lefschetz classes are the rational algebra generated by divisors
\cite[Theorem~3.2]{MilneLefschetz}.  Divided powers in Chow and integral
Fourier transforms are studied in~\cite{MoonenPolishchuk,BdGF}.  The local
result above instead keeps the weighted integral graph lattice and the
ordinary, rather than divided, product image.

\subsection{The six-axis packet}

We now apply the graph theorem to the polarization computed in
Section~\ref{sec:envelope}.

We use the following elementary depth-one lifting observation.  Let \(U\) be
a unimodular \(O\)-lattice with symmetric Gram matrix \(B\).  Every
endomorphism \(\bar T\) of \(U/pU\) that is self-adjoint for
\(\bar B^{-1}\) has a lift \(T\in\End_O(U)\) satisfying
\[
 T^{\mathsf t}B^{-1}=B^{-1}T.
 \tag{3.11}
 \label{eq:depth-one-self-adjoint-lift}
\]
The construction may be performed separately on orthogonal depth summands.
Choose any lift \(T_0\).  Its adjointness defect has the form
\[
 T_0^{\mathsf t}B^{-1}-B^{-1}T_0=pD,
\]
where \(D\) is skew-symmetric with zero diagonal.  Choose an integral matrix
\(C\) with \(C^{\mathsf t}-C=-D\), for example by assigning its entries
strictly above and below the diagonal.  Then \(T=T_0+pBC\) reduces to
\(\bar T\), and
\[
 T^{\mathsf t}B^{-1}-B^{-1}T
 =p(D+C^{\mathsf t}-C)=0.
\]
No division by two is used.  Applying the construction blockwise preserves
the depth decomposition.

\begin{lemma}[Local chart for the six-axis quotient]
\label{lem:six-axis-local-chart}
\coverage{fragment}%
\lean{sixAxisSmith_unit_line_and_depth_one_at_two_three,%
  sixAxisLocalChart_orthogonal_block,%
  sixAxisLocalChart_twoPrimary_quadraticSlopeModel,%
  sixAxisLocalChart_twoPrimary_minimalPolynomialModel,%
  sixAxisLocalChart_twoPrimary_splitsOverQuadraticRoot,%
  sixAxisLocalChart_twoPrimary_concreteFiniteEtaleSplitting,%
  sixAxisLocalChart_threePrimary_scalarSlopeModel,%
  sixAxisLocalChart_depthOne_selfAdjointLift,%
  sixAxisLocalChart_selfAdjointLift_orthogonalSummands,%
  sixAxisLocalChart_unitSummand_carries_no_discriminant,%
  sixAxisLocalChart_orthogonalDecomposition_and_unitLine}%
Let \(G=6I_5-J_5\).  At each prime \(p\mid6\), the coefficient lattice has
an orthogonal decomposition
\[
 (\Z_p^5,G)\simeq U_0\perp pU_1,
\]
where \(U_0\) has rank one and \(U_1\) rank four, both unimodular.  The
principal quotient is trivial on \(U_0\).  On \(U_1/pU_1\), its graph slope
has minimal polynomial \(t^2+t+1\) at \(p=2\); at \(p=3\) it is a scalar,
hence a one-point finite-\'etale block, after choosing a complementary
symplectic ruling if the selected half is vertical.  In both cases the slope has a
depth-preserving integral lift that is self-adjoint for the dual coefficient
form, so these data give a marked finite-\'etale graph presentation.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(e_1,\ldots,e_5\) be the standard basis.  Since
\(\langle e_1,e_1\rangle=5\) and \(\langle e_1,e_j\rangle=-1\), the vectors
\[
 v_j=e_j+\frac15e_1\qquad(2\leq j\leq5)
\]
span the orthogonal complement of \(e_1\) over \(\Z_p\).  Its Gram matrix is
\(\frac65(5I_4-J_4)\), and \(\det(5I_4-J_4)=5^3\).  This gives the asserted
unit line and depth-one block at two and three.  The unit line contributes
nothing to the \(p\)-primary discriminant group, so the \(p\)-primary
isogeny kernel is supported entirely on \(U_1/pU_1\).

At two, Proposition~\ref{prop:principal-gluing-packet} applies to every
smooth fibre and identifies its geometric kernel with one of the two exotic
graphs.  Isotropy says that either slope is self-adjoint for the reduced dual
form.  It is a root in \(\F_4\) of \(t^2+t+1\), so its generated algebra is
finite etale.  The preceding lifting argument supplies the required integral
self-adjoint lift on \(U_1\).  At three, the same
proposition says that every \(A_5\)-stable half is a scalar graph because the
coefficient heart is simple with commutant \(\F_3\) by
Lemma~\ref{lem:six-point-hearts}.  More precisely, the
four halves form \(\PP^1(\F_3)\).  If the selected member is vertical,
choose any other member as the complementary vertical ruling; the selected
half then becomes the graph of a scalar (including zero).  This change is
only in the symplectic ruling of the primary kernel.  The chosen symplectic
basis of its two-dimensional multiplicity space lifts from \(\F_3\) to
\(\Z_3\): equivalently, reduction
\(\operatorname{Sp}_2(\Z_3)\to\operatorname{Sp}_2(\F_3)\) is surjective.
Thus it lifts to an integral change of ruling preserving the alternating
form; the depth-one coefficient block and its scale \(P=3I\) are unchanged.
The algebra generated by the resulting scalar is
\(\F_3\), hence is a one-point finite-\'etale block, and the same lifting
argument applies.  Extend each lift by a scalar on \(U_0\).
No global choice of either slope is needed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Divided powers for the six-axis family]
\label{thm:six-axis-divided-powers}
\coverage{fragment}%
\lean{primeDenominatorMember_all_implies_mem,%
  rankOne_allDegree_integralProductMember_of_primeDenominators}%
For every smooth member \(X_b\) of the nonstandard \(A_5\)-cubic pencil, the
prescribed six-axis graph divisor lattice on \(J_b\) has all its integral
divided powers in the ordinary divisor-product image.  In particular,
\[
 \frac{\Theta_b^4}{4!}\in P_{J_b}^4.
 \tag{3.12}
 \label{eq:six-axis-minimal-class}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Only the primes two and three occur in the Smith form
\((1,6,6,6,6)\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:six-axis-local-chart} verifies the
hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:all-degree-graph-saturation} at both primes.
At every other prime the isogeny identifies the integral source and target
lattices; equivalently the marked presentation has only its unit block
\(P=I\), so Theorem~\ref{thm:all-degree-graph-saturation} applies trivially
(or directly by Lemma~\ref{lem:dvr-rank-one}).  Thus the image of each
desired divided power in the finitely
generated quotient \(H^{2k}(J_b,\Z)/P_{J_b}^k\) vanishes after tensoring with
\(\Z_p\) for every prime \(p\), and hence vanishes integrally: a finitely
generated abelian group splits as a free part and a finite part, and both are
detected by these completions.  Finally,
Lemma~\ref{lem:relative-six-axis} gives
\(f_b^*\Theta_b=\lambda_{\mathcal A_b}\).  The principal polarization is
therefore one of the descended graph divisors defining the prescribed
lattice, yielding
\eqref{eq:six-axis-minimal-class}.  Extra Neron--Severi classes at
a special fibre can only enlarge the ordinary product image needed for this
last assertion.
\end{proof}

Voisin identifies the primitive minimal class as the exact obstruction needed
for cubic threefolds.

\begin{corollary}[Universal \(CH_0\)-triviality]
\label{cor:universal-ch0}
\coverage{conditional_deduction}%
\lean{universalCH0_of_sixAxisMinimalClass_and_Voisin}%
Every smooth member of the nonstandard \(A_5\)-cubic pencil is universally
\(CH_0\)-trivial.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:six-axis-divided-powers}, the primitive minimal class of
the intermediate Jacobian is algebraic.  Voisin's Corollary~4.4
\cite{Voisin} says that, for a smooth complex cubic threefold, this is
equivalent to universal \(CH_0\)-triviality.
\end{proof}

Two comparisons place the pencil against the parts of the algebraic locus that
were known before.  Write \(M_{H_1}\) for the coarse-moduli image of the
pencil, as in Hartlieb's notation.  Both comparisons run through one classical
criterion.

\begin{lemma}[Eckardt criterion]
\label{lem:eckardt-rank}
\coverage{fragment}%
\lean{borderedHessian_rank_le_two_iff_borderDivides}%
Let \(X=\{F=0\}\subset\PP^4\) be a smooth cubic threefold and let \(p\in X\).
The tangent hyperplane section \(T_pX\cap X\) is a cone with vertex \(p\) --
that is, \(p\) is an \emph{Eckardt point} of \(X\) -- if and only if the
Hessian matrix of \(F\) at \(p\) has rank at most two.  Every smooth cubic
threefold in either of two classes carries an Eckardt point: those whose
defining form is a sum of cubic forms in pairwise disjoint nonempty groups of
at most three variables, and the members of the family of
\cite[Theorem~3.3]{YYZ}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose coordinates with \(p=[1:0:0:0:0]\) and write \(F=x_0^2L+x_0Q+C\) with
\(L\), \(Q\) and \(C\) in \(x_1,\dots,x_4\).  Smoothness at \(p\) makes \(L\)
nonzero, the tangent hyperplane is \(\{L=0\}\), and the section is a cone with
vertex \(p\) exactly when \(Q\) vanishes on \(\{L=0\}\), that is when
\(Q\in(L)\).  The Hessian of \(F\) at \(p\) is the matrix of the quadric
\(4x_0L+2Q\).  If \(Q=LR\) for a linear form \(R\), that quadric is
\(L(4x_0+2R)\), of rank at most two.  Conversely a quadric of rank at most
two is a product of two linear forms; the coefficient of \(x_0^2\) vanishes,
so at most one factor involves \(x_0\), and if neither did then \(L\) would
vanish.  Comparing the terms
linear in \(x_0\) shows that the other factor is proportional to \(L\), so
\(Q\in(L)\).

Let \(F=\sum_jF_j\) be separated, with the \(j\)-th form in \(n_j\le3\)
variables and \(\sum_jn_j=5\).  Either some \(n_j=2\), or the parts are
\(3,1,1\) or \(1,1,1,1,1\) and at least two of them are singletons.  In the
first case the binary cubic \(F_j\) has a nonzero root, and the corresponding
point of \(\PP^4\), supported on those two variables, lies on \(X\).  In the
second, let \(cx^3\) and \(c'y^3\) be two singleton summands, with \(c\) and
\(c'\) nonzero because \(X\) is smooth, and take the point supported on \(x\)
and \(y\) with coordinates satisfying \(ca^3+c'b^3=0\).  In both cases every
diagonal block of the Hessian belonging to another part vanishes there, since
a cubic form has vanishing Hessian at the origin, so the rank is at most two.

For the family of \cite[Theorem~3.3]{YYZ} work in their coordinates
\(x_1,\dots,x_5\) and take \(p\) to be the point where \(x_1=1\) and the
other four vanish.  It lies on the threefold; every partial derivative of
their form vanishes at \(p\) except the one in \(x_3\), whose value is their
nonzero coefficient \(t_1\), so the tangent hyperplane is \(\{x_3=0\}\); and
the restriction of their form to that hyperplane does not involve \(x_1\), so
the section is a cone with vertex \(p\).
\end{proof}

The general member of the pencil carries no such point.  That is a
computation, recorded in the evidence bundle named in the next proposition: in
two independent models of \(W_5\), the sum-zero subspace of the permutation
module on the six points of \(\PP^1(\F_5)\) over \(\mathbf Q\) and the
monomial model induced from a cubic character of a point stabilizer over
\(\mathbf Q(\zeta_3)\), explicit members are smooth with empty Eckardt
scheme.  The Fermat cubic threefold, which is a member of the pencil and
appears explicitly in the monomial model, returns its thirty Eckardt points in
both rings as a control.

\begin{proposition}[The Eckardt locus of the pencil]
\label{prop:A5-not-coprime}
\coverage{absent}%
\uses{lem:eckardt-rank}%
\evidence{a5-pencil-eckardt, a5-pencil-eckardt-locus}%
Exactly two members of the pencil are smooth cubic threefolds carrying an
Eckardt point: writing the pencil as \(p_3+bT_1\) in the six-point model of
\S\ref{sec:envelope}, they are the two roots of \(b^2+3b+9\), that is
\(b=3\omega\) for a primitive cube root of unity \(\omega\).  Both are
projectively equivalent over \(\C\) to the Fermat cubic threefold, so they
represent a single
point of the coarse-moduli image \(M_{H_1}\).  Every other point of \(M_{H_1}\)
is represented only by smooth cubic threefolds with no Eckardt point; in
particular no other point is represented by a cubic projectively equivalent to a
member of the family of \cite[Theorem~3.3]{YYZ}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(F_b\) be the defining form of \(X_b\).  By
Lemma~\ref{lem:eckardt-rank} a point \(p\) of a smooth \(X_b\) is an Eckardt
point exactly when \(\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{Hess}F_b(p)\le2\), so the
parameters to be found are those for which the ideal generated by \(F_b\) and
the three-by-three minors of its Hessian has a zero away from the origin.
Saturating that ideal by the irrelevant ideal and eliminating the five
coordinates gives \(b(b^2+3b+9)\), and doing the same for the Jacobian ideal
alone gives \(b(b+6)(b^2-3b-9)(7b^2+3b+9)\), whose zero set is the reduced
locus of singular parameters, that is the radical of the pencil's discriminant
rather than the discriminant itself.  Both computations are Gr\"obner
eliminations over \(\mathbf Q\), trusted as executions of a computer algebra
system rather than certificate-checked; the registered evidence bundle records
the replay commands and two independent cross-checks.  They are carried out in
the chart \(a=1\), and the remaining pencil point \((a:b)=(0:1)\) is the cubic
\(T_1\), whose singular locus and Eckardt scheme are both empty.

Elimination computes the ideal of the Zariski closure of the projection, so
this identifies the set of parameters sought only up to closure.  Here the two
agree: the set is constructible by Chevalley's theorem, its closure is finite,
and a constructible subset of the affine line with finite closure is itself
finite, hence closed.

The factor \(b\) common to the two eliminated ideals is the Segre cubic
threefold, which is singular, so it is not a member of \(B^\circ\) and
Lemma~\ref{lem:eckardt-rank} does not apply to it.  The roots of \(b^2+3b+9\)
are prime to that polynomial, so those two members are smooth, and they carry
an Eckardt point.  This proves the first claim.

By Remark~\ref{rem:fermat-in-pencil} the pencil contains the Fermat cubic
threefold, which is smooth and carries Eckardt points, so it is one of these two
members.  The two models of the pencil used there and here present the same
pencil: the invariant cubic space is two-dimensional and \(W_5\) is
irreducible, so any two realizations differ by an \(A_5\)-equivariant linear
isomorphism, unique up to scalar by Schur's lemma.

Both members are defined over \(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})\), and the two roots are
interchanged by the nontrivial element of
\(\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbf Q(\sqrt{-3})/\mathbf Q)\).  Choose an automorphism
\(\sigma\) of \(\C\) extending it, which exists since \(\C\) is algebraically
closed of infinite transcendence degree over \(\mathbf Q\); then \(\sigma\)
carries the first member to the second.  If a variety is projectively
equivalent over \(\C\) to one defined over \(\mathbf Q\), so is its image under
\(\sigma\): apply \(\sigma\) to the coordinate change and use that \(\sigma\)
fixes the \(\mathbf Q\)-form coefficientwise.  Both members are therefore
projectively equivalent to the Fermat cubic threefold over \(\C\), and they
represent the same point of \(M_{H_1}\).  Finally, every member of the family
of \cite[Theorem~3.3]{YYZ}
carries an Eckardt point, again by Lemma~\ref{lem:eckardt-rank}, and carrying
one is invariant under projective equivalence, which gives the last claim.
\uses{lem:eckardt-rank, thm:separation-family}%
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The separated-variable intersection]
\label{prop:A5-nonseparated}
\coverage{absent}%
Exactly one point of \(M_{H_1}\) is
represented by a smooth cubic threefold that is
projectively equivalent to a cubic whose defining form is a sum of cubic
forms in pairwise disjoint nonempty groups of at most three variables, namely
the Fermat point of Proposition~\ref{prop:A5-not-coprime}.
Consequently, every other point of \(M_{H_1}\) is represented
by universally \(CH_0\)-trivial cubic threefolds by
Corollary~\ref{cor:universal-ch0}, none of which is covered by the
separated-variable criterion of Colliot-Th\'el\`ene \cite{CT}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:eckardt-rank} a smooth cubic threefold of
separated-variable type carries an Eckardt point, and carrying one is
invariant under projective equivalence, so the points of \(M_{H_1}\)
represented by such cubics are among those of
Proposition~\ref{prop:A5-not-coprime}, which is the Fermat point alone.  That
point is represented by such a cubic: the Fermat form
\(x_1^3+\cdots+x_5^3\) is a sum of cubic forms in five disjoint groups of one
variable each.  Hence the two loci coincide.  Every remaining point is
represented by a universally \(CH_0\)-trivial cubic threefold by
Corollary~\ref{cor:universal-ch0}, and by no cubic projectively equivalent to
a separated-variable form, so the criterion of \cite{CT} does not apply to
it.
\uses{lem:eckardt-rank, prop:A5-not-coprime, cor:universal-ch0}%
\end{proof}

The order-three signatures give finiteness, though not the exact locus, by a route that uses no
computation.  Fix a primitive cube root of unity \(\zeta_3\).  The signature
of an order-three automorphism of a smooth cubic threefold is the tuple of
exponents of \(\zeta_3\) in a diagonalization of a linear lift of order three;
it is well defined up to permutation of the entries, replacement of
\(\zeta_3\) by \(\zeta_3^2\), and multiplication of the lift by a cube root of
unity \cite[Definition~2.1]{GonzalezAguileraLiendo}.  In a separated-variable
form, as in Proposition~\ref{prop:A5-nonseparated}, the groups of variables
are disjoint, nonempty and of size at most three, and they cover all five
variables, since a cubic threefold whose form omits a variable is singular at
the corresponding coordinate point; as five is not a multiple of three, at
least one group has size one or two.  Multiplying the variables of such a
group by \(\zeta_3\) fixes the form, so such a cubic admits an
order-three automorphism of signature \((0,0,0,0,1)\) or \((0,0,0,1,1)\).
Gonz\'alez-Aguilera--Liendo identify these two signatures as their families
\(T_3^1\) and \(T_3^2\) \cite[Theorem~2.5]{GonzalezAguileraLiendo}.  The union
of the two families therefore contains every point of \(M_{H_1}\) represented
by a cubic projectively equivalent to a separated-variable form.  Each family
is the image in
moduli of \(U^G/N_{\operatorname{GL}_5(\C)}(G)\), for \(U^G\) the locus of
smooth cubics fixed by the relevant group \(G\) and
\(N_{\operatorname{GL}_5(\C)}(G)\) its normalizer, under a finite morphism,
hence closed \cite[Lemmas~2.6--2.7]{Hartlieb}.  The
Klein cubic lies in the pencil \cite[Lemmas~2.6--2.7 and
Proposition~5.7]{Hartlieb}, its automorphism group
\(\operatorname{PSL}(2,\F_{11})\) has a single conjugacy class of elements of
order three, and its signature is \((0,0,1,1,2)\)
\cite[Proposition~2.6]{GonzalezAguileraLiendo}.  The multiplicities of the
eigenvalues \(1,\zeta_3,\zeta_3^2\) are \((2,2,1)\) for that signature and
\((4,1,0)\) and \((3,2,0)\) for the two signatures above.  Permuting the
entries leaves these triples unchanged, multiplying the lift by a cube root of
unity permutes them cyclically, and replacing \(\zeta_3\) by \(\zeta_3^2\)
transposes the last two, so the three moves compare the triples only as
multisets; as \(\{1,2,2\}\), \(\{0,1,4\}\) and \(\{0,2,3\}\) are distinct, the
Klein cubic lies in neither family.  Some \(b\in B^\circ\) has \(X_b\)
projectively equivalent to the Klein cubic, by the membership just recorded,
and \(B^\circ\) is a nonempty open subset of a \(\PP^1\), hence an irreducible
curve.  The preimage in \(B^\circ\) of each of the two loci under the map to
moduli is closed and omits that \(b\), so it is a proper closed subset of
\(B^\circ\) and therefore finite.  Since \(M_{H_1}\) is the image of
\(B^\circ\), each locus meets \(M_{H_1}\) in a finite set.  The finiteness of the
separated-variable locus therefore does not depend on the registered
computation; identifying that finite set as the single Fermat point, which is
what Propositions~\ref{prop:A5-not-coprime} and~\ref{prop:A5-nonseparated}
assert, does.

Every moduli point of the pencil but the Fermat point escapes the
separated-variable mechanism of \cite{CT} by
Proposition~\ref{prop:A5-nonseparated} and the explicit family of \cite{YYZ}
by Proposition~\ref{prop:A5-not-coprime}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:no-elliptic-product} closes the product route into
Voisin's components.  None of them asserts that the \(A_5\)-curve is disjoint
from every known part of Voisin's algebraic-minimal-class locus; the two
questions left open there are recorded after
Proposition~\ref{prop:no-elliptic-product}.

The argument is fibrewise.  It constructs neither a horizontal minimal cycle
over the pencil nor a relative decomposition of the diagonal.  Neither is
needed for Corollary~\ref{cor:universal-ch0}.
