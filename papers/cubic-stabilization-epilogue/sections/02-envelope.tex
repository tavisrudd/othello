\section{The six-axis polarization and the exotic packet}
\label{sec:envelope}

Let \(\Omega\) be the six conjugate \(D_5\)-subgroups of \(A_5\), equivalently
the six Sylow-\(5\) subgroups with their normalizers, and put
\[
 H_p=\operatorname{Aug}(\F_p^\Omega)/\langle\mathbf1\rangle
 \qquad(p=2,3).
\]
The modules \(H_2,H_3\) are simple, with
\(\End_{A_5}(H_2)=\F_4\) and \(\End_{A_5}(H_3)=\F_3\)
\cite[the \(A_5\) matrix-representation rows of dimension four over
\(\operatorname{GF}(2)\), reducible over \(\operatorname{GF}(4)\), and
dimension four over \(\operatorname{GF}(3)\)]{AtlasBrauer}.

The six axes arise from the Fano surface.  Let \(S\) be the
Fano surface of a smooth member of the \(A_5\)-pencil and
\((J,\Theta)=\Alb(S)\) its intermediate Jacobian.  Each of the six
\(D_5\)-subgroups gives an elliptic quotient \(q_H:J\to E_H\) and its Rosati
dual \(i_H:E_H\to J\).

The elliptic factor at the geometric generic point is non-CM.  Indeed, a CM
endomorphism there would place its \(j\)-invariant in the zero-dimensional CM
locus, so after a finite base change the elliptic factor would be isotrivial.
Hartlieb's isogeny \(J_b\sim E_b^5\) would then put the whole
intermediate-Jacobian family in the countable isogeny class of one fixed
abelian variety.  The image of an algebraic curve in a moduli space cannot be
both positive-dimensional and countable.  This contradicts the fact that the
closure of the intermediate-Jacobian image is a one-dimensional special
subvariety \cite[Proposition~5.7 and Remark~5.8]{Hartlieb}.

\begin{proposition}[Six-axis polarization]
\label{prop:six-axis-polarization}
After coherent identification of the elliptic factors, the Rosati Gram
matrix of the six maps is
\[
 G_6=6I_6-J_6.
\]
Any five axes therefore give an isogeny \(E^5\to J\) whose pulled-back
polarization has Smith type \((1,6,6,6,6)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We recall the numerical passage from Roulleau's genus-two configuration to
the six elliptic fibres.  Let \(D_g\subset S\) be the curve indexed by an
involution \(g\in A_5\), put \(D_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_gD_g\), and, for a
dihedral subgroup \(H\) of order ten, put
\[
 F_H=\sum_{g\in H,\;g^2=1}D_g.
\]
Roulleau proves that \(F_H\) is a fibre of \(S\to E_H\)
\cite[Theorem~11(D)]{Roulleau}, so \(F_H^2=0\).  His intersection table has
\(D_g^2=-4\), and distinct curves meet with multiplicity zero, two, or one
when the product of their labels has order two, three, or five, respectively;
Lemma~14 proves that these values are constant in the corresponding families
\cite[Lemma~14, Corollary~16, and (3.1)]{Roulleau}.  Among
the \(105\) unordered pairs of involutions, the product orders two, three,
and five occur \(15,30,60\) times.  Hence
\[
 D_{\mathrm{tot}}^2
 =15(-4)+2(30\cdot2+60\cdot1)=180.
\]
Every involution belongs to two of the six dihedral subgroups, and therefore
\(\sum_HF_H=2D_{\mathrm{tot}}\).  Two-transitivity on the six subgroups now
gives
\[
 30(F_H\cdot F_{H'})=(2D_{\mathrm{tot}})^2=720,
 \qquad F_H\cdot F_{H'}=24\quad(H\ne H').
\]

After coherent identification of the generic elliptic quotients,
two-transitivity makes the Rosati matrix have diagonal \(d\) and
off-diagonal \(m\).  Put
\[
 D_H=q_H^*[0]\in\NS(J),\qquad N_H=i_Hq_H\in\End(J).
\]
Then \(D_H|_S=F_H\), and \(N_H\) is the Rosati endomorphism attached to
\(D_H\).  Coherent identification gives \(q_Hi_H=[d]\) and
\(q_Hi_{H'}=[m]\) for \(H\ne H'\).  The invariant sum of the six axis maps
vanishes because the five-dimensional constituent has no trivial summand;
composing with \(q_H\) gives \(d+5m=0\).  The minimal-class identity
\([S]=\Theta^3/3!\) \cite{CG}, together with the polarized
Riemann--Roch trace formula
\[
 \frac{\alpha\beta\Theta^{g-2}}{(g-2)!}
 =\operatorname{tr}(N_\alpha)\operatorname{tr}(N_\beta)
  -\operatorname{tr}(N_\alpha N_\beta),
\]
obtained by polarizing the reduced-norm Riemann--Roch polynomial
\cite[Theorem~4.1]{Grieve}, gives the following identities.  Here
\(\operatorname{tr}\) is the degree-five trace in the coefficient
representation---the trace entering that reduced norm---rather than the
degree-ten trace on rational first homology:
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(N_H)=d,
 \qquad \operatorname{tr}(N_HN_{H'})=m^2,
 \qquad F_HF_{H'}=d^2-m^2.
\]
Thus \(d^2-m^2=24\).  Together with \(d+5m=0\) and \(d>0\), this forces
\((d,m)=(5,-1)\), hence \(G_6=6I-J\).  A five-by-five minor has Smith form
\((1,6,6,6,6)\).
\end{proof}

Let \(B^\circ\) be the connected smooth locus of the nonstandard
\(A_5\)-pencil, and let \(\mathcal J\to B^\circ\) be its relative
intermediate Jacobian.

\begin{lemma}[Relative six-axis source]
\label{lem:relative-six-axis}
There are an elliptic scheme \(\mathcal E\to B^\circ\) and a relative
isogeny
\[
 f:\mathcal A:=
 \mathcal E\otimes_{\mathbf Z}(\mathbf Z^\Omega/\mathbf Z\mathbf1)
 \longrightarrow\mathcal J
\]
which is \(A_5\)-equivariant.  The quotient lattice carries the form induced
by \(6I_6-J_6\); both the source and this form carry their natural
\(S_6\)-action.  Moreover,
\[
 f^*\lambda_\Theta=\lambda_{\mathcal A}.
 \tag{2.1}
 \label{eq:six-axis-polarization-pullback}
\]
After omitting one axis, \(\mathcal A\simeq\mathcal E^5\) and this form has
matrix \(6I_5-J_5\).
For \(p=2,3\), put \(\mathscr V_p=\mathcal E[p]\).  The \(p\)-primary
kernel of the source polarization has a natural \(A_5\)-equivariant
symplectic description
\[
 \mathscr D_p\simeq H_p\otimes_{\F_p}\mathscr V_p
 \qquad(p=2,3).
 \tag{2.2}
 \label{eq:primary-discriminants}
\]
The \(p\)-primary part \(\mathscr K_p\) of \(\ker f\) is an
\(A_5\)-stable relative maximal isotropic subgroup of \(\mathscr D_p\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For \(H\in\Omega\), the integral norm
\(n_H=\sum_{h\in H}h\) is a relative endomorphism of \(\mathcal J\).  Its
action on rational first homology is ten times the projector onto the
\(H\)-fixed summand.  Fibrewise, Hartlieb's character calculation and
isogeny decomposition give
\(H^1(J,\mathbf Q)\simeq W_5\otimes H^1(E,\mathbf Q)\), and hence the
self-product of an elliptic curve through the five-dimensional module
\(W_5\) \cite[Lemma~5.5 and Remark~5.8]{Hartlieb}.  Since
\(\dim W_5^H=1\), the fixed summand has rank two.  More concretely,
\(n_H^2=10n_H\).  The scheme-theoretic image of a homomorphism of abelian
schemes in characteristic zero is an abelian subscheme, and its quotient
factorization commutes with base change
\cite[Theorem~A]{ACMW}.  Thus the scheme-theoretic image
\(\mathcal E_H\subset\mathcal J\) is an abelian scheme of relative dimension
one.  It is therefore an elliptic scheme.  Since \(hn_H=n_H\) for every
\(h\in H\), the subgroup \(H\) acts trivially on \(\mathcal E_H\).  Moreover
\(N_{A_5}(H)=H\), so transport by an element carrying \(H\) to \(H'\) is
independent of the chosen transporter.  The six elliptic schemes are
therefore coherently isomorphic; fix one of them as \(\mathcal E\).

Write \(i_H:\mathcal E\to\mathcal J\) for the transported primitive
inclusions.  On the geometric generic fibre, Hartlieb's isogeny decomposition
identifies \(\operatorname{Hom}^0(E,J)\) with the irreducible module \(W_5\).
The coherently transported maps \((i_H)_{H\in\Omega}\) form one \(A_5\)-orbit,
so their sum is invariant.  Since \(W_5^{A_5}=0\), that sum is zero.  The
connected smooth curve \(B^\circ\) is integral, \(\mathcal E\) is integral,
and \(\mathcal J\) is separated.  Equality on the generic fibre therefore
gives \(\sum_Hi_H=0\) over \(B^\circ\).  Hence
\((i_H)_{H\in\Omega}\) descends from
\(\mathcal E^\Omega\) to the displayed map
\(f:\mathcal A\to\mathcal J\), and the construction is
\(A_5\)-equivariant.

Roulleau constructs the \(D_5\)-fibration from an Albanese quotient
\(q_H:J\to E_H\) by a connected four-dimensional abelian subvariety
\cite[Lemma~17 and the following proof of Theorem~11(D)]{Roulleau}.  Thus
\(F_H=q_H^*[0]|_S\).  The connected kernel makes the Rosati dual of \(q_H\) a
primitive inclusion, and its image is the unique \(H\)-fixed elliptic
subvariety.  It therefore agrees, up to an automorphism of the target
elliptic curve, with the norm image \(\mathcal E_H\hookrightarrow\mathcal J\).
Consequently the fibre classes in
Proposition~\ref{prop:six-axis-polarization} compute these relative
homomorphisms, rather than merely isogenous quotients.  On the
generic non-CM fibre they give diagonal entry \(5\) and off-diagonal entry
\(-1\).  Applying the same generic-density and separatedness argument to
these homomorphisms gives the identities over \(B^\circ\), hence
\eqref{eq:six-axis-polarization-pullback}.
Choosing any five coordinate classes identifies the quotient lattice with
\(\mathbf Z^5\), where its matrix is \(6I_5-J_5\).  Since this has Smith type
\((1,6,6,6,6)\), the source polarization is nondegenerate, so \(f\) is a
finite flat isogeny.  From
\(\lambda_{\mathcal A}=f^\vee\lambda_\Theta f\) one gets
\[
 |\ker\lambda_{\mathcal A}|=6^8,
 \qquad |\ker f|=\deg f=6^4.
\]
For \(x,y\in\ker f\), functoriality of the polarization commutator pairing
gives
\(e_{\lambda_{\mathcal A}}(x,y)=e_{\lambda_\Theta}(fx,fy)=1\).
Thus \(\ker f\) is isotropic and has square-root order in
\(\ker\lambda_{\mathcal A}\), hence is maximal isotropic.  Its
\(p\)-primary part has fibrewise order \(p^4\) for \(p=2,3\).

For \(p=2,3\), the primary discriminant local system is
\[
 \mathscr D_p=
 \ker\bigl(6I_6-J_6:\mathcal A[p]\longrightarrow\mathcal A^\vee[p]\bigr).
\]
Modulo \(p\), the radical quotient of the induced coefficient form is
canonically
\[
 \operatorname{Aug}(\mathbf F_p^\Omega)/\langle\mathbf1\rangle=H_p.
\]
The form on this coefficient discriminant is the normalized form
\[
 b_p(\bar x,\bar y)
 =\frac1p x^{\mathsf t}(6I_6-J_6)y\pmod p
 =
 \begin{cases}
  \bar x\mathbin{\boldsymbol\cdot}\bar y,&p=2,\\
  -\bar x\mathbin{\boldsymbol\cdot}\bar y,&p=3.
 \end{cases}
 \tag{2.3}
 \label{eq:normalized-primary-pairings}
\]
For augmentation lifts, the product of the coordinate sums is divisible by
\(p^2\), which proves the displayed formulas.  They are unchanged by adding
the constant vector or \(p\) times an integral vector.  The dot product on
the augmentation module has radical exactly the constant line, so \(b_p\)
is nondegenerate on \(H_p\); \(b_2\) is alternating and \(b_3\) is symmetric.
Tensoring with the Weil pairing on \(\mathscr V_p\) gives
\eqref{eq:primary-discriminants} as a symplectic isomorphism.  Since \(f\)
is \(A_5\)-equivariant, each \(\mathscr K_p\) is \(A_5\)-stable.  The
preceding order and isotropy calculation makes it a relative maximal
isotropic subgroup of this rank-eight local system.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Principal gluing packet]
\label{prop:principal-gluing-packet}
The \(A_5\)-stable two-primary principal kernels of the six-axis source are
parametrized by
\[
 \PP^1(\F_4)=\PP^1(\F_2)\sqcup\{\omega,\omega^2\}.
\]
The three \(\F_2\)-rational kernels are \(S_6\)-stable.  Each of the other
two has stabilizer exactly \(A_5\), and the outer normalizer exchanges them.
The intermediate Jacobian of the nonstandard \(A_5\)-cubic component uses
the latter pair.  The \(A_5\)-stable three-primary kernels form
\(\PP^1(\F_3)\); after choosing a complementary symplectic ruling, each is a
scalar graph.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At two the coefficient discriminant is the simple four-dimensional
\(\F_2A_5\)-module \(H_2\), whose commutant is \(\F_4\).  The full
discriminant is the semisimple module
\(H_2\otimes_{\F_2}\F_2^2\simeq H_2^{\oplus2}\).  Every stable half is
therefore one copy of \(H_2\), and Schur's lemma identifies such submodules
with the one-dimensional \(\F_4\)-subspaces of \(\F_4^2\).  This gives
\(\PP^1(\F_4)\).

Here is the polarization check explicitly.  Identify the coefficient heart
with the natural two-dimensional \(\F_4\)-module.  The trace-determinant form
is a nonzero invariant alternating form.  Relative to it, the normalized
form \(b_2\) of \eqref{eq:normalized-primary-pairings} differs by a nonzero
scalar in \(\F_4\); rescaling an \(\F_4\)-basis absorbs that scalar because
squaring permutes \(\F_4^\times\).  We may therefore put
\[
 b(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\F_4/\F_2}\det(x,y).
\]
On two multiplicity copies use
\[
 \Omega((x,y),(x',y'))=b(x,y')+b(y,x').
\]
Every scalar \(a\in\F_4\) is self-adjoint for \(b\), since
\(b(ax,y)=b(x,ay)\).  Thus the vertical line and all four graphs
\(\Gamma_a\) are isotropic of half dimension.  The five module-theoretic
possibilities are exactly the five polarized gluings; no further quadratic
condition removes the exotic pair.

The simplex group acts on both multiplicity copies of the coefficient heart
over \(\F_2\).  Hence the three lines defined over \(\F_2\) retain \(S_6\).
The stabilizer of an exotic graph is equally intrinsic.  A permutation that
fixes \(\Gamma_\omega\) commutes with \(\omega\), hence is \(\F_4\)-linear on
the two-dimensional \(\F_4\)-space \(H_2\).  If its determinant is
\(d\in\F_4^\times\), preservation of \(b\) says
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_{\F_4/\F_2}(dz)
 =\operatorname{Tr}_{\F_4/\F_2}(z)
 \qquad(z\in\F_4).
\]
The trace pairing is nondegenerate, so \(d=1\).  It therefore lies in
\(\operatorname{SL}_2(4)\), of order \(60\).  The given \(A_5\) already has
that order and lies in the stabilizer, proving equality.  The odd coset in
its order-\(120\) normalizer is semilinear and applies field Frobenius, so it
exchanges \(\omega\) and \(\omega^2\).

To pass from the generic fibre to the family, put
\[
 \mathscr P=\PP_{\F_4}(\F_4\otimes_{\F_2}\mathscr V_2).
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:relative-six-axis}, this is the five-sheet local system of
\(A_5\)-stable principal halves.  It has the degree-three sub-local system
\(\mathscr P_{\rm rat}=\PP_{\F_2}(\mathscr V_2)\) and the degree-two
complement \(\mathscr P_{\rm ex}\).  Here \(H_2\), together with its
commutant \(\F_4\), is constant, while monodromy acts only on
\(\mathscr V_2\).  Its action is therefore \(\F_4\)-linear, not semilinear,
and factors through \(\operatorname{GL}_2(\F_2)\); in particular it
preserves both subsets.  The relative kernel \(\mathscr K_2\) gives a
section of \(\mathscr P\).

At the geometric generic point this section cannot be rational.  Otherwise
the natural faithful \(S_6\)-action on
\(\mathcal A=\mathcal E\otimes(\mathbf Z^\Omega/\mathbf Z\mathbf1)\), which
is the identity on \(\mathcal E\) tensored with the faithful permutation
action on the quotient lattice, would preserve the rational kernel: under
\eqref{eq:primary-discriminants} it acts on \(H_2\) and trivially on
\(\mathscr V_2\), so every member of
\(\mathscr P_{\rm rat}\) is \(S_6\)-stable.  At three, the analogous
coefficient heart \(H_3\) is simple and
\(\End_{A_5}(H_3)=\F_3\).  The semisimple module
\(H_3^{\oplus2}\) has stable halves parametrized by \(\PP^1(\F_3)\).
The tensor pairing is \(b_3\) times the alternating multiplicity pairing, so
the three affine scalar graphs and the vertical line are all maximal
isotropic.  The natural \(S_6\)-action is the same on both multiplicity
copies, so it fixes every one of these four lines.  Since \(|\ker f|=6^4\),
its two- and
three-primary parts exhaust the kernel.  The full kernel would therefore be
\(S_6\)-stable.  Since this action preserves \(6I_6-J_6\), it would descend
through the principally polarized quotient.  It would act faithfully: if a
source automorphism acts
trivially on the quotient, the image of its difference from the identity is
both connected and contained in the finite isogeny kernel, hence zero.
Strong Torelli gives an exact sequence \cite[Theorem~3.1]{Hartlieb}
\[
 1\longrightarrow\{\pm1\}\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Aut}(J,\Theta)\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Aut}(X)\longrightarrow1.
\]
The kernel of the resulting map from \(S_6\) to
\(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\) would be a normal subgroup of order at most two;
it is trivial.  Thus \(S_6\) would act faithfully on a smooth cubic
threefold.  This contradicts the classification of faithful automorphism
groups \cite[Theorem~2.1]{Hartlieb}.  Five of the six listed ambient groups
have order smaller than \(|S_6|=720\).  The remaining group is
\((\mathbf Z/3\mathbf Z)^4\rtimes S_5\), of order \(9720\), which is not
divisible by \(720\).  None can contain \(S_6\).  The section is
therefore generically in \(\mathscr P_{\rm ex}\).  Its inverse image is open
and closed in the connected base \(B^\circ\), so every smooth fibre has
exotic two-primary kernel.
The same calculation at three gives the four lines parametrized by
\(\PP^1(\F_3)\); choosing any complementary line as the vertical ruling
makes the selected half an affine scalar graph.
\end{proof}

For the cycle-theoretic argument, the unordered pair is enough.  Both members
act on the heart with the same squarefree minimal polynomial
\(t^2+t+1\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:six-axis-divided-powers} uses only the
conclusion that the geometric kernel is not one of the three
\(\F_2\)-rational gluings.  At three it uses only scalarity of the possible
\(A_5\)-stable graphs, not the finer monodromy selection.

Hartlieb proves that the closure of its intermediate-Jacobian image is a
one-dimensional special subvariety \cite[Proposition~5.7]{Hartlieb}.  Since the cubic period
map is a locally closed embedding, the resulting smooth family is
non-isotrivial.  Only this landing and nonconstancy are used below; no
identification of compactified parameter curves is needed for the separation
theorem.
