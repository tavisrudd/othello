\section{The integral envelope and the exotic cubic sheet}
\label{sec:envelope}

Let \(\Omega\) be the six conjugate \(D_5\)-subgroups of \(A_5\), equivalently
the six Sylow-\(5\) subgroups with their normalizers.  Put
\[
 A(\Omega)=\ker\bigl(\Z^\Omega\xrightarrow{\sum}\Z\bigr),
 \qquad A(\Omega)^\vee=\Hom(A(\Omega),\Z).
\]
The perfect evaluation pairing defines a unimodular alternating form on
\[
 \mathcal H_{\mathrm{ref}}(\Omega)
   =A(\Omega)^\vee e\oplus A(\Omega)f,
 \qquad
 \langle xe,yf\rangle=y(x).
\]
This is the hyperbolic symplectic envelope of the oriented six-set.

\begin{proposition}[Universal envelope]
\label{prop:universal-envelope}
The construction \(\Omega\mapsto\mathcal H_{\mathrm{ref}}(\Omega)\) is
functorial for permutations of \(\Omega\).  Rationally it is the unique
rank-ten symplectic \(A_5\)-module whose two Lagrangians are copies of the
standard five-dimensional constituent and whose pairing restricts to the
root--weight evaluation pairing.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The permutation lattice and its augmentation kernel are functorial.  The
evaluation pairing between \(A(\Omega)\) and its dual is perfect, so the
displayed alternating form is unimodular.  Over \(\Q\), the augmentation
module is absolutely irreducible and its invariant symmetric form is unique
up to scale.  Schur's lemma therefore writes every symplectic module with
two such constituents as \(A(\Omega)_\Q\otimes U\), where \(U\) is a
symplectic plane.  Requiring the two integral Lagrangians to pair perfectly
fixes the root--weight lattice above.
\end{proof}

The finite golden orientation enters at the prime two.  Let
\[
 H_2=\operatorname{Aug}(\F_2^\Omega)/\langle\mathbf1\rangle.
\]
Then \(H_2\) is simple and \(\End_{A_5}(H_2)=\F_4\).  A normalized conference
operator acts integrally on \(\Z^\Omega\).  After labeling \(\Omega\), one
switching representative is
\[
B=\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&1&1&1&1\\
1&0&1&1&-1&-1\\
1&1&0&-1&1&-1\\
1&1&-1&0&-1&1\\
1&-1&1&-1&0&1\\
1&-1&-1&1&1&0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad B^2=5I.
\]
Passing to
the canonical saturation
\[
 D_6^\vee=\Z^\Omega+\Z\frac{\mathbf1}{2},
 \qquad \varphi=\frac{I+B}{2},
\]
gives \(\varphi^2-\varphi-1=0\).  Its reduction on the commutator heart is
one of the two primitive scalars \(\omega,\omega^2\in\F_4\), and replacing
\(B\) by \(-B\) applies Frobenius.

\begin{proposition}[Golden selection of the exotic pair]
\label{prop:golden-exotic}
The two orientations of the conference class determine the two primitive
scalars in \(\End_{A_5}(H_2)=\F_4\).  Under orientation reversal,
\[
 B\longmapsto-B,
 \qquad \omega\longmapsto\omega^2.
\]
Thus the golden-orientation torsor is canonically the Frobenius torsor of
the exotic two-primary principal gluings.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every column of \((I+B)/2\) has the same half-integral class modulo
\(\Z^\Omega\), so \(D_6^\vee\) is the least stable over-lattice.  The
commutator submodule of \(D_6^\vee/2D_6^\vee\) is the four-dimensional
six-point heart and its quotient is a trivial \(\F_4\)-line.  The extension
has no invariant \(\F_4\)-line, so the restriction of \(\bar\varphi\) to the
heart is primitive.  Finally \((I-B)/2=1-\varphi\), and in characteristic
two \(1-\omega=\omega^2\).
\end{proof}

We now recall the geometric source of the same lattice.  Let \(S\) be the
Fano surface of a smooth member of the \(A_5\)-pencil and
\((J,\Theta)=\Alb(S)\) its intermediate Jacobian.  Each of the six
\(D_5\)-subgroups gives an elliptic quotient \(q_H:J\to E_H\) and its Rosati
dual \(i_H:E_H\to J\).

\begin{proposition}[Six-axis polarization]
\label{prop:six-axis-polarization}
After coherent identification of the elliptic factors, the Rosati Gram
matrix of the six maps is
\[
 G_6=6I_6-J_6.
\]
Any five axes therefore give an isogeny \(E^5\to J\) whose pulled-back
polarization has Smith type \((1,6,6,6,6)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We recall the numerical passage from Roulleau's genus-two configuration to
the six elliptic fibres.  Let \(D_g\subset S\) be the curve indexed by an
involution \(g\in A_5\), put \(D_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_gD_g\), and, for a
dihedral subgroup \(H\) of order ten, put
\[
 F_H=\sum_{g\in H,\;g^2=1}D_g.
\]
Roulleau proves that \(F_H\) is a fibre of \(S\to E_H\)
\cite[Theorem~11(D)]{Roulleau}, so \(F_H^2=0\).  His intersection table has
\(D_g^2=-4\), and distinct curves meet with multiplicity two or one when
the product of their labels has order three or five, respectively.  Among
the \(105\) unordered pairs of involutions, the product orders two, three,
and five occur \(15,30,60\) times.  Hence
\[
 D_{\mathrm{tot}}^2
 =15(-4)+2(30\cdot2+60\cdot1)=180.
\]
Every involution belongs to two of the six dihedral subgroups, and therefore
\(\sum_HF_H=2D_{\mathrm{tot}}\).  Two-transitivity on the six subgroups now
gives
\[
 30(F_H\cdot F_{H'})=(2D_{\mathrm{tot}})^2=720,
 \qquad F_H\cdot F_{H'}=24\quad(H\ne H').
\]

After coherent identification of the generic non-CM elliptic quotients,
two-transitivity makes the Rosati matrix have diagonal \(d\) and
off-diagonal \(m\).  Put
\[
 D_H=q_H^*[0]\in\NS(J),\qquad N_H=i_Hq_H\in\End(J).
\]
Then \(D_H|_S=F_H\), and \(N_H\) is the Rosati endomorphism attached to
\(D_H\).  Coherent identification gives \(q_Hi_H=[d]\) and
\(q_Hi_{H'}=[m]\) for \(H\ne H'\).  The invariant sum of the six axis maps
vanishes because the five-dimensional constituent has no trivial summand;
composing with \(q_H\) gives \(d+5m=0\).  The minimal-class identity
\([S]=\Theta^3/3!\), together with the polarized Riemann--Roch trace formula
\[
 \frac{\alpha\beta\Theta^{g-2}}{(g-2)!}
 =\operatorname{tr}(N_\alpha)\operatorname{tr}(N_\beta)
  -\operatorname{tr}(N_\alpha N_\beta),
\]
obtained by polarizing the reduced-norm Riemann--Roch polynomial
\cite[Theorem~4.1]{Grieve},
gives
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(N_H)=d,
 \qquad \operatorname{tr}(N_HN_{H'})=m^2,
 \qquad F_HF_{H'}=d^2-m^2.
\]
Thus \(d^2-m^2=24\).  The positive solution is
\((d,m)=(5,-1)\), hence \(G_6=6I-J\).  A five-by-five minor has Smith form
\((1,6,6,6,6)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Principal gluing packet]
\label{prop:principal-gluing-packet}
The \(A_5\)-stable two-primary principal kernels of the six-axis source are
parametrized by
\[
 \PP^1(\F_4)=\PP^1(\F_2)\sqcup\{\omega,\omega^2\}.
\]
The three \(\F_2\)-rational kernels are \(S_6\)-stable.  Each of the other
two has stabilizer exactly \(A_5\), and the outer normalizer exchanges them.
The intermediate Jacobian of the nonstandard \(A_5\)-cubic component uses
the latter pair.  Every \(A_5\)-stable three-primary kernel is a scalar graph;
the \(\Gamma_0(3)\) multiplicity monodromy selects one of them.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At two the coefficient discriminant is the simple four-dimensional
\(\F_2A_5\)-module \(H_2\), whose commutant is \(\F_4\).  The full
discriminant is \(H_2\otimes_{\F_2}\F_2^2\).  An \(A_5\)-stable simple half
is therefore a one-dimensional \(\F_4\)-subspace of \(\F_4^2\), and every
such half is maximal isotropic for the tensor product of the coefficient
form with the alternating multiplicity form.  This gives
\(\PP^1(\F_4)\).

Here is the polarization check explicitly.  Identify the coefficient heart
with \(\F_4^2\) and put
\[
 b(x,y)=\operatorname{Tr}_{\F_4/\F_2}\det(x,y).
\]
On two multiplicity copies use
\[
 \Omega((x,y),(x',y'))=b(x,y')+b(y,x').
\]
Every scalar \(a\in\F_4\) is self-adjoint for \(b\), since
\(b(ax,y)=b(x,ay)\).  Thus the vertical line and all four graphs
\(\Gamma_a\) are isotropic of half dimension.  The five module-theoretic
possibilities are exactly the five polarized gluings; no further quadratic
condition removes the exotic pair.

The simplex group acts on both multiplicity copies of the coefficient heart
over \(\F_2\).  Hence the three lines defined over \(\F_2\) retain \(S_6\).
The stabilizer of an exotic graph is equally intrinsic.  A permutation that
fixes \(\Gamma_\omega\) commutes with \(\omega\), hence is \(\F_4\)-linear on
the two-dimensional \(\F_4\)-space \(H_2\).  If its determinant is
\(d\in\F_4^\times\), preservation of \(b\) says
\[
 \operatorname{Tr}_{\F_4/\F_2}(dz)
 =\operatorname{Tr}_{\F_4/\F_2}(z)
 \qquad(z\in\F_4).
\]
The trace pairing is nondegenerate, so \(d=1\).  It therefore lies in
\(\operatorname{SL}_2(4)\), of order \(60\).  The given \(A_5\) already has
that order and lies in the stabilizer, proving equality.  The odd coset in
its order-\(120\) normalizer is semilinear and applies field Frobenius, so it
exchanges \(\omega\) and \(\omega^2\).  Were the generic cubic kernel
\(\F_2\)-rational, its principal polarization would inherit the faithful
\(S_6\)-action.  Strong Torelli identifies
\(\operatorname{Aut}(J,\Theta)\) with
\(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\times\langle-1\rangle\).  Projection of this
\(S_6\) to \(\operatorname{Aut}(X)\) remains faithful: its kernel would be a
normal subgroup of \(S_6\) of order at most two, and no such nontrivial
subgroup exists.  This contradicts the generic automorphism group \(A_5\) on
the nonstandard component \cite[Section~5]{Hartlieb}.  Thus the generic
kernel is exotic.
The kernel is a finite
sub-local-system, so the same pair persists throughout the connected smooth
locus.

At three the coefficient heart \(H_3\) is simple and
\(\End_{A_5}(H_3)=\F_3\).  Hence its \(A_5\)-stable halves are the four graphs
parametrized by \(\PP^1(\F_3)\), all scalar in a suitable graph chart.  If
the family monodromy is retained, reduction of
\(\Gamma_0(3)\) modulo three is the upper-triangular Borel in
\(\operatorname{SL}_2(\F_3)\); it fixes exactly the line spanned by the first
basis vector.  Tensoring this unique invariant multiplicity line with
\(H_3\) gives the unique \(A_5\)- and monodromy-stable maximal isotropic half.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:golden-exotic} labels the two exotic members by the
finite golden orientation.

For the cycle-theoretic argument, the unordered pair is enough.  Both members
act on the heart with the same squarefree minimal polynomial
\(t^2+t+1\).  The golden orientation supplies the finer marked identification
with one member of the pair, but Theorem~\ref{thm:exotic-minimal-class} uses
only the conclusion that the geometric kernel is not one of the three
\(\F_2\)-rational gluings.  At three it uses only scalarity of the possible
\(A_5\)-stable graphs, not the finer monodromy selection.

Hartlieb proves that its intermediate-Jacobian image is a one-dimensional
special subvariety \cite[Proposition~5.7]{Hartlieb}.  Since the cubic period
map is a locally closed embedding, the resulting smooth family is
non-isotrivial.  Only this landing and nonconstancy are used below; no
identification of compactified parameter curves is needed for the separation
theorem.
