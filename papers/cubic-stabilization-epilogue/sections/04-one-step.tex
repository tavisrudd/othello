\section{The primitive-sixth-root atom}
\label{sec:one-step}

We use the geometric atomic \(F\)-bundle invariant of
Katzarkov--Kontsevich--Pantev--Yu \cite{KKPYY}.  Only three features enter:
its values lie in the free abelian group on atom-equivalence classes, it is
additive under blowups, and a rank-\(r\) projective bundle contributes \(r\)
copies of the base.

Concretely, the reduced unramified spectral cover of the Euler operator
decomposes the maximal big-quantum \(F\)-bundle into connected local atoms
\cite[Theorem~4.1 and Definition~5.10]{KKPYY}.  If \(\mathscr A\) denotes the
set of geometric atom classes, its chemical formula has the form
\[
 \CFF(Z)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathscr A}m_\alpha(Z)[\alpha]
 \in\Z^{(\mathscr A)},\qquad m_\alpha(Z)\ge0.
\]
The free group, rather than a quotient of a signed block ledger, is the
target used below.

\begin{definition}
For a geometric atom \(\alpha\), restrict its connection to the original
formal punctured \(u\)-disc.  Let \(M_{\mathrm f}(\alpha)\) be its intrinsic
formal-monodromy operator.  Equivalently, after a minimal
Levelt--Turrittin ramification, combine the regular-singular monodromies with
the deck permutation of the exponential factors and descend to the original
\(u\)-disc.  Let \(\FM(\alpha)\) be the eigenvalue multiset of this descended
operator, and define
\[
 \nu_6(\alpha)=
 \operatorname{mult}_{e^{\pi i/3}}\FM(\alpha)
 +\operatorname{mult}_{e^{-\pi i/3}}\FM(\alpha).
\]
Extend \(\nu_6\) additively to the free atom group.
\end{definition}

This definition uses eigenvalues in \(\C^\times\), not chosen logarithms.
It is independent of a further ramified refinement: the refined deck cycle
and the refined regular monodromy compose to the same descended operator on
the original differential module.  Integral gauge powers shift residue
exponents by integers and leave the operator unchanged up to conjugacy; see
\cite[Section~3.2]{vdPS} and \cite[Remark~5]{Cai} for the universal formal
solution ring and formal differential-module classification.

\begin{lemma}[Formal isomonodromy]
\label{lem:formal-isomonodromy}
Along a connected component of the reduced unramified spectral cover, the
formal-monodromy eigenvalue multiset of an atom is constant.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We use flatness in all bulk directions, rather than only the Witt-leaf
statement of \cite[Remark~3.14]{KKPYY}.  Work locally on the analytic base
and choose coordinates \(t_i\).  The big quantum connection has commuting
operators
\[
 \nabla_{t_i}=\partial_{t_i}+u^{-1}P_i,
 \qquad
 \nabla_u=\partial_u+L.
\]
Solve
\[
 \partial_{t_i}M=-u^{-1}P_iM,
 \qquad M|_{t=t_0}=I.
\]
The compatibility equations are precisely the flatness equations for the
\(t_i\)-directions, so the solution exists in the enlarged \(t\)-adic ring
used by Cai, locally of the form
\(\operatorname{GL}(H\otimes\mathscr K((u))[[t-t_0]])\), where
\(\mathscr K\) contains the
Novikov coefficients.  This is not an ordinary Laurent gauge over the
analytic base: its \((t-t_0)\)-coefficients may have negative \(u\)-orders
unbounded below.  Each coefficient nevertheless involves only integral
powers of \(u\).  Consequently
\[
 M^{-1}\nabla_{t_i}M=\partial_{t_i}.
\]
Commutation with \(M^{-1}\nabla_uM=\partial_u+\widetilde L\) now gives
\(\partial_{t_i}\widetilde L=0\) for every \(i\).  This is the bulk-gauge
argument used by Cai immediately before \cite[Proposition~6]{Cai}.

After this scalar extension, the resulting gauge identifies the formal
solution spaces without changing their fractional \(u\)-exponents:
coefficientwise formal monodromy fixes every integral power of \(u\).
Equivalently, it preserves the eigenvalue multiset of descended formal
monodromy.  On the reduced unramified spectral cover the unique local
spectral projector of \cite[Theorem~4.1]{KKPYY} tracks one chosen atomic
factor; on overlaps the resulting identifications differ only by conjugacy.
Hence its
formal-monodromy characteristic polynomial is locally constant on each
component and therefore constant because the component is connected.
Combining the factors with their deck action gives the same statement on the
original \(u\)-disc, independently of the chosen Levelt--Turrittin
refinement.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The sixth-root multiplicity is atomic]
\label{cor:nu6-atomic}
The function \(\nu_6\) is well defined on geometric atom-equivalence classes
and extends uniquely to a homomorphism
\[
 \nu_6:\Z^{(\mathscr A)}\longrightarrow\Z.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
An isomorphism of geometric \(F\)-bundles in
\cite[Definition~5.21]{KKPYY} is an isomorphism over the same formal
\(u\)-disc, so it conjugates formal monodromy without changing its
eigenvalues.  Lemma~\ref{lem:formal-isomonodromy} handles movement between
points of one connected unramified spectral component.  The blowup and
projective-bundle identifications used to pass from abstract atoms to
geometric atoms are likewise \(F\)-bundle isomorphisms over that same loop
coordinate \cite[Proposition~5.22]{KKPYY}; no ramified substitution
\(u\mapsto u^m\) is among the equivalences.  Definition~3.1 of
\cite{KKPYY} fixes the disc and its coordinate; base change acts on the base
factor and leaves \(u\) fixed.  Thus \(\nu_6\) is constant on
each class, and additivity on the free group is unique.
\end{proof}

For a smooth projective variety \(Z\), we abbreviate
\(\nu_6(Z)=\nu_6(\CFF(Z))\).

\begin{proposition}[Cubic and low-dimensional atoms]
\label{prop:atom-values}
Let \(X\) be a smooth cubic threefold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item The cubic zero atom \(\alpha_X\) satisfies \(\nu_6(\alpha_X)\ge2\).
\item Every atom represented by a point, a curve, or a smooth projective
surface has \(\nu_6=0\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Cai's calculation preceding Proposition~6 gives the indicial polynomial
\[
 \rho^2+\rho+\frac5{36},
\]
whose roots are \(-1/6\) and \(-5/6\), in the rank-two zero-eigenvalue block
of the even big cubic quantum connection \cite{Cai}.  Its bulk-variable
gauges use integral powers of the loop variable.  Parity preservation places
this rank-two block inside the unsplit zero atom of the full maximal
big-quantum \(F\)-bundle in \cite[Example~6.21]{KKPYY}; passage to that
bundle is scalar extension with the same loop coordinate.  Hence the two
monodromy eigenvalues \(e^{-\pi i/3}\) and \(e^{\pi i/3}\) occur in
\(\FM(\alpha_X)\).  We make no assertion about additional eigenvalues from
the odd summand, and obtain the stated lower bound.

A point has exponent zero, and \(\PP^1\) reduces to two point atoms by the
projective-bundle formula.  For a curve of genus \(g>0\), the explicit curve
connection has formal monodromy eigenvalues only in \(\{1,-1\}\): this
follows directly from Cai's Proposition~7 on the even block and the classical
odd block, and is also the curve-atom comparison used in
\cite[Example~6.21]{KKPYY}.  Thus no curve atom has a primitive sixth-root
eigenvalue.  For a minimal surface with nef canonical
class, at an even base point with vanishing \(H^0\)-coordinate the
parity-corrected connection has
nilpotent residue by \cite[Claim~6.15]{KKPYY}; undoing the integral grading
and half-parity correction permits only monodromy eigenvalues \(1\) and
\(-1\).  A minimal surface of Kodaira dimension \(-\infty\) is
\(\PP^2\) or ruled.  Projective space reduces to a point atom, and a ruled
surface reduces by the projective-bundle formula to curve atoms.  Finally,
every nonminimal surface is obtained from a minimal one by point blowups,
whose blowup formula adds only point atoms \cite[Theorem~4.5]{KKPYY}.  The
classification of smooth projective surfaces exhausts the possibilities and
proves (ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:every-cubic}]
Let \(Y=X\times\PP^1\).  The projective-bundle formula gives
\[
 \CFF(Y)=2\CFF(X)
 \qquad\text{\cite[Theorem~4.11 and Proposition~5.22(3)]{KKPYY}},
\]
so Proposition~\ref{prop:atom-values} gives \(\nu_6(Y)>0\).

Suppose that \(Y\) were rational.  Weak factorization of a birational map
\(Y\dashrightarrow\PP^4\), together with the blowup chemical formula
\cite[Proposition~5.22(2)]{KKPYY}, gives in the free abelian atom group
\[
 \CFF(Y)+\sum_i(r_i-1)\CFF(Z_i)
 =\CFF(\PP^4)+\sum_j(s_j-1)\CFF(W_j),
\]
where \(r_i,s_j\) are the corresponding codimensions.  Divisorial blowups
are trivial, so every effective center has codimension at least two and hence
dimension at most two.  Apply
\(\nu_6\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:atom-values}(ii) kills every center term,
and the projective-bundle formula over a point gives
\(\nu_6(\PP^4)=0\).  The left-hand side retains \(\nu_6(Y)>0\), a
contradiction.
\end{proof}

The proof uses the free atom group, not a signed informal block ledger.  In
particular the two copies supplied by \(\PP^1\) are positive multiplicities
and cannot cancel one another.
