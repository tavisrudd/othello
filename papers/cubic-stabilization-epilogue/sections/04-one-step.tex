\section{Framed formal monodromy}
\label{sec:one-step}

The obstruction used here belongs to the small even quantum connection itself.
We follow it through each blowup in a weak factorization, without passing to
equivalence classes of quantum atoms.  This avoids a subtlety: a formal bulk
gauge is coefficientwise Laurent in the loop coordinate, but need not
specialize to a meromorphic gauge at a nearby analytic point.

The proof has three stages.  We first define the framed multiplicity
\(\nu_6\).  We then prove its projective-bundle and blowup formulas, using
divisor tags when the center Novikov map is not injective.  Finally we show
that centers of dimension at most two have \(\nu_6=0\), so weak factorization
makes \(\nu_6\) birationally invariant through dimension four.

Let \(T\) be a smooth projective variety.  Write
\[
 \Lambda_T=\C[[\operatorname{NE}^{\mathbf Z}_{\rm num}(T)]]
\]
for the completed monoid ring of the image of effective homology classes in
the numerical curve lattice
\[
 N_1(T)=\operatorname{im}\!\left(
 H_2(T,\mathbf Z)\longrightarrow
 \operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{NS}(T)/\mathrm{tors},\mathbf Z)\right).
\]
Thus \(N_1(T)\) is torsion-free and is separated by integral divisors.  Let
\(\mathcal K_T\) be an algebraic closure of its fraction field.  The loop coordinate
\(z\) remains independent: in particular, \(\mathcal K_T\) contains no chosen root of
\(z\).  Apply the Levelt--Turrittin construction to the small even quantum
connection over \(\mathcal K_T((z))\).  Its formal monodromy is the action of one turn on
the original \(z\)-disc.  Thus it includes both regular-singular monodromy and
the deck permutation of ramified exponential factors.

\begin{definition}
\label{def:framed-sixth-multiplicity}
Let \(M_{\rm f}(T)\) be this framed formal-monodromy operator, and put
\[
 \nu_6(T)=
 \operatorname{mult}_{e^{\pi i/3}}M_{\rm f}(T)+
 \operatorname{mult}_{e^{-\pi i/3}}M_{\rm f}(T).
\]
The multiplicities are algebraic multiplicities in the characteristic
polynomial.
\end{definition}

The characteristic polynomial is unchanged by extending one algebraic
closure of the numerical Novikov fraction field to a common overfield.
Thus \(\nu_6(T)\) is independent of that choice.

The frame matters.  Adjoining roots of Novikov monomials is harmless, but
adjoining a root of \(z\) would replace the original loop and could erase the
deck part of the operator.

\begin{definition}[Pro-Laurent framed monodromy]
\label{def:pro-laurent-monodromy}
Let \(B\) be a complete separated multiplicatively filtered \(k\)-algebra,
and let \(V\) be a finite-dimensional \(k\)-vector space.  The
pro-Laurent gauge group is
\[
 \mathcal G^{\rm pro}_{B,F}(V)=
 \varprojlim_N\operatorname{GL}
 \bigl(V\otimes(B/F^NB)((z))\bigr).
\]
It is generally larger than
\(\operatorname{GL}(V\otimes B((z)))\), because the lower Laurent bound may
decrease with \(N\).  For a compatible differential-module system over the
quotients \(B/F^NB\), its framed-monodromy characteristic polynomial is the
compatible system \((\chi_N)_N\) of finite-level polynomials, provided
reduction sends \(\chi_{N+1}\) to \(\chi_N\).  Since \(B\) is complete and
separated and the matrix rank is finite, this system is equivalently one
polynomial over \(B\).  A pro-Laurent gauge preserves it if every finite-level
gauge uses only integral powers of \(z\).
\end{definition}

We first record two formal facts used in the comparison theorems.  They also
fix the completion convention in the proof.

\begin{lemma}[Coefficientwise base change]
\label{lem:pv-base-change}
Write \(\partial=d/dz\).  Let \(D\) be a formal differential module over
\(k((z))\), and let \(B\) be a complete separated \(I\)-adic \(k\)-algebra on
which \(\partial\) is zero.  Fix a Levelt--Turrittin solution algebra \(R\) for
\(D\), with a fundamental solution matrix and differential constants
\(R^\partial=k\).  Scalar extension in
the coefficient direction preserves the characteristic polynomial of framed
formal monodromy.  The same holds after a connection gauge whose entries use
only integral powers of \(z\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At every finite level, the constants of
\(R\otimes_k B/I^N\) are \(B/I^N\): expansion in a \(k\)-basis reduces this to
\(R^\partial=k\).  A fundamental solution matrix then identifies the horizontal
module with
\[
 \operatorname{Sol}(D)\otimes_k B/I^N.
\]
Passing to the inverse limit gives
\(\operatorname{Sol}(D)\widehat\otimes_k B\), with monodromy
\(M_{\rm f}(D)\otimes1\).  An integral-\(z\) gauge is single-valued on the
original disc and conjugates this action.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Formal base shift]
\label{lem:formal-base-shift}
Suppose a pullback of the even quantum connection of \(T\) is defined over a
complete separated multiplicatively filtered \(\C\)-algebra \(B\), and its
bulk coordinate has the form
\[
 a_0\mathbf1+a_2^\circ+\eta,
 \qquad a_2^\circ\in H^2(T)\otimes F^0B,
 \qquad \eta\in F^1B\widehat\otimes H^{\rm ev}(T).
\]
Assume that \(a_0\) and \(a_2^\circ\) are constant in the positive filtration.
Its framed formal-monodromy characteristic polynomial equals that of the
small connection after the divisor substitution, coefficientwise in \(B\),
\[
 Q^d\longmapsto e^{\langle a_2^\circ,d\rangle}Q^d.
 \tag{4.1}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The string equation removes \(a_0\mathbf1\) by a scalar exponential in
\(z^{-1}\); this is single-valued and has trivial framed monodromy.  The divisor
equation gives (4.1).  It remains to remove \(\eta\), including its component
in \(H^2(T)\).

Modulo \(F^N B\), the flat bulk equations
\[
 \partial_{\eta_i}G=-z^{-1}(\phi_i\star_\eta)G,
 \qquad G|_{\eta=0}=1,
\]
are compatible because the even quantum connection is flat: the mixed
derivatives of the quantum-product matrices agree, and those matrices commute
by commutativity and associativity.  To derive the Laurent bound, write
\(G=\sum_\alpha G_\alpha\eta^\alpha\).  The coefficient of
\(\eta^\alpha\) in the \(i\)-th equation is
\[
 (\alpha_i+1)G_{\alpha+e_i}
 =-z^{-1}\bigl[(\phi_i\star_\eta)G\bigr]_\alpha.
 \tag{4.1a}
\]
The positive integer \(\alpha_i+1\) is invertible over \(\C\).  Strong
induction on \(|\alpha|\), with flatness making the choices of \(i\)
consistent, gives the unique normalized solution; \(G_\alpha\) contains no
power of \(z\) below \(-|\alpha|\).  Full flatness also makes the
\(G\)-gauged \(z\)-connection independent of \(\eta\); at \(\eta=0\) it is
the small connection, so \(G\) is the required connection gauge.
After substituting \(\eta\), a
term of total bulk degree \(m\) lies in \(F^mB\); modulo \(F^NB\), only the
finitely many degrees \(m<N\) survive.  The resulting finite sum is congruent
to the identity modulo \(F^1B\), hence is invertible, and has a Laurent lower
bound at least \(-(N-1)\).  Uniqueness makes these
solutions compatible in \(N\).  Their inverse limit belongs to
\[
 \mathcal G^{\rm pro}_{B,F}(H^{\rm ev}(T)).
 \tag{4.1b}
\]
At each finite quotient the gauge uses only integral powers of \(z\), hence
is single-valued on the original disc.  Lemma~\ref{lem:pv-base-change} makes
the characteristic polynomial constant there.  Compatibility of the
finite-level solutions identifies these polynomials under every reduction
map, so the hypothesis in Definition~\ref{def:pro-laurent-monodromy} is
satisfied.  Completeness and separatedness identify this compatible system
with a polynomial over \(B\); its equality with the shifted small polynomial
therefore survives every subsequent coefficient-field extension.  No
Levelt--Turrittin decomposition over a larger analytic field, and no
evaluation at a nonnilpotent analytic bulk point, is used.
\end{proof}

We now apply Iritani's quantum blowup theorem and the Iritani--Koto
projective-bundle theorem.  Both are stated over completed homological Novikov
rings.  Iritani's subsequent notes record explicitly that the blowup change
of variables and decomposition preserve cohomological parity
\cite[Section~2]{IritaniNotes}.

\begin{lemma}[Numerical Novikov base change]
\label{lem:numerical-base-change}
The quotient from the effective homology monoid to its image in \(N_1(T)\)
extends continuously to completed monoid rings, and the quantum connection
and the two comparison theorems base-change along it.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Filter both monoids by intersection with an ample divisor.  Effective homology
classes below a fixed cutoff form a finite set, as follows from the finite-type
Chow varieties of cycles of bounded degree.  The quotient is therefore defined
on every truncated monoid ring and passes to the inverse limit.  Explicitly,
the coefficient of \(Q^{\bar d}\) in the image of \(\sum_da_dQ^d\) is
\[
 \sum_{d\mapsto\bar d}a_d.
\]
The sum is finite at every cutoff, and additivity of the quotient makes this
coefficientwise pushforward a unital homomorphism for completed convolution.
Thus the quantum products descend by grouping Gromov--Witten coefficients with
the same numerical class.  Pushforward of curves, pairing with
\(c_1(N_{C/T})\), and the derivations \(Q\partial_Q\) are constant on these
fibres.  Every coefficient formula in the comparison maps therefore commutes
with numerical pushforward.  Since the comparison theorems provide both a map
and its inverse, the two maps remain inverse after this base change.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Framed operation formulas]
\label{prop:framed-operations}
Let \(V\to T\) be a vector bundle of rank \(r\ge2\).  Then
\[
 \nu_6(\PP_T(V))=r\nu_6(T).
 \tag{4.2}
\]
Let \(\widetilde T=\operatorname{Bl}_C T\), where \(C\subset T\) is smooth of
codimension \(c\ge2\).  In Iritani's comparison field one has
\[
 \nu_6(\widetilde T)=\nu_6(T)+
 \sum_{j=0}^{c-2}\nu_6(C;\chi_j),
 \tag{4.3}
\]
where \(\chi_j\) is the numerical Novikov specialization of the \(j\)-th
center summand.  The endpoint terms in (4.3) are the intrinsic small
invariants; the center terms are computed after the displayed specializations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Iritani's Theorem~5.18 gives a formal coordinate isomorphism and a
quantum-\(D\)-module isomorphism
\[
 \operatorname{QDM}(\widetilde T)\cong
 \operatorname{QDM}(T)\oplus
 \operatorname{QDM}(C)^{\oplus(c-1)}
\]
over a Laurent extension in the exceptional Novikov variable
\cite[Theorem~5.18]{IritaniBlowup}.  The isomorphism and its inverse use only
integral powers of \(z\), and the mirror coordinates are independent of \(z\).
The comparison field adjoins \(q^{-1/s_c}\), where
\(s_c=c-1\) for even \(c\) and \(s_c=2(c-1)\) for odd \(c\); this is only a
coefficient extension.  The reconstruction coordinate below is
\(u=q^{-1/(c-1)}\).
The center base shift is controlled before passage to the final Laurent
chart.  Put \(u=q^{-1/(c-1)}\) and use the reconstruction coordinates of
\cite[Section~5.8.2]{IritaniBlowup}:
\[
 \varsigma_j=\varsigma_j^\circ+s_j,
 \qquad
 \varsigma_j^\circ=-(c-1)\lambda_j+h_{C,j}+O(u).
 \tag{4.4}
\]
For the \(j\)-th target summand, let \(R_j\) be the image of
\(Q_C^d\mapsto Q^{i_*d}u^{\rho_C\cdot d}\), together with \(u\) and \(s_j\),
and set
\[
 J_j=\bigl(u,\{Q^{i_*d}u^{\rho_C\cdot d}:d\ne0\},
            \text{components of }s_j\bigr)\subset R_j,
 \qquad B_j=\varprojlim_N R_j/J_j^N.
\]
The center connection is defined over this image ring by
\cite[Remark~5.6]{IritaniBlowup}.  Formula (4.4),
together with (5.45), (5.47), and the initial asymptotics (5.27)--(5.30),
shows that after the unit term \(-(c-1)\lambda_j\) and fixed divisor
\(h_{C,j}\) are removed, its target bulk coordinate lies in
\(J_jH^*(C)\).  The noninjective center monomial map merely replaces the
source ring by its image.

First normalize the target connection over \(\C((u))\) by the single-valued
string scalar for \(-(c-1)\lambda_j\) and by the fixed divisor shift.  The
normalized connection descends to \(B_j\).  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:formal-base-shift} modulo every \(J_j^N\), take the compatible
inverse limit, and only then extend scalars to Iritani's Laurent comparison
field and apply \(\Psi\).  The fixed-divisor substitution is a \(z\)-constant
coefficient automorphism, so it does not change \(\nu_6\); it is well defined
on the image because \(h_{C,j}\) is a scalar multiple of \(\rho_C\).  Thus each center target summand has the
framed monodromy of its specialized small connection.  The ambient target
uses \(\tau=\tau^\circ+t\), with \(\tau^\circ=O(q^{-1})\), and the same
finite-quotient argument.  Direct-sum additivity gives (4.3).

The endpoint map is faithful: on numerical curve lattices
\[
 \bar\beta\longmapsto
 \bigl(\phi_*\bar\beta,-E\cdot\bar\beta\bigr)
\]
is injective because
\(N^1(\widetilde T)=\phi^*N^1(T)\oplus\mathbf Z[E]\).  Hence the Laurent
extension preserves the intrinsic endpoint characteristic polynomials.

The projective-bundle proof is parallel, again in reconstruction coordinates.
Its comparison field adjoins \(q^{-1/r'}\), with \(r'=r\) when \(r-1\) is
even and \(r'=2r\) when \(r-1\) is odd; the reconstruction coordinate remains
\(u=q^{-1/r}\).  The extra root is again a coefficient extension.
Put \(u=q^{-1/r}\).  For its \(j\)-th target summand let \(R_j\) be the image
of \(Q_T^d\mapsto Q^d u^{c_1(V)\cdot d}\), together with \(u\) and \(s_j\),
and set
\[
 J_j=\bigl(u,\{Q^d u^{c_1(V)\cdot d}:d\ne0\},
            \text{components of }s_j\bigr)\subset R_j,
 \qquad B_j=\varprojlim_N R_j/J_j^N.
\]
Proposition~5.6 and Section~5.8 of Iritani--Koto give
\[
 \varsigma_j=\varsigma_j^\circ+s_j,
 \qquad
 \varsigma_j^\circ=r\lambda_j-\frac{2\pi\mathrm{i}j}{r}c_1(V)+O(u).
\]
This is Proposition~5.6 and Section~5.8, especially (5.11),
of~\cite{IritaniKoto}.
Normalize the Laurent unit and fixed divisor terms first.  The normalized
connection descends to \(B_j\); apply Lemma~\ref{lem:formal-base-shift} in its
finite quotients, and then extend scalars to the Laurent comparison field.
Again the divisor substitution is a \(z\)-constant coefficient automorphism;
it factors through the displayed \(u\)-exponent.
This gives \(r\)
copies of the intrinsic base module and proves (4.2).  The base Novikov map is the
embedding in \cite[(1.1) and Remark~5.2]{IritaniKoto}.  The theorem assumes
\(V^\vee\) globally generated.  Tensoring \(V\) by a sufficiently negative
line bundle achieves this without changing \(\PP_T(V)\)
\cite[Remark~1.2]{IritaniKoto}; the resulting divisor shift preserves framed
monodromy by Lemma~\ref{lem:formal-base-shift}.  All other Novikov extensions
are in the coefficient direction.  After the odd bulk variables vanish,
pullback, pushforward, Fourier transformation, and multiplication by the
characteristic-class factors all preserve cohomological parity.  Hence both
decompositions restrict to the even quantum connections used here.  Formula
(4.2) is tautological when \(r=1\).
\end{proof}

The center map in (4.3) is not necessarily injective
\cite[(5.15)]{IritaniBlowup}.  Thus generic vanishing for \(C\) does not by
itself imply that its specialized term vanishes.  The next lemma supplies the
missing implication.

\begin{definition}
\label{def:strict-novikov-admissible}
A monomial map \(\chi:\Lambda_T\to A\) is \emph{strictly
Novikov-admissible} if \(A\) is a complete separated valued domain with domain
associated graded, every monomial has nonzero image, and
\[
 v(\chi(Q^d))=\ell(d),
 \tag{4.5}
\]
where \(\ell\) is positive and proper on the effective numerical monoid.
\end{definition}

We write \(\nu_6(T;\chi)\) for the framed small-even invariant after scalar
extension along such a map.

The center maps in (4.3) are strictly admissible.  Give the exceptional
Laurent variable valuation zero and use an ample divisor \(H\) on \(T\).  Then
\[
 v_H\!\left(Q^{i_*d}q^{-\rho_C\cdot d/(c-1)}\right)
 =(H|_C)\cdot d.
\]
The restriction \(H|_C\) is ample, which gives positivity and properness; the
associated graded Laurent monoid ring is a domain.

\begin{lemma}[Divisor tagging]
\label{lem:divisor-tagging}
Let \(\chi:\Lambda_T\to A\) be strictly Novikov-admissible.  If the intrinsic
small even quantum connection of \(T\) has no primitive-sixth framed
monodromy, neither does its specialization along \(\chi\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose integral divisors \(D_1,\ldots,D_\rho\) whose pairings separate
\(N_1(T)\), and introduce formal variables
\(\boldsymbol t=(t_1,\ldots,t_\rho)\).  Define
\[
 \chi_{\boldsymbol t}(Q^d)=\chi(Q^d)
 \exp\!\left(\sum_i t_i(D_i\cdot d)\right).
 \tag{4.6}
\]
This map from \(\Lambda_T\) to \(A[[\boldsymbol t]]\) is injective.  To prove
this, take a nonzero series.  Properness in (4.5) makes its lowest-valuation
support finite.  Its initial image is a finite combination of distinct
exponential characters.  The finitely many exponent vectors are distinct
integral vectors.  Choose \(a=(a_i)\in\Z^\rho\) outside the finitely many
integral hyperplanes on which two dot products agree, and substitute
\(t_i=a_i s\).  The resulting exponents are distinct integers.  Their
derivatives at \(s=0\) form a Vandermonde matrix with nonzero integer
determinant, which remains invertible in the characteristic-zero fraction
field.  The characters are therefore linearly independent.  The domain
hypothesis on the associated graded prevents cancellation of the initial
terms.

Let \(k_\chi=\overline{\operatorname{Frac}A}\).  After embedding the two
fraction fields in a common algebraic closure, injectivity makes the
\(\chi_{\boldsymbol t}\)-module a scalar extension of the intrinsic module.
On the other hand, the divisor equation
identifies (4.6) with the degree-two formal bulk pullback of the \(\chi\)-module
over \(B=k_\chi[[\boldsymbol t]]\).  Apply the finite-quotient argument in
Lemma~\ref{lem:formal-base-shift} with
\(\eta=\sum_i t_iD_i\): its integral-\(z\) bulk gauge removes the positive
divisor component.  Hence vanishing over the intrinsic field implies
vanishing after \(\chi\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Low-dimensional vanishing]
\label{prop:low-dimensional-vanishing}
Let \(T\) be a point, a smooth projective curve, or a smooth projective
surface.  For every strictly Novikov-admissible specialization \(\chi\),
\[
 \nu_6(T;\chi)=0.
 \tag{4.7}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We first prove the intrinsic generic statement.  If \(K_T\) is nef, the proof
of \cite[Claim~6.15]{KKPYY} applies coefficient by coefficient over the small
numerical Novikov ring.  Set
\[
 \operatorname{Gr}=\frac{\operatorname{Deg}-\dim T}{2},
 \qquad g=\operatorname{Gr}+\frac12E,
\]
where \(E\) is the identity in cohomological degrees of parity opposite to
\(\dim T\) and is zero in the other degrees.  The operator \(g\) has integral
eigenvalues.  Coefficientwise in \(Q^\beta\), multiplication by \(c_1(T)\)
sends
\[
 H^i(T)\longrightarrow H^{i+2-2c_1(T)\cdot\beta}(T).
\]
If \(K_T\) is nef, then \(c_1(T)\cdot\beta\le0\), so the
\(\operatorname{Gr}\)-weight rises by
\(1-c_1(T)\cdot\beta\ge1\).
Conjugation by \(z^g\) therefore turns
\(
 \nabla+\frac12E\,dz/z
\)
into a regular-singular connection with nilpotent residue.  Undoing the
half-parity correction permits only the monodromy eigenvalues \(1\) and
\(-1\), never a primitive sixth root.

This treats curves of positive genus and minimal surfaces with nef canonical
class.  A point is immediate.  The remaining minimal varieties are
\(\PP^1\), \(\PP^2\), and ruled surfaces; (4.2) reduces them to a point or a
curve.  Every nonminimal surface is obtained from a minimal surface by point
blowups, and (4.3) adds only point summands.  Hence the intrinsic generic
invariant vanishes in every case.  Lemma~\ref{lem:divisor-tagging} gives (4.7).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Birational invariance through dimension four]
\label{thm:nu6-birational-invariance}
If \(Y\) and \(Y'\) are birational smooth projective complex varieties of
the same dimension at most four, then
\[
 \nu_6(Y)=\nu_6(Y').
 \tag{4.8}
\]
Consequently, for smooth projective threefolds,
\[
 Y\times\PP^1\dashrightarrow Y'\times\PP^1
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \nu_6(Y)=\nu_6(Y').
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Weak factorization decomposes a birational map between smooth varieties into
blowups and blowdowns with smooth centers of codimension at least two
\cite{AKMW}.  In dimension at most four every nontrivial center has dimension
at most two.  The calculation following
Definition~\ref{def:strict-novikov-admissible} shows that every center
specialization in (4.3) is strictly admissible.
Proposition~\ref{prop:low-dimensional-vanishing} therefore makes every center
term zero, so the endpoint values agree at each step.

If \(Y,Y'\) are smooth threefolds and
\(Y\times\PP^1\dashrightarrow Y'\times\PP^1\), apply the first assertion in
dimension four and (4.2):
\[
 2\nu_6(Y)=\nu_6(Y\times\PP^1)
 =\nu_6(Y'\times\PP^1)=2\nu_6(Y').
\]
Cancelling two in \(\Z\) proves the stabilization assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The cubic packet]
\label{prop:cubic-packet}
For every smooth cubic threefold \(X\),
\[
 \nu_6(X)=2.
 \tag{4.9}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In the basis \(1,P,P^2,P^3\), Cai explicitly block-diagonalizes the small even
quantum connection.  The rank-two zero-exponential block has indicial
polynomial
\[
 \rho^2+\rho+\frac5{36},
\]
with roots \(-1/6\) and \(-5/6\) \cite[Section~3 and Proposition~6]{Cai}.  His gauge uses only
integral powers of \(z\).  The two corresponding framed-monodromy eigenvalues
are \(e^{-\pi i/3}\) and \(e^{\pi i/3}\).  The two remaining rank-one even
blocks are displayed as exponentials in \(z^{-1}\) times series in nonnegative
integral powers of \(z\) \cite[pp.~5--7]{Cai}; their framed-monodromy factor is
therefore \(1\), so they contribute no primitive sixth root.
Thus the multiplicity is exactly two.  By definition, \(\nu_6\) uses the small
even connection, so no odd-cohomology block enters this count.  The numerical
curve lattice of a cubic
threefold has rank one, so the numerical-Novikov convention does not change
the calculation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:every-cubic}]
The projective-bundle formula and Proposition~\ref{prop:cubic-packet} give
\[
 \nu_6(X\times\PP^1)=2\nu_6(X)>0.
 \tag{4.10}
\]
Suppose that \(X\times\PP^1\) were rational.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:nu6-birational-invariance},
\[
 \nu_6(X\times\PP^1)=\nu_6(\PP^4)=0,
\]
where the last equality follows from (4.2) over a point.  This contradicts
(4.10).
\end{proof}

\subsection{A genus-eight Fano consequence}

Kuznetsov associates to every smooth prime Fano threefold \(V\) of genus
eight a smooth Pfaffian cubic threefold \(X\), together with rank-two vector
bundles \(U\) on \(V\) and \(E^*\) on \(X\), whose projectivizations are
related by a flop \cite[Theorems~2.17--2.18]{KuznetsovV14}.

\begin{corollary}[One stabilization of \(V_{14}\)]
\label{cor:v14-one-step}
For every smooth prime Fano threefold \(V\) of genus eight,
\[
 \nu_6(V)=2,
 \qquad
 V\times\PP^1\ \text{is irrational}.
 \tag{4.11}
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The flop gives a birational map between the smooth projective fourfolds
\(\PP_V(U)\) and \(\PP_X(E^*)\).  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:nu6-birational-invariance}, Proposition
\ref{prop:framed-operations}, and Proposition~\ref{prop:cubic-packet},
\[
 2\nu_6(V)=\nu_6(\PP_V(U))
 =\nu_6(\PP_X(E^*))=2\nu_6(X)=4.
\]
Hence \(\nu_6(V)=2\).  Formula (4.2) now gives
\(\nu_6(V\times\PP^1)=4\), whereas every rational smooth fourfold has
\(\nu_6=0\) by Theorem~\ref{thm:nu6-birational-invariance}.
\end{proof}

The argument is sharp for this invariant.  In a factorization of the fivefold
\(X\times\PP^2\), a center may have dimension three and may itself carry the
cubic packet.  The next stabilization therefore requires information about
the packet's Stokes or Rees placement, not only its multiplicity.
