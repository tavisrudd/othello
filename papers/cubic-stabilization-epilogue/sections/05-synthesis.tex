\section{The separation theorem}
\label{sec:synthesis}

Each separation statement of the introduction has the same two-line proof: a
source supplies universal \(CH_0\)-triviality of the threefold, the projective
bundle formula for Chow groups carries it to the product, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:every-cubic} supplies irrationality.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:voisin-separation}]
Voisin produces a nonempty countable union of subvarieties of codimension at
most three in the moduli space of smooth cubic threefolds along which the
primitive minimal class \(\Theta^4/4!\) is algebraic, hence along which
\(CH_0\) is universally trivial \cite[Theorem~4.5 and Lemma~4.6]{Voisin}.
The preamble supplies the product and the irrationality.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:fermat-separation}]
The Fermat equation is a sum of five forms in separated single variables, so
\cite{CT} applies, and the preamble supplies the rest.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:coprime-separation}]
The cubics of \cite[Theorem~3.3]{YYZ} carry unirational parametrizations of
degrees two and three, and \cite[Corollary~3.5]{YYZ} keeps both degrees on
\(X\times\PP^m\).  Unirational parametrizations of coprime degrees give a
decomposition of the diagonal, hence universal \(CH_0\)-triviality
\cite[Section~1]{YYZ}, and the preamble supplies the rest.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:separation-family}]
Corollary~\ref{cor:universal-ch0} gives universal \(CH_0\)-triviality of
\(X_b\).  The projective bundle formula for Chow groups gives the same
property for \(X_b\times\PP^1\).  Theorem~\ref{thm:every-cubic} proves that
this fourfold is irrational.  Non-isotriviality follows from the nonconstant
intermediate-Jacobian period map on the \(A_5\)-component: Hartlieb proves
that the closure of its image is a one-dimensional special subvariety
\cite[Proposition~5.7]{Hartlieb}.
\end{proof}

The algebraic class \(\Theta^4/4!\) removes the
decomposition-of-diagonal obstruction, while the primitive-sixth
framed-monodromy multiplicity survives one stabilization and obstructs a
rational factorization.  Both conclusions hold on the \(A_5\)-family.  They
also hold on the coprime-degree family of \cite{YYZ}, where the first one
comes from a decomposition of the diagonal rather than from an algebraic
minimal class.

The two detectors are independent already on smooth projective threefolds.
If \(U(Y)\) is the indicator of universal \(CH_0\)-triviality, then
\[
 \bigl(U(Y),\nu_6(Y)\bigr)=
 \begin{cases}
  (1,0),&Y=\PP^3,\\
  (1,2),&Y\text{ is a smooth member of the family above},\\
  (0,2),&Y\text{ is a very general cubic threefold}.
 \end{cases}
\]
Voisin's criterion~\cite[Corollary~4.4]{Voisin} and the evenness theorem
\cite[Theorem~1.3 and Corollary~1.4]{EdGFS} give the last line.
Thus neither universal \(CH_0\)-triviality nor \(\nu_6\) determines the other.

For \(X\times\PP^m\) with \(m\ge2\), weak-factorization centers may have dimension
at least three and can carry primitive-sixth quantum packets.  The
low-dimensional vanishing in
Proposition~\ref{prop:low-dimensional-vanishing} no longer proves
noncancellation.
Likewise, the fibrewise minimal-class argument gives no relative universal
cycle over the pencil.  These are separate problems and are not implicit in
the theorem above.

Vanishing of the decomposition-of-the-diagonal obstruction therefore does
not predict rationality after one explicit stabilization.
