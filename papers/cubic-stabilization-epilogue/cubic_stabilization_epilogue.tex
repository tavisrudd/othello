\title{Irrationality after One Stabilization of Universally
  \(CH_0\)-Trivial Cubic Threefolds}
\author{Tavis Rudd}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Does universal \(CH_0\)-triviality force rationality after stabilization?
We construct a non-isotrivial one-parameter family of smooth cubic
threefolds \(X_b\) for which \(X_b\) is universally \(CH_0\)-trivial but
\(X_b\times\PP^1\) is irrational.  More generally, we prove that
\(X\times\PP^1\) is irrational for every smooth cubic threefold \(X\).

The examples arise from the nonstandard \(A_5\)-invariant cubic pencil.
Six elliptic quotients of its intermediate Jacobians give an isogeny of
polarization type \((1,6,6,6,6)\).  Its two-primary kernel belongs to a
Frobenius-conjugate pair of semisimple gluings, while its three-primary
kernel is scalar.  A local mixed-adjugate argument therefore puts the
primitive class \(\Theta^4/4!\) of the intermediate Jacobian in the integral
divisor-product lattice.  Voisin's criterion gives universal
\(CH_0\)-triviality fibre by fibre.

For irrationality, we use the formal-monodromy atom of the big quantum
connection.  The cubic atom has primitive sixth-root eigenvalues.  The
projective-bundle formula gives two copies on \(X\times\PP^1\), whereas the
atoms of every point, curve, or surface have no primitive sixth-root formal
monodromy.  Additivity and weak factorization prevent cancellation.
Thus the cycle-theoretic and quantum detectors diverge on the same smooth
fourfolds.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} cubic threefold; universal \(CH_0\)-triviality;
irrationality; intermediate Jacobian; quantum connection; weak factorization.
