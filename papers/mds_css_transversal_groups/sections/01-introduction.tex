\section{Introduction}

A linear \([2m,m,m+1]_q\) maximum-distance-separable (MDS) code
\(C\leq\F_q^{2m}\) defines an equal-phase Calderbank--Shor--Steane (CSS)
state
\begin{equation}\label{eq:equal-phase-css-state}
 \lvert\Psi_C\rangle=q^{-m/2}\sum_{c\in C}\lvert c\rangle.
\end{equation}
It is an absolutely maximally entangled state, and choosing one tensor
factor as an input turns it into the normalized Choi state of a
\([[2m-1,1,m]]_q\) quantum MDS encoder.  We determine the logical unitaries
implemented by products of one-site physical operators.

Throughout, \emph{transversal} means a tensor product of one-site physical
operators with party labels fixed; the one-site factors may depend on the
site.  This is broader than the uniform \(U^{\otimes n}\) convention used in
several structural classifications.  Logical groups are projective, so scalar
phases are divided out.  A party-moving symmetry additionally permutes tensor
factors and is named explicitly.  The exact group has two branches, detected
by a linear nullity computation.

\begin{theorem}[Diagonal isoduality and the transversal group]
\label{thm:diagonal-isodual-transversal-group}
Let \(q\) be an odd prime, let \(m\geq2\), and let
\(C\leq\F_q^{2m}\) be a linear \([2m,m,m+1]_q\) MDS code.  Put
\[
 \mathcal D(C,C^\perp)
 =\{s\in\F_q^{2m}:\diag(s)C\subseteq C^\perp\}.
\]
This space has dimension zero or one.  Its dimension is one exactly when
\(C\) is diagonally isodual, meaning that \(SC=C^\perp\) for a nonsingular
diagonal matrix \(S\).  The projective group of logical unitaries admitting
transversal physical implementations is
\[
\begin{cases}
 \F_q^2\rtimes\mathrm{SL}_2(q),&\dim\mathcal D(C,C^\perp)=1,\\
 \F_q^2\rtimes T,&\dim\mathcal D(C,C^\perp)=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
T=\{\diag(a,a^{-1}):a\in\F_q^\times\}.
\]
Every nonzero diagonal-duality witness has full support and is unique up to
scalar.  On the isodual branch the linear \(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\) factor has a
coherent Weil lift, whereas the affine projective group has no homomorphic
lift through the scalar quotient because of the Weyl commutator.
\end{theorem}

The proof separates existence from exclusion.  A nonzero off-diagonal block
of a product Clifford produces a diagonal multiplier between \(C\) and
\(C^\perp\).  The MDS distance forces that multiplier to have full support
and forces the multiplier space to be a line.  If the line is absent, every
block is diagonal and the exact linear image is \(T\).  If it is present,
the multiplier and its inverse construct all upper and lower elementary
unipotents, hence all of \(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\).  Logical Paulis supply the
translation factor \(\F_q^2\).  The converse uses the following rigidity
theorem from the companion paper.

Rains organized global coordinatewise symplectic symmetries and universal
fault-tolerant operations through code-invariance algebras
\cite[Theorems~4 and~14]{RainsNonbinary1999}.  Recent complementary work
describes transversal Clifford structure through compatible symplectic bases
for self-dual CSS codes \cite[Theorem~1]{TansuwannontTakadaFujii2025},
uniform diagonal multi-block Clifford actions and endomorphism algebras of
qubit stabilizer codes \cite[Section~II and Theorem~6.1]{DasuBurton2025},
and code automorphisms or ZX-dualities with the required Pauli corrections
\cite[Sections~III--IV]{SayginelEtAl2025}.  The latter corrections parallel
Lemma~\ref{lem:pauli-phase-correction}, while their ZX-dualities are closest
to the party-moving exchanges analyzed in Appendix~\ref{sec:party-extensions}.
Prakash and Singhal show under the uniform \(V_F^{\otimes n}\) convention
that an odd-prime \([[n,1]]_p\) code with a complete transversal Clifford set
has a maximal-self-orthogonal CSS presentation
\cite[Section~IV.C]{PrakashSinghal2026}.  Albert concurrently gives normal
forms for code-preserving, transversal, and two-fold-transversal Clifford
groups of binary CSS codes \cite[Sections~III--V]{Albert2026}.

Our site-dependent convention is load-bearing on the isodual branch: a
logical \(A\in\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\) is propagated through generally different local
blocks \(\phi_i(A)\) in \((3.4)\), rather than one repeated physical block.
Proposition~\ref{prop:diagonal-multiplier-line} can be viewed as an MDS
collapse of the corresponding endomorphism problem: every off-diagonal
code-to-dual multiplier lies on at most one projective line.  That collapse
gives the exact \(T\)-versus-\(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\) dichotomy, and stabilizer-AME rigidity makes it
exhaustive among arbitrary product unitaries.  Tan proves the general
AME--QMDS LU-orbit and symmetry--transversal correspondences
\cite[Theorems~3.5--3.6]{Tan2026}, and computes the four-qutrit and six-qubit
dictionaries in concrete cases \cite[Section~5]{Tan2026} and
\cite[Section~3.4]{TanFiveQubit2026}; here the multiplier theorem computes the
exact fixed-party subgroup for this MDS--CSS family.

\begin{theorem}[Imported stabilizer-AME rigidity
\cite{RuddAMEStabilizerRigidity2026}]
\label{thm:lu-lc-rigidity}
For every prime power \(q\) and \(m\geq2\), every product-unitary
intertwiner between two stabilizer \(\AME(2m,q)\) states is Clifford on each
tensor factor.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Imported transversal Clifford no-go
\cite{RuddAMEStabilizerRigidity2026}]
\label{cor:transversal-clifford}
Every tensor-product conversion between the associated stabilizer
\([[2m-1,1,m]]_q\) encoders is Clifford on every physical factor and on the
logical factor.  In particular, no code in this family admits a transversal
non-Clifford logical unitary.
\end{corollary}

We also use the companion paper's phase-correction lemma: if a product
Clifford carries one stabilizer label group to another, a product Pauli
correction carries the corresponding stabilizer state rays.
\begin{lemma}[Imported stabilizer-character correction
\cite{RuddAMEStabilizerRigidity2026}]
\label{lem:pauli-phase-correction}
For stabilizer states in the standard finite-field Weyl convention, every
product-Clifford map of their stabilizer label groups can be corrected by a
product Pauli to map one state ray to the other.
\end{lemma}

\paragraph{Imported minimum-support atlas
\cite{RuddAMEStabilizerRigidity2026}.}
For a stabilizer \(\AME(2m,q)\) state with label group \(L\), every
\((m+1)\)-party subgroup \(L(A)\) projects bijectively to the local Pauli-label
plane at each party in \(A\), and these subgroups span \(L\).  After a party
relabelling, two such states are local-Clifford equivalent exactly when their
transition maps \(p_jp_i^{-1}\) are related by local trace-symplectic changes
of Weyl frame.

We use only these stated conclusions.  Their proofs, quantitative
rounding theory, and minimum-support atlas belong to the companion paper and
are not reproved here.  The MDS--CSS dictionary and all constructions that
distinguish the two exact Clifford branches are proved in this paper.

For generalized and extended generalized Reed--Solomon codes the standard
dual formula supplies a diagonal-duality witness, so the first branch recovers
the full one-qudit projective Clifford group.  An extended
\([8,4,5]_7\) Reed--Solomon code, for example, gives a
\([[7,1,4]]_7\) quantum code whose projective transversal logical group has
order \(7^2\lvert\mathrm{SL}_2(7)\rvert=16464\).  The nullity criterion also
applies to non-evaluation MDS codes.

At length six, the code has a projective-geometric model as a six-arc in
\(\PP^2(\F_q)\).  Diagonal isoduality becomes fixed-label Gale
self-association, which for a six-arc is equivalent to lying on a conic.
Thus the all-length group theorem specializes to the
\(\mathrm{SL}_2(q)\)-versus-split-torus logical phase.  An explicit non-GRS
pencil then admits a degree-eight quotient \(z\): over odd fields it
classifies projective and monomial-code equivalence, and over odd prime
fields the imported rigidity theorem upgrades the classification to local
Clifford and local unitary equivalence.  The extension-field Frobenius
sectors are stated separately; no full extension-field Clifford
classification is claimed.

The remaining results explain and test this structure.  The Clebsch
\([6,3,4]_{11}\) code relates extremal generalized-\(X\) syndromes to twelve
conic directions while remaining on the nonconic split-torus branch.  Exact
marginal and four-copy contractions distinguish selected scalar orbits, but
every fixed-copy scalar contraction is generically constant on a regular
code family.  This contrast explains why operator-valued Weyl-frame data can
classify where bounded scalar invariants cannot.
