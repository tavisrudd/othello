\begin{abstract}
Let \(\Theta\) be the theta divisor of the intermediate Jacobian of a
smooth complex cubic threefold \(X\), and let
\(M=\operatorname{Bl}_0\Theta\).  We compute the integral middle lattice of
\(M\), equivalently the degree-three intersection cohomology of \(\Theta\).
It is free of rank \(130\) and fits into an abstractly split exact sequence
with terms \(\bigwedge^3H^1(J,\Z)\) and \(H^3(X,\Z)\).  Its simultaneous
restriction to \(X\) and Gysin image in \(J\) has nontrivial mod-two glue.
If \(L(\alpha)=\theta\wedge\alpha\), then
\[
 b_*H^3(M,\Z)=
 \Sat\bigl(L\textstyle\bigwedge^3H^1(J,\Z)\bigr)
 =L\textstyle\bigwedge^3H^1(J,\Z)
  +\theta^{[2]}\wedge H^1(J,\Z),
\]
and the saturation quotient is \((\Z/2)^{10}\).  The proof reduces the
integral Lefschetz defect to the unsigned vertex--edge incidence matrix of a
complete graph.  The degree-six difference map of the Fano surface then
identifies the geometric glue through a Pontryagin-product formula.  By
duality, the degree-five quotient not pulled back from \(J\) is free of rank
ten, while exceptional Gysin classes form its doubled sublattice.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Cubic threefold; intermediate Jacobian; theta
divisor; intersection cohomology; symplectic lattice; Fano surface.
