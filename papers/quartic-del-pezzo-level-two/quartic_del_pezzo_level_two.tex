\title{Level-Two Stable Rationality for Quartic del Pezzo Surfaces}
\author{Tavis Rudd\thanks{\texttt{tavisrudd@damnsimple.com}}}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{abstract}
Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic zero and let \(S/k\) be a smooth
quartic del Pezzo surface.  We prove that if \(S(k)\ne\varnothing\) and
\(\Pic(S_{\bar k})\) is a stably permutation Galois lattice, then
\(S\times\A^2\) is \(k\)-rational.  In particular every stably rational
quartic del Pezzo surface has stable-rationality level at most two.  The
proof uses the Cox compactification of a universal torsor.  A saturated
rank-three subtorus has four surviving Cox-weight blocks whose weights form
a unimodular tetrahedron.  A codimension-three tangent section of the OADP
compactification meets each general orbit once; the residual torus has rank
two and is rational.  This is an instance of a general unimodular-window
quotient theorem for torus actions on varieties with one apparent double
point.  Applied to the two cubic families of Tschinkel and Zhang, the result
gives \(X\times\PP^2\) rational in every dimension in which their construction
applies.  For their cubic threefolds, the known irrationality of
\(X\times\PP^1\) makes the stabilization threshold exactly two and gives
smooth projective nonrational fourfolds \(Y\) for which \(Y\times\A^1\) is
rational.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} quartic del Pezzo surface; stable rationality;
universal torsor; OADP variety; algebraic torus; cancellation.

\section{Results and mechanism}

For a stably rational variety \(V/k\), write
\[
 s(V)=\min\{m\geq0:V\times\PP^m\text{ is }k\text{-rational}\}.
\]
The value depends on the ground field.  Our main result gives a uniform
upper bound for quartic del Pezzo surfaces.

\begin{theorem}[Level two for quartic del Pezzo surfaces]
\label{thm:level-two}
\coverage{absent}%
\imports{manin-stable-permutation,tz-type-classification,tz-universal-torsor,voskresenskii-rank-two}%
\evidence{c925-slice-cover}%
Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic zero and let \(S/k\) be a smooth
quartic del Pezzo surface.  Suppose that \(S(k)\ne\varnothing\) and that
\(\Pic(S_{\bar k})\) is a stably permutation Galois lattice.  Then
\[
 S\times\A^2
\]
is \(k\)-rational.  Consequently every stably \(k\)-rational smooth quartic
del Pezzo surface satisfies the same conclusion.
\end{theorem}

The mechanism has two parts.  Tschinkel and Zhang prove that a universal
torsor with a rational point is rational by compactifying it to an OADP
variety, that is, a projective variety through whose general ambient point
passes one secant line \cite[Theorem~3.4]{TZ}.  We use the same
compactification equivariantly.  A rank-three subtorus leaves four Cox-weight
blocks outside a fixed boundary space.  Their weights are the vertices of a
unimodular tetrahedron.  Three tangent equations therefore select one point
of each general orbit.  The quotient is rational, and the remaining torus has
dimension two.

\begin{corollary}[The Tschinkel--Zhang cubic families]
\label{cor:cubics}
\coverage{absent}%
\uses{thm:level-two}%
\imports{tz-cubic-families}%
Every cubic hypersurface \(X/\mathbf Q\) in the two families of
Tschinkel--Zhang Propositions~5.1 and~5.2 satisfies
\[
 X\times\PP^2\ \text{is rational over }\mathbf Q.
\]
\end{corollary}

For the two cubic threefolds this determines the exact level.  The equations
are
\begin{align}
X_1={}&\{(x_4-2x_3)x_1^2+3(x_4+2x_3)x_2^2
 +3x_3^2x_4-x_4^3+x_5^3=0\},\label{eq:cubic-one}\\
X_3={}&\{x_4(x_1^2+2x_1x_2)+x_3(x_1^2+x_1x_2+x_2^2)
 +x_3^3-x_3^2x_4+x_4^3+2x_5^3=0\}.\label{eq:cubic-three}
\end{align}

\begin{corollary}[Exact threshold and cancellation]
\label{cor:cancellation}
\coverage{absent}%
\uses{cor:cubics}%
\imports{rudd-one-stabilization}%
For either smooth cubic threefold \(X=X_1,X_3\subset\PP^4\),
\[
 s(X)=2.
\]
Moreover \(Y=X\times\PP^1\) is a smooth projective nonrational fourfold over
\(\mathbf Q\), while \(Y\times\A^1\) is \(\mathbf Q\)-rational.
\end{corollary}

Tschinkel and Zhang obtain stable rationality from rational universal torsors
and stable-permutation Picard lattices.  Theorem~\ref{thm:level-two} uses
their universal-torsor theorem and their four-type classification; it
sharpens the resulting stabilization bound.  Thus their geometric input is
load-bearing rather than a consequence of the argument here.

\section{A unimodular-window quotient theorem}

Let \(Z\subset\PP(V)\) be a geometrically integral \(n\)-fold over an infinite
field \(k\).  We say that \(Z\) has the tangent-projection property if
projection from \(T_pZ\) at a general \(p\in Z(k)\) is birational to
\(\PP^n\).  An OADP \(n\)-fold in \(\PP^{2n+1}\) with dense \(k\)-points has
this property \cite[Theorem~2.4]{TZ}.

\begin{theorem}[Unimodular-window quotient]
\label{thm:window}
\coverage{absent}%
Let a rank-\(r\) \(k\)-torus \(T\) act linearly and generically freely on a
variety \(Z\subset\PP(V)\) with the tangent-projection property.  Over
\(\ks\), suppose that \(V\) has nonzero weight blocks
\[
 V_{w_0},\ldots,V_{w_r}
\]
such that \(w_1-w_0,\ldots,w_r-w_0\) is a \(\Z\)-basis of
\(X^*(T_{\ks})\).  Suppose also that a \(k\)-defined linear space \(B\)
contains every other weight block.  Assume that for a general
\(p\in Z(k)\) there is a \(k\)-defined codimension-\(r\) linear space
\[
 \Lambda\supset\langle T_pZ,B\rangle
\]
whose equations restrict to an \(r\)-by-\((r+1)\) coefficient matrix of rank
\(r\) with every coordinate of its kernel nonzero.  Then the Rosenlicht
quotient \(Z/T\) is \(k\)-rational.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Tangent projection maps \(\Lambda\cap Z\) birationally to
\(\PP^{n-r}\).  On a general geometric orbit, the equations of \(\Lambda\)
involve only the \(r+1\) surviving characters.  Their coefficient matrix
fixes every ratio
\(\chi^{w_j-w_0}(t)\).  Since the weight differences form an integral basis,
these ratios are coordinates on \(T_{\ks}\); hence the slice contains exactly
one point of the open orbit.  The construction is defined over \(k\), so this
geometric point is the generic point of a degree-one rational slice over
\(k\).  Therefore
\(\Lambda\cap Z\dashrightarrow Z/T\) is birational.

The orbit correction is explicit.  Let
\(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\) be equations of \(\Lambda\), write \(q_j\)
for the component of \(q\) in \(V_{w_j}\), and set
\[
 A(q)_{ij}=\lambda_i(q_j),\qquad
 \kappa_j(q)=(-1)^j\det A(q)_{\widehat j}.
\]
Cramer's identity and the nonvanishing of the maximal minors give the unique
element \(t(q)\) through
\[
 \chi^{w_j-w_0}(t(q))=\frac{\kappa_j(q)}{\kappa_0(q)},
 \qquad 1\leq j\leq r.
\]
No root extraction occurs.  Uniqueness makes the formula Galois equivariant,
even when the individual weight blocks do not descend.  The map
\(q\mapsto t(q)q\) is the rational retraction to the slice.
\end{proof}

The rank-one case says that two adjacent weights suffice.  The higher-rank
form explains the role of saturation: a finite-index simplex would produce a
finite cover of the orbit rather than a one-point slice.

\section{The saturated rank-three window}

Let \(\mathcal T\to S\) be a universal torsor and \(T_0\) the rank-five
projective Neron--Severi torus obtained after quotienting the anticanonical
scalar.  Over \(\bar k\), the projective Cox closure
\[
 Z=\PP(\Spec\operatorname{Cox}(S))\subset\PP^{15}
\]
is a seven-dimensional OADP variety \cite[Lemma~3.2]{TZ}.

For the maximal type \(I_3\), the last three standard cocharacters span the
saturated invariant lattice
\[
 N_3=\Z e_3\oplus\Z e_4\oplus\Z e_5.
\]
The two generators act in this basis by
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\-1&0&1\\-1&1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}1&0&-1\\0&1&-1\\0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The four surviving weight blocks are
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}cl@{}}
\toprule
weight & Cox coordinates\\
\midrule
\((0,1,1)\) & \(L_{13},L_{23},L_{35}\)\\
\((1,0,1)\) & \(L_{14},L_{24},L_{45}\)\\
\((1,1,0)\) & \(E_1,E_2,E_5\)\\
\((1,1,1)\) & \(L_{12},L_{15},L_{25}\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
and every other coordinate lies in the invariant boundary space
\[
 B=\PP\langle E_3,E_4,L_{34},Q\rangle.
\]
The differences from the first weight have determinant one.

\begin{remark}[Why the apparent double cover disappears]
\label{rem:saturation}
\coverage{absent}%
For type \(I_1\), the three visible sign cocharacters in the last three
coordinates are
\[
 (0,1,1),\quad(1,0,1),\quad(1,1,0).
\]
They generate the even-sum sublattice of \(\Z^3\), of index two.  Its
saturation is all of \(\Z^3\), obtained by adjoining
\[
 (1,1,1)=\tfrac12\bigl((0,1,1)+(1,0,1)+(1,1,0)\bigr).
\]
The corresponding map from the three parameter tori to the image subtorus
has kernel \(\boldsymbol\mu_2\).  The degree two seen in the unsaturated orbit
coordinates counts these two parameter values; it is not a double cover of
the geometric quotient.  Saturation removes the kernel and makes the four
weights above a unimodular tetrahedron.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Uniform tangent slice]
\label{prop:slice}
\coverage{absent}%
\uses{thm:window}%
\imports{tz-cox-model}%
\evidence{c925-slice-cover}%
For every smooth geometric quartic del Pezzo Cox model, the rank-three
subtorus above admits a tangent codimension-three slice satisfying the
hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:window}.  The good slice descends to every
twist of type \(I_0,I_1,I_2\), or \(I_3\) over an infinite field.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Use the standard blowup parameters
\[
 p_1=(1:0:0),\ p_2=(0:1:0),\ p_3=(0:0:1),\
 p_4=(1:1:1),\ p_5=(1:a:b),
\]
where
\(\Delta=ab(a-1)(b-1)(a-b)\ne0\).  Four exact pairs consisting of a tangent
point and an orbit test point give evaluation determinants \(D_i\) and
tangent smoothness minors \(M_i\).  On \(\Delta\ne0\), the first three
products can vanish simultaneously only on
\[
 3a-b-2=0
\]
and either
\[
 Q_0(b)=31223016b^2-435944529b+1306078948=0
\]
or \((3b-26)(3b-13)=0\).  On this line the primitive numerator of the fourth
product is
\[
 -(b-1)^4(4b-17)(16b-85)
 (83246b^2-872181b+2185995).
\]
It is coprime to the two remaining equations: the four cross-evaluations are
\[
 \frac{69121705}{4},\quad-\frac{4117757269}{32},\quad
 \frac{7918133}{9},\quad-\frac{272530}{9},
\]
and the resultant of the two quadratics is
\(-48353381094689774718359510\).  Thus a good geometric incidence exists on
every smooth fibre.  The exact witness data and the eight-branch reduction
are recorded in the verification bundle.

Goodness is open in the tangent centre, the three-frame of equations, and
the orbit test point.  Its projection to the tangent-centre factor contains
a nonempty open.  Rational points on a universal torsor with one rational
point are dense \cite[Lemma~2.1]{TZ}; hence a good centre can be chosen over
\(k\).  For that centre the good frames form a nonempty open in affine frame
space, which has a \(k\)-point because \(k\) is infinite.  This proves descent
without requiring an individual split-coordinate witness to be Galois fixed.

The type \(I_3\) boundary and lattice are Galois stable.  Up to conjugacy in
the Weyl group, types \(I_0,I_1,I_2\) are subgroups of \(I_3\)
\cite[Lemma~4.2]{TZ}.  Restriction preserves the saturated subtorus, boundary
space, unimodularity, and good incidence open.  Weyl conjugation only changes
the marking of the Cox coordinates.
\uses{thm:window}%
\imports{tz-cox-model,tz-type-classification}%
\end{proof}

Among the four Galois images allowed by the hereditary \(H^1\)-condition,
Proposition~\ref{prop:slice} is a complete classification: all four admit the
same saturated unimodular window after conjugacy.  We do not claim a
classification for arbitrary subgroups of \(W(D_5)\).

\section{Proof of the level-two theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:level-two}]
Assume first that \(S\) is \(k\)-minimal.  Tschinkel--Zhang's classification
places its Galois image in one of the four types above
\cite[Proposition~4.1 and Lemma~4.2]{TZ}.  Their rationality theorem for
universal torsors supplies a rational projective Cox closure with dense
\(k\)-points \cite[Theorem~3.4]{TZ}.  Proposition~\ref{prop:slice} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:window} give
\[
 Z/T_3\quad\text{rational over }k.
\]

The required product identity is equivariant.  If \(T\) is the
Neron--Severi torus, the stable-permutation hypothesis gives
\(H^1(F,T)=0\) for every extension \(F/k\)
\cite[Remark~2.2]{TZ}.  A section over \(k(S)\) gives
\[
 S\times T\dashrightarrow\mathcal T,\qquad (s,t)\longmapsto\sigma(s)t,
\]
equivariantly for \(T\).  After quotienting the anticanonical scalar,
\(Z\sim_k S\times T_0\) equivariantly.  Therefore
\[
 Z/T_3\sim_k S\times(T_0/T_3).
\]
The residual torus has rank two and is rational by Voskresenskii's theorem
\cite{Voskresenskii1967}.  Hence \(S\times\A^2\) is rational.

If \(S\) is not \(k\)-minimal, contract a \(k\)-defined union of disjoint
exceptional curves and iterate.  The resulting del Pezzo surface has degree
at least five and a rational point, hence is \(k\)-rational by the classical
rationality theorem for del Pezzo surfaces of degree at least five
\cite{Manin1966}.  Thus \(S\) is already rational.

Finally, stable rationality over an infinite field supplies a rational point,
and Manin's theorem makes \(\Pic(S_{\bar k})\) stably permutation
\cite{Manin1966}.  This proves the last assertion.
\uses{prop:slice,thm:window}%
\imports{manin-stable-permutation,tz-type-classification,tz-universal-torsor,voskresenskii-rank-two}%
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:cubics}]
Projection from the distinguished line makes each Tschinkel--Zhang cubic
birational to a quartic-del-Pezzo fibration over \(\PP^{n-2}\).  The generic
fibre satisfies Theorem~\ref{thm:level-two}.  Over the function field of the
base it becomes rational after adjoining two variables.  Hence
\(\mathbf Q(X)(u,v)\) is purely transcendental over \(\mathbf Q\), which is
equivalent to rationality of \(X\times\PP^2\).
\uses{thm:level-two}%
\imports{tz-cubic-families}%
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:cancellation}]
The one-stabilization theorem says that every smooth complex cubic
threefold \(X\) has \(X\times\PP^1\) irrational
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{RuddM1}.  Corollary~\ref{cor:cubics} gives rationality at
level two, so \(s(X)=2\).  If \(Y=X\times\PP^1\) were rational over
\(\mathbf Q\), it would be rational after base change to \(\mathbf C\), a
contradiction.  Finally
\[
 \mathbf Q(Y\times\A^1)=\mathbf Q(X)(u,t)
\]
is purely transcendental because \(X\times\PP^2\) is rational.
\uses{cor:cubics}%
\imports{rudd-one-stabilization}%
\end{proof}

\section{Verification boundary}

The lattice saturation, generator actions, weight blocks, determinant-one
window, and tangent-incidence cover use exact integer and rational arithmetic.
The tracked verification bundle checks the four witnesses and the complete
eight-branch cover over \(\Delta\ne0\).  A separate implementation checks the
lattice saturation, Galois actions, window, residual quotient action, and the
displayed numerical slice minors without importing the primary certificate.
The conceptual quotient theorem, equivariant generic splitting, incidence
descent, and function-field passage are proved in the text rather than by the
programs.  The repository README records commands, hashes, and the trust
boundary.

The result also recovers the stable-rationality conclusion for the four
minimal Picard types with a quantitative bound.  It does not reprove the
rationality of universal torsors or the classification of the four types;
those are the inputs from Tschinkel and Zhang.  Nor does it classify every
subgroup of \(W(D_5)\) admitting some other unimodular Cox window.
