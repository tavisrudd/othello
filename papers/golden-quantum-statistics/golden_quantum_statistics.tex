\begin{abstract}
Exchange statistics determine which information a many-particle
interferometer can retain about its one-particle transfer.  Building on the
Golden conference-operator and Joubert--Segre construction of the Clebsch
series, we study this distinction in its exact six-mode transfer.  For a
diagonal path control \(D_x\), the postselected block

\[
 K_T(x)=Q_{T,-}^{\mathsf T}D_xQ_{T,+}
\]

has top-exterior amplitude \(\det K_T(x)\), proportional to a signed Joubert
coordinate on the Segre cubic.  This amplitude is orientation-covariant,
although its filled-fermion probability \(|\det K_T(x)|^2\) is sign-blind.
There is no intrinsic permanent counterpart: permanents depend on the chosen
input and output port frames, whereas a canonical basis-independent bosonic
aggregate is the symmetric-cube trace \(h_3(K_T^\dagger K_T)\), a function
only of the singular values.  At each of twenty balanced controls, all six transfers have squared
singular spectrum \(\{1/5,4/5,4/5\}\), giving
\(h_3=313/125\) (uniform-input average \(313/1250\)) and exterior-cube
probability \(e_3=16/125\); the other
44 Boolean controls have zero fermionic amplitude.  Calibrated determinant
signs, inferred from phase-referenced one-particle tomography rather than a
direct many-fermion phase measurement, form a distance-six simplex code
supporting three-, five-, and ten-cut readout.  We give a six-mode optical
compilation and quantitative certification thresholds.  Present photonic
components support coherent transfer tomography and bosonic controls; direct
three-fermion emulation additionally requires a totally antisymmetric
three-photon qutrit resource.  The result is a theory and design-limit
analysis, not a report of a built device or a dynamical gauge-theory
simulation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let \(K\) be a three-input, three-output block of a linear one-particle
transfer.  If all three inputs and outputs are occupied, the fermionic
transition amplitude is \(\det K\), while the collision-free bosonic amplitude
is \(\per K\).  This familiar distinction does not by itself say whether the
two amplitudes encode the same geometric information.  The answer depends on
which changes of input and output coordinates belong to the physical data.

We consider a six-mode transfer obtained from a real symmetric conference
matrix \(C\) of order six, so that
\[
 C^{\mathsf T}=C,\qquad C_{ii}=0,\qquad C_{ij}\in\{\pm1\},
 \qquad C^2=5I.
\]
The two eigenspaces of \(C\), with eigenvalues \(\pm\sqrt5\), both have
dimension three.  A diagonal path control couples them through a
three-by-three block \(K\).  Six coherently marked conference matrices produce
six such blocks.  The Clebsch source papers construct this Golden operator
family from the regular six-point two-graph and prove that its determinants
are the six classical Joubert coordinates on the Segre cubic
\cite{ClebschI,ClebschIII}.  The present paper builds on that theorem: it asks
what the transfer retains under symmetric and antisymmetric exchange
statistics, how its marked determinant signs can be read in a calibrated
apparatus, and where a photonic realization stops.

The central point is an orientation boundary.  The Golden data determine the
two three-dimensional port spaces but not ordered orthonormal frames inside
them.  Changing those frames acts on \(K\) from the left and right by
orthogonal matrices.  The top exterior power is intrinsically a map between
the two determinant lines; after both lines are oriented, it becomes the
signed scalar \(\det K\), while the ordinary filled-fermion probability
\(|\det K|^2\) is sign-blind.  The permanent does not descend through the same
port-frame action.  Consequently the canonical intrinsic bosonic aggregate
used here is not a second signed Segre coordinate: it is a spectral quantity
on the symmetric cube.

Theorems~\ref{thm:orbit-boundary} and~\ref{thm:exchange-boundary} state this
distinction and its exact balanced specialization.  The Golden spectrum gives
all six protocols one calibration target, the Boolean rank theorem gives a
sharp \(20/44\) control boundary, and the simplex words reduce calibrated sign
readout.  These are the operational gains of the exceptional family.
The remaining sections separate intrinsic
observables from calibrated ones, give a short arithmetic application, and
identify the boundary of a photonic implementation.  The permanent
description of multiphoton scattering is standard~\cite{Scheel}; immanants
organize the intermediate symmetry sectors~\cite{Tichy}, and general
partial-distinguishability formalisms precede the complementarity identities
used here~\cite{Shchesnovich}.  General
boson--fermion complementarity in linear interferometers is due to
Jabbour and Cerf~\cite{JabbourCerf}; its extension to partially distinguishable
particles is due to Robbio, Jabbour, and Cerf~\cite{RobbioJabbourCerf}.  The
present contribution is the exact Golden specialization, the port-gauge
obstruction, and the associated measurement and resource package.

\section{The Golden transfer}

Here is a reproducible local model of the source object.  On the ordered path
set \(X=\{0,\ldots,5\}\), take
\[
C_0=\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&1&1&-1&-1\\
1&0&-1&-1&-1&-1\\
1&-1&0&1&1&-1\\
1&-1&1&0&-1&1\\
-1&-1&1&-1&0&-1\\
-1&-1&-1&1&-1&0
\end{pmatrix},\qquad C_0^2=5I.
\]
Let \(\mathcal T\) be the six synthematic totals, that is, the six labelled
one-factorizations of \(K_X\); below \(ij\) denotes the edge \(\{i,j\}\).
One total is
\[
\begin{split}
T_0=\{&
\{01,23,45\},\{02,14,35\},\{03,15,24\},\\
&\{04,13,25\},\{05,12,34\}\}.
\end{split}
\]
The \(S_6\)-orbit of \(T_0\) has six elements.  For each
\(T\in\mathcal T\), choose the lexicographically first permutation
\(\pi_T\) carrying \(T_0\) to \(T\), let \(P_T\) be its permutation matrix,
and put \(C_T=P_TC_0P_T^{\mathsf T}\).  This is the frozen convention used by
the artifact.  Transporting the ordered path basis fixes the Hodge and
determinant-line orientations, including the signs \(\varepsilon_T\) below.
The Clebsch source theorem proves that this marked family is the
conference-operator realization of the six Joubert coordinates
\cite[Theorem~5.1]{ClebschIII}; Paper~I supplies its upstream
two-graph provenance~\cite{ClebschI}.

For one member \(C_T\) of this marked conference family, let
\(Q_{T,+}\) and \(Q_{T,-}\) be matrices whose columns are ordered orthonormal
frames for the \(\pm\sqrt5\)-eigenspaces.  In the base gauge these frames are
obtained by Gram--Schmidt from
\(P_{\pm}e_0,P_{\pm}e_1,P_{\pm}e_2\), where
\(P_\pm=(I\pm C_0/\sqrt5)/2\), and are transported by \(P_T\).  For
\(x=(x_0,\ldots,x_5)\), put
\[
 D_x=\operatorname{diag}(x_0,\ldots,x_5),\qquad
 K_T(x)=Q_{T,-}^{\mathsf T}D_xQ_{T,+}.
\]
When \(|x_i|=1\), the six-mode transfer
\[
 U_T(x)=
 [Q_{T,+}\ Q_{T,-}]^{\mathsf T}D_x[Q_{T,+}\ Q_{T,-}]
\]
is orthogonal.  Values \(|x_i|<1\) describe a diagonal filter followed by
postselection.

The six determinant amplitudes satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:golden-amplitude}
 Z_T(x)=\varepsilon_T\,10\sqrt5\det K_T(x),
 \qquad \varepsilon_T\in\{\pm1\},
\end{equation}
with signs fixed by the marked orientations.  They obey
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:segre-nulls}
 \sum_T Z_T(x)=0,\qquad \sum_T Z_T(x)^3=0.
\end{equation}
These are the Segre equations in Joubert coordinates.  The equations and the
outer \(S_6\)-action are classical invariant theory
\cite{HowardMillsonSnowdenVakil}; the point used below is their realization as
six amplitudes of one marked transfer.

The six coordinate lines obtained from either three-dimensional eigenspace
form the classical real \(\operatorname{ETF}(3,6)\) associated with a symmetric
conference matrix~\cite{FickusMixon}.  In measurement language these are the
six icosahedral lines of the real three-level SIC~\cite{BengtssonZyczkowski};
they are not the nine-line informationally complete SIC for a complex qutrit.
This distinction matters here because the transfer is real and its port
orientation is part of the calibrated data.

\section{Why the determinant retains orientation}

Write \(H=K^\dagger K\).  For the intrinsic comparison, define
\[
 B_{\mathrm{sym}}(K)=\operatorname{tr}(\Sym^3 H)=h_3(H),
 \qquad
 F_{\mathrm{ext}}(K)=\operatorname{tr}(\bigwedge^3 H)
 =e_3(H)=\det H.
\]
The dagger notation keeps the statement valid for the complex port-gauge
extension; throughout the real Golden construction it is simply transpose.
The first expression is the sum of all-transferred probabilities, averaged
over the choice of normalized three-boson input occupation only after division
by the ten-dimensional symmetric-cube input space.  Thus the operational
uniform-input average is \(B_{\mathrm{sym}}/10\).  The second expression is
the filled three-fermion probability.

Geometrically, the singular values of \(K\) are the cosines of the principal
angles between the output space and the controlled image of the input space.
The absolute determinant is their product.  Its sign, after orientations are
chosen, records whether the induced map between the two determinant lines
preserves or reverses orientation.

\begin{theorem}[Left--right orbit and orientation boundary]
\label{thm:orbit-boundary}
Let \(V\) and \(W\) be real Euclidean spaces of the same dimension
\(n\geq2\), and let \(K:V\to W\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item A change of ordered orthonormal frames sends the matrix of \(K\) to
\[
 K\longmapsto R_-^{\mathsf T}KR_+,
 \qquad R_\pm\in O(n).
\]
The \(O(W)\times O(V)\) orbit is determined by the singular values.  If
\(K\) is invertible, this orbit splits into two
\(SO(W)\times SO(V)\) orbits, distinguished by the sign of the oriented
determinant.  If \(K\) is singular, it does not split.
\item Intrinsically, \(\bigwedge^nK\) is a map
\(\det V\to\det W\).  Choosing orientations turns it into a scalar, and a
frame change multiplies that scalar by
\(\det(R_-)\det(R_+)\).  Thus its absolute value is an orthogonal invariant,
while its sign is an invariant of oriented port spaces.
\item The permanent of a matrix does not descend to either the orthogonal or
the special-orthogonal double orbit.  Hence it is a calibrated port-frame
amplitude rather than a scalar attached to \(K\) as an unframed Euclidean
map.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The singular-value decomposition proves that two matrices lie in the same
\(O(W)\times O(V)\) orbit exactly when they have the same singular values.
For an invertible map, special-orthogonal changes preserve the sign of the
oriented determinant.  Conversely, singular-value decompositions of two maps
with the same singular values and determinant sign can be chosen with the
same pair of orientation parities, so special-orthogonal left and right
factors relate them.  When a singular value is zero, reflecting its kernel
direction changes one parity without changing the diagonal singular-value
matrix.  The two orientation classes therefore merge on the singular locus.

The functorial map on top exterior powers gives (ii), including its frame
transformation law.  For (iii), let \(R_\theta\) be a plane rotation and use
the special-orthogonal family
\(R_\theta\oplus I_{n-2}\).  Along the right orbit of the identity matrix,
\[
 \per(R_\theta\oplus I_{n-2})=\cos(2\theta),
 \qquad
 \det(R_\theta\oplus I_{n-2})=1.
\]
The permanent therefore varies inside a single special-orthogonal double
orbit.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The determinant is the minimal orientation carrier]
\label{prop:unique-orientation-carrier}
Up to scale, \(\det K\) is the unique degree-\(n\) polynomial in the entries
of \(K\) satisfying
\[
 F(R_-^{\mathsf T}KR_+)
 =\det(R_-)\det(R_+)F(K)
 \qquad (R_\pm\in O(n)).
\]
In particular, an orientation-covariant amplitude of the lowest possible
degree is forced to be the top exterior amplitude.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply diagonal sign changes on the left.  Every monomial of a degree-\(n\)
relative invariant must have odd degree in each of the \(n\) rows, hence
degree one in every row.  Right sign changes give degree one in every column.
The polynomial is therefore a linear combination of the \(n!\) permutation
monomials.  Invariance under orientation-preserving rotations in each pair of
rows, followed by a reflection in one row, forces the coefficients of
permutations differing by a transposition to have opposite signs.  All
coefficients are thus one common scalar times the permutation sign, which is
the determinant expansion.
\end{proof}

Every scalar invariant under the full orthogonal port action consequently
factors through the singular values.  This statement concerns the unframed
map.  A laboratory may freeze ordered phase-referenced port frames and then
measure additional calibrated amplitudes.

\begin{corollary}[Orientation chambers]
On the invertible real transfer space, the two oriented double orbits over a
fixed singular-value point are exchanged by reversing one port orientation.
They meet only on the determinantal hypersurface.  Thus \(\det K=0\) is
exactly the boundary at which the oriented top-exterior label disappears.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Complex port gauges]
For complex port frames, \(O(n)\) and \(SO(n)\) are replaced by \(U(n)\) and
\(SU(n)\).  Singular values again classify the full double orbit.  On the
invertible locus, the residual label is the phase of the oriented determinant;
on the singular locus it disappears.  The real Golden transfer and a coherent
phase reference reduce this determinant-line phase to the sign studied here.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Golden exchange-sector benchmark]
\label{thm:exchange-boundary}
Let \(K_T(x)\) be a Golden transfer block.  At every balanced control with
three entries \(+1\) and three entries \(-1\), for each of the six marked
protocols,
\[
 \operatorname{spec}H=\left\{\frac15,\frac45,\frac45\right\}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 B_{\mathrm{sym}}=\frac{313}{125},\qquad
 F_{\mathrm{ext}}=\frac{16}{125},\qquad
 B_{\mathrm{sym}}-F_{\mathrm{ext}}=\frac{297}{125}.
\]
The corresponding uniform average over the ten normalized symmetric-cube
input occupations is \(B_{\mathrm{sym}}/10=313/1250\).
The mixed exchange sector has
\[
 \operatorname{tr}\bigl(S_{(2,1)}(H)\bigr)=s_{(2,1)}(H)=\frac85,
\]
and the Schur--Weyl checksum is
\[
 h_3(H)+2s_{(2,1)}(H)+e_3(H)
 =\operatorname{tr}(H)^3=\frac{729}{125}.
\]
In particular, every scalar invariant under the full orthogonal port action
has the same value on all these balanced blocks and cannot distinguish their
marked Segre nodes or orientations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(D=D_x\) and \(P_{T,-}=(I-C_T/\sqrt5)/2\).  Since \(D^2=I\),
\[
 H=Q_{T,+}^{\mathsf T}DP_{T,-}DQ_{T,+}.
\]
Substituting \(P_{T,\pm}=(I\pm C_T/\sqrt5)/2\) gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:balanced-first-trace}
 \operatorname{tr}H
 =\frac14\left(6-\frac15\operatorname{tr}(C_TDC_TD)\right).
\end{equation}
Because \(C_{T,ij}^2=1\) off the diagonal,
\(\operatorname{tr}(C_TDC_TD)=(\sum_i x_i)^2-6=-6\) for a balanced
control.  For the displayed \(C_0\), direct multiplication on the ten
projective balanced supports also gives
\(\operatorname{tr}((C_0DC_0D)^2)=-42\).  Expanding the four projectors in
\(\operatorname{tr}(P_+DP_-DP_+DP_-D)\) therefore yields
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(H^2)
 =\frac1{16}\left(18-\frac45\operatorname{tr}(C_0DC_0D)
 +\frac1{25}\operatorname{tr}((C_0DC_0D)^2)\right)
 =\frac{33}{25}.
\]
Together with Eq.~\eqref{eq:balanced-first-trace}, this proves
\[
 \operatorname{tr}H
 =\operatorname{tr}(P_{T,+}DP_{T,-}D)=\frac95,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{tr}(H^2)
 =\operatorname{tr}(P_{T,+}DP_{T,-}DP_{T,+}DP_{T,-}D)
 =\frac{33}{25},
\]
and the triangle cubic of the same displayed matrix has
\(|Z_T(x)|=8\).  Since \(Z_T^2=500\det H\)
\cite[Theorem~5.1]{ClebschIII}, this gives
\[
 \det H=\frac{16}{125}.
\]
Complementing a support negates \(K_T\) and leaves \(H\) fixed.  Transport by
\(P_T\) merely permutes the supports, so these identities hold for all six
protocols.  Newton's identities give
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:balanced-characteristic-polynomial}
 \det(\lambda I-H)
 =\lambda^3-\frac95\lambda^2+\frac{24}{25}\lambda-\frac{16}{125}
 =\left(\lambda-\frac15\right)\left(\lambda-\frac45\right)^2.
\end{equation}
which proves the displayed spectrum without an exhaustive numerical search.
The complete and elementary symmetric functions then give
\[
 h_3\left(\frac15,\frac45,\frac45\right)=\frac{313}{125},
 \qquad
 e_3\left(\frac15,\frac45,\frac45\right)=\frac{16}{125}.
\]
Finally, the degree-three symmetric-function identity
\(h_3-e_3=p_1p_2\) gives \(297/125\).
For the remaining partition of three,
\[
 s_{(2,1)}=\frac{p_1^3-p_3}{3}=\frac85.
\]
Schur--Weyl decomposition
\[
 V^{\otimes3}=\Sym^3V\oplus
 2S_{(2,1)}V\oplus\bigwedge^3V
\]
then gives the checksum.  The final assertion follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:orbit-boundary}.
\end{proof}

The theorem does not say that calibrated bosonic measurements lose all
control information.  Once physical port axes are fixed, a permanent is an
ordinary measurable amplitude.  It says that such a value belongs to the
calibrated apparatus rather than to the unmarked Golden operator.  Likewise,
the determinant sign belongs to the oriented amplitude: the probability
\(F_{\mathrm{ext}}\) does not retain it.  The precursor below infers that sign
from phase-referenced one-particle tomography; it does not measure a direct
many-fermion phase.

\section{Balanced controls and calibrated readout}

The necessity of balance is independent of the Golden conference matrix.

\begin{proposition}[Universal balance obstruction]
\label{prop:balance-obstruction}
Let \(V_+\oplus V_-\) be an orthogonal splitting of
\(\mathbb R^{2d}\) into two \(d\)-planes.  For a Boolean path control with
negative support \(S\), the cross block \(K_S:V_+\to V_-\) satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{rank}K_S\leq\min\{|S|,2d-|S|\}.
\]
Consequently a nonzero filled \(d\)-fermion amplitude is possible only for a
balanced \(d+d\) control.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Writing \(P_S\) for the coordinate projection onto the negative paths and
using orthogonality of the two port spaces gives
\[
 K_S=-2Q_-^{\mathsf T}P_SQ_+,
\]
so \(\operatorname{rank}K_S\leq|S|\).  Complementing the control negates its
cross block and gives the second bound.
\end{proof}

There are \(64\) Boolean controls \(x\in\{\pm1\}^6\).  The twenty balanced
controls are the common nonzero determinant optimum.  For each one,
\[
 |\det K_T|=\frac{4}{5\sqrt5},\qquad
 P_F=|\det K_T|^2=\frac{16}{125}.
\]
The other 44 controls have zero filled-fermion amplitude.  This gives a sharp
Boolean boundary rather than a numerical search criterion.

Proposition~\ref{prop:balance-obstruction} makes every unbalanced mask rank at
most two.  There are
\(2\binom60+2\binom61+2\binom62=44\) such masks.  The common balanced spectrum
proves that all \(\binom63=20\) remaining masks are invertible.

Freeze the port frames by a reproducible pivot convention.  In that gauge the
collision-free bosonic probabilities on balanced controls take the values
\[
 P_B(111\mid111)\in
 \left\{\frac{16}{3125},\frac{36}{3125},\frac{64}{3125}\right\}.
\]
These values are controls, not invariants.  Coherent access to the calibrated
amplitudes distinguishes all twenty oriented masks; squaring pairs antipodal
controls and loses the overall orientation.

Across the ten projective balanced cuts, the six determinant sign words have
pairwise Hamming distance six.  Three selected signs identify a marked
protocol once Golden membership is already known.  Five signs and magnitudes
jointly test membership and identify the protocol.  Measuring all ten signs
supports soft matched-filter decoding; hard nearest-word decoding corrects
two sign errors.  No relative sign word fixes the global orientation without
a coherent port reference.

For an explicit schedule, order the projective cuts lexicographically by the
negative supports containing path zero:
\[
012,013,014,015,023,024,025,034,035,045.
\]
The first three positions \(012,013,014\) classify the six protocols after
membership is known.  The five-cycle
\(012,015,023,034,045\) jointly tests the common Golden magnitudes and
classifies the protocol.  These are fixed schedules for the transported
marking above, not protocol-dependent choices.

In the common calibrated orientation, the complete three-cut decoder is

\subsection*{Bounded arithmetic corollary}

The same coordinate map has the following arithmetic specialization.  It is
not used by the optical benchmark.

The rational diagonal filter
\[
 x=\left(1,\frac7{13},\frac17,-\frac15,-\frac12,-1\right)
\]
gives
\[
 Z(x)=\frac{72}{455}(11,-10,-8,5,4,-2).
\]
The integer vector on the right has vanishing sum and vanishing sum of cubes,
so it is an anomaly-free chiral charge vector for six Weyl fermions.  General
parametrizations of Abelian anomaly equations are known
\cite{CostaDobrescuFox,CostaDobrescuFoxFew}; the contribution here is the
operator filter and its exact postselection cost.  In the present
normalization,
\[
 P_{F,T}=\frac{(72/455)^2q_T^2}{500},
 \qquad q=(11,-10,-8,5,4,-2).
\]

This construction realizes arithmetic coordinates satisfying the anomaly
equations.  It contains no gauge field, regulator, anomalous current, or
gauge-theory dynamics.  The extension to two \(U(1)\) factors is governed by
lines on the Segre cubic and has its own established algebraic geometry
\cite{GripaiosNguyen}; it is outside the scope of this paper.

\subsection*{Circuit and port convention}

Put \(O_T=[Q_{T,+}\ Q_{T,-}]\), inject the first three eigenports numbered

\subsection*{Readout, nulls, and quantitative gates}

Six single-input intensity settings determine the transfer moduli.  Five
two-input scans against one fixed reference determine the remaining phases.
After fixing the input and output phase gauges, the determinant of the real
reconstructed block has a calibrated sign.  This is the demonstrated
six-mode characterization method of Rahimi-Keshari et
al.~\cite{RahimiKeshari}, not a direct observation of a many-fermion phase.
Programmable twelve-mode processors have separately operated with three
photons~\cite{Somhorst}.

The first acceptance test is structural, before comparison with any target
probability.  For the six reconstructed amplitudes set
\[
 r_1=\sum_T\widehat Z_T,\qquad
 r_3=\sum_T\widehat Z_T^3.
\]
All six amplitudes must first be expressed in one calibrated gain and
orientation convention.  Independent normalization of each protocol would
erase the content of these cross-protocol identities.
For reporting across transmission levels, use the dimensionless scores
\[
 \rho_1=\frac{|r_1|}{\sum_T|\widehat Z_T|},\qquad
 \rho_3=\frac{|r_3|}{\sum_T|\widehat Z_T|^3},
\]
whenever the respective denominator is nonzero.
These are descriptive algebraic residuals, not goodness-of-fit statistics;
experimental uncertainties must be propagated from the transfer
reconstruction.
Diagonal real loss changes \(x\) but leaves both nulls exact.  Coherent
diagonal phase error also leaves them exact over \(\mathbb C\), although a
literal real sign then needs the phase reference.  Mesh error can move the
amplitude word off the common Golden carrier: \(r_1\) is the primary
falsification test and \(r_3\) the nonlinear confirmation.  Passing both is
necessary for carrier membership but does not certify the target transfer;
the calibrated spectrum and probabilities supply the subsequent tests.
Mismatch among the three emulator copies is a separate failure, because then
no single block \(K_T\) describes the process.  Each copy must be tomographed
and matched before the determinant interpretation is applied.  Uniform
amplitude transmission \(\lambda\) scales amplitudes by \(\lambda^3\) and
probabilities by \(\lambda^6\), while \(\rho_1\) and \(\rho_3\) remain
unchanged.

For two contraction blocks \(K\) and \(K+E\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:determinant-stability}
 |\det(K+E)-\det K|\leq3\lVert E\rVert_2.
\end{equation}
Indeed \(A(t)=K+tE\) is a contraction for \(0\leq t\leq1\), and
\[
 \left|\frac{d}{dt}\det A(t)\right|
 \leq \lVert\operatorname{adj}A(t)\rVert_*
       \lVert E\rVert_2
 \leq3\lVert E\rVert_2,
\]
because the three singular values of the adjugate are pairwise products of
singular values of \(A(t)\), hence at most one.  Integration gives the bound.
The balanced sign is certified by
\(\lVert E\rVert_2<0.1193\).  The smallest chiral amplitude requires
\(\lVert E\rVert_2<0.00472\) for its sign and
\(4.72\times10^{-4}\) for ten-percent relative amplitude precision.  These
are operator-norm requirements on transfer tomography, not phase-shifter
specifications.

The shot budget assumes independent Bernoulli accepted trials, ten-percent
relative precision, a normal approximation, and simultaneous 95-percent
coverage of all six probabilities.  It excludes source preparation,
coupling, propagation, and detector loss; these enter only when an accepted-
trial rate is converted to laboratory time.
With Bonferroni allocation over six two-sided intervals, put
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shot-budget}
 z=\Phi^{-1}\left(1-\frac{0.05}{12}\right),\qquad
 n(p)=\left\lceil
 \frac{z^2(1-p)}{0.1^2p}
 \right\rceil .
\end{equation}

\subsection*{External resource dependency}

The emulator of Matthews et al.~\cite{Matthews} sends an entangled resource
through identical copies of a one-particle process.  Here direct
three-fermion emulation requires the totally antisymmetric state of three
photonic qutrits.  Goyal et al.~\cite{Goyal} give a linear-optical preparation
proposal, and Piccolini et al.~\cite{PiccoliniSinglet} give a second
interferometric proposal.  To our knowledge, no experiment has prepared and
characterized this three-photon qutrit singlet.  The source is therefore an
external dependency, not a component claimed here.  A demonstrated
three-qutrit GHZ state~\cite{Hu} has different exchange symmetry and neither
supplies the resource nor predicts its engineering performance.

Resource quality is more restrictive than shot noise under adversarial error
models.  If preparation noise is an arbitrary mixture of weight \(1-F\),
keeping probability bias below ten percent requires \(F\geq0.9872\) for the
balanced event and \(F\geq0.999980\) for the smallest chiral event.  With only
a fidelity bound and the trace-distance worst case, the corresponding gates
are \(F\geq0.999836\) and \(F\geq0.9999999996\).  A characterized noise model
could relax these bounds, but the conclusion would then be model-dependent.
For the mixture model, an event probability changes by at most \(1-F\), so
ten-percent relative bias at ideal probability \(p\) requires
\(F\geq1-0.1p\).  Here
\(F=(\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{\sqrt\rho\,\sigma\sqrt\rho})^2\) is the squared
Uhlmann fidelity.  With fidelity alone, trace distance bounds the probability
bias by \(\sqrt{1-F}\).  Thus the two generic gates are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:source-fidelity-gates}
 F\geq1-0.1p\quad\text{(mixture model)},\qquad
 F\geq1-0.01p^2\quad\text{(fidelity only)}.
\end{equation}
Substitution of \(p=16/125\) and
\(p=5184/25878125\) gives the four displayed gates.

\section{Discussion}

The Golden transfer separates three levels of information.  Its singular
values determine every intrinsic bosonic scalar.  A calibrated port frame
adds permanent amplitudes and oriented control information.  Antisymmetric
exchange supplies the determinant as a one-dimensional, orientation-covariant
amplitude of the port-frame action; its event probability discards the sign.
The proposed precursor recovers the signed matrix element by coherent
one-particle tomography after the laboratory frames have been compiled and
oriented.  The Segre coordinate is therefore not an arbitrary cubic
observable, but neither is its sign claimed here as a direct many-fermion
measurement.

The example also separates a feasible optical certification from a direct
many-particle realization.  Current mesh and tomography technology can test
the one-particle carrier and its bosonic shadows.  Direct photonic emulation of
the three-fermion amplitude depends on an additional antisymmetric resource.
This boundary is part of the result rather than an experimental claim.
