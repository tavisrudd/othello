\title{Orientation, exchange statistics, and rigidity in the Golden six-mode conference interferometer}
\author{Tavis Rudd}
\date{August 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Which features of a one-particle transfer remain observable when its port
spaces are unframed, oriented, or fully phase-calibrated?  Singular values
classify an unframed real transfer.  Orienting the port spaces adds the sign
of the top exterior amplitude; up to scale, the determinant is the unique
minimal-degree polynomial carrying this relative orientation.  Fully calibrated
frames expose matrix-dependent amplitudes such as the permanent.  For every
symmetric conference matrix, we identify the balanced exchange spectrum with
a normalized cross-Gram spectrum and prove that it is cut-independent exactly
when each half has size at most three.  Among symmetric conference matrices,
order six is the unique nontrivial uniform case, with spectrum
\(\{1/5,4/5,4/5\}\).  For every symmetric conference matrix of higher order,
the spectrum varies with the cut, while a classical aligned \(3\)-design fixes
the exact mean and variance of cut purity.

We realize this exceptional endpoint in the Golden six-mode interferometer.
For a diagonal path control \(D_x\), the postselected block
\(K_T(x)=Q_{T,-}^{\mathsf T}D_xQ_{T,+}\) has oriented top-exterior amplitude
\(\det K_T(x)\), but its filled-fermion probability is sign-blind.  The
balanced symmetric-cube trace and filled-fermion probability are \(313/125\)
and \(16/125\), respectively; all 44 unbalanced Boolean controls have zero
fermionic amplitude.  In the Clebsch realization, the six determinant
amplitudes are calibrated Joubert coordinates; across the ten projective
balanced cuts, the six protocol sign words form an equidistant binary code
with pairwise Hamming distance six.  A compiled architecture and quantitative
gates separate common-reference tomography, ordinary-photon controls, and
direct three-fermion emulation, whose totally antisymmetric three-photon
qutrit source remains an external dependency.  This is a theory and
design-limit analysis, not a report of a built device.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let \(K\) be a real input--output block of a linear one-particle transfer.
Without port frames, its singular values are intrinsic.  Orienting the two
port spaces turns the top exterior map into the signed determinant.  Fixing
ordered phase-calibrated frames then exposes matrix-dependent amplitudes,
including bosonic collision-free permanents.  These are different levels of
information, not three notations for the same observable;
Figure~\ref{fig:observable-hierarchy} records the hierarchy.

We consider a six-mode transfer obtained from a real symmetric conference
matrix \(C\) of order six, so that
\[
 C^{\mathsf T}=C,\qquad C_{ii}=0,\qquad C_{ij}\in\{\pm1\},
 \qquad C^2=5I.
\]
The two eigenspaces of \(C\), with eigenvalues \(\pm\sqrt5\), both have
dimension three.  A diagonal path control couples them through a
three-by-three block \(K\).  Six coherently marked conference matrices produce
six such blocks.  The Clebsch source papers construct this Golden operator
family from the regular six-point two-graph and prove that its determinants
are the six classical Joubert coordinates on the Segre cubic
\cite{ClebschI,ClebschIII}.  The present paper builds on that theorem: it asks
what the transfer retains under symmetric and antisymmetric exchange
statistics, how its marked determinant signs can be read in a calibrated
apparatus, and where a photonic realization stops.
The name \emph{Golden} is retained from that series and refers to the paired
\(\pm\sqrt5\) conference eigenspaces; \emph{conference interferometer} names
the operational realization studied here.

The paper has two principal results.  First, the left--right orbit theorem
classifies the information retained by unframed and oriented port spaces and
proves that the determinant is the unique minimal-degree polynomial carrying
their relative orientation.  Second, every balanced Boolean control in the
six-mode conference family has squared singular spectrum
\(\{1/5,4/5,4/5\}\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-exchange-rigidity} proves that no symmetric conference
matrix of higher order has a cut-independent balanced spectrum.  At higher
orders a classical aligned \(3\)-design instead fixes the
switching-class-independent mean and variance of the two-copy purity.  The
order-six statement follows from the signed triangle identity and the \(3+3\)
conference block equations, not a finite census.  It gives exact symmetric,
mixed, and exterior exchange-sector benchmarks and the sharp split between
twenty invertible and 44 singular Boolean controls.  The singular-value
classification supplies the language; the contribution is its combination
with the minimal orientation carrier and calibrated readout boundary.

The mechanism is the determinant line.  The Golden data determine the two
three-dimensional port spaces but not ordered orthonormal frames inside them.
Changing those frames acts on \(K\) from the left and right by orthogonal
matrices.  The top exterior power is intrinsically a map between the two
determinant lines; after both lines are oriented, it becomes the signed scalar
\(\det K\), while the filled-fermion probability \(|\det K|^2\) is
sign-blind.  The permanent does not descend through the same port-frame
action.  The canonical intrinsic bosonic aggregate is therefore a spectral
quantity on the symmetric cube, not a second signed Segre coordinate.

Theorems~\ref{thm:orbit-boundary},
\ref{thm:general-exchange-rigidity}, and~\ref{thm:exchange-boundary} separate
the general orientation boundary, the conference classification, and the
exact Golden benchmark.

Their experimental consequence has three levels.  Phase-referenced
one-particle tomography infers calibrated determinant signs; ordinary photons
measure bosonic controls; direct antisymmetric emulation needs an additional
three-qutrit resource.

The permanent description of multiphoton scattering is
standard~\cite{Scheel}; immanants
organize the intermediate symmetry sectors~\cite{Tichy}, and general
partial-distinguishability formalisms precede the complementarity identities
used here~\cite{Shchesnovich}.  General boson--fermion complementarity in
linear interferometers is due to
Jabbour and Cerf~\cite{JabbourCerf}; its extension to partially distinguishable
particles is due to Robbio, Jabbour, and Cerf~\cite{RobbioJabbourCerf}.  The
Clebsch source paper already records the determinant-line interpretation and
the failure of the permanent to descend through port-frame changes
\cite[Section~5]{ClebschIII}.  The present contribution is the general orbit
classification and minimal-degree theorem, their exact Golden exchange-sector
specialization, and the associated measurement and resource package.

\section{The Golden transfer}

Fix the following local model of the source object.  On the ordered path set
\(X=\{0,\ldots,5\}\), take
\[
C_0=\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&1&1&-1&-1\\
1&0&-1&-1&-1&-1\\
1&-1&0&1&1&-1\\
1&-1&1&0&-1&1\\
-1&-1&1&-1&0&-1\\
-1&-1&-1&1&-1&0
\end{pmatrix},\qquad C_0^2=5I.
\]
Let \(\mathcal T\) be the six synthematic totals, that is, the six labelled
one-factorizations of \(K_X\); below \(ij\) denotes the edge \(\{i,j\}\).
One total is
\[
\begin{split}
T_0=\{&
\{01,23,45\},\{02,14,35\},\{03,15,24\},\\
&\{04,13,25\},\{05,12,34\}\}.
\end{split}
\]
The \(S_6\)-orbit of \(T_0\) has six elements.  For each
\(T\in\mathcal T\), choose the lexicographically first permutation
\(\pi_T\) carrying \(T_0\) to \(T\), let \(P_T\) be its permutation matrix,
and put \(C_T=P_TC_0P_T^{\mathsf T}\).  We use this convention throughout.
Transporting the ordered path basis fixes the Hodge and
determinant-line orientations, including the signs \(\varepsilon_T\) below.
The Clebsch source theorem proves that this marked family is the
conference-operator realization of the six Joubert coordinates
\cite[Theorem~5.1]{ClebschIII}; Paper~I supplies its upstream
two-graph provenance~\cite{ClebschI}.

For one member \(C_T\) of this marked conference family, let
\(Q_{T,+}\) and \(Q_{T,-}\) be matrices whose columns are ordered orthonormal
frames for the \(\pm\sqrt5\)-eigenspaces.  In the base gauge these frames are
obtained by Gram--Schmidt from
\(P_{\pm}e_0,P_{\pm}e_1,P_{\pm}e_2\), where
\(P_\pm=(I\pm C_0/\sqrt5)/2\), and are transported by \(P_T\).  For
\(x=(x_0,\ldots,x_5)\), put
\[
 D_x=\operatorname{diag}(x_0,\ldots,x_5),\qquad
 K_T(x)=Q_{T,-}^{\mathsf T}D_xQ_{T,+}.
\]
When \(|x_i|=1\), the six-mode transfer
\[
 U_T(x)=
 [Q_{T,+}\ Q_{T,-}]^{\mathsf T}D_x[Q_{T,+}\ Q_{T,-}]
\]
is orthogonal.  Values \(|x_i|<1\) describe a diagonal filter followed by
postselection.

The six determinant amplitudes satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:golden-amplitude}
 Z_T(x)=\varepsilon_T\,10\sqrt5\det K_T(x),
 \qquad \varepsilon_T\in\{\pm1\},
\end{equation}
with signs fixed by the marked orientations.  They obey
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:segre-nulls}
 \sum_T Z_T(x)=0,\qquad \sum_T Z_T(x)^3=0.
\end{equation}
These are the Segre equations in Joubert coordinates.  The equations and the
outer \(S_6\)-action are classical invariant theory
\cite{HowardMillsonSnowdenVakil}; here we use their realization as six
amplitudes of one marked transfer.

The six coordinate lines obtained from either three-dimensional eigenspace
form the classical real \(\operatorname{ETF}(3,6)\) associated with a symmetric
conference matrix~\cite{FickusMixon}.  In measurement language these are the
six icosahedral lines of the real three-level SIC~\cite{BengtssonZyczkowski};
they are not the nine-line informationally complete SIC for a complex qutrit.
This distinction matters here because the transfer is real and its port
orientation is part of the calibrated data.

\section{Why the determinant retains orientation}

The source construction identifies the determinant line and gives the basic
plane-rotation counterexample for the permanent
\cite[Section~5]{ClebschIII}.  We sharpen that observation by classifying the
full and oriented double orbits in every dimension and proving that the
determinant is the unique relative invariant of minimal degree.

Write \(H=K^\dagger K\).  For the intrinsic comparison, define
\[
 B_{\mathrm{sym}}(K)=\operatorname{tr}(\Sym^3 H)=h_3(H),
 \qquad
 F_{\mathrm{ext}}(K)=\operatorname{tr}(\bigwedge^3 H)
 =e_3(H)=\det H.
\]
The dagger notation keeps the statement valid for the complex port-gauge
extension; throughout the real Golden construction it is simply transpose.
The first expression sums, over the ten normalized three-boson input
occupations, the probabilities that all three bosons transfer.  Dividing by
ten gives the uniform-input average \(B_{\mathrm{sym}}/10\).  The second
expression is the filled three-fermion probability.

For an orthogonal sign control, the singular values of \(K\) are the cosines
of the principal angles between the output space and the controlled image of
the input space.  For a filtered control they remain transfer singular values,
but need not be principal-angle cosines.  Their product is the absolute
determinant.  After orientations are chosen, its sign records whether the
induced map between the determinant lines preserves or reverses orientation.

\begin{theorem}[Left--right orbit and orientation boundary]
\label{thm:orbit-boundary}
Let \(V\) and \(W\) be real Euclidean spaces of the same dimension
\(n\geq2\), and let \(K:V\to W\).
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=*]
\item A change of ordered orthonormal frames sends the matrix of \(K\) to
\[
 K\longmapsto R_-^{\mathsf T}KR_+,
 \qquad R_\pm\in O(n).
\]
The \(O(W)\times O(V)\) orbit is determined by the singular values.  If
\(K\) is invertible, this orbit splits into two
\(SO(W)\times SO(V)\) orbits, distinguished by the sign of the oriented
determinant.  If \(K\) is singular, it does not split.
\item Intrinsically, \(\bigwedge^nK\) is a map
\(\det V\to\det W\).  Choosing orientations turns it into a scalar, and a
frame change multiplies that scalar by
\(\det(R_-)\det(R_+)\).  Thus its absolute value is an orthogonal invariant,
while its sign is an invariant of oriented port spaces.
\item The permanent of a matrix does not descend to either the orthogonal or
the special-orthogonal double orbit.  Hence it is a calibrated port-frame
amplitude rather than a scalar attached to \(K\) as an unframed Euclidean
map.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The singular-value decomposition proves that two matrices lie in the same
\(O(W)\times O(V)\) orbit exactly when they have the same singular values.
For an invertible map, special-orthogonal changes preserve the sign of the
oriented determinant.  Conversely, singular-value decompositions of two maps
with the same singular values and determinant sign can be chosen with the
same pair of orientation parities, so special-orthogonal left and right
factors relate them.  When a singular value is zero, reflecting its kernel
direction changes one parity without changing the diagonal singular-value
matrix.  The two orientation classes therefore merge on the singular locus.

The functorial map on top exterior powers gives (ii), including its frame
transformation law.  For (iii), let \(R_\theta\) be a plane rotation and use
the special-orthogonal family
\(R_\theta\oplus I_{n-2}\).  Along the right orbit of the identity matrix,
\[
 \per(R_\theta\oplus I_{n-2})=\cos(2\theta),
 \qquad
 \det(R_\theta\oplus I_{n-2})=1.
\]
The permanent therefore varies inside a single special-orthogonal double
orbit.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The determinant is the minimal orientation carrier]
\label{prop:unique-orientation-carrier}
Up to scale, \(\det K\) is the unique degree-\(n\) polynomial in the entries
of \(K\) satisfying
\[
 F(R_-^{\mathsf T}KR_+)
 =\det(R_-)\det(R_+)F(K)
 \qquad (R_\pm\in O(n)).
\]
In particular, an orientation-covariant amplitude of the lowest possible
degree is forced to be the top exterior amplitude.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply diagonal sign changes on the left.  Every monomial of a degree-\(n\)
relative invariant must have odd degree in each of the \(n\) rows, hence
degree one in every row.  Right sign changes give degree one in every column.
The same parity argument shows that every nonzero relative invariant has
degree at least \(n\).
The polynomial is therefore a linear combination of the \(n!\) permutation
monomials.  Invariance under orientation-preserving rotations in each pair of
rows, followed by a reflection in one row, forces the coefficients of
permutations differing by a transposition to have opposite signs.  All
coefficients are thus one common scalar times the permutation sign, which is
the determinant expansion.
\end{proof}

Every scalar invariant under the full orthogonal port action consequently
factors through the singular values.  This statement concerns the unframed
map.  A laboratory may freeze ordered phase-referenced port frames and then
measure additional calibrated amplitudes.

\begin{corollary}[Orientation chambers]
On the invertible real transfer space, the two oriented double orbits over a
fixed singular-value point are exchanged by reversing one port orientation.
They meet only on the determinantal hypersurface.  Thus \(\det K=0\) is
exactly the boundary at which the oriented top-exterior label disappears.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Complex port gauges]
For complex port frames, \(O(n)\) and \(SO(n)\) are replaced by \(U(n)\) and
\(SU(n)\).  Singular values again classify the full double orbit.  On the
invertible locus, the residual label is the phase of the oriented determinant;
on the singular locus it disappears.  The real Golden transfer and a coherent
phase reference reduce this determinant-line phase to the sign studied here.
\end{remark}

The six-mode benchmark is the exceptional endpoint of a general conference
classification, not the first member of a uniform family.  Let
\(C=C^{\mathsf T}\) be a zero-diagonal sign matrix of order \(2d\) with
\(C^2=qI\), where \(q=2d-1\).  For a balanced half \(Y\), write
\[
 C=\begin{pmatrix}A&R\\R^{\mathsf T}&E\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 H_Y=Q_+^{\mathsf T}D_YP_-D_YQ_+,
\]
where \(P_\pm=(I\pm C/\sqrt q)/2\), \(Q_+\) is an isometry onto
\(\operatorname{im}P_+\), and \(D_Y\) is the sign involution of the cut.
Complementing the half gives \(D_{Y^c}=-D_Y\) and hence
\(H_{Y^c}=H_Y\); all cut counts below are therefore projective.

\begin{theorem}[Conference exchange rigidity]
\label{thm:general-exchange-rigidity}
For every symmetric conference matrix,
\[
 \operatorname{spec}(H_Y)
 =\operatorname{spec}\!\left(\frac1qRR^{\mathsf T}\right)
 =\left\{1-\frac{\alpha_i^2}{q}:1\leq i\leq d\right\},
\]
where the \(\alpha_i\) are the eigenvalues of \(A\).  This spectrum is
independent of the balanced half exactly when \(d\leq3\).  Hence order six is
the unique nontrivial symmetric conference case with uniform balanced
exchange spectrum.  The order-two case is the trivial one-mode case, and no
symmetric conference matrix of order four exists.

For a four-set \(J\), let \(w(J)\) be the sum of its three signed
Hamilton-cycle products, call \(J\) \emph{aligned} when \(w(J)=3\), and let
\(c_Y\) count aligned four-sets contained in \(Y\).  Then
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(H_Y^2)=\frac{F_d+32c_Y}{q^2},\qquad
 F_d=dq^2-2qd(d-1)+d(d-1)+12\binom d3-8\binom d4.
\]
Hence the purity is cut-independent exactly in the same small orders.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Put \(Q=C/\sqrt q\) and \(L=[D_Y,Q]\).  The commutator anticommutes with
\(Q\), and on the positive eigenspace \(Lv=2P_-D_Yv\).  In cut coordinates,
\[
 L=\frac2{\sqrt q}
 \begin{pmatrix}0&R\\-R^{\mathsf T}&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Thus \(4H_Y\) is \(L^*L\) on that eigenspace, and \(H_Y\) has the squared
singular values of \(R/\sqrt q\).  Since the principal block of \(C^2=qI\)
is \(RR^{\mathsf T}=qI-A^2\), the spectral formula follows.

The identities \(\operatorname{tr}(A^2)=d(d-1)\) and
\[
 \operatorname{tr}(A^4)=d(d-1)+12\binom d3
   +8\sum_{\substack{J\subset Y\\|J|=4}}w(J)
\]
give the displayed purity formula, because \(w(J)\) is \(3\) on aligned
four-sets and \(-1\) otherwise.  Suppose \(d\geq4\) and this purity is
independent of \(Y\).  The sums of the aligned-set indicator over all
\(d\)-subsets are then constant.  The characteristic-zero inclusion matrix
from four-sets to \(d\)-sets has full column rank
\cite[Theorem~1]{JolliffeInclusion}, so the indicator is constant on
four-sets.

Switch \(C\) so that every edge incident with one vertex is positive.  For a
four-set containing that vertex, if the other three edge signs are \(x,y,z\),
then \(w=xy+xz+yz\).  If the common value is \(3\), every triangle on the
other \(2d-1\) vertices is monochromatic; all its edges then have one sign,
contradicting orthogonality of two conference rows.  If the common value is
\(-1\), the edge two-colouring has no monochromatic triangle, and
\(R(3,3)=6\) gives \(2d-1\leq5\), again a contradiction.

For \(d=1\) the claim is immediate, and for \(d=2\) every two-by-two
principal sign block squares to \(I\).  For \(d=3\), if \(\tau\) is the
product of the three edge signs, then \(A^2=2I+\tau A\), so \(A^2\) always
has spectrum \(\{4,1,1\}\).  This proves both directions.
\end{proof}

At higher orders only statistics remain universal.  Greaves and Suda proved
that the aligned sets form a
\(3\)-\((2d,4,(d-3)/2)\) design~\cite[Example~2.3]{GreavesSuda}.
Classical Johnson inclusion calculus~\cite{IntersectionMatrices} gives, for a
uniformly chosen balanced half and \(d\geq4\),
\[
 \mathbb E c_Y=\rho\binom d4,\qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(c_Y)=
 \frac{\binom{2d}{4}\binom{2d-8}{d-4}}{\binom{2d}{d}}
 \rho(1-\rho),
 \qquad \rho=\frac{d-3}{2(2d-3)}.
\]
Thus the purity has switching-class-independent mean and variance, obtained
from these formulas by the affine change above; its possible values are
quantized in steps of \(32/q^2\).  At order ten the aligned sets form a
Steiner system and force the 126 projective cuts to split into 36 with
\(\operatorname{tr}(H_Y^2)=145/81\) and 90 with
\(\operatorname{tr}(H_Y^2)=59/27\).
Paper~III gives the same general theorem at the conference-operator stage
\cite[Theorem~5.1]{ClebschIII}; the local proof above keeps the present
observable claim independently assessable.  The aligned design and Johnson
calculus are classical; the present use is the exchange interpretation and
rigidity classification.

\begin{remark}[Collective inverse boundary]
At order \(2d\geq10\), the complete labelled purity landscape recovers every
aligned four-set by inclusion inversion.  Paper~III proves that this family
determines the discrete signing up to switching and global negation
\cite[Theorem~5.2]{ClebschIII}; one calibrated triangle product selects the
remaining bit relative to that calibration.  This is a collective inverse
corollary, not a local optical observable: the mean and variance alone are
universal, and each individual \(H_Y\) remains orientation-blind.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Golden six-mode exchange benchmark]
\label{thm:exchange-boundary}
Let \(K_T(x)\) be a Golden transfer block.  At every balanced control with
three entries \(+1\) and three entries \(-1\), for each of the six marked
protocols,
\[
 \operatorname{spec}H=\left\{\frac15,\frac45,\frac45\right\}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 B_{\mathrm{sym}}=\frac{313}{125},\qquad
 F_{\mathrm{ext}}=\frac{16}{125},\qquad
 B_{\mathrm{sym}}-F_{\mathrm{ext}}=\frac{297}{125}.
\]
The corresponding uniform average over the ten normalized symmetric-cube
input occupations is \(B_{\mathrm{sym}}/10=313/1250\).
The mixed exchange sector has
\[
 \operatorname{tr}\bigl(S_{(2,1)}(H)\bigr)=s_{(2,1)}(H)=\frac85,
\]
and the Schur--Weyl checksum is
\[
 h_3(H)+2s_{(2,1)}(H)+e_3(H)
 =\operatorname{tr}(H)^3=\frac{729}{125}.
\]
In particular, every scalar invariant under the full orthogonal port action
has the same value on all these balanced blocks and cannot distinguish their
marked Segre nodes or orientations.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Order the coordinates by the negative support and its complement, and write
\[
 C_T=\begin{pmatrix}A&R\\R^{\mathsf T}&E\end{pmatrix}.
\]
If \(\tau\) is the product of the three off-diagonal entries of \(A\), then
\[
 A^2=2I+\tau A,
 \qquad \det(tI-A)=t^3-3t-2\tau.
\]
Thus \(A^2\) has spectrum \(\{4,1,1\}\).  The cross-Gram identity
\(RR^{\mathsf T}=5I-A^2\) and
Theorem~\ref{thm:general-exchange-rigidity} give the displayed spectrum of
\(H\) for every balanced support, without a census.  Equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:balanced-characteristic-polynomial}
 \det(\lambda I-H)
 =\lambda^3-\frac95\lambda^2+\frac{24}{25}\lambda-\frac{16}{125}
 =\left(\lambda-\frac15\right)\left(\lambda-\frac45\right)^2.
\end{equation}
The complete and elementary symmetric functions then give
\[
 h_3\left(\frac15,\frac45,\frac45\right)=\frac{313}{125},
 \qquad
 e_3\left(\frac15,\frac45,\frac45\right)=\frac{16}{125}.
\]
Finally, the degree-three symmetric-function identity
\(h_3-e_3=p_1p_2\) gives \(297/125\).
For the remaining partition of three,
\[
 s_{(2,1)}=\frac{p_1^3-p_3}{3}=\frac85.
\]
Schur--Weyl decomposition
\[
 V^{\otimes3}=\Sym^3V\oplus
 2S_{(2,1)}V\oplus\bigwedge^3V
\]
then gives the checksum.  The final assertion follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:orbit-boundary}.
\end{proof}

Calibrated bosonic measurements retain more than the spectral aggregate.  Once
physical port axes are fixed, a permanent is an ordinary transition amplitude,
accessible only through a phase-sensitive multiphoton protocol; photon
counting measures its squared modulus.  Such a value belongs to the calibrated
apparatus rather than to the unmarked Golden operator.  Likewise, the
determinant sign belongs to the oriented amplitude: the probability
\(F_{\mathrm{ext}}\) does not retain it.  The precursor below infers that sign
from phase-referenced one-particle tomography; it does not measure a direct
many-fermion phase.

\section{Balanced controls and calibrated readout}

The necessity of balance is independent of the Golden conference matrix.

\begin{proposition}[Universal balance obstruction]
\label{prop:balance-obstruction}
Let \(V_+\oplus V_-\) be an orthogonal splitting of
\(\mathbb R^{2d}\) into two \(d\)-planes.  For a Boolean path control with
negative support \(S\), the cross block \(K_S:V_+\to V_-\) satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{rank}K_S\leq\min\{|S|,2d-|S|\}.
\]
Consequently a nonzero filled \(d\)-fermion amplitude is possible only for a
balanced \(d+d\) control.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Writing \(P_S\) for the coordinate projection onto the negative paths and
using orthogonality of the two port spaces gives
\[
 K_S=-2Q_-^{\mathsf T}P_SQ_+,
\]
so \(\operatorname{rank}K_S\leq|S|\).  Complementing the control negates its
cross block and gives the second bound.
\end{proof}

There are \(64\) Boolean controls \(x\in\{\pm1\}^6\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:balance-obstruction} bounds the rank of the
\(2\binom60+2\binom61+2\binom62=44\) unbalanced masks by two.  The common
balanced spectrum makes all \(\binom63=20\) remaining masks invertible.
Thus exactly the balanced controls have nonzero determinant, and all attain
the same Boolean optimum:
\[
 |\det K_T|=\frac{4}{5\sqrt5},\qquad
 P_F=|\det K_T|^2=\frac{16}{125}.
\]
Hence the Boolean boundary is exact.

\subsection*{Readout, nulls, and quantitative gates}

Inject each of the six inputs coherently and measure the complex field at all
outputs against one common local oscillator.  This determines the transfer in
six input settings, each with the phase sampling required by the detector.
The common output reference is essential: intensity data and input-only
interference leave independent output-row phases free and therefore cannot
determine a block-determinant sign.  The intensity/interference reconstruction
of Rahimi-Keshari et al.~\cite{RahimiKeshari} supplies the demonstrated
transfer-characterization primitive; the determinant-sign claim here assumes
the additional common input--output phase calibration.  It is not a direct
observation of a many-fermion phase.
Programmable twelve-mode processors have separately operated with three
photons~\cite{Somhorst}.

The first acceptance test is structural, before comparison with any target
probability.  For the six reconstructed amplitudes set
\[
 r_1=\sum_T\widehat Z_T,\qquad
 r_3=\sum_T\widehat Z_T^3.
\]
All six amplitudes must first be expressed in one calibrated gain and
orientation convention.  Independent normalization of each protocol would
erase the content of these cross-protocol identities.
For reporting across transmission levels, use the dimensionless scores
\[
 \rho_1=\frac{|r_1|}{\sum_T|\widehat Z_T|},\qquad
 \rho_3=\frac{|r_3|}{\sum_T|\widehat Z_T|^3},
\]
whenever the respective denominator is nonzero.
These are descriptive algebraic residuals, not goodness-of-fit statistics;
experimental uncertainties must be propagated from the transfer
reconstruction.
Diagonal real loss changes \(x\) but leaves both nulls exact.  Coherent
diagonal phase error also leaves them exact over \(\mathbb C\), although a
literal real sign then needs the phase reference.  Mesh error can move the
amplitude word off the common Golden carrier: \(r_1\) is the primary
falsification test and \(r_3\) the nonlinear confirmation.  Passing both is
necessary for carrier membership but does not certify the target transfer;
the calibrated spectrum and probabilities supply the subsequent tests.
Mismatch among the three emulator copies is a separate failure, because then
no single block \(K_T\) describes the process.  Each copy must be tomographed
and matched before the determinant interpretation is applied.  Uniform
amplitude transmission \(\lambda\) scales amplitudes by \(\lambda^3\) and
probabilities by \(\lambda^6\), while \(\rho_1\) and \(\rho_3\) remain
unchanged.

For two contraction blocks \(K\) and \(K+E\),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:determinant-stability}
 |\det(K+E)-\det K|\leq3\lVert E\rVert_2.
\end{equation}
Indeed \(A(t)=K+tE\) is a contraction for \(0\leq t\leq1\), and
\[
 \left|\frac{d}{dt}\det A(t)\right|
 \leq \lVert\operatorname{adj}A(t)\rVert_*
       \lVert E\rVert_2
 \leq3\lVert E\rVert_2,
\]
because the three singular values of the adjugate are pairwise products of
singular values of \(A(t)\), hence at most one.  Integration gives the bound.
The balanced sign is certified by
\[
 \lVert E\rVert_2<\frac{4}{15\sqrt5}=0.119256\ldots .
\]
This is an operator-norm requirement on transfer tomography, not a
phase-shifter specification; experimental entrywise confidence regions must
be propagated to this norm before the gate is applied.

The shot budget assumes independent Bernoulli accepted trials, ten-percent
relative precision, a normal approximation, and simultaneous 95-percent
coverage of all six probabilities.  It excludes source preparation,
coupling, propagation, and detector loss; these enter only when an accepted-
trial rate is converted to laboratory time.
With Bonferroni allocation over six two-sided intervals, put
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shot-budget}
 z=\Phi^{-1}\left(1-\frac{0.05}{12}\right),\qquad
 n(p)=\left\lceil
 \frac{z^2(1-p)}{0.1^2p}
 \right\rceil .
\end{equation}

\subsection*{External resource dependency}

The emulator of Matthews et al.~\cite{Matthews} sends an entangled resource
through identical copies of a one-particle process.  Here direct
three-fermion emulation requires the totally antisymmetric state of three
photonic qutrits.  Goyal et al.~\cite{Goyal} give a linear-optical preparation
proposal, and Piccolini et al.~\cite{PiccoliniSinglet} give a second
interferometric proposal.  To our knowledge, no experiment has prepared and
characterized this three-photon qutrit singlet.  The source is therefore an
external dependency, not a component claimed here.  A demonstrated
three-qutrit GHZ state~\cite{Hu} has different exchange symmetry and neither
supplies the resource nor predicts its engineering performance.

The value \(P_F=\det H\) assumes normalized antisymmetric input and output
states.  In the emulator the six permutation amplitudes are combined
coherently in one normalized antisymmetric output projection, so no factor of
\(3!\) multiplies the stated probability.  Resource fidelity, photon
indistinguishability, matching of the three transfer copies, and analyzer
calibration are separate requirements.

Under adversarial error models, resource quality is more restrictive than
shot noise.  If preparation noise is an arbitrary mixture with target-state
weight \(\eta\), an event probability changes by at most \(1-\eta\).
Keeping the relative bias below ten percent at ideal probability \(p\)
therefore requires \(\eta\geq1-0.1p\).
If only the squared Uhlmann fidelity
\[
 F_{\rm U}
 =(\operatorname{tr}\sqrt{\sqrt\rho\,\sigma\sqrt\rho})^2
\]
is known, trace distance bounds the probability bias by
\(\sqrt{1-F_{\rm U}}\).  The two generic gates are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:source-fidelity-gates}
 \eta\geq1-0.1p\quad\text{(mixture model)},\qquad
 F_{\rm U}\geq1-0.01p^2\quad\text{(fidelity only)}.
\end{equation}
At the balanced value \(p=16/125\), these become
\(\eta\geq0.9872\) and \(F_{\rm U}\geq0.999836\).  A characterized noise model
could relax these bounds, but the conclusion would then be model-dependent.

\section{Discussion}

The Golden transfer separates three levels of information.  Its singular
values determine every intrinsic bosonic scalar.  Orienting the determinant
lines adds the sign of the top exterior amplitude.  Fixing phase-referenced
port frames then exposes matrix-dependent amplitudes such as the permanent.
Antisymmetric exchange realizes the determinant amplitude, but its event
probability discards the sign.  The precursor recovers that sign by coherent
one-particle tomography after the laboratory frames have been compiled and
oriented.  The Segre coordinate is therefore not an arbitrary cubic
observable, and its sign is not claimed here as a direct many-fermion
measurement.

The six-mode benchmark is also the only nontrivial symmetric conference case
in which every balanced control has the same singular spectrum.  Higher
orders retain a universal statistical envelope---the exact purity mean and
variance---but not a universal spectrum.  This distinction is operational:
singular statistics remain orientation-blind, while the determinant signs
used by the calibrated decoder carry the missing relative orientation.

The example also separates a feasible optical certification from a direct
many-particle realization.  Current mesh and tomography technology can test
the one-particle carrier and its bosonic shadows.  Direct photonic emulation of
the three-fermion amplitude depends on an additional antisymmetric resource.
The paper proves this design boundary; it does not claim a built experiment.
