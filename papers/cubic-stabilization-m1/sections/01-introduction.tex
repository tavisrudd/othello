\section{Introduction}

Let \(X\) be a smooth complex cubic threefold.  Clemens and
Griffiths proved that \(X\) is irrational \cite{CG}, but their
intermediate-Jacobian argument does not directly obstruct the rationality of
\(X\times\PP^1\): in a weak factorization of a birational map between smooth
fourfolds, the blowup formula for a surface center \(S\) contributes
\(H^3(S)(-1)\) in degree five, while \(H^3(X)(-1)\) occurs in
\(H^5(X\times\PP^1)\).  We prove that the stabilized fourfold is nevertheless
irrational.

\begin{theorem}[One-step irrationality]
\label{thm:every-cubic}
\coverage{conditional_deduction}%
\lean{rankTwoResidueMarker_eq_of_birational,%
  cubicThreefold_oneProjectiveLine_conclusion_of_residueMarker}%
For every smooth complex cubic threefold \(X\), the fourfold
\(X\times\PP^1\) is irrational.
\end{theorem}

The stable-rationality picture varies across the moduli space.  Hassett and
Tschinkel identified varieties birational to cubic threefolds as the
exceptional Fano-threefold case not covered by their stable-irrationality
theorem \cite{HassettTschinkel}.  More recently, Engel, de~Gaay Fortman, and
Schreieder proved that a very general complex cubic threefold is not stably
rational \cite[Corollary~1.4]{EGFS}, whereas Tschinkel and Zhang constructed
explicit stably rational smooth cubic threefolds over \(\mathbf Q\)
\cite[Theorem~1.3]{TschinkelZhangTorsors}.  The theorem above is uniform
across this divide: it also obstructs the first stabilization of those
stably rational examples.  A companion paper proves that this bound is sharp:
it gives two explicit examples for which \(X\times\PP^2\) is rational
\cite{RuddSharpness}.

The proof uses the quantum \(D\)-module (QDM): the cohomology module over the
ring of Novikov and bulk parameters, equipped with its flat quantum connection
\cite{IritaniQDM}.
The Novikov parameters record curve classes, while the bulk parameters are
coordinates on cohomology.  By the even QDM we mean that the odd bulk variables
are set to zero and the module is restricted to even cohomology.  We then
replace the ring of numerical Novikov and even bulk
parameters by its fraction field; this is what \emph{generic} means below.  In
the direction of the connection variable \(z\), the leading coefficient is
quantum multiplication by the Euler field.  Over an algebraic closure of the
generic coefficient field, its generalized eigenspaces decompose the formal
connection into blocks.

We record the block isomorphism classes in a free commutative monoid.  For an
additive marker on this monoid, Iritani's blowup theorem gives a formula for
its change under blowup, with each center summand retained.  Combined with
weak factorization, this formula makes any such marker that vanishes on every
center occurring in a weak factorization of smooth fourfolds a birational
invariant.

The marker counts rank-two blocks for which the centered leading term \(N\) of
Euler multiplication is nonzero and satisfies \(N^2=0\).  Horizontality of the
Poincar\'e pairing makes a canonical elementary modification regular singular,
while flatness gives a Lax equation for its residue and hence preserves its
eigenvalues.  We count the block when the two residue eigenvalues are distinct
modulo \(\Z\), equivalently when the two formal exponent classes are distinct.
Using Beauville's quantum product formulas, we compute the exponent
representatives \(-1/6\) and \(-5/6\) for the cubic block, with difference
\(2/3\).  The resulting marker
vanishes on points, curves, and surfaces and on projective space.
Iritani--Koto's projective-bundle theorem shows that it doubles after product
with \(\PP^1\).  This proves the theorem.

This use of Euler eigenspace blocks in birational geometry belongs to the same
circle of ideas as the Hodge-atom framework of
Katzarkov--Kontsevich--Pantev--Yu \cite{KKPYYu}.  Their framework combines the
generalized eigenspaces of Euler multiplication, Iritani's blowup theorem, and
weak factorization to produce birational invariants.  In particular, their
Example~6.21 uses the zero spectral piece, enhanced by the Serre operator and
formal monodromy, to recover the irrationality of every smooth cubic
threefold \cite[Example~6.21]{KKPYYu}.  Recent applications and refinements
include work on cubic, Gushel--Mukai, and K\"uchle fourfolds
\cite{GuereCubicFourfold,BenedettiManivelPerrin,
BenedettiFayGuereManivelPerrin}.

The marker used here is narrower: it uses no Hodge structure and records only
the rank-two formal-exponent condition above.  Its purpose is the
one-stabilization problem.  The additional ingredients are a specialization
that keeps the center summands distinct in Iritani's blowup comparison, the
vanishing of the marker on points, curves, and surfaces, and Iritani--Koto's
projective-bundle comparison for stabilization
\cite{IritaniBlowup,IritaniKoto}.  Gyenge gives a complementary
formal tensor-product theorem for quantum \(D\)-modules of products and proves
multiplicativity for Hodge atoms \cite{GyengeProduct}.  Cai independently
computes the same two exponent representatives and uses them with Iritani's
blowup theorem to prove symplectic irrationality of cubic threefolds
\cite{Cai}; that argument does not address stabilization.

The same construction gives a threefold criterion without stabilization.

\begin{corollary}[Threefold marker criterion]
\label{cor:threefold-marker}
\coverage{fragment}%
\lean{threefold_not_rational_of_occurrenceIndexedMarker}%
Let \(Y\) be a smooth projective complex threefold.  If its generic even QDM
contains a block counted by the formal-exponent marker defined in
\eqref{eq:atomic-fold}, then \(Y\) is irrational.
\end{corollary}

Indeed, the only centers in a weak factorization of threefolds have dimension
at most one, where the marker vanishes, while the generic quantum product of
\(\PP^3\) is semisimple.  This gives an irrationality criterion formulated in
terms of the quantum \(D\)-module.  We do not claim that this marker coincides
with a semiorthogonal or derived-categorical rationality obstruction.

A further application comes from Kuznetsov's construction.  For an arbitrary
smooth prime Fano threefold \(V\) of genus eight, it associates a smooth
Pfaffian cubic threefold \(X\) and rank-two projective bundles over \(V\) and
\(X\) related by a flop
\cite[Theorems~2.17--2.18]{KuznetsovV14}.
Tschinkel and Zhang study equivariant and twisted stable-birational
refinements of this Pfaffian--Grassmannian correspondence
\cite{TschinkelZhangV14}.
This gives the following consequence.

\begin{corollary}[Irrationality after one stabilization for \(V_{14}\)]
\label{cor:v14-one-step}
\coverage{conditional_deduction}%
\lean{genusEight_oneProjectiveLine_not_rational_of_residueContext}%
For every smooth prime Fano threefold \(V\) of genus eight,
\(V\times\PP^1\) is irrational.
\end{corollary}

The theorem also yields universally \(CH_0\)-trivial irrational fourfolds.
Universal \(CH_0\)-triviality passes to a projective bundle by
the projective-bundle formula for Chow groups.

\begin{corollary}[Separation in codimension at most three]
\label{cor:voisin-separation}
\coverage{fragment}%
\lean{primitiveMinimalClassFamily_universalCH0_and_irrational_stabilization,%
 exists_universalCH0_cubic_with_irrational_stabilization}%
\uses{thm:every-cubic}%
\imports{Voisin:minimal-class-locus}%
There is a nonempty countable union of subvarieties of codimension at most
three in the moduli space of smooth cubic threefolds such that, for every
member \(X\), both \(X\) and \(X\times\PP^1\) are universally
\(CH_0\)-trivial, while \(X\times\PP^1\) is irrational.
\end{corollary}

In the next statement, the displayed hypersurface lies in \(\PP^4\) and is
the Fermat cubic threefold.

\begin{corollary}[Fermat cubic]
\label{cor:fermat-separation}
\coverage{conditional_deduction}%
\lean{fermatCubic_universalCH0_and_irrational_stabilization}%
\uses{thm:every-cubic}%
\imports{CT:separated-variable-criterion}%
The fourfold
\[
  \{x_1^3+x_2^3+x_3^3+x_4^3+x_5^3=0\}\times\PP^1
\]
is universally \(CH_0\)-trivial and irrational.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Coprime-degree cubics]
\label{cor:coprime-separation}
\coverage{conditional_deduction}%
\lean{coprimeUnirationalDegrees_universalCH0_and_irrational_stabilization}%
\uses{thm:every-cubic}%
\imports{YYZ:coprime-degree-family, YYZ:coprime-degrees-persist}%
Let \(X\) be one of the cubic threefolds of
\cite[Theorem~3.3]{YYZ}.  Then \(X\times\PP^1\) admits unirational
parametrizations of coprime degrees, is universally \(CH_0\)-trivial, and is
irrational.
\end{corollary}

Voisin proves the codimension-at-most-three statement
\cite[Theorem~4.5]{Voisin}.  The Fermat case follows from
Colliot-Th\'el\`ene's separated-variable criterion \cite{CT}.  Yang--Yu--Zhu construct
the coprime-degree family \cite[Theorems~1.1 and 3.3]{YYZ}.  The last
corollary answers the \(m=1\) case of their question; it does not address all
stabilizations.
