\section{Cubic partners over extensions of bounded degree}
\label{sec:arithmetic-partners}
\begin{corollary}[Geometric finiteness]
\label{cor:bounded-degree-partners}\coverage{absent}%
Fix a finitely generated field $K\subset\C$ of characteristic zero, a smooth
cubic threefold $X/K$ and a positive integer $D$. There are finitely many
geometric isomorphism classes of smooth cubic threefolds $Y/L$, where
$L/K$ is finite of degree at most $D$, for which
$X_{\overline K}\times\PP^1$ and $Y_{\overline K}\times\PP^1$ are birational.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Achter's arithmetic intermediate Jacobian gives $A=J(X)/K$ and $J(Y)/L$
\cite[Theorem~B]{AchterTorelli}. Theorem~\ref{thm:hodge-conservation} gives
an isogeny of their complex abelian varieties. Homomorphisms of abelian
varieties do not change under extension of algebraically closed fields,
so the isogeny exists over $\overline K$.
Orr's bounded-degree theorem, in its corrected version, supplies an isogeny
\[
 A_{\overline K}\longrightarrow J(Y)_{\overline K},\qquad
 \deg\le c(A,K)D^\kappa
\]
\cite[Theorem~5.1]{OrrIsogenies}. The isogeny itself need not be defined
over an extension of degree at most $D$.

Choose an integer $N\ge c(A,K)D^\kappa$ and put
$M=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,N)$. Every possible kernel is a subgroup of
the finite group $A[M](\overline K)$. Hence there are finitely many possible
quotients $A/H$. Each quotient has finitely many principal polarizations
modulo automorphisms \cite[Theorem~1.1]{NarasimhanNori}.
Cubic Torelli \cite{CG} identifies at most one cubic geometric class for
each resulting principally polarized intermediate Jacobian.
\uses{thm:hodge-conservation}%
\imports{Achter:intermediate-jacobian,Orr:bounded-isogenies,NN:polarizations,CG:cubic-torelli}%
\end{proof}
The count is of geometric classes. It gives neither twist finiteness over
$K$ nor an effective number of partners.
