\begin{abstract}
Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic zero and \(S/k\) a smooth quartic
del Pezzo surface.  We prove that \(S\times\A^2\) is \(k\)-rational if
\(S(k)\ne\varnothing\) and the Galois module
\(\Pic(S_{\bar k})\) is stably permutation.  Thus every stably
\(k\)-rational quartic del Pezzo surface becomes rational after adjoining
two independent variables.  The proof uses the projective Cox model of a
universal torsor.  A saturated rank-three subtorus has four Galois-stable
groups of Cox generators whose weight differences form an integral basis.
A codimension-three linear section containing a general tangent space meets
a general subtorus orbit in one point.  Generic tangent projection then
identifies the rational quotient with projective four-space, and the
remaining two-dimensional torus is rational.  Applied to both families of
Tschinkel and Zhang, the theorem gives \(X\times\PP^2\) rational for every
cubic hypersurface in those families.  For their cubic threefolds, a
separate theorem on \(X\times\PP^1\) makes the stabilization index equal to
two and yields smooth projective nonrational fourfolds \(Y\) such that
\(Y\times\A^1\) is rational.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} quartic del Pezzo surface; stable rationality;
universal torsor; variety with one apparent double point; algebraic torus;
rationality after affine-line stabilization.

\section{Results}

For a projective variety \(V/k\), put
\[
 \ell_k(V)=\min\{m\geq0:V\times\A^m\text{ is }k\text{-rational}\}.
\]
Since \(\A^m\) and \(\PP^m\) are birational, one obtains the same integer
with \(\PP^m\) in place of \(\A^m\).  Tschinkel and Zhang use this notion in
their discussion of levels of stable rationality \cite[p.~13]{TZ}.

\begin{theorem}[Two-dimensional stabilization]
\label{thm:two-variable}
\coverage{absent}%
\imports{high-degree-del-pezzo,manin-stable-permutation,tz-type-classification,tz-universal-torsor,voskresenskii-rank-two}%
\evidence{c925-slice-cover}%
Let \(k\) be a field of characteristic zero and let \(S/k\) be a smooth
quartic del Pezzo surface.  Suppose that \(S(k)\ne\varnothing\) and that
\(\Pic(S_{\bar k})\) is a stably permutation
\(\operatorname{Gal}(\bar k/k)\)-module.  Then
\[
 S\times\A^2\quad\text{and, equivalently,}\quad S\times\PP^2
\]
are \(k\)-rational.  In particular, this holds for every stably
\(k\)-rational smooth quartic del Pezzo surface.
\end{theorem}

Tschinkel and Zhang prove stable rationality under these hypotheses by
constructing a rational universal torsor and a stable-permutation resolution
of its N\'eron--Severi torus \cite[Corollary~4.3]{TZ}.  Their resolution gives
the bound \(S\times\A^{11}\) rational in the minimal cases.  Theorem
\ref{thm:two-variable} uses their Cox model and classification but replaces
the eleven-dimensional quasi-trivial torus by a rational quotient and a
two-dimensional torus.  Their geometric results remain essential inputs.

For \(r\geq0\), let \(X_{1,r},X_{3,r}\subset\PP^{r+4}_{\mathbf Q}\) be the
smooth cubics
\begin{align}
X_{1,r}:\quad &(x_4-2x_3)x_1^2+3(x_4+2x_3)x_2^2
 +3x_3^2x_4-x_4^3+x_5^3+\sum_{i=1}^r w_i^3=0,
 \label{eq:cubic-one}\\
X_{3,r}:\quad &x_4(x_1^2+2x_1x_2)+x_3(x_1^2+x_1x_2+x_2^2)
 +x_3^3-x_3^2x_4+x_4^3+2x_5^3+\sum_{i=1}^r w_i^3=0.
 \label{eq:cubic-three}
\end{align}

\begin{corollary}[The Tschinkel--Zhang cubic families]
\label{cor:cubics}
\coverage{absent}%
\uses{thm:two-variable}%
\imports{tz-cubic-families}%
For every \(r\geq0\) and \(j\in\{1,3\}\), the variety
\(X_{j,r}\times\PP^2\) is rational over \(\mathbf Q\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Stabilization index and affine-line stabilization]
\label{cor:cancellation}
\coverage{absent}%
\uses{cor:cubics}%
\imports{rudd-one-stabilization}%
For \(j\in\{1,3\}\), let \(X_j=X_{j,0}\subset\PP^4_{\mathbf Q}\).  Then
\[
 \ell_{\mathbf Q}(X_j)=\ell_{\mathbf C}((X_j)_{\mathbf C})=2.
\]
Moreover, \(Y_j=X_j\times\PP^1\) is a smooth projective nonrational
fourfold over \(\mathbf Q\), whereas \(Y_j\times\A^1\) is rational over
\(\mathbf Q\).
\end{corollary}

Here the last assertion concerns birational rationality after taking a
product with \(\A^1\).  It is distinct from the Zariski cancellation
problem, which asks whether isomorphic affine cylinders have isomorphic
bases.

\section{A criterion for rational torus quotients}

Let \(Z\subset\PP(V)\) be a geometrically integral \(n\)-fold over an
infinite field \(k\).  At a smooth point \(p\in Z(k)\), write
\begin{equation}
 \pi_p:Z\dashrightarrow\PP(V/\widehat T_pZ)
 \label{eq:tangent-projection}
\end{equation}
for projection from the embedded tangent space; the hat denotes the affine
cone.  In the application, \(Z\subset\PP^{2n+1}\) has one apparent double
point, and \(\pi_p\) is birational for general \(p\) by
\cite[Theorem~2.4]{TZ}.

\begin{theorem}[A quotient criterion]
\label{thm:torus-quotient}
\coverage{absent}%
Let a rank-\(r\) \(k\)-torus \(T\) act linearly and generically freely on
\(Z\subset\PP(V)\), and suppose that the generic tangent projection
\(\pi_p\) is birational to \(\PP^n\).  Over \(\ks\), suppose that \(V\) has
nonzero weight spaces \(V_{w_0},\ldots,V_{w_r}\) such that
\(w_1-w_0,\ldots,w_r-w_0\) is a \(\Z\)-basis of
\(X^*(T_{\ks})\).  Assume that a \(k\)-defined linear subspace
\(B\subset\PP(V)\) contains all other weight spaces.

Suppose that, for a general \(p\in Z(k)\), there is a \(k\)-defined
codimension-\(r\) linear subspace
\[
 \Lambda\supset\langle T_pZ,B\rangle
\]
with equations \(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r\) such that, at a general
\(q\in Z\), the matrix
\begin{equation}
 A(q)_{ij}=\lambda_i(q_j),\qquad q_j\in V_{w_j},
 \label{eq:coefficient-matrix}
\end{equation}
has rank \(r\) and all \(r+1\) maximal minors are nonzero.  Then the
Rosenlicht quotient \(Z/T\) is \(k\)-rational.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The image of \(\Lambda\) under \(\pi_p\) is a linear
\(\PP^{n-r}\subset\PP^n\), and
\begin{equation}
 \pi_p|_{\Lambda\cap Z}:\Lambda\cap Z\dashrightarrow\PP^{n-r}
 \label{eq:restricted-tangent-projection}
\end{equation}
is birational on the open where \(\pi_p\) is an isomorphism.  It remains to
show that \(\Lambda\cap Z\) is a rational section of the \(T\)-action.

For a geometric point \(q=\sum q_j+q_B\), where \(q_B\) is supported on
the other weight spaces, define
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_j(q)=(-1)^j\det A(q)_{\widehat j},\qquad 0\leq j\leq r.
 \label{eq:signed-minors}
\end{equation}
The equations of \(\Lambda\) vanish on \(B\), so \(tq\in\Lambda\) is
equivalent to
\[
 A(q)\bigl(\chi^{w_0}(t),\ldots,\chi^{w_r}(t)\bigr)^{\mathsf t}=0.
\]
Cramer's identity says that the kernel is generated by
\((\kappa_0(q),\ldots,\kappa_r(q))\).  The basis hypothesis gives a unique
\(t(q)\in T(\ks)\), in projective coordinates, through
\begin{equation}
 \chi^{w_j-w_0}(t(q))=\frac{\kappa_j(q)}{\kappa_0(q)},
 \qquad 1\leq j\leq r.
 \label{eq:orbit-correction}
\end{equation}
These are Laurent monomials in the displayed ratios.  Uniqueness makes
\(t(q)\) Galois equivariant.  Thus \(s(q)=t(q)q\) is a \(k\)-rational,
orbit-invariant retraction to \(\Lambda\cap Z\) on the open where the
minors do not vanish.  Hence \(\Lambda\cap Z\dashrightarrow Z/T\) is
birational.

We record the inverse and its domain.  Choose linear forms
\(\rho_0,\ldots,\rho_{n-r}\) whose restrictions give homogeneous coordinates
on the image of \(\Lambda\) in \(\PP(V/\widehat T_pZ)\).  Let
\(U_p\subset Z\) be an open on which \(\pi_p\) is an isomorphism, and let
\(U\subset U_p\) be the further open where all \(\kappa_j\),
\(\rho_0(s(q))\) is nonzero and the action is free.  Then
\begin{equation}
 \Phi:U/T\longrightarrow\PP^{n-r},\qquad
 [q]\longmapsto[\rho_0(s(q)):\cdots:\rho_{n-r}(s(q))]
 \label{eq:quotient-map}
\end{equation}
is the quotient parametrization.  If \(R_\alpha(x)\) are homogeneous
equations for \(Z\), the graph of its inverse over \(\Phi(U/T)\) is the
unique component dominating that open of
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 R_\alpha(x)&=0 &&\text{for every }\alpha,\\
 \lambda_i(x)&=0 &&1\leq i\leq r,\\
 y_j\rho_0(x)-y_0\rho_j(x)&=0 &&1\leq j\leq n-r.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:inverse-graph}
\end{equation}
Write its unique point over \(y\) as \(x(y)\) and set
\(\Theta(y)=[x(y)]\).  The last line gives
\(\Phi(\Theta(y))=y\).  Conversely, \(s(q)\in\Lambda\cap U_p\) and has the
same tangent-projection coordinates as \(x(\Phi([q]))\); injectivity of
\(\pi_p|_{U_p}\) gives
\[
 \Theta(\Phi([q]))=[s(q)]=[q]\quad\text{in }U/T.
\]
Thus both composites are the identity on the stated dense opens.  In the
Cox application, the \(R_\alpha\) in \eqref{eq:inverse-graph} are the twenty
quadrics in \cite[Section~3]{TZ}.  Equations \eqref{eq:orbit-correction}
and \eqref{eq:inverse-graph} give the rational map and its inverse without
an unrecorded finite cover.
\end{proof}

The integral-basis condition is essential.  If the weight differences span
a proper finite-index sublattice, the same equations generally define a
finite cover of a \(T\)-orbit rather than a rational section.

\section{The rank-three subtorus of the projective Cox model}

Let \(T\) be the N\'eron--Severi torus, so
\(X^*(T_{\bar k})=\Pic(S_{\bar k})\).  The Galois-invariant cocharacter
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_{-K}:\Gm\longrightarrow T,
 \qquad D\longmapsto(-K_S,D),
 \label{eq:anticanonical-cocharacter}
\end{equation}
acts by simultaneous scalar multiplication on the sixteen Cox generators.
Write \(T_0=T/\lambda_{-K}(\Gm)\), a torus of rank five.  Over \(\bar k\),
the projective Cox model
\[
 Z=\operatorname{Proj}\operatorname{Cox}(S)\subset\PP^{15}
\]
is a seven-dimensional variety with one apparent double point
\cite[Lemma~3.2]{TZ}.

Use the marking of \cite[Sections~3--4]{TZ}.  For the type \(I_3\) Galois
image, the sublattice
\begin{equation}
 N_3=\Z e_3\oplus\Z e_4\oplus\Z e_5\subset X_*(T_{0,\bar k})
 \label{eq:rank-three-lattice}
\end{equation}
is saturated and Galois stable.  The two generators act on this basis by
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}-1&0&0\\-1&0&1\\-1&1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 \begin{pmatrix}1&0&-1\\0&1&-1\\0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Let \(T_3\subset T_0\) be the corresponding rank-three subtorus.  Four
Galois-stable groups of Cox generators have the following \(T_3\)-weights:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}cl@{}}
\toprule
weight & Cox generators\\
\midrule
\((0,1,1)\) & \(L_{13},L_{23},L_{35}\)\\
\((1,0,1)\) & \(L_{14},L_{24},L_{45}\)\\
\((1,1,0)\) & \(E_1,E_2,E_5\)\\
\((1,1,1)\) & \(L_{12},L_{15},L_{25}\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Every other Cox generator lies in the Galois-stable subspace
\begin{equation}
 B=\PP\langle E_3,E_4,L_{34},Q\rangle.
 \label{eq:boundary-subspace}
\end{equation}
The determinant of the three weight differences from \((0,1,1)\) is one.

\begin{remark}[Saturation and the projective kernel]
\label{rem:saturation}
\coverage{absent}%
In the type-\(I_1\) description, the three visible sign cocharacters in the
last three coordinates are
\[
 (0,1,1),\quad(1,0,1),\quad(1,1,0).
\]
They span the even-sum sublattice of \(\Z^3\), of index two.  Its saturation
is \(\Z^3\), obtained by adjoining
\[
 (1,1,1)=\tfrac12\bigl((0,1,1)+(1,0,1)+(1,1,0)\bigr).
\]
The map from the three parameter copies of \(\Gm\) to their image in the
projective torus has kernel \(\boldsymbol\mu_2\).  The degree two obtained
from the unsaturated parameters counts this kernel; it is not the degree of
the Rosenlicht quotient.  Saturation gives the integral-basis condition in
Theorem~\ref{thm:torus-quotient}.
\end{remark}

tangent point \(p\) and a codimension-three linear subspace

\section{Proofs of the rationality statements}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-variable}]
Suppose first that \(S\) is \(k\)-minimal.  Since a stably permutation
module has vanishing \(H^1\) on every open subgroup, Proposition~4.1 and
Lemma~4.2 of \cite{TZ} place the Galois image in one of the four types used
in Proposition~\ref{prop:tangent-section}.  Lemma~3.2 and Theorem~2.4 of
\cite{TZ}, together with Theorem~\ref{thm:torus-quotient}, give
\begin{equation}
 Z/T_3\sim_k\PP^4.
 \label{eq:rational-quotient}
\end{equation}

Let \(\mathcal T\to S\) be a universal torsor under the N\'eron--Severi
torus \(T\), chosen to have a \(k\)-point above a fixed point of \(S(k)\).
The stable-permutation hypothesis and Hilbert's Theorem~90 give
\begin{equation}
 H^1(F,T)=0\quad\text{for every field extension }F/k
 \label{eq:H1-vanishing}
\end{equation}
by \cite[Remark~2.2]{TZ}.  Thus the generic fibre over \(k(S)\) has a point.
Translation by the resulting rational section \(\sigma\) defines the
\(T\)-equivariant birational map
\begin{equation}
 S\times T\dashrightarrow\mathcal T,
 \qquad (s,t)\longmapsto\sigma(s)t.
 \label{eq:equivariant-trivialization}
\end{equation}
The split scalar subtorus in \eqref{eq:anticanonical-cocharacter} acts as
common multiplication on every Cox coordinate.  Quotienting
\eqref{eq:equivariant-trivialization} by it gives a \(T_0\)-equivariant
birational map \(S\times T_0\dashrightarrow Z\).  Since
\(T_3\subset T_0\) is a \(k\)-subtorus, Rosenlicht quotients give
\begin{equation}
 Z/T_3\sim_k S\times(T_0/T_3).
 \label{eq:product-quotient}
\end{equation}
The quotient \(T_0/T_3\) is a two-dimensional \(k\)-torus, hence is
\(k\)-rational by \cite{Voskresenskii1967}.  Equations
\eqref{eq:rational-quotient} and \eqref{eq:product-quotient} prove that
\(S\times\A^2\) is \(k\)-rational.

If \(S\) is not \(k\)-minimal, a \(k\)-defined union of pairwise disjoint
exceptional curves contracts to a del Pezzo surface \(S'\) of degree at
least five.  The image of a \(k\)-point of \(S\) is a \(k\)-point of \(S'\),
so \(S'\), and therefore \(S\), is \(k\)-rational by
\cite[Theorem~2.1]{VarillyAlvarado}.

Finally, stable rationality over the infinite field \(k\) supplies a
\(k\)-point, and Manin's theorem makes \(\Pic(S_{\bar k})\) stably
permutation \cite{Manin1966}.  This proves the last assertion.
\uses{prop:tangent-section,thm:torus-quotient}%
\imports{high-degree-del-pezzo,manin-stable-permutation,tz-type-classification,tz-universal-torsor,voskresenskii-rank-two}%
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:cubics}]
For \(X=X_{j,r}\), Tschinkel and Zhang project from a plane containing a
distinguished line.  The base is \(\PP^{r+1}_{\mathbf Q}\), with function
field \(K=\mathbf Q(t_1,\ldots,t_{r+1})\), and the generic cubic-surface
fibre is \(K\)-birational, after contracting the distinguished line, to a
smooth quartic del Pezzo surface \(S/K\) with a \(K\)-point.  Their
calculation identifies the Galois image as type \(I_1\) for \(j=1\) and type
\(I_3\) for \(j=3\) \cite[Propositions~5.1--5.2]{TZ}.  Theorem
\ref{thm:two-variable} gives
\[
 K(S)(u,v)\simeq K(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4).
\]
Since \(\mathbf Q(X)=K(S)\) as extensions of \(K\),
\[
 \mathbf Q(X)(u,v)
 \simeq\mathbf Q(t_1,\ldots,t_{r+1},z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4)
\]
is purely transcendental.  This is rationality of \(X\times\A^2\),
equivalently of \(X\times\PP^2\).  The argument is an identity of function
fields; no specialization assertion is used.
\uses{thm:two-variable}%
\imports{tz-cubic-families}%
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:cancellation}]
The one-stabilization theorem states that, for every smooth complex cubic
threefold \(X\), the variety \(X\times\PP^1\) is irrational
\cite[Theorem~1.1]{RuddM1}.  It applies to \((X_j)_{\mathbf C}\).  Therefore
neither \(X_j\) nor \(X_j\times\PP^1\) can be rational over \(\mathbf Q\),
because rationality survives extension of the ground field.  Corollary
\ref{cor:cubics} gives rationality at \(m=2\) over \(\mathbf Q\), and hence
also over \(\mathbf C\).  This proves both equalities of stabilization
indices.

Set \(Y_j=X_j\times\PP^1\).  It is nonrational, while
\[
 \mathbf Q(Y_j\times\A^1)=\mathbf Q(X_j)(u,t)
\]
is purely transcendental by Corollary~\ref{cor:cubics}.  Equivalently,
\(\PP^1\times\A^1\) is birational to \(\A^2\).  Hence
\(Y_j\times\A^1\) is rational.
\uses{cor:cubics}%
\imports{rudd-one-stabilization}%
\end{proof}

\section{Verification and scope}

The cocharacter saturation, type-\(I_3\) actions, Cox weights, determinant-one
weight differences, and tangent-incidence cover use exact integer and
rational arithmetic.  The verification bundle checks all four pairs
\((D_i,M_i)\), the eight localized branches, and the B\'ezout identity above
with SymPy~1.14.0.  A separate implementation checks the saturated lattice,
the full type-\(I_3\) action, the rank-two quotient lattice, and the displayed
numerical tangent matrices.  The quotient criterion, equivariant generic
trivialization, incidence descent, and function-field argument are proved in
the text and are not delegated to those programs.

No Lean development formalizes Theorem~\ref{thm:two-variable}, Theorem
\ref{thm:torus-quotient}, Proposition~\ref{prop:tangent-section}, or either
corollary.  The formal-coverage annotations therefore say
\texttt{absent}.

The result uses Tschinkel--Zhang's universal-torsor geometry and their
classification of the four hereditary-\(H^1\) Galois images.  It does not
reprove those results and does not classify all subgroups of \(W(D_5)\).
The bounded literature audit recorded with the manuscript found no displayed
predecessor for the two-dimensional conclusion, but citation-graph closure
was unavailable.  Accordingly, the paper makes no global priority claim.
