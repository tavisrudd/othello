\section{A common master and birational transport}
\label{sec:global-transport}

The obstruction in Sections~\ref{sec:incomplete-gamma}--\ref{sec:two-wall}
comes from comparing two local receivers at an intermediate chamber.  We now
avoid that comparison.  A single equivariant cobordism carries both endpoint
quantum Kirwan maps, and virtual localization compares their point rows before
either endpoint Novikov completion is taken.

\subsection{The common point row}

\begin{definition}[Admissible marked completion]
\label{def:gauged-admissible}
A smooth projective \(\Gm\)-completion \(W\), two extreme linearizations,
and a point \(p\) in their common birational open are
\emph{gauged-admissible} if the following conditions hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item The extreme quotients \(Y_-\) and \(Y_+\) are smooth and free, and
stable equals semistable for the chamber polarizations used to define their
gauged theories, which are equivariant and ample as
Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit-cylinder-disjoint} requires.  This equality is not imposed at a critical wall
polarization; the wall is handled by the master stacks in \textup{(ii)}.
\item Degree by degree, the polarization master stacks are proper
Deligne--Mumford stacks with Woodward's relative perfect obstruction theories,
and the localized large-area formula applies coefficientwise.  This includes
the virtual Kalkman identity for the polarization sweep, with the endpoint
normalization recalled in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:support-collapse}: the two endpoint components enter with
opposite signs and a common normalization.
\item Finitely many equivariant numerical divisor classes separate every
allowed total equivariant degree, and the effective infinity-side degree
semigroup is pointed.
\item There is a class \(a_p\in H^*_{\Gm}(W)\) whose ordinary Kirwan
restrictions are the two endpoint point classes and whose restriction to
every fixed component that is semistable for some polarization interpolating
the two chamber linearizations is zero.  Its construction uses one further
input, bistability of the cylinder orbit, recorded with the other unproved
inputs of Proposition~\ref{prop:support-collapse} in Section~\ref{sec:scope}.  This \(a_p\) is the global
orbit-cylinder class of Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit-cylinder-disjoint}; it is
unrelated to the Gu--Yu--Yu wall-local class written \(a_p\) in
Section~\ref{sec:simple-wall}.
\end{enumerate}
These are assumptions on the gauged completion, not consequences of smooth
projectivity alone.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Why \textup{(iv)} is restricted to semistable fixed
components]
\label{rem:iv-semistable-restriction}
Condition \textup{(iv)} cannot be strengthened to vanishing on every
component of \(W^{\Gm}\).  For a smooth projective \(W\) with \(\Gm\)-action
the Bia{\l}ynicki--Birula decomposition is filtrable and its cells are affine
bundles over the fixed components, and the decomposition is perfect, so
\(H^*_{\Gm}(W;\Q)\) is a free module over
\(H^*(B\Gm;\Q)\), in particular torsion free.  Perfectness is not a parity
statement: the fixed components of the completions used here can carry odd
cohomology, so the connecting maps are not killed by evenness of the cell
dimensions.  It holds because each cell is an affine bundle over a smooth
projective base, so its Borel--Moore homology is pure and the weight-shifting
connecting maps vanish by strictness; equivalently, by Frankel's theorem the
moment map of the circle action on the compact K\"ahler \(W\) is a perfect
Morse--Bott function.  By the localization theorem the
kernel of the restriction
\(H^*_{\Gm}(W)\to H^*_{\Gm}(W^{\Gm})\) is a torsion \(H^*(B\Gm;\Q)\)-module;
freeness alone would not give this.  The two facts together make the
restriction injective, so a class vanishing on
every fixed component is zero and cannot have the two endpoint
point classes as its Kirwan restrictions.  The vanishing needed in the
polarization sweep is only on the fixed components that the distinguished
output evaluation can reach at an intermediate polarization.  Those components
are semistable for the corresponding interpolated linearization, which is the
reachability statement proved inside
Proposition~\ref{prop:support-collapse} from
\cite[Lemma~3.17]{GonzalezWoodward}, and on such components the vanishing is
supplied by Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit-cylinder-disjoint}\textup{(b)}.  In particular
the two limits of the cylinder orbit, whose weights stay strictly nonzero
along the whole interpolation, are outside the scope of \textup{(iv)} by
design.
\end{remark}

The four conditions have different standing.  Condition \textup{(iii)} is the
one most plausibly checkable from the geometry of a given completion, though
not automatic.  Equivariant projectivity is expected to supply the separating
divisor classes, but the separation required is of the total equivariant
degree, including the affine cocharacter degree, which the endpoint divisor
spaces need not detect; and pointedness of the infinity-side degree semigroup
is a positivity hypothesis on the chosen completion rather than a consequence
of projectivity.  Condition \textup{(ii)} has the shape of the properness,
obstruction-theory, and large-area localization package developed by
Woodward for gauged maps \cite{WoodwardQKIII}; what is assumed is that this
package applies to the polarization master stacks of the given completion at
every degree used, and no existing theorem asserts this for an arbitrary
W{\l}odarczyk completion.  Condition \textup{(i)} bundles two requirements of
different standing.  Freeness of the extreme quotients is close to what the
cobordism construction already supplies: its source and sink pieces are
punctured line-bundle opens with the two endpoint varieties as geometric
quotients \cite[Proposition~2(B')]{Wlodarczyk}.  What is not proved here is
that the semistable loci of the particular chamber linearizations used to
define the gauged theories are those opens.  Stable equals semistable is a
separate position assumption on the chamber polarizations; it is the standard
VGIT genericity condition, it does not subsume freeness, since GIT stability
requires only finite stabilizers, and we do not prove that every completion
admits polarizations satisfying it.  The class in condition \textup{(iv)} is
constructed below by the orbit-cylinder argument following
Proposition~\ref{prop:support-collapse}.

\begin{remark}[Endpoint-only hypotheses for the cubic application]
\label{rem:endpoint-only}
Theorem~\ref{thm:intro-cubic-conditional} needs gauged-admissibility and
the threshold comparisons of Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-wall}
and~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-zero} only for the birational maps
\(X\times\mathbf P^m\dashrightarrow\mathbf P^{m+3}\).  Its proof applies
Theorem~\ref{thm:birational-point-primary} once for each \(m\), to a map
supplied by the assumed rationality.  It therefore suffices to assume: for
every \(m\geq0\) and every birational map
\(\varphi\colon X\times\mathbf P^m\dashrightarrow\mathbf P^{m+3}\), some
W{\l}odarczyk completion of \(\varphi\) is gauged-admissible and satisfies
both threshold hypotheses.  The universal quantifier over maps with these
endpoints cannot be dropped, because the rationality being contradicted
supplies an unknown map.  The weakened assumption is still an infinite
family, over all \(m\), all such maps, and all Artin levels, ordinary
degrees, neutral directions, and thresholds of the chosen completions, but
it no longer quantifies over arbitrary pairs of birational varieties.
\end{remark}

Fix such a completion.  We work coefficientwise at sufficiently large area.
Write \(\kappa_\pm\) for
the corresponding quantum Kirwan maps, write
\(\operatorname{Kir}_\pm:H^*_{\Gm}(W)\to H^*(Y_\pm)\) for the ordinary
cohomological Kirwan maps, and write \(\tau_{Y_\pm,-}\) for Woodward's
localized graph potentials.  The
derivatives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-gauged-maps}
 A_\pm=D\tau_{Y_\pm,-}\,D\kappa_\pm
\end{equation}
are the endpoint localized gauged maps.  The display is a definition; what is
cited is the localized adiabatic identity
\cite[Equation~(68)]{WoodwardQKIII}, which equates the composite
\(\tau_{Y_\pm,-}\circ\kappa_\pm\) with the gauged graph potential in the
distinguished output slot, and \eqref{eq:endpoint-gauged-maps} is its
derivative in the input directions.

The marked class has the restrictions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marked-class-restrictions}
 \operatorname{Kir}_\pm(a_p)=[p_\pm],\qquad
 a_p|_F=0
\end{equation}
for every fixed component \(F\) that is semistable for some polarization
interpolating the two chamber linearizations.
Here \(p_-\) and \(p_+\) lie in the common birational open set.

\begin{lemma}[Point insertion and the Gamma row]
\label{lem:point-insertion-row}
Let \(R\) be a finite Artin quotient of the Novikov--bulk coefficient ring.
Write \(\mathcal H_Y(R)\) for the cohomological input module and
\(\mathcal S_Y(R)\) for the corresponding module of flat sections.  With
Woodward's graph-space normalization,
\[
 D\tau_{Y,-}:\mathcal H_Y(R)\longrightarrow\mathcal S_Y(R).
\]
If \(\epsilon_{Y,p}\) is the normalized Poincar\'e covector defined by a
point insertion and \(n=\dim Y\), then there is an invertible scalar
\(c_n(z)\), depending only on \(n\) and the two normalization conventions,
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:point-insertion-row}
 \rrow_{Y,p}\,D\tau_{Y,-}=c_n(z)\epsilon_{Y,p}.
\end{equation}
The insertion is at the output of \(D\tau_{Y,-}\): the point class is paired
against the flat section, which is the slot in which
\eqref{eq:endpoint-gauged-maps} is evaluated.
Consequently the endpoint functional with distinguished output insertion
\(\operatorname{Kir}(a_p)=[p]\) is
\[
 c_n(z)^{-1}\rrow_{Y,p}D\tau_{Y,-}D\kappa.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
One input is taken from the normalization and not proved here: in Woodward's
graph-space normalization, \(D\tau_{Y,-}\) is the genus-zero fundamental
solution of the quantum connection on \(Y\), and it is unitary for the
\(z\mapsto e^{-\pi i}z\)-twisted pairing \eqref{eq:flat-euler-pairing}.
Everything below is a consequence of that normalization, and a different
normalization would change only the scalar \(c_n(z)\).
The Gamma framing of \(\OO_p\) is the graph fundamental solution applied to
the top cohomology class.  The Gamma and Chern-class factors act trivially on
that class.  The remaining dimension sign, power of \(z\), and power of
\(2\pi i\) are invertible and depend only on \(n\) and on the chosen Gamma and
graph-space normalizations; their product is \(c_n(z)\).  Unitarity of the
fundamental solution then identifies pairing against \(s_Y(\OO_p)\) with
\(c_n(z)\) times the Poincar\'e point covector.  This is
\eqref{eq:point-insertion-row}, and composition with \(D\kappa\)
gives the last assertion.  The argument is coefficientwise over \(R\), so it
commutes with input and bulk differentiation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Support collapse]
\label{prop:support-collapse}
At every fixed equivariant degree, the point-marked virtual Kalkman formula
has no intermediate-polarization contribution.  Hence its two endpoint
coefficients agree in the extended Novikov coefficient-function space.
After the Rees homogenization \eqref{eq:rees-substitution} and in any common
realization of the two endpoint series, this reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:support-collapse-row}
 \rrow_{Y_-,p_-}A_- =\rrow_{Y_+,p_+}A_+.
\end{equation}
The identity is coefficientwise in all remaining Novikov and bulk
variables; the proposition does not construct the common realization.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Retain rotation of the parametrized graph curve, with equivariant parameter
\(\zeta\), as in \cite{WoodwardQKIII}; this is the same parameter that
appears in the Gamma factors below, and the wall cocharacter is written
\(\mathsf w\) to keep the two apart.
At each ordinary input marking insert the normalized equivariant
point class of \(0\in\mathbf P^1\).  Rotation localization then puts every
input on the zero-side bubble tree.  Next choose equivariant divisor classes
\(D_1,\ldots,D_s\) spanning the full equivariant numerical dual of the
allowed total degrees; their endpoint Kirwan restrictions in particular span
the endpoint numerical divisor spaces.  Insert Woodward's Liouville classes
with parameters \(t_j\).  On a graph fixed locus their contribution is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:liouville-character}
 \exp\!\left(\sum_jt_jD_j\right)
 \prod_j x_j^{D_j\cdot \widetilde d_+},
 \qquad x_j=\exp(t_j\zeta),
\end{equation}
where \(\widetilde d_+\) is the total infinity-side equivariant degree: the
bubble degree together with the affine cocharacter degree of the gauged
principal component \cite[Corollary~9.10(c)]{WoodwardQKIII}.  The variables
\(x_j\) are not independent of the \(t_j\), so the extraction is by linear
independence of characters: the total is a finite sum, over the allowed
degrees, of a polynomial in \(t\) times
\(\exp(\zeta\sum_jt_j(D_j\cdot\widetilde d_+))\), and keeping the terms whose
exponential rate vanishes identically as a linear form in \(t\) forces
\(D_j\cdot\widetilde d_+=0\) for every \(j\), hence \(\widetilde d_+=0\) by
condition \textup{(iii)}.  The infinity
factor is therefore the unstable identity: positivity on the effective
infinity-side semigroup leaves no nonconstant degree-zero component.  The
zero factor is exactly the localized graph potential in
\eqref{eq:endpoint-gauged-maps}.  Using the
full equivariant divisor lattice is important: endpoint divisor spaces alone
need not separate an affine cocharacter degree.

The distinguished class \(a_p\) is inserted at the distinguished output
evaluation of the graph factor, not at an ordinary input marking.  This is the
evaluation through which Woodward's localized graph potential is defined by
pushforward: on the locus of bundles with sections that are constant in some
trivialization near the distinguished parametrized point, it is the evaluation
there, valued in the inertia stack of the quotient
\cite[Section~9.4]{WoodwardQKIII}.  Its value is the principal-component value,
because the section is constant near that point; it is not the value at
\(0\) or \(\infty\), where the bubble trees attach and where the value is a
cocharacter limit carrying no semistability.
The ordinary inputs remain at their own markings.

For each fixed degree and sufficiently large area, a nonendpoint
polarization-fixed graph is a Mundet-semistable \(\C^\times_\mathsf w\)-fixed
object for a wall cocharacter \(\mathsf w\) --- the symbols need care
here: \cite{GonzalezWoodward} write \(\zeta\) for this cocharacter and
\(\xi\) for the equivariant parameter, while we write \(\mathsf w\) for the
cocharacter and \(\zeta\) for the equivariant parameter, following
\cite{WoodwardQKIII} --- and its principal component
maps to
\(W^{\mathsf w,t}/G_\mathsf w\), where \(W^{\mathsf w,t}\) is the locus of
\(L_t\)-semistable points of the \(\mathsf w\)-fixed locus and \(t\) runs over the
interpolation between the two chamber linearizations
\cite[Lemma~3.17 and Proposition~3.18]{GonzalezWoodward}.  In the bubble-tree
description of that fixed locus, the principal factor is a gauged-map stack
over \(W^{\mathsf w,t}\) framed at its attaching points, and the bubble factors are
glued to it along those framings
\cite[Remark~3.19]{GonzalezWoodward}; note that the framings are compared in
the ambient \(W^\mathsf w\), so it is the principal side that carries the
semistability.  The distinguished output
evaluation is a principal-component value, and therefore lands in a fixed
component which is \(L_t\)-semistable,
so \(a_p\) restricts to zero on it by
\eqref{eq:marked-class-restrictions} and the wall term vanishes
before division by the virtual normal Euler class.

The placement is what makes this work, and the weaker statement for an
arbitrary marking would not.  Proposition~3.15(c) of the same source places an
arbitrary node or marking only in the full fixed locus \(W^\mathsf w\), with no
semistability, so a marking carried off on a bubble tree may land on a fixed
component that is unstable for every interpolated polarization --- for
instance on a component containing one of the two limits of the cylinder
orbit below, where the class of
Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit-cylinder-disjoint} has no reason to restrict to zero.
Remark~\ref{rem:iv-semistable-restriction} shows that no class with the
required Kirwan restrictions vanishes on every fixed component, so the
vanishing must come from the placement of the distinguished insertion rather
than from a stronger property of the class.

Only the two endpoints survive the virtual Kalkman
identity.  The two surviving components are the gauged-map stacks at \(L_-\)
and \(L_+\).  Their normal directions in the polarization master stack are the
two ends of the master-space fibre: the obstruction theory of
\(M^G_n(C,X,L_-,L_+)\) differs from that of \(M^G_n(C,X)\) by the trivial
factor coming from the fibre of \(\mathbf P(D(L_-)\oplus D(L_+))\)
\cite[Lemma~3.21 and its proof]{GonzalezWoodward}, whose two sections carry
opposite weights.  After the residue extraction the two endpoint
contributions therefore enter with opposite signs and a common normalization,
which is why the vanishing of all wall terms gives the equality
\eqref{eq:support-collapse-row} rather than a proportionality.  We do not
exhibit the master-space normal complex; that the virtual Kalkman formula
applies with this normalization at every degree used is the content of
Definition~\ref{def:gauged-admissible}\textup{(ii)}.  At either endpoint there
is no wall, and the
distinguished output evaluation is exactly the slot through which
\(\tau_{Y_\pm,-}\) is defined, so the distinguished equivariant
insertion restricts through \(\operatorname{Kir}_\pm\), hence becomes
\([p_\pm]\) by \eqref{eq:marked-class-restrictions}; the quantum Kirwan
derivatives remain inside
\(A_\pm\).  Lemma~\ref{lem:point-insertion-row} is stated for that slot, so it
applies verbatim and identifies the resulting
endpoint covector with
\(c_{\dim Y_\pm}(z)^{-1}\rrow_{Y_\pm,p_\pm}D\tau_{Y_\pm,-}\);
differentiating the affine-gauged input gives the remaining factor
\(D\kappa_\pm\).  The
endpoints have the same dimension, so the common invertible scalar
\(c_{\dim Y_\pm}(z)\) cancels.  Thus the
coefficientwise Kalkman identity is \eqref{eq:support-collapse-row} as an
equality of functionals on
the common equivariant input module.  Applying arbitrary further input and
bulk derivatives before coefficient extraction gives the polarized row
identity, not only its value on the unit.  Those derivatives add and
differentiate ordinary input markings; they leave the distinguished output
evaluation untouched, so they commute with the insertion of \(a_p\) and both
the wall vanishing and the endpoint identification above hold unchanged under
them.
\end{proof}

For a birational map between smooth projective varieties, the required class
exists globally.  W{\l}odarczyk's smooth birational cobordism admits a smooth
projective equivariant completion whose source and sink quotients are the two
endpoints \cite[Proposition~2(B')]{Wlodarczyk}.  Choose \(p\) in the common
birational open supplied as a trivialized cylinder by the construction.
There the cobordism is a trivial orbit cylinder.  The following lemma
produces the class \(a_p\) from that orbit.  It is stated for an arbitrary
smooth projective \(\Gm\)-variety carrying the two chamber polarizations of
Definition~\ref{def:gauged-admissible}\textup{(i)}, so it applies directly
to the resolved completion.

\begin{lemma}[Orbit-cylinder disjointness]
\label{lem:orbit-cylinder-disjoint}
Let \(W\) be a smooth projective variety with a \(\Gm\)-action and two
equivariant ample linearizations \(L_-\) and \(L_+\) at which stable equals
semistable and the semistable loci are free.  For a rational
\(u\in[0,1]\), write \(L_u=L_-^{1-u}\otimes L_+^{u}\) for the interpolated
polarization, and for a fixed point \(w\) write \(\mu_u(w)\) for the weight
of the \(\Gm\)-action on the fibre of \(L_u\) at \(w\).  Let \(x\in
W^{\mathrm{ss}}(L_-)\cap W^{\mathrm{ss}}(L_+)\), let
\(O=\Gm\cdot x\), and let
\(w_0=\lim_{t\to0}t\cdot x\) and \(w_\infty=\lim_{t\to\infty}t\cdot x\) be
its Bia{\l}ynicki--Birula limits in \(W\).  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\alph*)}]
\item \(\overline O=O\cup\{w_0,w_\infty\}\), and \(w_0\ne w_\infty\) are
  fixed points;
\item if a fixed component \(F\) is semistable for some \(L_u\) with
  \(u\in[0,1]\), then \(\overline O\cap F=\emptyset\);
\item \(\overline O\cap W^{\mathrm{ss}}(L_\pm)=O\), and the equivariant
  Poincar\'e dual \(a=\mathrm{PD}[\overline O]\in H^*_{\Gm}(W)\) satisfies
  \(\operatorname{Kir}_\pm(a)=[q_\pm]\), where \(q_\pm\) are the images of
  \(x\) in the two quotients, and \(a|_F=0\) for every fixed component
  \(F\) as in \textup{(b)}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We use the numerical criterion for \(\Gm\) in the fibre-weight form: a point
\(y\) is semistable for an equivariant ample \(M\) if and only if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gm-weight-criterion}
 \mu_M(y_\infty)\leq 0\leq\mu_M(y_0),
\end{equation}
where \(y_0,y_\infty\) are its two limits and \(\mu_M\) is the fibre
weight; \(y\) is stable if and only if both inequalities are strict.  This
is Mumford's numerical criterion
\cite[Chapter~2, \S2.1, Theorem~2.1]{MumfordGIT} made explicit for the
two one-parameter subgroups of \(\Gm\): embed \(W\) equivariantly in
\(\mathbf P(V)\) by sections of a power of \(M\).  For
\(v=\sum_kv_k\) with \(t\cdot v_k=t^kv_k\), the limit of \([t\cdot v]\) at
\(t\to0\) is \([v_{k_{\min}}]\) and at \(t\to\infty\) is
\([v_{k_{\max}}]\), where \(k_{\min}<k_{\max}\) bound the support of \(v\);
the fibre of \(\OO(1)\) at the fixed point \([v_k]\) has weight \(-k\); and
\([v]\) is semistable exactly when \(k_{\min}\leq0\leq k_{\max}\).  A fixed
point is its own pair of limits, so it is semistable for \(M\) exactly when
\(\mu_M\) vanishes on it.

\textup{(a)}  The stabilizer of \(x\) is trivial because the semistable
loci are free, so \(O\cong\Gm\) and its closure in the projective \(W\) is
the image of the completed parametrization \(\mathbf P^1\to W\),
\(t\mapsto t\cdot x\); it adds exactly the two limits, which are fixed.
Strict inequalities in \eqref{eq:gm-weight-criterion} for \(L_-\) give
\(\mu_0(w_0)>0>\mu_0(w_\infty)\), so \(w_0\ne w_\infty\).

\textup{(b)}  The fibre weight \(\mu_u(w)\) is affine in \(u\).  Stability
of \(x\) at \(u=0\) and \(u=1\) gives \(\mu_u(w_0)>0\) at both ends, hence
\(\mu_u(w_0)>0\) for every \(u\in[0,1]\); likewise \(\mu_u(w_\infty)<0\) on
all of \([0,1]\).  A fixed component \(F\) semistable at \(L_u\) has
\(\mu_u(F)=0\) by the criterion applied to its points.  Since \(\mu_u\) is
constant on \(F\), neither \(w_0\) nor \(w_\infty\) lies in \(F\).  The
open orbit \(O\) contains no fixed point, so
\(\overline O\cap F=\emptyset\).

\textup{(c)}  The limits are unstable for both \(L_\pm\): their
\(\mu_u\)-values at \(u\in\{0,1\}\) are nonzero.  Hence
\(\overline O\cap W^{\mathrm{ss}}(L_\pm)=O\).  Restriction of a Poincar\'e
dual to an open invariant subset is the Poincar\'e dual of the intersected
cycle, so \(a\) restricts on \(W^{\mathrm{ss}}(L_\pm)\) to
\(\mathrm{PD}[O]\).  Under the free quotient this is the point class of the
image of \(O\), which is \([q_\pm]\).  The vanishing \(a|_F=0\) follows
from \textup{(b)}, since the restriction of \(\mathrm{PD}[\overline O]\) to
a closed subvariety disjoint from \(\overline O\) is zero.
\end{proof}

Applied to the resolved completion, with \(x\) the cylinder point over
\(p\), the lemma produces the class \(a_p\) in
\eqref{eq:marked-class-restrictions}.  The cylinder orbit is free.  That it is
semistable for both chamber polarizations is the registered input recorded in
Section~\ref{sec:scope}, and not a consequence of
Definition~\ref{def:gauged-admissible}\textup{(i)} as stated.  What makes it
the expected one is that the identification of the
extreme quotients with the two endpoints in \textup{(i)} is an identification
of birational varieties: if it restricts over the common open to the cobordism
quotients, which are defined at \(p\), then the free semistable fibre over
\(p_\pm\) is the cylinder orbit itself.  That restriction property is the
content of the registered assumption.  Every fixed component reached by
the distinguished output evaluation at an intermediate polarization is
semistable for the corresponding interpolated \(L_u\), so
clause \textup{(b)} covers every wall term in the polarization sweep.  It does
not cover the two limits \(w_0,w_\infty\), whose weights stay strictly nonzero
along the whole interpolation by clause \textup{(c)}, and by
Remark~\ref{rem:iv-semistable-restriction} no admissible class could make them
vanish; this is why the distinguished insertion sits at the output evaluation.
The
lemma answers the completion question intrinsically: its hypotheses are
conditions on the resolved completion itself, namely
Definition~\ref{def:gauged-admissible}\textup{(i)} and bistability of the
cylinder orbit, so no compatibility between the resolution and the orbit
limits is needed.  In particular it does not matter that the limits
\(w_0,w_\infty\) lie outside the cylinder over which the resolution is an
isomorphism; the numerical criterion is evaluated upstairs.  This assertion
concerns the ordinary Kirwan
restrictions only; no vanishing of positive-degree corrections to the quantum
Kirwan maps is used.  The cited cobordism theorem supplies the underlying
completion and cylinder; it does not by itself verify conditions
\textup{(i)}--\textup{(iii)} of
Definition~\ref{def:gauged-admissible}.  Those conditions remain explicit
assumptions in the global theorem.

There is a terminology point in the source.  W{\l}odarczyk calls the open
cobordism \emph{projective} when it is quasiprojective.  We use the resolved
proper equivariant completion constructed in the proof of
Proposition~2(B'): the equivariant projective completion of the glued
double line-bundle space, followed by its canonical equivariant resolution.
The glued space is smooth, being glued from two line bundles over smooth
varieties, and a canonical resolution is functorial and therefore an
isomorphism over the smooth locus --- a standard property of canonical
resolution, not part of the cited proposition.  So the resolution is an
isomorphism
over it, in particular over the source, sink, and common-open cylinder.
Thus the extreme quotients and the cylinder orbit are unchanged.
Lemma~\ref{lem:orbit-cylinder-disjoint} uses nothing else about the
completion.

\subsection{Neutral tails and threshold comparison}

The two maps in \eqref{eq:endpoint-gauged-maps} initially belong to opposite
Novikov completions.  The
comparison needed for the point-row Boolean is smaller than a common field
for the complete source.  Fix a finite ample-energy and bulk Artin quotient
of the equivariant Novikov ring.  For a primitive affine gauge direction
\(\delta\), all ordinary curve degrees and fixed graph types are then finite;
only the integer coordinate along \(\delta\) can remain unbounded.  We first
control those unbounded tails.

The argument separates the fixed geometry from the remaining analytic
comparison.  A rank-one derived-intersection theorem makes the fixed clutching
data constant along each affine tail; the moving normal weights then give a
Gamma recurrence.  Marked threshold compatibility subsequently joins the resulting
cyclic \(z\)-modules across the finitely many changes of sign, stability, and
zero-mode rank.

Call the direction neutral when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:neutral-direction}
 c_1^{\Gm}(TW)\cdot\delta=0.
\end{equation}
We compare formal monodromy after the Rees substitution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rees-substitution}
 Q^d\longmapsto z^{c_1^{\Gm}(TW)\cdot d}Q^d.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Gamma-ratio reduction]
\label{prop:gamma-ratio-reduction}
At any finite Artin level, every individual rotation-fixed graph coefficient
in a neutral direction is a finite nilpotent derivative of a vector-valued
balanced Gamma-ratio kernel.  Its total affine slope is
\(c_1^{\Gm}(TW)\cdot\delta\).  In a nonneutral direction, every homogeneous
coefficient is Laurent-finite.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Woodward's classification of the rotation-fixed graph locus is exhaustive
for sufficiently large area \cite[Corollary~9.10]{WoodwardQKIII}.  His
virtual-normal splitting gives, in the signed Euler-class form of
\cite[equations~(59) and~(64)]{WoodwardQKIII},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:virtual-normal-euler}
 \operatorname{Eul}(N_\pm)=
 \operatorname{Eul}\!\left(
  (R\pi_*\operatorname{ev}^*T(W/\Gm))_{\mathrm{mov}}
 \right)(\mp\zeta)(\mp\zeta-\psi).
\end{equation}
The last two factors move the attaching point and smooth the node,
respectively.  They are
independent of the affine gauge degree, so gluing neither removes one end of
the degree lattice nor creates a degree-dependent pole.  Formula
\eqref{eq:virtual-normal-euler}
applies when a nontrivial attached component is present; in the irreducible
unstable type those factors are absent and only the moving index remains
\cite[Example~9.15]{WoodwardQKIII}.

Pass to a finite cover which clears stabilizer denominators and apply the
splitting principle; the domain there is an ordinary line, and slopes are
counted per unit of the primitive affine direction \(\delta\) on both sides of
the identity below, while consecutive degrees inside a tail differ by the
stabilizer order of Proposition~\ref{prop:app-mu-k}\textup{(b)}.  The index
downstairs is the invariant part of the index on that cover, and the age
contributions of the orbifold Riemann--Roch formula are bounded independently
of the affine degree, so they enter the rank as a constant and cannot change a
coefficient of \(k\).  A virtual
line in the moving index of the principal
weighted component has Chern root \(\alpha_a\) and degree
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine-moving-degree}
 n_a(k)=h_ak+s_a.
\end{equation}
For every integer \(n\), its inverse index Euler factor is the single
meromorphic expression
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-index-factor}
 \frac{1}{\prod_{m=0}^{n}(\alpha_a+m\zeta)}
 =\zeta^{-n-1}
   \frac{\Gamma(\alpha_a/\zeta)}
        {\Gamma(\alpha_a/\zeta+n+1)}.
\end{equation}
For \(n<0\), the left side denotes the Euler class of \(H^1\); for
\(n\geq0\), it denotes the inverse Euler class of \(H^0\).  Thus the two
degree rays are not assigned unrelated Gamma products.  The root
\(\alpha_a\) is a moving root: it carries a nonzero equivariant weight, and
only its correction is nilpotent at Artin level.  This is what makes the
\(m=0\) factor invertible, and it is the standing hypothesis that the virtual
normal Euler class is invertible, which is implied by
Definition~\ref{def:gauged-admissible}\textup{(ii)}, since virtual
localization inverts that class; a purely nilpotent root would not be.

The total affine slope of the moving index carries no ray sign:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:total-moving-slope}
 \sum_a h_a=c_1^{\Gm}(TW)\cdot\delta,
\end{equation}
where the integers \(h_a\) themselves may have either sign.  Indeed the
virtual line with degree \eqref{eq:affine-moving-degree} contributes
\(\chi(\OO(n_a))=n_a+1\) to the virtual rank on both degree rays: for
\(n_a<0\) the group \(H^1\) replaces \(H^0\), and its negative contribution to
the index is already what makes \(\chi=n_a+1\).  Summing the slopes of these
ranks is the equivariant Riemann--Roch statement
\eqref{eq:total-moving-slope}, with no further factor of
\(\operatorname{sign}(n_a)\) inserted.
The gauge Lie algebra has affine slope zero, and so does the fixed part of the
index, which Theorem~\ref{thm:tailwise-derived} identifies from degree to
degree along the tail; the whole affine slope therefore sits in the moving
index.  Moving modes on attached fixed
bubbles have fixed rank at the chosen ordinary degree.  Their Euler factors are
finite products of linear functions of \(k\), possibly inverted.  Each
inverse factor is an adjacent Gamma ratio
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adjacent-gamma-ratio}
 (hk+a)^{-1}=\frac{\Gamma(hk+a)}{\Gamma(hk+a+1)},
\end{equation}
so it has zero affine slope and does not alter
\eqref{eq:total-moving-slope}.  Equivariant inputs
are polynomial in \(k\); Chern roots and psi classes are nilpotent at Artin
level; Liouville classes supply the Fourier exponential.

Under \eqref{eq:neutral-direction}, equations
\eqref{eq:virtual-normal-euler}--\eqref{eq:adjacent-gamma-ratio} therefore
give a balanced Gamma-ratio kernel coefficientwise.  Polynomial equivariant
insertions are Fourier
derivatives, while nilpotent Chern-root and bulk shifts give only finitely
many parameter derivatives.  For comparison, one moving-character residue
has the exact normalization
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simple-gamma-residue}
 \operatorname*{Res}_{h\sigma+\alpha=-m}
 \Gamma(h\sigma+\alpha)\,d\sigma
 =\frac{(-1)^m}{h\,m!}.
\end{equation}
The factorial is the moving-mode Euler factor, \(1/h\) is the
one-character Jacobian factor, and the sign is the complex orientation.

If \eqref{eq:neutral-direction} fails, the virtual-dimension equation fixes
\(k\) in each homogeneous
coefficient, hence the coefficient is Laurent-finite.  This proves the last
assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Higher-pole localization boundary]
\label{rem:higher-pole-localization-boundary}
Equation~\eqref{eq:simple-gamma-residue} is used only to normalize one simple
moving-character pole.  Coincident or higher poles require derivatives and
the full localization expression, including graph automorphism factors; no
product-of-simple-residues formula is used or asserted.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Exact analytic boundary]
\label{rem:neutral-boundary}
Aleshkin--Liu prove the relevant contour statement for a
finite-dimensional linear abelian GLSM with adjacent secondary-fan chambers,
a circuit, Calabi--Yau charges, and a grade-restriction window
\cite[Definition~5.18, Remark~5.20 and Theorem~5.21]{AleshkinLiu}.  Proposition
\ref{prop:gamma-ratio-reduction} does not construct those data for a nonlinear
virtual fixed graph.  Their theorem therefore supplies neither the
derived-intersection argument below nor the marked threshold comparisons.

Gonz\'alez--Woodward's crepant wall-crossing theorem gives a weaker
distributional statement: neutral Picard shifts produce sums of derivatives
of delta distributions
\cite[Remark~1.18(d), Remark~4.6, and equation~(47)]{GonzalezWoodward},
and the authors explicitly do not deduce analytic continuation without an
independent convergence input \cite[Remark~4.7]{GonzalezWoodward}.  This does
not replace Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-wall}
and~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-zero}.  Proposition
\ref{prop:punctual-corner} exhibits the underlying obstruction: information
supported at a Fourier boundary can reappear with full support after inverse
transform, so almost-everywhere equality does not identify the marked formal
packet.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Rank-one tailwise derived identification]
\label{thm:tailwise-derived}
For every finite Artin level, ordinary bubble degree, and rotation-fixed
graph type, after splitting the affine degrees into stabilizer congruence
classes and removing the finite sign, stability, and zero-mode thresholds,
there are isomorphisms of the fixed clutching stacks and their universal
domains which identify consecutive degrees in each remaining tail.  These
isomorphisms identify the evaluation maps and the fixed parts of the relative
perfect obstruction theories as
morphisms to the corresponding cotangent complexes, hence also their virtual
classes.  They respect stack automorphisms, gluing over the inertia quotient,
and reduction between Artin levels.  Stable degree-zero attached bubbles are
retained as fixed stable-map factors; the irreducible unstable type is treated
by its separate convention.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix the generic semistable locus and the two endpoint fixed components
specified by the graph type.  Write \(W_F^a\) for the corresponding
Bia{\l}ynicki--Birula stratum: its points lie in that generic locus and satisfy
\(\lim_{t\to0}t^a x\in F\).  Evaluation at \(1\) identifies fixed sections on
one parametrized affine chart with \(W_F^a\): the section determined by
\(x\) is \(z\mapsto z^a x\), and it extends across the origin with limit in
\(F\) exactly when \(x\in W_F^a\).  A principal clutching section has two such
extensions with the same generic value.  Let \(\mathfrak S_{a_-,a_+}\) denote
the derived fixed section stack for the chosen clutching bundles, stabilizer
lift, and graph component.  Evaluation at \(1\) is a functorial equivalence on
derived bases
\[
 \mathfrak S_{a_-,a_+}\simeq
 \mathfrak Z:=
 [W_{F_-}^{a_-}/\Gm]\mathop{\times}^{\mathbf R}_{[W/\Gm]}
 [W_{F_+}^{a_+}/\Gm].
\]
Here is an affine verification of the derived assertion.  On a
\(\Gm\)-invariant Sumihiro chart \(\operatorname{Spec} A\), write
\(A=\bigoplus_w A_w\).  For a derived test algebra \(R\), an equivariant map
from the parametrized affine chart is a graded morphism
\(A\to R[z]\); after evaluation at \(1\), a homogeneous element of weight
\(w\) has the unique extension \(f\mapsto f(x)z^{aw}\).  Regularity at the
origin says exactly that the coefficients with \(aw<0\) vanish.  Thus the
functor factors through the derived base change of the attractor algebra
\(A/(A_w:aw<0)\), with the component and semistability open imposed.  This
calculation is valid for simplicial commutative test algebras degreewise,
commutes with base change, and glues over the equivariant affine cover.  After
quotienting by change of trivialization it proves the one-chart equivalence
with \([W_F^a/\Gm]\), including stabilizers and nilpotent derived families.
Derived descent over the two-chart cover then gives the displayed homotopy
fibre product.  Its inverse equips a family \(x\) with the prescribed two
clutching bundles and the sections \(z\mapsto z^{a_\pm}x\).  The chosen strata
impose the generic stability and graph-component conditions.
Appendix~\ref{app:attractor} carries out this affine verification, together
with chart independence and the descent step.
The attached stable-map factors in Woodward's equations (54)--(57) are added
by derived fibre products over the two inertia evaluations.

For \(\Gm\), replacing a nonzero endpoint exponent by another integer of the
same sign leaves the relevant stratum and limit map unchanged.  Passing to a
fixed stabilizer congruence class also fixes the inertia label.  Thus the
displayed derived intersection, its universal domain and evaluations, and its
derived fibre products with the fixed ordinary stable-map factors are
identical throughout one tail.  The universal gauged maps
\(z\mapsto z^a x\) themselves are not identified when \(a\) changes; it
remains to compare the fixed deformation complexes.

All tangent and cotangent complexes in the next calculation are relative to
the fixed moduli of marked domains, denoted \(\mathfrak M\).  The tangent
complex of the displayed derived intersection is
\[
 \left[
  T(W_{F_-}^{a_-}/\Gm)\oplus T(W_{F_+}^{a_+}/\Gm)
  \longrightarrow T(W/\Gm)
 \right].
\]
It retains the degree-one term when the two attracting loci meet
nontransversely.  Equivalently, trivialize the pulled-back tangent complex on
the open orbit by evaluation at \(1\).  Taking invariants in the two-chart
\v{C}ech resolution gives
\[
 \left[
  F^0_{\leq0}V\oplus F^\infty_{\leq0}V\longrightarrow V
 \right],
\]
where the two terms are the extension subbundles at the endpoints.  They are
the tangent bundles of the two attracting loci.  Thus this invariant
\v{C}ech complex agrees with the preceding tangent complex without choosing
an equivariant splitting.  More precisely, in perfect complexes on
\(\mathfrak Z\), functoriality of restriction to the two charts gives
\[
 \left(R\pi_*u_a^*T(W/\Gm)\right)^{\mathrm{rot}}
 \simeq
 \left[F^0_{\leq0}V\oplus F^\infty_{\leq0}V\longrightarrow V\right]
 \simeq T_{\mathfrak Z/\mathfrak M}.
\]
The composite is the differential of evaluation at \(1\): on the two-chart
cover, both it and the tangent map of the derived fibre product are induced by
the same restriction morphisms to the open orbit.  This identifies the
complex together with its canonical map to the tangent complex, not only its
class in \(K\)-theory.  Along a tail the signs of all endpoint weights are
constant, and the zero-weight changes were removed as thresholds.  The two
extension subbundles and the restriction morphism are therefore constant,
even though the universal gauged map varies with \(a\).

Put
\(E_a=((R\pi_*u_a^*T(W/\Gm))^{\mathrm{rot}})^\vee\).  Woodward's relative
perfect obstruction theory is the natural truncation map
\(E_a\to L_{t_0\mathfrak S_{a_-,a_+}/\mathfrak M}\)
\cite[Definition~7.13]{WoodwardQKIII}.  The mapping-stack cotangent formula
identifies \(E_a\) with
\(\tau_{[-1,0]}L_{\mathfrak S_{a_-,a_+}/\mathfrak M}\).  Under the derived
equivalence \(\operatorname{ev}_1:\mathfrak S_{a_-,a_+}\simeq\mathfrak Z\),
the comparison is the following \(2\)-commutative square:
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
 E_a & \longrightarrow & L_{t_0\mathfrak S_{a_-,a_+}/\mathfrak M}\\
 \big\downarrow\scriptstyle\sim && \big\downarrow\scriptstyle\sim\\
 \tau_{[-1,0]}L_{\mathfrak Z/\mathfrak M}
   & \longrightarrow & L_{t_0\mathfrak Z/\mathfrak M}.
\end{array}
\]
The lower arrow is the canonical truncation map.  The square is the
naturality square of the truncation transformation evaluated at the
equivalence \(\operatorname{ev}_1\), which is what makes it
\(2\)-commutative; the two-chart \v{C}ech presentation additionally
identifies the left vertical arrow strictly, both sides being the same
difference of restrictions to the open orbit
(Proposition~\ref{prop:app-square}).  The left vertical arrow is therefore a
quasi-isomorphism, and the two perfect obstruction theories are isomorphic as
morphisms to the cotangent complex, not merely as perfect complexes.
Appendix~\ref{app:square} also records what the argument imports, what it
fixes by convention, and what it proves internally.

For an attached stable-map factor, the fixed graph stack is a derived fibre
product over its inertia evaluation.  Its cotangent triangle is the direct
sum of the two factor obstruction theories with the pullback of the inertia
cotangent complex removed.  The comparison above is the identity on the
stable-map and inertia terms, so it gives an isomorphism of these exact
triangles.  This is the perfect-obstruction-theory compatibility used in
Woodward's cutting formula
\cite[Proposition~7.14]{WoodwardQKIII}, and it identifies the virtual classes.

On a \(k\)-fold orbifold chart the same calculation takes place on
\([\operatorname{Spec}R[z^{1/k}]/\mu_k]\).  In characteristic zero,
\(\mu_k\)-invariants are exact.  Fixing the stabilizer congruence fixes the
graded universal bundle and its twisted-sector evaluation, so the preceding
equivalence and the \(2\)-commutative obstruction-theory square are
\(\mu_k\)-equivariant.  Rigidification quotients both sides by the same finite
central gerbe; pullback to that gerbe is conservative, and hence the
equivalence and obstruction theory descend.  Stabilizer and graph-automorphism
groups, including their localization multiplicities, are unchanged.  The
parametrized principal component has no domain automorphisms, while the fixed
bubble factors retain theirs.  Appendix~\ref{app:rigidification} expands the
orbifold chart, the invariants, and the descent along the gerbe.

For the irreducible unstable convention there is no stable-map factor.  Its
fixed deformation complex is exactly
\([F^0_{\leq0}V\oplus F^\infty_{\leq0}V\to V]\); the moving complement is
the normal index, and the node and attaching factors are absent.  Thus the
same square applies without a gluing term.  Every construction above is a
finite derived base change, so it commutes with Artin reduction.  This proves
every assertion.
\end{proof}

% anchor for cross-references and for the release-surface gate.  The
% statement says (D)-finite, which is what is proved.
\begin{proposition}[Clutching-tail \((D)\)-finiteness]
\label{prop:clutching-tail-holonomicity}
Every point-marked endpoint tail is a finite nilpotent derivative of one
Gamma-ratio recurrence with affine arguments.  Its generating series is
\((D)\)-finite over the finite Artin coefficient algebra: it satisfies a linear
recurrence with polynomial coefficients in the degree.  We do not say
\emph{holonomic}, which has no standard meaning over a coefficient ring with
nilpotents.  In a neutral direction its coefficients have
polynomial-logarithmic growth after one exponential rescaling, so the tail
has radial boundary values which are distributions of finite order.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~9.10 and equations (54), (56), and (57) of
\cite{WoodwardQKIII} express a rotation-fixed component as a fibre product of
two clutching factors over the inertia quotient.  Each factor already
contains the complete ordinary stable-map space attached at that end.  Thus
arbitrary bubble trees occur in fixed proper stable-map factors once their
ordinary degrees and markings are fixed; no induction over bubble-tree edges
is required.  Theorem~\ref{thm:tailwise-derived} supplies the degree-shift
identification of those fixed factors, their obstruction theories, and their
evaluations.  This is stronger than the normal-complex identity in equations
(58)--(59) of \cite{WoodwardQKIII}.

For \(G=\Gm\), an affine clutching cocharacter is an integer.  On one of the
identified tails, apply the splitting principle to the moving part of the
normal complex.  A character root of weight \(h\) has degree \(hk+s\), so
the complementary moving weights give \eqref{eq:gamma-index-factor}, with
moving Chern roots whose corrections are nilpotent on
the fixed coefficient stack.  Stable attached components, including
degree-zero bubbles stabilized by markings and nodes, retain the node and
attaching factors in \eqref{eq:virtual-normal-euler}.  Only the irreducible
unstable type has no such
factors.  They are adjacent Gamma ratios and do not change the total affine
slope \eqref{eq:total-moving-slope}.

Only finitely many parameter derivatives are therefore needed.  Gamma
arguments do not cross a pole inside a tail, since every zero-mode crossing
was removed as a threshold.  Clearing recurrence denominators is therefore
legitimate there.  If \(c_k\in R_N^r\) is the resulting virtual pushforward,
Gamma recurrence gives a rational finite-state recurrence in \(k\).  Clearing
its denominators yields
\[
 \sum_{i=0}^d p_i(k)c_{k+i}=0,
 \qquad p_i(k)\in R_N[k],
\]
on the tail, with scalar \(p_i\): fix a \(\C\)-basis of \(R_N\), view the
Gamma recurrence as a first-order system of size \(r\dim_{\C}R_N\) over
\(\C(k)\), and take a linear relation among the first \(r\dim_{\C}R_N+1\)
shifts.  Hence \(F(x)=\sum_{k\geq k_0}c_kx^k\) is \((D)\)-finite over
\(R_N\).  Finite Chern-root, input, and bulk derivatives commute with this
identity and preserve \((D)\)-finiteness.  Under
\eqref{eq:neutral-direction}, Stirling's
formula cancels the
\(k\log k\) terms.  Sizes here are those of the coefficients in a fixed
\(\C\)-basis of \(R_N\) after specializing the rotation parameter \(\zeta\) to
a nonzero complex number, so the estimate is componentwise and the nilpotent
parts of the moving roots contribute only polylogarithmically bounded factors,
which the error terms below absorb.  The cancellation
is uniform in
the two degree rays
because each virtual line contributes the same expression to \(\log|c_k|\) on
both of them.  Write \(x_a=\alpha_a/\zeta\).  For \(n_a\geq0\) the factor
\eqref{eq:gamma-index-factor} is an inverse \(H^0\) Euler class and has
log-magnitude
\[
 -\sum_{m=0}^{n_a}\log|\alpha_a+m\zeta|
 =-(n_a+1)\log|\zeta|-\sum_{m=0}^{n_a}\log|m+x_a|,
\]
which is \(-n_a\log n_a+n_a-n_a\log|\zeta|+O(\log n_a)\),
and for \(n_a<0\) the
\(H^1\) Euler class \(\prod_{m=n_a+1}^{-1}(\alpha_a+m\zeta)\), a product of
\(|n_a|-1\) factors, has
log-magnitude \(|n_a|\log|n_a|-|n_a|+|n_a|\log|\zeta|+O(\log|n_a|)
=-n_a\log|n_a|+n_a-n_a\log|\zeta|+O(\log|n_a|)\).  The two rays therefore
contribute the same expression, the \(\zeta\)-scale term included.  With
\eqref{eq:affine-moving-degree} this gives
\[
 \log|c_k|=
 -\Bigl(\sum_ah_a\Bigr)k\log k
 -k\sum_ah_a\log|h_a|
 +\Bigl(\sum_ah_a\Bigr)k
 -\Bigl(\sum_ah_a\Bigr)k\log|\zeta|
 +O(\log k),
\]
where the sums run over the roots with \(h_a\neq0\); a root with \(h_a=0\) has
constant degree along the tail and contributes a bounded factor.  The last
displayed term is the one contributed by the scale of \(\zeta\); it is written
out because the display is the general expansion, before neutrality is
imposed.  Thus
\eqref{eq:total-moving-slope} together with \eqref{eq:neutral-direction}
kills the \(k\log k\) term, the term \(\bigl(\sum_ah_a\bigr)k\), and that
scale term; the remaining term \(-k\sum_ah_a\log|h_a|\) is also linear in
\(k\) and is not killed.  That remaining
exponential rate \(-\sum_ah_a\log|h_a|\) is removed by one rescaling, after
which the coefficients grow by a power of \(k\) and
\(\log k\).  After that rescaling, radial limits of \(F\) therefore define
finite-order distributions, with polynomial-logarithmic bounds in the radial
parameter.  This is the meaning of tempered radial boundary values here, and
the implication is elementary.  Work componentwise in a \(\C\)-basis of
\(R_N\) and suppose \(|c_k|\leq Ck^N(\log k)^M\) for \(k\geq2\).  Choose an
integer \(q>N+2\) and set \(b_k=c_k/(ik)^q\) for \(k\neq0\) and \(b_0=0\); the
one omitted term is a constant and changes nothing.  Then
\(\sum_kb_k\) converges absolutely, so \(\sum_kb_kr^ke^{ik\theta}\) converges
uniformly in \(\theta\) for \(r\leq1\) and its radial limit at \(r=1\) is a
continuous function of \(\theta\); applying \(\partial_\theta^q\) recovers
\(\sum_kc_kr^ke^{ik\theta}\).  A uniform limit of continuous functions
differentiated \(q\) times converges in \(\mathcal D'(S^1)\), so the radial
limit exists there and has order at most \(q\).  The order depends only on the
exponent \(N\), and at a fixed Artin level the finitely many tails, threshold
classes, and basis components admit a common \(N\), so one \(q\) serves all of
them.
Finite threshold terms are Laurent polynomials and do not alter the
conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Two-tail threshold obstruction]
\label{rem:two-tail-threshold-obstruction}
Tailwise \((D)\)-finiteness does not identify the two selected solutions.  The
bilateral sequence
\[
 c_k=\begin{cases}2^k,&k\geq0,\\3^{-k},&k<0\end{cases}
\]
has oriented germs
\[
 F_0(x)=\frac1{1-2x},\qquad F_\infty(x)=\frac3{x-3}.
\]
Both tails are first-order hypergeometric and tempered on their natural
boundary circles.  Away from the single threshold, the bilateral sequence
is annihilated by \((E-2)(E-1/3)\); multiplying by a polynomial in the index
absorbs the finite defect.  Nevertheless the two displayed rational
functions are not branches of one meromorphic function.  Thus neither a
polynomial recurrence nor tailwise \((D)\)-finiteness determines the finite
connection data across a threshold.
\end{remark}

Fix a finite local Artin coefficient algebra \(R_N\), a ramified admissible
sector \(S\), and the duality convention of Section~\ref{sec:point-row}.  Let
\(\mathcal C_N(S)\) be the exact category of finite locally free dual Rees
\(z\)-differential modules on \(S\) with good Stokes filtration and ramified
deck action; morphisms are horizontal and strict for the Stokes and Rees
filtrations.  We use nearby and vanishing cycles only for the specified
meromorphic families in Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-zero}; their
existence, strictness, and finite local freeness are part of that hypothesis.  They are
taken after passage to the dual category, so our convention is
\(\operatorname{var}:\phi_tM\to\psi_tM\); dualizing first exchanges the
usual canonical and variation maps.

\begin{definition}[Finite dual cyclic Rees module]
\label{def:finite-dual-cyclic-rees}
If \(L\subset H\) is a submodule of an ambient Rees--Stokes module, its
\emph{strict Rees--Stokes saturation} is
\[
 \operatorname{Sat}^{\mathrm{str}}_H(L)
 :=H\cap L[z^{-1}]\subset H[z^{-1}],
\]
equipped with the induced Stokes filtration
\(F_\alpha\operatorname{Sat}^{\mathrm{str}}_H(L)
=\operatorname{Sat}^{\mathrm{str}}_H(L)\cap F_\alpha H\).
Whenever this operation is used below, finite local freeness of the result and
strictness of its inclusion are part of
Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-wall}
and~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-zero}.

Let \(T\) be normalized formal monodromy on an ambient object of rank \(d\),
let \(G\) be its finite ramified deck group, and let \(r\) be the marked
horizontal row.  A \emph{finite dual cyclic Rees \(z\)-differential module}
is the strict Rees--Stokes saturation, inside that ambient object, of the
finite submodule generated by
\[
 \{g(rT^k):g\in G,\ 0\leq k<d\}.
\]
The threshold hypotheses require this saturation to be finite locally free.
Cayley--Hamilton makes it stable under \(T\), and it inherits the connection;
it is not generated by applying the connection to a horizontal section.  Its
distinguished formal-monodromy Krylov subspace is \(C_T(r)\) from
Lemma~\ref{lem:cyclic-row-support}.
\end{definition}

\begin{hypothesis}[Marked threshold compatibility: sign and stability
thresholds]
\label{hyp:marked-threshold-wall}
At every finite Artin level and fixed ordinary degree, choose a common
ramified admissible \(z\)-sector.  At a sign or stability threshold, let
\(K_j^-\) and \(K_j^+\) be the finite dual cyclic Rees
\(z\)-differential modules of the two adjacent tails.  There must be a
simply connected local domain \(U_j\) in the Fourier variable, with oriented
cuts fixed, and one marked finite locally free Rees module
\((\mathcal K_j,\mathfrak r_j)\) on \(U_j\times S\).  It carries compatible
integrable connections in the Fourier and \(z\) directions, a relative good
Stokes filtration, and deck action; its two oriented boundary restrictions
are the adjacent cyclic modules with their marked rows.  Choose an oriented
path \(\gamma_j\subset U_j\) between those restrictions.  The threshold map
is parallel transport along \(\gamma_j\); it is not an arbitrary isomorphism
of two abstract cyclic modules.  The boundary identifications use the common
equivariant-input module on which \eqref{eq:support-collapse-row} is stated and
are the identity on its marked coefficient functions and their chosen input
and bulk derivatives; a wall-dependent change of input frame is not allowed.
Remark~\ref{rem:two-tail-threshold-obstruction} explains why tailwise
\((D)\)-finiteness cannot replace this one-object condition.

Denote by \(r_j^\pm\) the marked rows and by \(T_j^\pm\) the
half-Tate-normalized formal monodromies of \(K_j^\pm\).  The one-object
continuation induces an invertible comparison
\begin{equation}\label{eq:marked-threshold-map}
 \Phi_j:K_j^-\xrightarrow{\sim}K_j^+,
 \qquad
 \Phi_jT_j^-=T_j^+\Phi_j,
 \qquad \Phi_j(r_j^-)=r_j^+.
\end{equation}
It is horizontal for the tail and \(z\)-connections, preserves the chosen
Stokes filtration, is equivariant for the ramified deck action, and commutes
with input and bulk derivatives, quotient maps between Artin levels, and the
Rees substitution \eqref{eq:rees-substitution}.  It identifies the marked
boundary germs of the two adjacent \((D)\)-finite tails constructed in
Proposition~\ref{prop:clutching-tail-holonomicity}.  These maps are
compatible over the complete separated inverse system of Artin quotients.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{definition}[Reduced nearby cycles of a marked meromorphic family]
\label{def:reduced-nearby}
Let \(\mathcal M\) be a specified marked meromorphic Rees family in a
transverse parameter \(t\), with ramified Stokes-filtered nearby- and
vanishing-cycle functors in the standard triangle
\[
 i^*\mathcal M\longrightarrow\psi_t\mathcal M
 \xrightarrow{\operatorname{can}}\phi_t\mathcal M
 \xrightarrow{+1},
 \qquad
 \operatorname{var}:\phi_t\mathcal M\longrightarrow\psi_t\mathcal M.
\]
Let \(d\) be the rank of \(\psi_t\mathcal M\) and let
\(V_t(\mathcal M)\subset\psi_t\mathcal M\) be the strict
Rees--Stokes saturation of the
finite submodule generated by
\[
 \{g(T^k v):g\in G,\ 0\leq k<d,\
   v\in\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{var})\}.
\]
Cayley--Hamilton gives stability under formal monodromy, while the full
\(G\)-orbit in the generators gives deck stability.
The \emph{reduced nearby cycles} of \(\mathcal M\) are
\[
 \psi_t^{\mathrm{red}}\mathcal M
 :=\psi_t\mathcal M/V_t(\mathcal M).
\]
Thus reduced nearby cycles are defined by the nearby/vanishing-cycle triangle,
not by a choice of a complementary summand.  The usual
canonical/variation relation makes \(V_t(\mathcal M)\) the canonical
monodromy--deck-stable part generated by vanishing variation.  Whenever this
construction is used below, strictness of the inclusion
\(V_t(\mathcal M)\subset\psi_t\mathcal M\) and finite local freeness of the
quotient over \(R_N\) are part of
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-zero}; functoriality of
\(\operatorname{var}\) then makes the induced monodromy and deck actions
well defined.
\end{definition}

\begin{hypothesis}[Marked threshold compatibility: zero modes]
\label{hyp:marked-threshold-zero}
At a zero-mode rank change, choose a transverse parameter \(t\) and a
specified marked meromorphic Rees family \(\mathcal M_j\) whose two punctured
sectorial boundary germs are the adjacent modules \(M_j^-\) and \(M_j^+\),
with the same fixed equivariant-input coordinate identifications.  The
inclusion \(V_t(\mathcal M_j)\subset\psi_t\mathcal M_j\) of
Definition~\ref{def:reduced-nearby} must be strict, and the reduced nearby
cycles \(\psi_t^{\mathrm{red}}\mathcal M_j\) must be finite locally free
over \(R_N\).  Let \(K_j^\pm\) be the strict Rees--Stokes saturations of the finite
monodromy--deck orbits of the two specialized marked rows in the single object
\(\psi_t^{\mathrm{red}}\mathcal M_j\).  Let
\(\widetilde K_j^\pm\subset M_j^\pm\) be
the row-generated cyclic modules on the adjacent tails.  Specialization is
required to induce strict isomorphisms
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zero-mode-specialization}
 \operatorname{sp}_j^\pm:\widetilde K_j^\pm
   \xrightarrow{\sim}K_j^\pm
\end{equation}
in \(\mathcal C_N(S)\).  These isomorphisms carry the marked rows to their
specializations and intertwine the \(z\)-connection, formal monodromy, Stokes
filtration, and deck action.  Thus no statement about an individual primary
projection is included in the zero-mode assumption.

Denote by \(r_j^\pm\) the specialized marked rows and by \(T_j^\pm\) the
half-Tate-normalized formal monodromies in
\(\psi_t^{\mathrm{red}}\mathcal M_j\).  The one-object continuation through
\(\mathcal M_j\) must induce an
invertible comparison \(\Phi_j:K_j^-\xrightarrow{\sim}K_j^+\) satisfying
\eqref{eq:marked-threshold-map} together with every property listed after it
in Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-wall}.  Here \(\Phi_j\) compares the
two row-generated reduced nearby-cycle modules, not ambient modules of
unequal rank.  Together with \eqref{eq:zero-mode-specialization}, it
therefore gives a strict isomorphism between the two adjacent row-generated
cyclic modules.  These maps are compatible over the complete separated
inverse system of Artin quotients.
\end{hypothesis}

We refer to Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-wall}
and~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-zero} together as \emph{marked threshold
compatibility}.

At a zero mode, the unproved assertion therefore has two parts: construct the
marked meromorphic threshold family, and prove that its vanishing-cycle
submodule removes no part of the row-generated cyclic module.  The second
part is exactly the strict specialization isomorphism
\eqref{eq:zero-mode-specialization}.

Condition \eqref{eq:marked-threshold-map} concerns only the cyclic module and
allows arbitrary
connection on the complementary source.  The cleanest assertion known to us
that would imply marked threshold compatibility is a one-object Gamma/window
comparison.  We record it as a conjecture; it is new to this manuscript, not
a theorem from the literature.

\begin{conjecture}[Marked Gamma/window continuation]
\label{conj:gamma-window}
At every finite Artin level, ordinary degree, neutral direction, and
threshold of a gauged-admissible completion, the local Fourier object of
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-wall}, respectively the marked
meromorphic family of Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-zero}, exists and
admits a window presentation: in the fixed common equivariant-input frame,
its two oriented boundary restrictions are generated by the Gamma-framed
sections of one common window of topological \(K\)-classes; the window
contains the skyscraper class of the marked common-open point, whose Gamma
section induces the two marked rows under the flat pairing; the two boundary
identifications of the window differ by a mutation whose cone is generated
by classes supported on the wall; and, at a zero mode, the vanishing-cycle
submodule \(V_t(\mathcal M_j)\) is generated by the Gamma sections of
wall-supported window classes.
\end{conjecture}

\smallskip
\noindent\emph{Sketch of implication.}  What follows indicates why
Conjecture~\ref{conj:gamma-window} would imply both hypotheses.  It is
motivation rather than a proof, and nothing later in the paper depends on
it.  A window
mutation is supported on the wall, whereas the skyscraper of a point in the
common open is unchanged by the mutation and Euler-orthogonal to
wall-supported objects, so the induced one-object continuation carries the
marked row.  At a zero mode, wall-supported generation makes
\(V_t(\mathcal M_j)\) Euler-orthogonal to the row-generated module, which is
what the strict specialization isomorphisms
\eqref{eq:zero-mode-specialization} require.  The last step of that
implication is not proved here.  Orthogonality and nondegeneracy of the flat
pairing on the window span give
\(V_t(\mathcal M_j)\cap\widetilde K_j^\pm=0\) only if the two together span
that window; the conjecture as stated does not supply this, and neither does
nondegeneracy on the whole span by itself.
\smallskip

Pairing preservation and formal exponential labels alone do not
imply \eqref{eq:marked-threshold-map}, as
Sections~\ref{sec:incomplete-gamma}--\ref{sec:two-wall}
show.

The primary Boolean does not imply marked threshold compatibility.
Equality of the zero or nonzero primary projections supplies neither a
horizontal map of Rees modules nor compatibility with Stokes filtrations,
deck actions, boundary germs, or the Artin inverse system.  Thus the
hypotheses are independently falsifiable marked irregular-connection
statements.

\begin{remark}[Verification status and obstruction]
\label{rem:verification-status}
No instance of marked threshold compatibility is proved in this paper for a
geometric threshold of a projective master;
Remark~\ref{rem:neutral-boundary} records the analytic boundary.  Two
linear-toric calibrations are known, and the obstruction to going further
can be stated exactly.

There is a geometric calibration in the linear toric model.  For a torus
acting on a vector space, Woodward identifies the localized gauged graph
potential with Givental's \(I\)-function
\cite[Example~9.15]{WoodwardQKIII}.  Iritani constructs one global
Landau--Ginzburg Brieskorn module whose chamber completions are the toric
quantum \(D\)-modules and, for weak-Fano toric blowups, identifies its
sectorial decomposition with the Gamma-framed ambient and residual
\(K\)-groups \cite[Theorems~1.3, 7.25, 7.31 and 7.33]{IritaniToricGlobal}.
For a point in the common open set, its skyscraper class lies in the ambient
summand and its Euler row annihilates every residual summand.  Hence the
analytic one-object and marked-row clauses have a genuine linear toric-gauged
model induced by one Fourier object; the blowup
\(\operatorname{Bl}_{p}\mathbf P^2\to\mathbf P^2\) is the smallest example.
In its rank-two affine presentation with weights
\((1,0),(1,0),(1,1),(0,1)\), the common marked input is already visible in
ordinary equivariant cohomology: if \(x,y\) are the two character classes,
then the chamber maps are \((x,y)\mapsto(H,0)\) and
\((x,y)\mapsto(H-E,E)\).  Thus \(x(x+y)\) maps to the point class in both
chambers and restricts to zero on the intermediate fixed quotient
\cite[Example~5.23]{WoodwardQKII}.
This discrepancy-one direction is nonneutral, so the example calibrates the
one-object and marking conventions but is not an instance of the
load-bearing neutral hypothesis.  The ordinary-flop theorem in
Section~\ref{sec:ordinary-flop} gives the corresponding path-local neutral
model; it does not identify an arbitrary threshold inside one global master.
This does not verify marked threshold compatibility for the projective
cobordism used here: one must still compare Woodward's clutching tails on
that cobordism with the boundary modules of the toric mirror object, in the
fixed common input-and-derivative frame required above.  No such
reduction-in-stages comparison is used below.  In particular, merely
relaxing projectivity of the master does not remove this gap: the affine
presentation has a rank-two gauge torus, and passing to the rank-one
cobordism is itself the missing common-frame comparison.

There is, however, a genuinely neutral linear-toric model.  Across a crepant
toric wall, Coates--Iritani--Jiang construct global quantum connections for
the two chambers and a continuation gauge between them which intertwines the
quantum and grading connections, preserves the pairing, and carries
Gamma-framed flat sections by the Fourier--Mukai transform
\cite[Theorems~6.1 and 6.3]{CoatesIritaniJiang}.  Gluing along this gauge gives
a local one-object calibration.  After taking the non-equivariant
specialization, the graph Fourier--Mukai transform sends \(\OO_p\) to
\(\OO_{p'}\) for a point in the common open.  If \(\Theta\) denotes the
continuation gauge, its pairing compatibility and
\(\Theta s(\OO_p)=s(\OO_{p'})\) give
\[
 \rrow_{X_+,p'}(\Theta v)
 = [\Theta v,\Theta s(\OO_p))
 = [v,s(\OO_p))
 = \rrow_{X_-,p}(v).
\]
Thus it carries the marked point row exactly.  Together with Woodward's toric
\(I\)-function formula, this verifies the intrinsic marked-continuation part
of the ordinary, non-zero-mode mechanism at the QDM/\(I\)-function level in
the neutral linear-toric model.  It does not provide the
inverse-system comparison for an arbitrary projective master or the reduced
nearby-cycle assertion at a zero mode.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Cyclic-row spectral support]
\label{lem:cyclic-row-support}
Let \(T\) be an endomorphism of a finite dimensional vector space \(V\), and
let \(r\in V^*\).  Put
\[
 C_T(r)=\operatorname{span}\{r,rT,rT^2,\ldots\}\subset V^*.
\]
For every eigenvalue \(\lambda\), the restriction of \(r\) to the generalized
\(\lambda\)-eigenspace of \(T\) is nonzero if and only if the
\(\lambda\)-primary part of \(C_T(r)\) is nonzero.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(e_\lambda(T)\) be the polynomial B\'ezout projector onto the generalized
\(\lambda\)-eigenspace.  The relevant primary projection of the row is
\(re_\lambda(T)\).  It belongs to \(C_T(r)\) and vanishes exactly when the
restriction of \(r\) does.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The finite cyclic packet}

Formal smooth-endpoint quantum Kirwan surjectivity is elementary in this
setting.  Modulo the positive-energy ideal, \(D\kappa_\pm\) is the derivative
of the classical Kirwan map and is surjective.  Choose a linear section of
the reduction.  Its arbitrary lift has composite \(1+N\), with \(N\) in the
augmentation ideal, and the convergent Neumann series
\(\sum_{j\geq0}(-N)^j\) gives a right inverse.  Since
\(D\tau_{Y_\pm,-}\) is an invertible fundamental solution, the maps
\eqref{eq:endpoint-gauged-maps}
are surjective as well.

The Rees factor in \eqref{eq:rees-substitution} is needed to compare formal
monodromy.  Homogeneity
of \(D\kappa\) gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rees-homogeneity}
 \mu_YA-A\mu_W=\frac12A-\Theta_QA,
\end{equation}
because the acting group has dimension one and
\(\Theta_Q(Q^d)=c_1^{\Gm}(TW)\cdot d\,Q^d\).  After
\eqref{eq:rees-substitution}, the
\(\Theta_Q\)-term becomes \(z\partial_z\), so both endpoint maps intertwine
the \(z\)-connections with the same half-Tate shift.  The Euler-product term
intertwines because the virtual-dimension identity makes the quantum Kirwan
map homogeneous and its derivative a quantum-product morphism.  The target
bulk point \(\kappa(0)\) has positive energy and is formally parallel to the
large-radius point; this transport preserves the point row.

Choose finitely many common equivariant inputs whose images under each
surjection in \eqref{eq:endpoint-gauged-maps} span the corresponding endpoint
module; take the union
of two lift sets if necessary.  Evaluation on these images embeds the dual
endpoint module into one finite coordinate space.  After
\eqref{eq:rees-substitution}, this
embedding intertwines the dual \(z\)-connections up to the same half-Tate
shift.  Let \(T_\pm\) denote the induced half-Tate-normalized formal
monodromies.  The Krylov spans
\[
 \operatorname{span}\{\rrow_\pm,\rrow_\pm T_\pm,
   \rrow_\pm T_\pm^2,\ldots\}
\]
are the distinguished formal-monodromy subspaces inside the finite dual cyclic
modules in that coordinate space.  Injectivity here is simply dual to the
surjectivity of \eqref{eq:endpoint-gauged-maps}.

Proposition~\ref{prop:support-collapse} identifies the marked coefficient
functions before either endpoint completion is taken.
Theorem~\ref{thm:tailwise-derived} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:clutching-tail-holonomicity} supply their \((D)\)-finite tail
systems, while nonneutral directions are Laurent-finite by
Proposition~\ref{prop:gamma-ratio-reduction}.  The following elementary gluing
step records exactly what the remaining hypotheses add.

\begin{lemma}[Finite marked threshold gluing]
\label{lem:finite-threshold-gluing}
Fix \(R_N\), an ordinary degree, and a neutral direction.  Suppose the ordered
affine line is cut into finitely many tails by its thresholds, and suppose the
adjacent tail modules carry the maps in
Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-wall}
and~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-zero}.  Then their marked boundary germs glue
to one object of \(\mathcal C_N(S)\).  Its endpoint realizations have the same
zero or nonzero primary projections.  These glued objects form an inverse
system under \(R_{N+1}\to R_N\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At a sign or stability threshold, glue by the strict horizontal isomorphism
\(\Phi_j\).  It identifies the marked germs, and polynomial functional
calculus in \eqref{eq:marked-threshold-map} identifies every primary
projection.  At a zero-mode threshold, first apply the specified
strict specialization isomorphism to reduced nearby cycles, use \(\Phi_j\)
there, and return through the inverse specialization isomorphism to the
adjacent tail.  This composite intertwines formal monodromy, so polynomial
functional calculus gives the same zero or nonzero primary projections.
There are finitely many thresholds at fixed
\(R_N\), so these maps have a finite ordered composite.  Their compatibility
with Artin reduction makes the composites an inverse system.  No colimit of
the complementary equivariant source is formed.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional birational invariance of the point-row primary Boolean]
\label{thm:birational-point-primary}
Let \(Y_-\) and \(Y_+\) be smooth projective birational complex varieties.
Suppose a W{\l}odarczyk completion for this birational map is
gauged-admissible in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:gauged-admissible} and
satisfies Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-wall}
and~\ref{hyp:marked-threshold-zero} at every Artin level,
ordinary degree, neutral affine gauge direction, and threshold.
Then, for every formal-monodromy eigenvalue
\(\lambda\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:point-row-primary-boolean}
 \rrow_{Y_-}|_{\cP_\lambda(Y_-)}\ne0
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \rrow_{Y_+}|_{\cP_\lambda(Y_+)}\ne0.
\end{equation}
Here the packets are realized through the marked cyclic continuation in the
hypothesis; no continuation of the complementary source is part of the
statement.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Use the gauged-admissible completion and its orbit-cylinder class.
Lemma~\ref{lem:finite-threshold-gluing} compares the two endpoint marked
cyclic modules at every finite Artin level.  More explicitly,
polynomial functional calculus in \eqref{eq:marked-threshold-map} gives
\[
 \Phi_j\bigl(r_j^-e_\lambda(T_j^-)\bigr)
   =r_j^+e_\lambda(T_j^+)
\]
at every threshold.  Hence the relevant primary row vanishes on one side if
and only if it vanishes on the other.  At a zero-mode threshold this equality
is taken first in the row-generated reduced nearby-cycle modules and then
transported to the adjacent tails by the strict isomorphisms
\eqref{eq:zero-mode-specialization}.  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:cyclic-row-support} at the endpoints.
Because the Artin inverse system is separated, a projected row is nonzero
exactly when it survives at some finite level.  This proves both implications.
\end{proof}

Under marked threshold compatibility,
Theorem~\ref{thm:birational-point-primary} is the
global replacement for Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:rank-zero-target}.  Without those
inputs, Remark~\ref{rem:two-tail-threshold-obstruction} and the earlier
countermodels show why the conclusion
cannot be obtained from tailwise \((D)\)-finiteness, localized one-wall receivers,
or unmarked Fourier boundary data alone.
