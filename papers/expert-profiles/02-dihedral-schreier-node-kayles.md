# Expert profile map — Dihedral Schreier Node Kayles

**Paper:** `dihedral-schreier-node-kayles`<br>
**Checked:** 2026-07-15<br>
**Purpose:** editorial/referee planning and research-question discovery. No contact has been made.

## Paper spine and venue fit

The manuscript classifies the conic-only Node-Kayles residuals produced by legal tame dihedral
triples of projective involutions. Its nontrivial lead is the exact infinite nimber catalogue:
fixed-point deletion turns projective-line orbits into a short list of Schreier templates
(`K_4`, ladders, prisms, Möbius ladders, and deleted exceptional orbits), whose nimbers give
closed finite-field formulas, periodicity, a half-density theorem over admissible primes, and a
converse realization theorem.

This requires three kinds of referee competence that rarely coincide:

1. impartial games / Node Kayles and exact nimbers;
2. permutation groups, Schreier/coset actions, and involution-generated subgroups;
3. finite conic geometry and `PGL_2(q)`.

The best current fit is **Electronic Journal of Combinatorics** if the orbit-template theorem and
finite-field classification lead; **INTEGERS** is natural if the explicit nimber sequences and
periodicity lead. A graph-algorithms venue would expect substantially more complexity or
parameterized-algorithm content. An incidence-geometry venue would expect the nonregular
polyhedral or full-`PGL_2` geometry to carry more weight. These are editorial inferences.

## Conflict and selection policy

- Philippe Tranchida's 2025 paper is the closest geometric source: it develops the same dictionary
  between triples of `PGL_2(q)` involutions and off-conic points. He is the most important
  **priority-sensitive reader** and geometry commentator but a high-proximity referee.
- Tony Wong and coauthors supply exact Node-Kayles values used for ladder/prism families. Wong is a
  valuable game-value checker and **priority-sensitive reader**; pair him with an independent
  novelty referee because the manuscript consumes results from his paper directly.
- Melissa Huggan is a defining author for the upstream Nofil reduction. She is best used as a
  bridge commentator rather than the only referee.
- The safest panel is one game/algorithm referee plus one incidence/group or finite-geometry
  referee.

## Named profiles

### 1. Philippe Tranchida

- **Current affiliation / fit:** Current public records place him with the Algebra and
  Combinatorics group at Université libre de Bruxelles; his researcher page describes an FNRS
  postdoctoral position there. He authored *Triples of involutions in `PGL(2,q)` and their
  incidence geometries*, the paper's nearest object-level source.
  [ULB group](https://algcomb.ulb.be/) · [researcher page](https://sites.google.com/view/ptranchi/home) ·
  [2025 article](https://msp.org/iig/2025/22-1/p02.xhtml)
- **Role classification:** indispensable **priority-sensitive reader** and **expert commentator**
  on geometry; anonymous-referee caution because of the direct seam.
- **Caution:** the manuscript's novelty begins explicitly after his involution/triangle
  correspondence. The boundary between his coset geometries and the new fixed-point-deleted
  Schreier residual must be stated precisely.
- **Questions he is likely to press (inference):** Is legality exactly expressible in his
  self-polar/non-self-polar language? Are all subgroup possibilities and tame exceptions correct?
  Is the deleted set geometrically natural or generator-dependent? Can the residual graph be
  attached directly to the associated hypertope or coset geometry?
- **What would genuinely excite him (inference):** a theorem making the Node-Kayles nimber an
  invariant of a rank-three incidence geometry, or a completed nonregular `S_4`/`A_5` and
  `PSL_2`/`PGL_2` catalogue that feeds back new distinctions among his involution triples.

### 2. Dimitri Leemans

- **Current affiliation / fit:** Full Professor at Université libre de Bruxelles; the official
  group page lists incidence geometry, algebra, combinatorics, and computational mathematics. His
  work centers on coset geometries, hypertopes, and groups generated by involutions.
  [ULB group profile](https://algcomb.ulb.be/) · [current bio](https://leemans.dimitri.web.ulb.be/studies.html)
- **Role classification:** **likely referee fit** for incidence/groups or senior **expert
  commentator**.
- **Caution:** former supervisor and coauthor of Tranchida; if Tranchida's priority seam is
  disputed, Leemans is not the most independent adjudicator.
- **Questions he is likely to press (inference):** Is the orbit-template object invariant under
  changing a generating triple within its automorphism orbit? Does fixed-point deletion preserve
  any coset-geometry properties? Are (T)-colored edges or the uncolored simple graph the correct
  invariant? Can Magma reproduce the subgroup and orbit classifications?
- **What would genuinely excite him (inference):** a categorical or incidence-geometric meaning
  for the template nimber, or a classification showing that game values distinguish otherwise
  similar hypertopes/trialities.

### 3. Nándor Sieben

- **Current affiliation / fit:** Mathematics Professor at Northern Arizona University; his
  official page lists combinatorics and combinatorial game theory. His finite-group
  achievement/avoidance work is the clearest precedent for compressing games by subgroup/action
  data. [NAU profile](https://in.nau.edu/department-mathematics-statistics/sieben-nandor/)

- **Questions he is likely to press (inference):** Does the parity-of-orbit-multiplicity theorem

  deleting set is not `G`-stable? Can the Burnside-group reformulation be made a homomorphism or
  quotient analogous to a structure diagram? What is gained over enumerating each graph?
- **What would genuinely excite him (inference):** a general Burnside-ring-to-nimbers formalism
  with computable kernels, or a classification extending beyond dihedral groups in which subgroup
  structure predicts nimbers without brute force.

## Questions this panel collectively says the paper begs

1. **Nonregular polyhedral completion:** determine all `S_4` and `A_5` coset-template nimbers
   and their field-dependent orbit multiplicities, not only the regular row.
2. **Wild characteristic:** solve one meaningful case where the characteristic divides the
   subgroup order and identify which tame formulas survive.
3. **Full `PSL_2`/`PGL_2` escape:** find a structural bound, periodic family, or quotient for the
   generic residual rather than treating it as an unrelated wall.
4. **Burnside-ring structure:** decide whether `Φ_T` factors through a natural quotient or has a
   useful kernel beyond pair cancellation.
5. **Algorithmic consequence:** given `q` and a triple, can the residual nimber be computed in
   time polynomial in `log q` from subgroup/orbit data?
6. **Strategy extraction:** turn every zero-nimber row into a direct symmetry or pairing proof and
   every nonzero row into an explicit winning-move rule.
7. **Back to the full game:** give a sealing condition under which the conic-only value determines
   a reachable full Nofil/cap position.
8. **Sequence byproducts:** archive and, where appropriate, submit the new Möbius-ladder and
   dihedral-Cayley nimber sequences with proof-quality programs.
