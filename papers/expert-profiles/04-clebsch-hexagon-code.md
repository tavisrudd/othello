## Paper spine and likely venue fit

The paper studies the non-GRS `[6,3,4]_11` MDS code from the Clebsch hexagon. Its projective
distance-three syndrome directions are exactly a conic. The new spine is not the classical
hexagon, its `A_5` stabilizer, or its exterior-set property; it is the coding-theoretic rigidity
characterization among all six-arcs, the sharp global/local gap, the support-chirality structure,
the exact decoder/leader consequences, and the all-field argument isolating q=11. Dye, Edge, BSW,
Storme--Van Maldeghem, and Sadeh delimit the historical spine.

The likely home is *Designs, Codes and Cryptography* or *Finite Fields and Their Applications*.
With the decoding section fully integrated and the computer-assisted theorem presented as a
complete archived proof, *Advances in Mathematics of Communications* is plausible. A referee team
must span classical finite geometry and deep-hole/MDS coding theory; either audience alone can miss
half the contribution.

The names below are likely expert readers, not claims about who an editor would invite. Check all
ordinary collaboration and personal conflicts before proposing anyone.

  non-GRS distinction, the orbit claims, and whether the Clebsch example changes the deep-hole
  story rather than merely illustrating it.
- **Questions likely to press (inference).** Is “deep-hole locus is a conic” invariant under the

  classification? Is the covering-radius-three proof independent of the conic identification? Can

- **What would genuinely excite him (inference).** A conceptual classification of non-GRS MDS
  codes whose farthest syndromes form a low-degree variety; an orbit-theoretic derivation of the
  full error-distance distribution; or a theorem making the Clebsch code the first member of a

- **Caution.** Kaipa coauthored the PRS source used as the main comparison. That is an intellectual

## Historical intellectual referees

- **R. H. Dye.** His 1991 paper owns projective uniqueness, the five self-polar triangles,
  associated conic/polarity, `A_5`, the ten Brianchon points, and the relevant edge criterion. The
  paper should be able to answer: what precise statement remains after importing Dye's
  classification? The exciting answer is that the *decoder* or *uncovered locus* reconstructs
  Dye's geometry, not that the geometry exists.
- **W. L. Edge.** Edge's 1956 q=11 construction and two-system phenomenon are the priority test for
  chirality prose. Any support-bipartition claim should say exactly why it is more than translating
  Edge's two systems.
- **Ákos Seress and H. A. Wilbrink.** BSW coauthors whose paper defines the exterior-set comparison.
  They are not included in the current candidate slate because present professional availability
  was not verified in this pass; their paper nevertheless supplies questions a modern referee will
  ask about linearity, exceptional q, and the q=31 Petersen configuration.

## What belongs in this submission versus a follow-on

**High-value upgrades to the current submission:** integrate the all-field Clebsch uncovered-locus
formula; present the conceptual `A_5` fixed-point/orbit proof with the census demoted to verification;
package the complete distance oracle and ambiguity enumerator as coding theorems; state precisely
whether the leader relation reconstructs Dye's five triangles and polarity; and publish the exact
artifact. These directly answer Kaipa, Storme, Van de Voorde, Lavrauw, and De Beule.

**Follow-on questions:** compare the q=31 BSW Petersen configuration in coordinates; analyze Dye's
five-valent graph of Clebsch hexagons; classify conic-filling loci beyond `k<=7`; and seek
higher-dimensional or higher-genus deep-hole varieties. Those are the potentially striking results
for Ball, Wan, Blokhuis, and De Beule, but they should be posed rather than half-developed here.

## Cross-candidate questions the paper should answer before submission

1. What exact implications in the rigidity equivalence are new after Dye, Edge, BSW, and the
   six-arc classification are imported?
2. Can the conceptual `A_5` orbit/fixed-point proof replace the finite sweep in the headline
   theorem, leaving computation as verification?
3. Does the complete decoder reconstruct the five triangles, polarity, conic, and stabilizer from
   code data alone?
4. What is the operational meaning of chirality, stated without overstating equivariant selection?
5. Is the q=31 BSW Petersen configuration the same duad/syntheme mechanism or a genuinely distinct
   occurrence?
6. Can the all-field Clebsch formula and the `k<=7` theorem be made the conceptual ending, with
   q=11 emerging rather than being selected at the outset?
7. What part of the result extends to another non-GRS MDS code, another arc size, or a
   higher-dimensional syndrome variety?
8. Is every cited computation in the repository and immutable archive, with a complete
   computer-assisted proof rather than a “proof sketch”?
9. Is the comparison with RS/PRS decoding complexity precise about fixed-code preprocessing and
   the cost of syndrome formation?
10. Would a reader from classical geometry recognize immediately which conic, polarity, and
    exterior-line conventions are in force?
