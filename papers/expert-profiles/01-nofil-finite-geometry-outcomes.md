# Expert profile map — Nofil finite-geometry outcomes

**Paper:** `nofil-finite-geometry-outcomes`<br>
**Checked:** 2026-07-15<br>
**Purpose:** editorial/referee planning and research-question discovery. This is not an
endorsement list, and no contact has been made.

## Paper spine and venue fit

The paper's strongest eventual spine is a complete outcome classification for several
structured avoidance/achievement games, unified by a fixed-point-free mirror theorem and
accompanied by a sharp map of where that method fails. Its three audiences are:

1. combinatorial game theory and the Nofil/strong-placement-game community;
2. design theory, especially Steiner triple systems;
3. finite affine/projective geometry and caps.

The natural first venue class is **INTEGERS** (its combinatorial-games section) or
**Discrete Applied Mathematics**. **Journal of Combinatorial Designs** becomes more natural if
the final manuscript foregrounds the STS/design consequences rather than the general game
mechanism. **Electronic Journal of Combinatorics** is plausible if the projective classification
and exact method boundary are fully integrated. This venue assessment is an inference from the
paper's content and the venues of its closest cited work, not a statement by an editor.

## Conflict and selection policy

- Melissa Huggan, Svenja Huntemann, and Brett Stevens wrote the defining Nofil paper. They are
  the best **priority-sensitive readers** and terminology commentators but should normally be
  treated cautiously as anonymous referees: the new manuscript explicitly extends their Nofil
  framework and adjudicates what is first within it.
- Richard Nowakowski coauthored relevant strong-placement-game work in the same research network.
  He is exceptionally useful as a commentator, but an editor should perform the ordinary recent-
  coauthorship check before using him as an independent referee.
- A robust referee panel should contain at least one game specialist and one finite geometer.
  No single person below should be assumed to cover the sum-free, game-semantic, STS, and
  projective-geometry layers.

## Named profiles

### 1. Melissa A. Huggan

- **Current affiliation / fit:** Professor, Vancouver Island University. VIU's current faculty
  page confirms the appointment; her work includes combinatorial games, and she is a coauthor of
  the original Nofil-on-STS paper. [VIU profile](https://scitech.viu.ca/mathematics/faculty/melissa-huggan)
- **Role classification:** **priority-sensitive reader** and **expert commentator**; not a
  first-choice anonymous referee.
- **Caution:** closest prior-art author and therefore central to novelty adjudication. This is a
  scholarly-proximity caution, not an allegation of a formal conflict under any particular
  journal's policy.
- **Questions she is likely to press (inference):** Is every claimed game genuinely Nofil or only
  in a broader hypergraph-avoidance genus? Does the STS specialization use exactly the original
  normal-play convention? Which small STS values reproduce the published table? Does the abstract
  distinguish outcome class from a full nimber determination?
- **What would genuinely excite her (inference):** an infinite STS family with exact nimber zero,
  plus one non-mirror family solved by a new strategy; or a theorem that predicts Nofil values from
  recognizable design automorphisms rather than treating each design computationally.

### 2. Svenja Huntemann

- **Current affiliation / fit:** Assistant Professor at Mount Saint Vincent University; her
  official profile lists combinatorial game theory, pursuit-evasion, combinatorics, the Nofil
  paper, and her work connecting placement games with simplicial complexes.
  [MSVU profile](https://www.msvu.ca/academics/bachelor-of-science-bsc/mathematics/meet-our-faculty/svenja-huntemann/)
- **Role classification:** **priority-sensitive reader** and **expert commentator** for
  strong-placement/game-complex adequacy; referee caution because she is a Nofil coauthor.
- **Questions she is likely to press (inference):** Is the abstract mirror theorem stated at the
  level of legal complexes/links? Does it preserve only P/N outcome or the full game value? Are the
  affine and sum-free arguments instances of one complex automorphism theorem? Are terminal and
  residual conventions compatible with the strong-placement-game literature?
- **What would genuinely excite her (inference):** a functorial or simplicial-complex formulation
  of the mirror theorem, especially one that yields new enumerative/topological information about
  the legal complex in addition to a second-player strategy.

### 3. Brett Stevens

- **Current affiliation / fit:** Professor at Carleton University. His official page lists design
  theory, combinatorial game theory, and graph theory—an unusually exact match to the manuscript's
  bridge. [Carleton profile](https://carleton.ca/math/people/brett-stevens/)
- **Role classification:** **priority-sensitive reader** and **expert commentator** for design
  theory and venue fit; referee caution as a Nofil coauthor.
- **Questions he is likely to press (inference):** Which statement is genuinely about Steiner
  triple systems rather than arbitrary 3-uniform hypergraphs? Can the automorphism condition be
  read directly from a design? Do the projective and affine examples suggest a classification by
  design family? Are computed nimbers archived as reproducible design invariants?
- **What would genuinely excite him (inference):** a design-theoretic characterization of a broad
  class of STS with Nofil value zero, or a construction producing infinitely many P and N designs
  with controlled automorphism groups.

### 4. Richard J. Nowakowski

- **Current affiliation / fit:** Professor Emeritus, Dalhousie University; the official profile
  lists combinatorial game theory and games on graphs. He co-developed the games-and-complexes and
  strong-placement viewpoint used in the paper's background.
  [Dalhousie profile](https://www.dal.ca/faculty/science/math-stats/faculty-staff/our-faculty/mathematics/richard-nowakowski.html)
- **Role classification:** **expert commentator** or scoped statements-only adequacy reader;
  **likely referee fit** only
  after checking collaboration conflicts.
- **Questions he is likely to press (inference):** Is the paper proving outcome classes or game
  values? Where does Sprague–Grundy additivity actually enter? Is the mirror argument a familiar
  strategy presented as a mechanism, or does it yield a reusable theorem with sharp hypotheses?
  What happens to followers and disjunctive sums?
- **What would genuinely excite him (inference):** an honest new structural quotient of the game,
  a nontrivial nimber law rather than only P/N, or a bridge that makes the legal-complex structure
  predict game values.

### 5. Nándor Sieben

- **Current affiliation / fit:** Mathematics Professor at Northern Arizona University; his
  official page lists operator algebras, combinatorics, and combinatorial game theory. His work on
  algebraic achievement/avoidance games and structure diagrams makes him an independent expert on
  algebraically compressed game states.
  [NAU profile](https://in.nau.edu/department-mathematics-statistics/sieben-nandor/)
- **Role classification:** **likely referee fit** for CGT/algebra.
- **Questions he is likely to press (inference):** Can the cyclic sum-free game be compressed by an
  algebraic structure diagram? Does the mod-6 law extend from cyclic groups to finite abelian
  groups via their 2- and 3-torsion? Which quotient preserves P/N and which preserves the full
  nimber? Is the solver table evidence or theorem?
- **What would genuinely excite him (inference):** a finite-abelian-group classification, or a
  rigorously proved algebraic quotient that computes nimbers rather than merely supporting the
  outcome theorem.

### 6. J. Robert Johnson

- **Current affiliation / fit:** Senior Lecturer in Pure Mathematics at Queen Mary University of
  London; his official centre page lists extremal, graph, and probabilistic combinatorics. He is a
  coauthor of *Transitive Avoidance Games*, the closest independent source for the involution and
  transitivity questions behind the mirror spine.
  [QMUL profile](https://www.seresearch.qmul.ac.uk/ccant/people/jjohnson/)
- **Role classification:** **likely referee fit** for the mirror theorem and its claimed boundary.
- **Questions he is likely to press (inference):** Is fixed-point-free involution really necessary,
  or merely sufficient? How does the result sit inside transitive avoidance games, where
  transitivity alone does not force a second-player win? Can boards without such an involution be
  N or P? Is there a recognition or existence theorem for the required automorphism?
- **What would genuinely excite him (inference):** a sharp converse or density theorem for
  mirrorable transitive designs, or a solved highly transitive example where every fixed mirror
  fails and an adaptive strategy succeeds.

## Questions this panel collectively says the paper begs

1. **All finite abelian groups:** is the cyclic mod-6 law the visible shadow of a theorem in terms
   of `G[2]`, `G[3]`, and negation orbits?
2. **A converse to the mirror theorem:** within transitive triple systems, when is a P outcome
   forced by an involution, and how often do P positions occur without one?
3. **One non-mirror victory:** can a parabolic, Hermitian, or odd-projective board whose fixed
   mirrors provably fail be solved by an adaptive strategy?
4. **Design recognition:** which standard STS constructions inherit fixed-point-free
   automorphisms compatible with every block, and what infinite P families follow?
5. **Beyond P/N:** can any flagship family admit a closed nimber formula or a quotient respecting
   disjunctive sums?
6. **Game/geometry separation:** which classical cap invariants predict followers or outcomes, and
   which provably do not?
