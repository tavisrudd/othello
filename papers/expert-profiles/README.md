This directory maps likely expert readership and proof-design lenses. It is a planning aid for adversarial review,
positioning, venue selection, seminar conversations, and eventual cover-letter
referee suggestions.  It is **not** a claim that any named person is available,
willing, independent, or suitable under a particular journal's conflict policy.

Paper-specific dossiers are **required only when working on proofs for the exact paper they name**.
Do not preload or reuse them as general context for another paper.

## Recurrent-reader caution

Ball, Lavrauw, Van de Voorde, Storme, Bartoli, and De Beule recur because several papers genuinely
share finite-geometry infrastructure.  That is useful for adversarial manuscript review but poor
submission strategy if all papers move at once.  Do not repeatedly nominate the same small group;
rotate toward the paper-specific coding, game, graph, or reconstruction specialist named in each
file, and recheck recent coauthorship immediately before every submission.

## Reading the profiles

Each paper file separates three roles:

- **Likely referee fit:** technically close enough to assess the main proof and
  significance.  Final suitability still requires a fresh conflict check.
- **Expert commentator:** especially valuable for pre-submission criticism,
  historical context, or one part of the paper, but not necessarily an appropriate
  anonymous referee.
- **Priority-sensitive reader:** an author of the closest predecessor or a person
  whose classification the paper reuses.  Such a reader may be indispensable for
  accuracy while also requiring editorial caution as a referee suggestion.

The questions and excitement assessments are reasoned predictions from the person's
published work, not quotations or claims about private preferences.  They are labelled
as inference wherever the distinction might be missed.

## Evidence and maintenance rules

1. Use an authoritative institutional page, personal publication list, ORCID record,
   or publisher/venue page to verify current research fit.  Record the verification
   date and link the source.
2. Prefer recent active researchers in the referee shortlist.  Foundational or
   historical figures may remain as intellectual profiles even when they are not
   realistic referee candidates.
3. Do not record private contact information, inferred personal details, or
   unsupported claims about temperament.
4. Mark obvious risks: close coauthorship, direct ownership of a disputed priority
   claim, same institution, recent collaboration, thesis supervision, or uncertain
   current status.  Recheck all conflicts immediately before submission.
5. Keep the paper spine primary.  A famous name whose expertise is only decorative is
   less useful than a less prominent reader who can test the load-bearing theorem.
6. Distinguish what would improve the current submission from a question that would
   create a separate follow-on paper.

## Cross-paper use

Several experts legitimately recur because the papers share finite-projective,
coding, or impartial-game infrastructure.  Their profile should nevertheless be
paper-specific: the same reader may press an arc-classification question in Paper 3,
a decoding/rigidity question in Paper 4, and a reconstruction question in Paper 7.

The authoritative paper list and ship order remain in
[`../papers-index.md`](../papers-index.md); these files do not create a second paper
registry.
