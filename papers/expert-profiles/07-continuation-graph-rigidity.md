## Paper spine and venue fit

The recommended standalone target is the internally claimed finite-field combinatorial rigidity
theorem for the four-point-frame continuation graph: for `q >= 13`, every abstract graph
automorphism is induced by the ambient semilinear frame stabilizer. Equivalently, the proposed N1
package says that a single uncoloured four-map coincidence relation on `M_{0,5}(F_q)` recovers the
four forgetful-map fiber systems and admits no exotic permutations. There is not yet a manuscript
or Lean package, so these profiles must not be read as external validation of the proof.

The proposed continuation-complex reconstruction theorem is a separable second spine. The internal
package claims reconstruction of the plane, secant arrangement, and arc from binary and ternary
minimal nonfaces, but N2 remains gated by unread closest prior art and open extremal quantities. It
must be positioned as an arc-specific refinement of the classical complement/pseudocomplement
embedding literature, not as a new completion paradigm.

Likely venues: **Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series A**, **Journal of Algebraic Combinatorics**,
**Designs, Codes and Cryptography**, or **Finite Fields and Their Applications**. N1 can attract an
algebraic-graph/permutation audience; N2 alone fits finite geometry better. Combining them risks a
paper with two referee cultures and should occur only if one reconstruction principle truly unifies
them.

## Named candidates

### 1. Cheryl Praeger

- **Current position / fit:** Emeritus Professor and Senior Honorary Research Fellow, University of
  Western Australia; [UWA profile](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/persons/cheryl-praeger/).
  Her current research remains permutation groups, algebraic graph theory, designs, geometry, and
  group algorithms. She coauthored the closest cross-ratio-graph automorphism work.
- **Role:** premier **priority-sensitive reader / expert commentator** and possible senior referee for N1; because her work is
  the closest method/genre predecessor, pair with an independent referee.
- **Questions she is likely to press (inference):** Why do standard automorphism-method or
  overgroup arguments not already apply? Is the `q >= 13` threshold structural or proof-induced?
  What are all orbital/coherent-configuration relations behind the uncoloured graph?
- **What would excite her (inference):** a classification of every reduct with the same automorphism
  group; a clean primitive-group explanation excluding exotic automorphisms; sharp small-`q`
  exceptions with identified groups.

### 2. Sanming Zhou

- **Current position / fit:** Professor, University of Melbourne;
  [institutional profile](https://ms.unimelb.edu.au/people/profile?id=77) and
  [research page](https://researchers.ms.unimelb.edu.au/~sanming%40unimelb/). His interests include
  algebraic graph theory and symmetric graphs, and he coauthored *Cross ratio graphs*.
- **Role:** **Priority-sensitive reader / likely referee fit** for N1's graph-theoretic novelty and automorphism computation.
  Close-prior-art caution as for Praeger.
- **Questions:** Is the continuation graph a fusion of orbitals of a known association scheme? What
  are its spectrum, diameter, and rank? Could two nonisomorphic fields or frames yield isomorphic
  graphs?
- **Excitement:** a full graph identification—parameters, spectrum, automorphism group, and small
  exceptions—rather than an automorphism theorem in isolation.

### 3. John Bamberg

- **Current position / fit:** Associate Professor, University of Western Australia;
  [UWA profile](https://research-repository.uwa.edu.au/en/persons/john-bamberg/). He works at the
  intersection of finite geometry, group actions, graph theory, algebraic combinatorics, and
  computation.
- **Role:** excellent cross-spine **likely referee fit**: able to assess both graph rigidity and recovery
  of incidence geometry.
- **Questions:** Can clique/fiber recovery be phrased canonically via coherent configurations? Can
  FinInG/GAP verify all small fields and expose the exceptional automorphism groups? Does the graph
  recognize the field order without being told it?
- **Excitement:** a reconstruction functor with a practical recognition algorithm; a new symmetric
  graph family or association scheme arising from continuation geometry.

### 4. Massimiliano Mella

- **Current position / fit:** Professor of Geometry and department director, University of Ferrara;
  [institutional profile](https://docente.unife.it/docenti-en/massimiliano.mella/). His joint work with
  Andrea Bruno proves the algebraic automorphism theorem for `M_{0,n}` that is the most obvious—but
  non-subsuming—comparison for N1.
- **Role:** high-value **priority-sensitive reader / expert commentator** and plausible referee for the moduli-space positioning;
  not the sole reviewer for the finite graph proof.
- **Questions:** Precisely which structure of the four forgetful maps is recoverable from the graph?
  Does the finite-point permutation theorem lift to an algebraic or scheme-theoretic statement? Is
  there an `M_{0,n}` generalization?
- **Excitement:** a general theorem that a sparse uncoloured finite-field reduct determines the
  forgetful-map geometry of `M_{0,n}`, thereby creating a genuine bridge to moduli rigidity.

### 5. Klaus Metsch

- **Role:** indispensable **priority-sensitive reader / expert commentator** and strong referee for N2, but close-prior-art

- **Questions:** Is the arrangement-completion lemma already a corollary of a general embedding

- **Excitement:** a substantially sharper profile-sensitive completion bound or a genuinely new
  uniqueness theorem for high-multiplicity secant arrangements, not merely a Deza-style reproof.

### 8. Simeon Ball

- **Current position / fit:** Universitat Politècnica de Catalunya;

- **Role:** strong **likely referee fit** for the arc-specific content and whether the recovery theorem

- **Questions:** Can tangent-polynomial or MDS-extension techniques reconstruct the frame more

- **Excitement:** a broad rigidity theorem for natural arc families, or a sharp characterization of

## Conflict and close-prior-art cautions

- Praeger and Zhou are authors of the closest cross-ratio-graph work; Mella is author of the closest
  `M_{0,n}` automorphism theorem; Metsch is author of the closest embedding corpus. That makes their
  technical reactions unusually valuable, but no one of them should be the sole novelty gate.
- Praeger and Bamberg are both at UWA and have coauthored relevant group/graph work. Do not place
  them in nominally independent slots on the same slate. More generally, editors must perform the
  ordinary recent-coauthorship and institutional-conflict screening.
- N2's complement-completion engine has substantial prior art. What may excite a specialist is the
  recovery of the *distinguished arc* and full faithfulness, especially with improved bounds—not the
  generic slogan “the complex reconstructs the plane.”
- Drake--Sané and Beutelspacher belong to the closest complement/pseudocomplement embedding lineage,
  but their full-text relevance and current professional availability were not verified in this
  pass. Their omission from the named slate is a documented open profile task, not evidence that
  Metsch alone represents that prior-art community.

## Questions this community leaves for the paper spine

- Determine the exact automorphism group for every small `q`, and explain each exception.
- Compute the spectrum/coherent configuration and decide whether the graph is a known fusion or a
  genuinely new algebraic graph family.
- Generalize the finite `M_{0,5}` reduct theorem to `M_{0,n}` or other forgetful-map systems.
- Find the minimum relational data—graph, colored graph, or ternary hypergraph—needed to recover an
  arbitrary arc and its plane.
- Sharpen the completion bounds using the high-multiplicity profile special to secants of an arc.
- Develop stability and recognition algorithms: how much corrupted continuation data can be
  tolerated while the underlying geometry remains recoverable?
